\begin{abstract}
We formulate gauge redundancy as a precise lens-type realization rather than
as the unique response to every nonfaithful description.  For a declared group
action $\Gg\curvearrowright\Conn$, the orbit map
$q:\Conn\to\Conn/\Gg$ identifies gauge-related representatives.  At the
set level, every invariant map factors uniquely through this quotient.  That
universal property organizes redundancy but does not select the group, action,
bundle, connection, observables, or dynamics.

We separate two global-section questions that were conflated in the first
version.  A principal bundle $P\to Y$ is globally defined by its transition
cocycle even when it has no global section; a global section exists precisely
when $P$ is trivial.  A field-space gauge fixing is instead a section of the
orbit map on a specified regularity class and orbit stratum.  Nonunique
representatives alone do not obstruct such a section.  A genuine global
gauge-fixing obstruction must be proved in the chosen topological, smooth, or
stacky category, as in Gribov--Singer phenomena.

On a supplied principal bundle, a connection gives local gauge potentials and
covariant curvature.  Yang--Mills dynamics follows only after one supplies a
base metric, structure group, invariant bilinear form, coupling, matter
representation, boundary conditions, and local action.  Other gauge-invariant
actions exist, so lens-type redundancy does not prove uniqueness of
Yang--Mills theory or select the Standard Model gauge group.  Program B2
therefore establishes a typed redundancy and gauge-fixing framework plus a
conditional Yang--Mills realization.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Revision note for version 2}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[Supersedes.]
Version~1 of Program B2.

\item[Reason.]
The first version treated nonunique representatives as a failure of global
description, asserted that every lens obstruction forces a unique gauge
encoding, conflated a section of a spacetime principal bundle with a section
of the field-space orbit map, and described connections and Yang--Mills
structure as if they followed from redundancy alone.

\item[Resolution.]
Version~2 fixes the acting group and category, proves the quotient universal
property, distinguishes the two section problems, and makes global gauge
fixing a typed right-inverse question.  Principal bundles and connections are
canonical gauge realizations only after their geometric data are supplied.
Yang--Mills equations are derived only from a declared local action.

\item[Retained content.]
Gauge transformations can organize redundant representatives; invariant
quantities factor through gauge orbits; local potentials are related by bundle
transition functions; and a connection compares local representatives without
selecting a preferred gauge.

\item[Open boundary.]
No theorem here selects the gauge group, principal bundle, global gauge
fixing, Standard Model representation content, coupling constants, anomaly
cancellation, or a unique Yang--Mills action from the lens profile.
\end{description}

\section*{How to read Program B2}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How to read Program B2}
B2 begins with redundancy and asks how much extra structure is needed before
that redundancy becomes gauge theory.  The answer proceeds through a declared
group action, its orbit quotient, a principal bundle, a connection, a
field-space gauge-fixing problem, and finally a selected local action.

\paragraph{The central picture in plain language.}
A gauge orbit is a family of different mathematical representatives assigned
the same declared physical content.  An invariant quantity has the same value
on every member of that orbit, so it factors through the orbit space.  This
universal property organizes redundancy.  It does not tell us which group
acts, which orbits are physical, or which invariant quantities form a complete
observable set.

\paragraph{Two different section problems.}
A section of $P\to Y$ chooses one point in each principal-bundle fiber over
spacetime and exists globally exactly when that bundle is trivial.  A section
of $q:\Conn\to\Conn/\Gg$ chooses one connection representative from each
gauge orbit in a declared field-space domain.  A bundle can be globally
well-defined without the first section, and local gauge slices can exist
without gluing to the second.  Conflating these maps turns ordinary bundle
topology into a false gauge-fixing conclusion.

\paragraph{A simple abelian case.}
For a $U(1)$ potential, local representatives related by
$A\mapsto A+d\chi$ lie on one gauge orbit after the allowed boundary behavior
of $\chi$ is fixed.  Curvature $F=dA$ is invariant under this transformation.
This illustrates orbit redundancy and an invariant diagnostic, but it does
not select $U(1)$, the base manifold, a coupling, or the Maxwell/Yang--Mills
action.

\paragraph{Argument map.}
Sections~1--2 define the group action, quotient, and invariant
factorization.  Section~3 separates principal-bundle topology from
field-space gauge fixing.  Sections~4--5 introduce connections, local
potentials, stabilizers, slices, and genuine global obstructions.  Section~6
derives Yang--Mills equations only from the supplied geometric and action
data.  Sections~7--9 separate gravity, quantization, and Standard Model claims
and state the exact remaining source problem.

\paragraph{Scope boundary.}
Lens-type nonfaithfulness is compatible with many groups, bundles, actions,
and physical interpretations.  B2 provides the typed gauge framework and a
conditional Yang--Mills realization.  It does not select the Standard Model
gauge group, matter representations, anomalies, couplings, or quantization.

\section{Scope and Imported Data}

Program A0 supplies typed reductions, admissible domains, and exact
factorization criteria~\cite{A0}.  Program B0 defines a lens profile as
nonfaithfulness, redundancy, or multiple effective representatives under a
declared reduction; it does not identify every lens profile with a lens space
or gauge theory~\cite{B0}.  Program B1 proves that smooth transport becomes a
connection only on a supplied bundle and warns that internal gauge transport
and frame transport are different physical types~\cite{B1}.

The present paper asks:
\begin{quote}
When does descriptive redundancy have the mathematical form of gauge
redundancy, when does a global gauge choice fail, and what additional data are
required to obtain Yang--Mills dynamics?
\end{quote}

The answer has four levels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a group action and its quotient;
\item a principal bundle and its local sections;
\item a connection and field-space gauge fixing; and
\item a local gauge-invariant action.
\end{enumerate}
Each level imports data not contained in the coarse predicate ``more than one
representative survives.''

\section{Typed Redundancy and the Orbit Quotient}

\subsection{A declared action}

\begin{definition}[Gauge-redundancy datum]\label{def:gauge-datum}
A \emph{gauge-redundancy datum} consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a configuration object $\Conn$ in a declared category;
\item a group or group object $\Gg$;
\item an action $\Gg\curvearrowright\Conn$; and
\item a declaration of which subgroup acts redundantly, including its boundary
and asymptotic conditions.
\end{enumerate}
At the set level, the orbit of $A\in\Conn$ is
\[
 [A]=\{u\cdot A:u\in\Gg\},
\]
and the orbit map is
\[
 q:\Conn\longrightarrow\Conn/\Gg,\qquad q(A)=[A].
\]
\end{definition}

The category matters.  A set-theoretic orbit space, a topological quotient, a
smooth quotient, and the quotient stack $[\Conn/\Gg]$ retain different
information.  In particular, the stack remembers stabilizer groups that a
coarse orbit space can discard.

\begin{definition}[Gauge lens-type realization]\label{def:lens}
The datum of Definition~\ref{def:gauge-datum} is a \emph{gauge lens-type
realization} when the orbit map is noninjective on the declared domain:
\[
 A\ne A'\quad\text{and}\quad q(A)=q(A')
\]
for at least one pair.  It is a \emph{gauge-fixing obstruction} only when the
orbit map has no section of the required regularity on the required domain or
stratum.
\end{definition}

This separates redundancy from obstruction.  Redundancy says that an orbit
contains several representatives.  Obstruction says that no globally
compatible representative can be selected in a specified category.

\subsection{The quotient universal property}

\begin{theorem}[Set-level invariant factorization]\label{thm:quotient}
Let $Z$ be a set.  A map $F:\Conn\to Z$ is gauge invariant,
\[
 F(u\cdot A)=F(A)
 \qquad
 (u\in\Gg,\ A\in\Conn),
\]
if and only if there is a unique map
$\overline F:\Conn/\Gg\to Z$ such that
\[
 F=\overline F\circ q.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $F$ is invariant, define $\overline F([A])=F(A)$.  Invariance makes this
well defined.  Since $q$ is surjective, the factorization determines
$\overline F$ uniquely.  The converse is immediate because
$q(u\cdot A)=q(A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What is universal]
The orbit quotient is universal for invariant set-valued diagnostics.  This
does not make one physical gauge theory universal or unique.
\end{corollary}

In a topological or smooth category, the same conclusion requires the
corresponding categorical quotient and continuity or smoothness of
$\overline F$.  Such a quotient may be singular or fail to be a manifold.

\subsection{Nonuniqueness is not failure}

\begin{proposition}[Redundancy alone is not a global obstruction]
\label{prop:redundancy}
Noninjectivity of $q$ neither implies that the underlying principal bundle is
undefined nor proves that a global field-space gauge fixing fails.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The trivial bundle $Y\times G$ is globally defined and has a global section,
yet its connection space has many representatives related by nonconstant gauge
transformations.  Conversely, failure of a global section of a nontrivial
principal bundle concerns $P\to Y$, while field-space gauge fixing concerns
$q:\Conn\to\Conn/\Gg$.  These are different maps.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Invariant versus complete observables}

\begin{definition}[Separating invariant family]
A family of invariant functions $\{F_i:\Conn\to Z_i\}_{i\in I}$ is
\emph{separating} on a domain if
\[
 F_i(A)=F_i(A')\text{ for all }i
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 q(A)=q(A').
\]
\end{definition}

Gauge invariance alone does not imply separation of orbits.  A selected
observable family may be incomplete.  Consequently, failure of chosen
diagnostics to distinguish two configurations is not by itself proof that the
configurations are gauge equivalent.

\section{Principal Bundles and the First Section Problem}

\subsection{Global bundles from local data}

Let $Y$ be a paracompact smooth manifold, $G$ a Lie group, and
$\{U_\alpha\}$ an open cover.  Smooth transition functions
\[
 g_{\beta\alpha}:U_\alpha\cap U_\beta\longrightarrow G
\]
with
\[
 g_{\alpha\alpha}=e,\qquad
 g_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\beta\alpha}^{-1},\qquad
 g_{\gamma\beta}g_{\beta\alpha}=g_{\gamma\alpha}
\]
on triple overlaps glue the local products $U_\alpha\times G$ into a
globally defined principal $G$-bundle $P\to Y$~\cite{KobayashiNomizu,Steenrod}.

\begin{proposition}[No preferred section is required]
\label{prop:no-section-required}
The bundle $P$ is globally defined by the transition cocycle.  Its definition
does not require a preferred global section.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient of the disjoint union
$\coprod_\alpha U_\alpha\times G$ by
\[
 (x,h)_\alpha\sim(x,g_{\beta\alpha}(x)h)_\beta
\]
is well defined and transitive precisely because of the cocycle equation.
Local products supply the bundle charts.
\end{proof}

\subsection{When a spacetime section exists}

\begin{theorem}[Principal-bundle section criterion]
\label{thm:bundle-section}
A principal $G$-bundle $P\to Y$ admits a continuous global section if and only
if it is continuously trivial.  In the smooth category, it admits a smooth
global section if and only if it is smoothly trivial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $s:Y\to P$ is a section, the map
\[
 Y\times G\longrightarrow P,\qquad (x,h)\longmapsto s(x)h
\]
is a principal-bundle isomorphism, with the corresponding regularity.
The trivial bundle has the section $x\mapsto(x,e)$.
\end{proof}

Thus lack of a global section is a nontriviality statement about $P\to Y$.
It is not a claim that the bundle lacks global meaning.

\subsection{Three section notions}

The word ``section'' is used for three different maps:
\begin{description}
\item[Bundle section.]
$s:Y\to P$, with $\pi_P\circ s=\id_Y$.

\item[Associated-field section.]
$\phi:Y\to E=P\times_\rho V$, describing a field in an associated bundle.

\item[Orbit-map gauge fixing.]
$\sigma:\Orb_0\to\Conn_0$, with
$q\circ\sigma=\id_{\Orb_0}$ on a declared field-space domain or stratum.
\end{description}
Existence of one does not imply existence of the others.

\section{Gauge Transformations and Connections}

\subsection{Vertical automorphisms}

For a fixed principal bundle $P\to Y$, its gauge group is
\[
 \Gg(P)=\Aut_Y(P),
\]
the $G$-equivariant bundle automorphisms covering $\id_Y$.  Equivalently,
under standard identifications,
\[
 \Gg(P)\cong\Gamma(\Ad P),
\qquad
\Ad P=P\times_{\Ad}G.
\]

Let $\Conn(P)$ denote the affine space of principal connections on $P$.
Locally, a connection is represented by a Lie-algebra-valued one-form
$A_\alpha$.  A local gauge transformation $u_\alpha:U_\alpha\to G$ acts by
\[
 A_\alpha\longmapsto A_\alpha^{u_\alpha}
 =u_\alpha^{-1}A_\alpha u_\alpha+u_\alpha^{-1}du_\alpha.
\]
The curvature
\[
 F_{A_\alpha}=dA_\alpha+A_\alpha\wedge A_\alpha
\]
transforms covariantly:
\[
 F_{A_\alpha^{u_\alpha}}
 =u_\alpha^{-1}F_{A_\alpha}u_\alpha.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Local potentials, global connection]
\label{prop:global-connection}
Local potentials satisfying the connection transition law on overlaps define
one global principal connection.  Distinct local potentials related by gauge
transformations can therefore represent the same global connection data in
different local sections.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The overlap law is exactly the compatibility condition for local connection
one-forms to arise as pullbacks of one principal connection by local sections.
Changing a local section produces the displayed gauge-transformation law.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Redundant and physical transformations}

The choice of $\Gg$ must include boundary conditions.  Transformations that
approach the identity at a boundary may be declared redundant, while large,
asymptotic, or boundary-supported transformations can act on charges or edge
degrees of freedom.  Quotienting by the wrong group can erase physical data.
Program B2 therefore does not identify every vertical-looking transformation
with pure redundancy.

\subsection{Gauge and circle profiles may coexist}

A gauge connection has holonomy around loops.  Its orbit redundancy is a
lens-type feature, while its return transport is a circle-type feature in the
terminology of Program B0.  The profiles can coexist in one realization.  This
is another reason not to identify one obstruction label with one unique
physical sector.

\section{Field-Space Gauge Fixing: The Second Section Problem}

\subsection{The orbit map}

Fix a Sobolev or smooth completion $\Conn_k(P)$, a compatible gauge group
$\Gg_{k+1}(P)$, boundary conditions, and a domain or orbit stratum
$\Conn_0\subseteq\Conn_k(P)$.  Let
\[
 \Orb_0=q(\Conn_0).
\]

\begin{definition}[Global field-space gauge fixing]
\label{def:gauge-fixing}
A \emph{global gauge fixing} in a declared category is a section
\[
 \sigma:\Orb_0\longrightarrow\Conn_0,
\qquad
 q\circ\sigma=\id_{\Orb_0},
\]
with the required continuity, smoothness, locality, covariance, or other
regularity.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Typed gauge-fixing obstruction criterion]
\label{thm:gauge-fixing}
The gauge lens-type realization has a global gauge-fixing obstruction in the
declared category exactly when the orbit map $q:\Conn_0\to\Orb_0$ has no
section with the declared regularity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the right-inverse definition of a section.  Its value is the typing:
the domain, orbit stratum, quotient, boundary conditions, and regularity cannot
be omitted.
\end{proof}

Noninjectivity of $q$ is necessary for nontrivial redundancy but does not decide
Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-fixing}.  Some actions admit global slices; others do
not.

\subsection{Stabilizers and singular strata}

The stabilizer of $A$ is
\[
 \Gg_A=\{u\in\Gg:u\cdot A=A\}.
\]
If stabilizers vary, the coarse quotient is generally stratified rather than a
smooth manifold.  On a free proper finite-dimensional action, the orbit map is
a principal $\Gg$-bundle and a global section exists exactly when that
bundle is trivial.  Infinite-dimensional gauge theory requires analytic
completions and slice theorems before this analogy may be used.

\begin{proposition}[Local slices do not imply a global slice]
\label{prop:local-global}
Suppose a slice theorem supplies local sections of $q$ near every orbit in
$\Orb_0$.  It does not follow that these local sections glue to one global
section.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On overlaps, two local slices differ by transition functions valued in the
gauge group or residual stabilizer.  A global slice requires these transitions
to be a trivial cocycle in the chosen category.  Local existence alone does
not prove that.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gribov--Singer phenomena}

For important nonabelian gauge theories on compact bases, no single global
continuous gauge choice covers the full configuration space under the usual
hypotheses.  This is the content of Gribov--Singer-type results
~\cite{Gribov,Singer}.  They are examples of
Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-fixing}, not a theorem that every redundancy action has
the same obstruction.

Practical gauge conditions such as Coulomb, Lorenz, or axial gauge must record:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the function space and regularity;
\item the allowed gauge group and boundary behavior;
\item the orbit stratum and stabilizers;
\item residual gauge transformations; and
\item whether the condition is local, global, unique, or only perturbative.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conditional Yang--Mills Realization}

\subsection{Required data}

\begin{assumption}[Yang--Mills realization]\label{ass:ym}
Supply:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an oriented pseudo-Riemannian base $(Y,g)$;
\item a principal $G$-bundle $P\to Y$;
\item a connection $A$ with curvature $F_A$;
\item an $\Ad$-invariant nondegenerate bilinear form
$B$ on $\mathfrak g=\Lie(G)$;
\item a coupling normalization $g_{\rm YM}$;
\item boundary conditions and, if present, matter representations and a matter
action.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

The pure Yang--Mills action is
\[
 S_{\rm YM}[A]
 =-\frac{1}{2g_{\rm YM}^2}
 \int_Y B(F_A\wedge *F_A).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Gauge invariance and Yang--Mills equation]
\label{thm:ym}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:ym}, the action is invariant under gauge
transformations preserving the declared boundary conditions.  Its stationary
points under compactly supported connection variations satisfy
\[
 d_A *F_A=0.
\]
With a gauge-covariant matter action, the right-hand side is the corresponding
covariantly conserved current.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Curvature transforms by conjugation.  $\Ad$-invariance of $B$ makes the
integrand gauge invariant.  For a variation $A\mapsto A+ta$,
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}F_{A+ta}=d_Aa.
\]
Integration by parts, using the boundary conditions, gives
\[
 \delta S_{\rm YM}
 =-\frac{1}{g_{\rm YM}^2}
 \int_Y B(a\wedge d_A*F_A),
\]
up to the dimension- and sign-dependent conventional placement of $*$.
Arbitrariness of $a$ yields the equation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Redundancy does not select the action}

\begin{theorem}[No unique Yang--Mills theory from a lens profile]
\label{thm:no-unique-ym}
The gauge lens-type data of Definition~\ref{def:lens} do not determine a unique
gauge group, principal bundle, invariant pairing, coupling, matter
representation, or local action.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Different groups, including $U(1)$, $SU(2)$, and product groups, all act
nontrivially on connection spaces.  Even after $(Y,G,P)$ are fixed, distinct
$\Ad$-invariant bilinear forms and coupling coefficients can be used.  In four
dimensions one may add the gauge-invariant topological term
\[
 \theta\int_Y B(F_A\wedge F_A),
\]
and effective actions may include gauge-invariant higher-derivative operators.
These choices leave the existence of gauge orbits unchanged while altering
the action or quantum theory.  Hence the coarse redundancy predicate cannot
select them.
\end{proof}

For a compact simple Lie algebra, an invariant symmetric bilinear form is
unique up to scale, but that statement already imports the group and
simplicity.  Product and abelian factors admit additional couplings or kinetic
mixing.  A uniqueness theorem for a particular Yang--Mills action must state
its dimension, locality, derivative order, parity, field content, and
equivalence relation.

\section{Relation to Gravity, Quantization, and the Standard Model}

\subsection{Gauge versus gravitational connections}

Program B1 and this paper both use connections because connections encode
smooth parallel transport.  The physical typing differs:
\begin{itemize}
\item a gravitational Levi--Civita or spin connection acts on tangent, frame,
coframe, or spin geometry after a metric is supplied;
\item an internal gauge connection acts on a declared internal principal
bundle and its associated matter representations.
\end{itemize}
These may be combined in one product or extension bundle, but equality is not
implied by shared formalism.

\subsection{Quantization is downstream}

Gauge quotienting is a classical structural operation.  BRST/BV complexes,
ghosts, gauge-fixed propagators, Hilbert spaces, path integrals, anomalies, and
quantum states require independent constructions.  No Born probability or
complex-Hilbert theorem follows from the orbit quotient.

\subsection{No Standard Model selection at B2}

The Standard Model requires, among other data:
\begin{itemize}
\item the global form of the gauge group;
\item representations and hypercharge normalization;
\item anomaly cancellation;
\item chiral family content;
\item Higgs and Yukawa sectors;
\item coupling values and scale transport; and
\item an observable comparison map.
\end{itemize}
Program B2 supplies none of these from redundancy alone.  Later finite-carrier
and q79 results may provide compatible realizations at their declared tiers,
but B2 cannot retroactively convert compatibility into uniqueness or
no-parameter prediction.

\section{Scoped B2 Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Gauge redundancy with conditional Yang--Mills realization]
\label{thm:scoped}
For the typed data declared in this paper:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a gauge lens-type realization is a noninjective orbit map for a declared
group action;
\item invariant set-valued diagnostics factor uniquely through the orbit
quotient;
\item nonunique representatives do not imply failure of a global bundle or a
global field-space gauge fixing;
\item a principal bundle is globally defined by its cocycle and has a global
section exactly when it is trivial;
\item a field-space gauge fixing is a section of the orbit map on a declared
domain, stratum, and regularity class;
\item local slices do not by themselves provide a global slice;
\item a supplied principal connection yields the standard local gauge
potential and curvature laws; and
\item Yang--Mills equations follow from the action of
Assumption~\ref{ass:ym}, while the lens profile alone selects neither that
action nor the gauge group.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items 1--2 are Definition~\ref{def:lens} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient}.  Item~3 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:redundancy}.  Item~4 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-section-required} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:bundle-section}.  Items 5--6 are
Theorem~\ref{thm:gauge-fixing} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:local-global}.  Item~7 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:global-connection}.  Item~8 is
Theorems~\ref{thm:ym} and~\ref{thm:no-unique-ym}.
\end{proof}

\section{Version Delta and Research Frontier}

Relative to version~1, this revision:
\begin{itemize}
\item replaces ``gauge is necessary and unique'' by a typed group-action and
quotient construction;
\item retains the quotient universal property only in its stated category;
\item separates multiple representatives from failure of a section;
\item distinguishes sections of $P\to Y$, associated fields, and the
field-space orbit map;
\item states that a principal bundle is global even without a global section;
\item makes gauge-fixing obstruction a right-inverse theorem on a specified
domain and regularity class;
\item treats Gribov--Singer results as realization-specific no-go theorems,
not as consequences of redundancy alone;
\item distinguishes redundant gauge transformations from boundary or
asymptotic transformations that may carry physical charges;
\item permits lens and circle profiles to coexist in one gauge connection;
and
\item derives Yang--Mills equations only from an explicit action while
withdrawing uniqueness of the group and dynamics.
\end{itemize}

The next source theorem must start from one selected upper MTT carrier and emit:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the internal structure group and its global form;
\item the principal/associated bundles and transition cocycles;
\item the physical redundancy subgroup including boundary conditions;
\item the connection, invariant pairing, and action normalization;
\item the chiral matter representations and anomaly certificate; and
\item a commuting descent to the accepted finite operators and observables.
\end{enumerate}
Until then, B2 is a rigorous gauge-redundancy framework and conditional
Yang--Mills reconstruction, not a derivation of the Standard Model gauge
sector.

\section{Conclusion}

Gauge redundancy is a particularly clear realization of the lens profile once
the acting group and quotient are declared.  Its exact universal statement is
the factorization of invariant diagnostics through the orbit space.  That
statement organizes representatives; it does not imply that a global bundle
fails to exist or that no gauge fixing is possible.

The geometry becomes precise on a supplied principal bundle.  Local sections
give local potentials, a connection gives their compatible transport, and
curvature transforms covariantly.  A global spacetime section and a global
field-space gauge fixing remain different questions.  Yang--Mills dynamics
then requires a metric, invariant pairing, coupling, boundary conditions, and
action.  This revised hierarchy retains the useful MTT insight that gauge
freedom can encode redundancy while removing uniqueness and inevitability
claims that the lens profile alone cannot prove.
