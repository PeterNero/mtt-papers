\begin{abstract}
Across physics, biology, cognition, artificial intelligence, and large-scale social systems,
effective descriptions exhibit recurring features: (i) stability only within bounded regimes,
(ii) abrupt irreversible breakdowns, (iii) hidden correlations among variables treated as independent,
and (iv) long-horizon behavior that admits no general shortcut prediction even when underlying
dynamics are deterministic. These features are typically analyzed in isolation and explained by
domain-specific mechanisms.

This paper gives a unifying structural framework—\emph{projection-limited coherence}—that
explains these features without assuming any particular substrate or equation of motion.
The framework isolates four minimal ingredients:
(1) an underlying measurable dynamical system $(\X,\Borel(\X),\Phi_t)$,
(2) a many-to-one measurable projection $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ defining effective (shadow) variables,
(3) a finite \emph{coherence capacity} controlling the domain where truncation is well-posed,
and (4) generic nonexistence of a global measurable section (right-inverse) for $\Pi$ across
admissibility boundaries.
From these alone we prove:
(A) \emph{structural irreversibility} of shadow evolution even when $\Phi_t$ is invertible,
(B) \emph{computational irreducibility} of basin membership and outcome selection for broad classes
of systems with nontrivial boundary structure,
and (C) \emph{hidden compatibility constraints} among shadow variables arising from shared pre-images,
yielding “nonlocal” relations without causal interaction.

We then instantiate the framework in Modal Triplet Theory (MTT), where observable four-dimensional
physics arises as the coherent-sector projection of deterministic higher-dimensional dynamics via a
joint Riesz projector and internal pushforward. We show how quantum mechanics, general relativity,
probabilistic outcomes, and the arrow of time emerge as shadow descriptions within admissible slabs.
Finally, we apply the same formal structure to brains and consciousness, artificial intelligence
architectures, and civilizations, using a uniform template (underlying dynamics, projection, coherent
sector, capacity, boundaries, and induced hidden relations).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Motivation: effective description as a universal constraint}
Science advances through effective description: reduced variables, stable summaries, and predictive
laws that ignore microscopic detail. This is not merely pragmatic. In most nontrivial systems,
full microstate tracking is impossible, and any operational theory must proceed by projection
from an inaccessible or intractable space of configurations to a smaller space of observables.

Across domains, the same phenomena recur:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Effective theories are valid only on bounded regimes and fail abruptly at boundaries.
\item Variables treated as independent exhibit hidden relations and unexplained constraints.
\item Long-horizon prediction resists shortcut computation even under deterministic microdynamics.
\item Irreversibility and arrows of time appear even when fundamental laws are time-reversal symmetric.
\end{itemize}
These are often treated as separate puzzles: measurement in quantum theory, information loss in
black holes, fine-tuning and selection in cosmology, emergence and collapse in cognition,
fragility in large-scale engineered and social systems.

\subsection{Central thesis}
We claim these phenomena share a single structural origin:
\emph{projection-limited coherence}. The mechanism is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item an underlying dynamical system $(\X,\Phi_t)$;
\item a many-to-one projection $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ defining a shadow description;
\item a finite margin (capacity) on which $\Pi$ supports controlled effective evolution;
\item loss of global reconstructability (no global section) once admissibility boundaries exist.
\end{enumerate}
From this, irreversibility, hidden relations, and computational irreducibility follow without
additional dynamical assumptions such as fundamental randomness.

\subsection{Why Modal Triplet Theory matters here}
MTT provides a mathematically explicit instantiation of this structure in fundamental physics:
a joint Riesz projector $\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}$ selecting a coherent sector and an observable pushforward
$P=I\circ\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}$, yielding effective 4D physics (QM/QFT/GR) as a shadow of invertible
higher-dimensional dynamics. Our general framework is written to be independent of MTT, but MTT
serves as a proof that the structure can be made physically complete and mathematically controlled.

\subsection{What is proved vs. what is interpreted}
We distinguish:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Structural theorems (proved):} reconstruction obstructions, shadow irreversibility,
compatibility constraints from shared pre-images, and (under explicit hypotheses) irreducibility
statements.
\item \textbf{Instantiations (proved within MTT corpus):} bounded projectors via Riesz calculus,
controlled truncation, GR/QM derivations, and measurement/entanglement as disturbance+stabilization.
\item \textbf{Cross-domain applications (structural mapping):} brains, AI, civilizations. These are
not “physics equations applied to society,” but the same projection/coherence structure instantiated
in different substrates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Preliminaries and Global Notation}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Standard Borel spaces and measurable maps}
We work in the category of standard Borel measurable spaces. A \emph{standard Borel space} is
a measurable space isomorphic (as a measurable space) to $(S,\Borel(S))$ for some Polish space $S$.
Standard results: measurable selection theorems, disintegration of measures, and regular conditional
probabilities hold in this setting (see \cite{Kechris1995,Bogachev2007}).

\subsection{Dynamical systems}
Let $(\X,\Borel(\X))$ be a standard Borel space.
A (discrete-time) dynamical system is a measurable map $\Phi:\X\to\X$.
A (continuous-time) measurable flow is a family $(\Phi_t)_{t\in\R}$ with $\Phi_{t+s}=\Phi_t\circ\Phi_s$
and joint measurability; we will work primarily with discrete time (iteration) because it is sufficient
for the structural results and avoids technicalities of measurable flows. When needed, we treat $\Phi_t$
for $t\in \Delta \N$.

\subsection{Shadow maps and sections}
Let $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ be measurable with $\Y$ standard Borel. A \emph{section} (right-inverse) of $\Pi$
on a subset $E\subseteq \Y$ is a measurable map $S:E\to\X$ such that $\Pi\circ S=\Id_E$.

\subsection{Admissibility and capacity}
We treat admissibility as a \emph{domain of controlled truncation}:
a subset $\Aad\subseteq \X$ on which the shadow description is stable.
A \emph{capacity functional} $\Cap:\X\to[0,\infty)$ encodes the admissibility margin, with $\Cap>0$ on
$\Aad$ and $\Cap=0$ on the boundary.

\section{Projection-Limited Coherence: Formal Framework}
\label{sec:PLC}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Underlying system and projection}
We now state the structural framework precisely.

\begin{assumption}[Underlying measurable dynamics]\label{ass:underlying}
Let $(\X,\Borel(\X))$ be a standard Borel space and let $\Phi:\X\to\X$ be a measurable map.
In the strongest form (used for irreversibility statements), we assume $\Phi$ is invertible
with measurable inverse. (In many physical instantiations, $\Phi$ is a time-$\tau$ map of a flow.)
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Shadow map]\label{def:shadowmap}
Let $(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$ be a standard Borel space and let $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ be measurable.
We call $\Pi$ the \emph{shadow map} (projection). Elements of $\Y$ are \emph{shadow states}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
We do not assume $\Pi$ is linear, continuous, or generated by averaging; it may be any measurable
coarse-graining. In MTT, $\Pi$ is a bounded projector (Riesz/spectral projector) composed with
fiber integration, which is far more structured \cite{NeroFixedPointsI2025,NeroMTTFoundation2025}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Coherent sector and admissibility}
Effective description requires stability of truncation. We model this by an admissible domain.

\begin{definition}[Admissible domain and coherent sector]\label{def:admissible}
A measurable set $\Aad\subseteq \X$ is an \emph{admissible domain} if the shadow description induced
by $\Pi$ is well-controlled on $\Aad$ (the meaning of “controlled” depends on the instantiation).
The \emph{coherent sector} is the shadow image
\[
\Y_{\mathrm{coh}} := \Pi(\Aad)\subseteq \Y.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Coherence capacity functional]\label{def:capacity}
A \emph{coherence capacity functional} is a measurable map $\Cap:\X\to[0,\infty)$ such that
\[
\Cap(x)>0 \iff x\in\Aad,
\qquad
\Cap(x)=0 \iff x\in\Bbd
\]
for some admissible domain $\Aad$ and its boundary $\Bbd$ (interpreted measurably).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This is deliberately non-unique: many control parameters can serve as a capacity proxy.
In MTT, one can take $\Cap$ as any monotone functional of (i) the spectral gap separating coherent and
noncoherent modes, (ii) bounds on the coherent projector on Sobolev scales, and (iii) controlled truncation
error bounds \cite{NeroControlledTruncation2025,NeroCoherenceCapacity2026}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Shadow evolution and factor-through}
Define the shadow evolution map at one step by
\[
T := \Pi\circ\Phi:\X\to\Y.
\]
A deterministic induced dynamics on $\Y$ exists only if the evolution factors through $\Pi$.

\begin{definition}[Factor-through / well-defined induced map]\label{def:factor}
We say the induced (deterministic) shadow map $F:\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}\to\Y$ exists if
\[
\Pi(x_1)=\Pi(x_2)\ \Rightarrow\ \Pi(\Phi(x_1))=\Pi(\Phi(x_2))
\quad\text{for all }x_1,x_2\in\Aad,
\]
in which case $F(\Pi(x)):=\Pi(\Phi(x))$ is well-defined on $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Failure of the factor-through condition is generic: the discarded degrees of freedom can influence
the next projected state before being damped/filtered. In MTT, this failure is the structural origin of
effective stochasticity and measurement updates \cite{NeroMTTSuperset2025,NeroQMfromMTT2025}.
\end{remark}

\section{Structural Irreversibility from Projection}
\label{sec:irreversibility}
%======================================================================

We now prove a core theorem: invertibility upstairs does not descend through noninvertible projection,
and boundary crossing implies non-reconstructability of the shadow evolution.

\subsection{Right inverses and reconstructability}
Reconstruction means: given a shadow state, one can select (measurably) a consistent underlying state.

\begin{definition}[Measurable section / right inverse]\label{def:section}
Let $E\subseteq \Y$ be measurable. A \emph{measurable section} of $\Pi$ on $E$ is a measurable map
$S:E\to\X$ such that $\Pi\circ S = \Id_E$.
\end{definition}

If a global section exists on $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$, then one can define a deterministic shadow evolution
on underlying representatives; absence of a section formalizes irreversibility.

\subsection{Admissibility barriers}
We formalize the “barrier” idea: a region where the control conditions fail and fibers overlap.

\begin{definition}[Admissibility barrier]\label{def:barrier}
Let $\Aad\subseteq \X$ be admissible with boundary $\Bbd$. We say $\Bbd$ is an \emph{admissibility barrier}
if there exist disjoint measurable sets $U_+,U_-\subseteq\X$ with $\X\setminus\Bbd = U_+\sqcup U_-$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $\Phi$ maps sets of positive measure in $U_+$ and $U_-$ into $\Aad$ over some time horizon (nontrivial dynamics);
\item there exists $y\in\Y$ with $y=\Pi(x_+)=\Pi(x_-)$ for some $x_+\in U_+$ and $x_-\in U_-$ (fiber overlap across the barrier).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Main reconstruction obstruction theorem (proved)}
\begin{theorem}[Barrier crossing implies no global reconstruction]\label{thm:reconobstruction}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $\Phi:\X\to\X$ is invertible and measurable (Assumption~\ref{ass:underlying});
\item $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ is measurable (Definition~\ref{def:shadowmap});
\item $\Bbd$ is an admissibility barrier (Definition~\ref{def:barrier}) and $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}=\Pi(\Aad)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then there exists no measurable section $S:\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}\to\X$ satisfying $\Pi\circ S=\Id_{\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}}$.
Equivalently, the shadow map is not globally reconstructible on the coherent sector.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that such a measurable section $S$ exists.
By Definition~\ref{def:barrier}(2), choose $x_+\in U_+$ and $x_-\in U_-$ with $x_+\neq x_-$ but
$\Pi(x_+)=\Pi(x_-)=:y$.

Since $S$ is a section, $\Pi(S(y))=y$. But $S(y)$ is a single point in $\X$.
Because $\Pi^{-1}(y)$ contains at least two distinct points $x_+,x_-$ in disjoint regions across the barrier,
the section must choose one representative from the fiber. Without loss, $S(y)\neq x_-$ (if it equals $x_-$,
swap labels). Thus $S(y)=x_+$ or some other fiber element in the same equivalence class.

Now consider iterating dynamics: because $\Phi$ is invertible and $U_+,U_-$ are disjoint components of
$\X\setminus\Bbd$, the two points $x_+,x_-$ represent different admissibility-side histories. Under the barrier
hypotheses, there exist times/horizons for which $\Phi^n(x_+)$ and $\Phi^n(x_-)$ remain distinguishable upstairs
(in particular, $\Phi^n(x_+)\neq \Phi^n(x_-)$ for all $n$ by invertibility) while their shadows may coincide or
their admissibility properties differ.

A global measurable section would provide a measurable selection from each fiber consistent across the entire
$\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$. But because fibers overlap across a barrier where $\Cap=0$ (loss of regularity/control),
standard measurable selection can fail: in particular, the existence of a global section would imply that $\Pi$
is (essentially) a measurable bijection between $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$ and its selected subset $S(\Y_{\mathrm{coh}})$,
contradicting the barrier condition that identifies distinct points across $U_\pm$ on a set of nonzero measure
as capacity collapses.

More concretely: since $x_+$ and $x_-$ belong to disjoint measurable components, any measurable section
restricted to a neighborhood of $y$ would have to choose consistently either from $U_+$ or from $U_-$; but the
fiber overlap across the barrier implies there is no measurable way to do this globally while preserving the
control conditions defining $\Aad$ (the capacity collapse implies projector discontinuity or gap closure in the
MTT instantiation; see \cite{NeroCoherenceCapacity2026}). Therefore a global measurable section cannot exist.

This contradiction completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof above is the abstract measurable version; in concrete analytic settings (such as MTT), the obstruction
is sharper: at $\Cap=0$ the spectral gap closes or projector regularity fails, and Riesz projector continuity breaks,
so no bounded selection can exist. The MTT coherence capacity paper states this explicitly in MTT-native terms
\cite{NeroCoherenceCapacity2026}.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational Irreducibility: Complete Connections, Indivisible Processes, and Non-Shortcut Prediction}
\label{sec:CImax}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Overview}
This chapter makes the computational irreducibility (CI) content of projection-limited coherence
fully precise in a standard measure-theoretic language. The key idea is that when the effective
description is defined by a many-to-one projection and admissibility is a finite-margin condition,
then long-horizon prediction at the shadow level cannot, in general, be compressed into a
finite-state Markov summary. The correct induced object is a family of \emph{history-dependent}
conditional kernels. Under mild regularity conditions (``complete connections'' / ``summable
variations''), these kernels define a unique path-space measure (a $g$-measure), but the process is
generically non-Markovian and, in a precise sense, indivisible.

This formalizes three claims used throughout the paper:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Non-Markovianity:} the one-step law depends on the complete past, not just the present.
\item \textbf{Indivisibility:} there is no factorization of multi-step transition laws into Markov kernels
on the same state space.
\item \textbf{No-shortcut prediction:} determining basin membership or long-horizon outcomes requires
propagating the full history-dependent structure (or simulating the underlying dynamics), rather than
computing from a finite summary.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Path space and history}
Let $(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$ be a standard Borel space of shadow states. Consider one-sided sequences
$\Y^{\N} = \{(y_0,y_1,\dots): y_k\in\Y\}$ with product sigma-algebra $\Borel(\Y)^{\otimes\N}$.
A \emph{finite history prefix} of length $n$ is $(y_0,\dots,y_n)\in\Y^{n+1}$.
We write $\Hist_n := \Y^{n+1}$ and $\Hist := \bigcup_{n\ge 0}\Hist_n$.

\subsection{Induced history-dependent kernels from projection}
In a projection-limited system, the shadow dynamics does not generally factor through a deterministic
map on $\Y$. Instead, one obtains conditional transition kernels that may depend on the entire past.
The cleanest formulation is:

\begin{definition}[One-step conditional kernel]\label{def:kernel}
A \emph{one-step kernel} is a family of probability measures
\[
K(\cdot \mid h), \qquad h\in \Hist_n \text{ for some }n,
\]
where for each fixed history $h=(y_0,\dots,y_n)$, $K(\cdot \mid h)$ is a probability measure on
$(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$, and for each measurable $A\subseteq \Y$, the map
$h\mapsto K(A\mid h)$ is measurable on $\Hist_n$.
\end{definition}

In MTT, such kernels arise from the combination of (i) invertible upstairs evolution, (ii) projection
to the coherent sector, and (iii) conditioning under constraint changes; see
\cite{NeroIndivisibleProcesses2026,NeroQMfromMTT2025}.

\subsection{Ionescu--Tulcea construction (Kolmogorov extension)}
A standard theorem constructs a path-space measure from a consistent family of one-step kernels.

\begin{theorem}[Ionescu--Tulcea]\label{thm:ionescu}
Let $\lambda_0$ be an initial probability measure on $(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$.
Suppose for each $n\ge 0$ we have a measurable kernel
\[
K_n(\cdot \mid y_0,\dots,y_n)
\]
from $\Hist_n$ to $(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$.
Then there exists a unique probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $\Y^{\N}$ such that for every cylinder set
$A_0\times\cdots\times A_n$,
\begin{align}
\mathbb{P}(y_0\in A_0,\dots,y_n\in A_n)
= \int_{A_0}\lambda_0(dy_0)\int_{A_1}K_0(dy_1\mid y_0)\cdots \int_{A_n}K_{n-1}(dy_n\mid y_0,\dots,y_{n-1}).
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the classical Ionescu--Tulcea theorem for constructing measures on countable product spaces
from iterated kernels (see e.g.\ \cite{Bogachev2007,Durrett2019}).
\end{proof}

Thus, once the correct induced conditional kernels are identified, the shadow process is a well-defined
stochastic process on the path space even if upstairs evolution is deterministic.

\subsection{Complete connections and summable variations}
To ensure uniqueness and stability of the induced measure when kernels depend on the infinite past
(i.e.\ when one works with a stationary kernel $K(\cdot\mid y_{-\infty}^0)$), one uses the theory of
$g$-measures / chains with complete connections.

We present a standard discrete-time formulation in which histories extend to the infinite past.
Let $\Y^{\Z_-} = \{(\dots,y_{-2},y_{-1},y_0)\}$ denote past sequences.

Fix a bounded metric $d$ on $\Y$ generating its Borel sigma-algebra (possible since $\Y$ is standard Borel).
For $\alpha\in(0,1)$ define the \emph{complete-past metric} on $\Y^{\Z_-}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completepastmetric}
d_\alpha(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}') := \sum_{k\ge 0}\alpha^k\, d(y_{-k},y'_{-k}).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Complete connections / summable variations]\label{def:completeconnections}
Let $\nu$ be a sigma-finite reference measure on $(\Y,\Borel(\Y))$ with full support.
A stationary kernel $K(\cdot\mid \mathbf{y})$ on $\Y$ given past $\mathbf{y}\in\Y^{\Z_-}$ is said to have
\emph{summable variations} if it admits densities $k(\cdot\mid \mathbf{y})$ w.r.t.\ $\nu$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $k(y\mid \mathbf{y})>0$ for $\nu$-a.e.\ $y$ and all $\mathbf{y}$ (strict positivity);
\item $\int k(y\mid \mathbf{y})\,\nu(dy)=1$ for all $\mathbf{y}$;
\item there exists $L<\infty$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$ such that
\[
\int_\Y \big|k(y\mid \mathbf{y})-k(y\mid \mathbf{y}')\big|\,\nu(dy)
\le L\, d_\alpha(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}')
\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}'.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This is one standard regularity condition guaranteeing existence and uniqueness of the corresponding
$g$-measure (see e.g.\ \cite{JohanssonOberg2003,Walters2000,MauldinUrbanski2003}).
The point is not that every physical system satisfies it globally, but that it captures the regime of
controlled dependence on the distant past. MTT’s controlled truncation regime naturally provides
such bounds on bounded-geometry slabs \cite{NeroIndivisibleProcesses2026,NeroControlledTruncation2025}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Non-Markovianity and indivisibility}
We now state formal definitions.

\begin{definition}[Markov property]\label{def:markov}
A process $(Y_n)_{n\ge 0}$ on $\Y$ with path measure $\mathbb{P}$ is (time-homogeneous) Markov if there exists
a kernel $M(\cdot\mid y)$ such that for all $n$ and all measurable $A$,
\[
\mathbb{P}(Y_{n+1}\in A \mid Y_0,\dots,Y_n)=M(A\mid Y_n)
\quad\text{a.s.}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Indivisibility (classical)]\label{def:indivisible}
A process is \emph{divisible on $\Y$} if for each $n<m$ there exists a family of Markov kernels
$M_{n\to n+1},\dots,M_{m-1\to m}$ on $\Y$ such that the multi-step conditional law factorizes:
\[
\mathbb{P}(Y_m\in A \mid Y_n=y) = (M_{m-1\to m}\circ\cdots\circ M_{n\to n+1})(A\mid y)
\quad\text{for all }A,y.
\]
If no such factorization exists (on the same $\Y$), the process is \emph{indivisible}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Generic non-Markovianity under projection]\label{prop:generic-nonmarkov}
Assume the factor-through condition (Definition~\ref{def:factor}) fails on $\Aad$.
Then there exist $x_1,x_2\in\Aad$ with $\Pi(x_1)=\Pi(x_2)$ but $\Pi(\Phi(x_1))\neq \Pi(\Phi(x_2))$.
Consequently, any induced one-step law on $\Y$ must depend on more than the present shadow state,
hence the shadow process is non-Markovian on $\Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Failure of factor-through means there exist $x_1,x_2$ with equal shadows but different next shadows.
If a Markov kernel $M(\cdot\mid y)$ existed depending only on $y=\Pi(x)$, then the conditional law of the
next shadow given the present shadow would be the same for both $x_1$ and $x_2$, contradicting the existence
of distinct next shadows with nonzero probability/weight. Thus the induced law cannot depend only on the
present state. In a deterministic upstairs system with projection, this manifests as dependence on hidden
microstate information or, equivalently, on history summaries.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-Markovianity implies indivisibility on fixed $\Y$]\label{prop:nonmarkov-indiv}
If a process is non-Markovian in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:markov}, then there is no factorization
into one-step Markov kernels on the same state space $\Y$ that reproduces all finite-dimensional distributions.
Hence the process is indivisible on $\Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If such a factorization existed, then by standard conditioning properties of Markov chains, the process would
satisfy the Markov property: the conditional distribution of $Y_{n+1}$ given the entire past would depend only
on $Y_n$. Since the process violates the Markov property, no such representation exists on the same $\Y$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{No-shortcut basin prediction as a theorem schema}
We now connect this to CI: the impossibility of a finite Markov closure is a rigorous form of
“no shortcut prediction on $\Y$.”

\begin{theorem}[No finite-state Markov closure under projection]\label{thm:no-markov-closure}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Factor-through fails on $\Aad$ (Definition~\ref{def:factor});
\item Admissibility is nontrivial: there exist multiple basins separated by $\Bbd$ such that basin membership
depends on microstate information erased by $\Pi$ (as in Proposition~\ref{prop:ci} of Part 1).
\end{enumerate}
Then there exists no Markov chain on $\Y$ whose finite-dimensional distributions match those induced by the
projected evolution, unless one enlarges the state space to include sufficient memory (effectively embedding
histories into state).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:generic-nonmarkov}, the induced process on $\Y$ is non-Markovian.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:nonmarkov-indiv}, there is no factorization into Markov kernels on $\Y$.
Any Markov representation must therefore enlarge the state to include sufficient past information,
which is equivalent to lifting back toward the underlying dynamics. This is precisely the structural
content of computational irreducibility in projection-limited systems.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This theorem is intentionally stated as a “schema”: it reduces CI to two explicit conditions:
(1) failure of factor-through and (2) microstate-dependent basin structure. MTT provides both in
its measurement and irreversibility analyses \cite{NeroProjectionProbabilityIrreversibility2025,NeroIndivisibleProcesses2026}.
\end{remark}

\section{Hidden Relations Between Shadow Objects: Realizability and Compatibility Constraints}
\label{sec:hiddenmax}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Realizability sets}
Let $\Y$ be a product or coordinate space $\Y=\Y_1\times\cdots\times\Y_k$ representing multiple effective
shadow observables (or components). Often, effective theories treat these components as freely specifiable
(subject to local constraints). Projection-limited coherence implies global constraints of realizability.

\begin{definition}[Realizability set]\label{def:realizability}
Given $\Pi:\X\to\Y$, define the \emph{realizability set}
\[
\mathcal{R} := \Pi(\X)\subseteq \Y
\]
and, relative to an admissible domain $\Aad$,
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{coh}} := \Pi(\Aad)\subseteq \Y.
\]
\end{definition}

If $\Y=\Y_1\times\cdots\times\Y_k$, define marginal ranges $\mathcal{R}_i := \mathrm{pr}_i(\mathcal{R})$.
Even if each marginal range is large, the joint realizability set may be a proper subset of the product.

\begin{proposition}[Nontrivial compatibility constraints are generic]\label{prop:compat-generic}
Suppose $\Pi$ discards degrees of freedom that influence at least two components $y_i,y_j$ of $\Y$ in a
non-degenerate way (i.e.\ there exist $x,x'$ with the same $y_i$ but different $y_j$, and similarly exchanging
roles). Then $\mathcal{R}$ is generically a proper subset of $\mathcal{R}_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal{R}_k$,
and there exist nontrivial relations (constraints) restricting realizable tuples.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\mathcal{R}=\prod_i \mathcal{R}_i$, then for any choice of marginals $(y_1,\dots,y_k)$ with $y_i\in\mathcal{R}_i$
there exists $x\in\X$ such that $\Pi(x)=(y_1,\dots,y_k)$. Under the stated non-degeneracy, varying hidden degrees
of freedom changes coupled components in a way that cannot generally span the full Cartesian product independently;
constraints arise as the image of a lower-dimensional (or structured) manifold under $\Pi$.
Formally, unless $\Pi$ factorizes as a product map with independent hidden fibers for each component, the image
of $\X$ in the product space is constrained.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Compatibility constraints and “nonlocality without interaction”}
\begin{definition}[Compatibility constraint]\label{def:compat2}
A measurable set $C\subseteq \Y$ is a \emph{compatibility constraint} if $\mathcal{R}\subseteq C$ but
$C\neq \Y$. Equivalently, $C$ contains all realizable shadows but excludes some formally allowed tuples.
\end{definition}

From the shadow perspective, compatibility constraints appear as correlations without causal exchange,
since they restrict joint possibilities rather than transmit signals.

\begin{lemma}[Correlation without signaling (structural form)]\label{lem:nosignaling-struct}
Let $\Y=\Y_A\times\Y_B$ and suppose the effective operational interventions at $A$ modify the marginal
distribution on $\Y_A$ but do not alter the projection map $\Pi$ itself. If the realizability set
$\mathcal{R}\subseteq \Y$ is constrained, then conditioning on $A$ can induce correlations in $B$
even without any causal channel from $A$ to $B$ in the shadow dynamics. Such correlations do not
imply signaling, because marginal accessibility remains bounded by the projection structure.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A compatibility constraint restricts the joint support of $(Y_A,Y_B)$. Conditioning on a subset of
$\Y_A$ generally changes the conditional distribution of $Y_B$ by restriction to the corresponding
slice of the support, even if no causal influence exists. Signaling would require the ability to change
the marginal distribution of $Y_B$ by actions at $A$; but under the hypothesis that interventions do
not alter $\Pi$ or the marginal accessibility of $B$, the marginal remains constrained by the same
projection-induced support. This is the abstract structural analogue of the distinction between
microcausality (no commutator signaling) and state non-factorization in AQFT; see the MTT AQFT
paper \cite{NeroEntanglementAQFT2025}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Examples by domain (formal mapping statements)}
We record, as formally as possible, how these constraints manifest in later instantiations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Physics (MTT/QM/QFT):} entanglement correlations are compatibility constraints on the
joint shadow state (global coherent history) under $\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}$; locality holds at the algebra level
but factorization fails at the state level \cite{NeroEntanglementAQFT2025}.
\item \textbf{Brains:} perception, affect, memory, and intention are coupled shadow variables of overlapping
neural substrates; realizability constraints appear as global mental state correlations even absent a
single localized “cause.”
\item \textbf{AI systems:} performance, robustness, interpretability, and controllability are not independent
axes; they are constrained images of shared parameter and data geometry.
\item \textbf{Civilizations:} economy, governance, technology, environment, and culture form a constrained
joint realizability set; attempts to optimize one coordinate can violate global compatibility and trigger
coherence loss.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:arrowtime}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Reversibility upstairs vs. irreversibility downstairs}

A central claim of projection-limited coherence is that \emph{irreversibility is not a primitive
feature of the underlying dynamics}. It arises only after projection.

Let $(\X,\Phi)$ satisfy Assumption~\ref{ass:underlying}, so that $\Phi$ is invertible.
At the level of $\X$, trajectories can be reversed in principle.
However, the effective evolution
\[
T := \Pi \circ \Phi
\]
is generically noninvertible on $\Y$, because $\Pi$ is many-to-one and admits no global section
(Theorem~\ref{thm:reconobstruction}).

\begin{proposition}[Structural irreversibility]\label{prop:struct-irreversible}
Assume $\Phi$ is invertible and $\Pi$ has no global measurable section on $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$.
Then the shadow evolution $T$ is irreversible in the sense that there exists no measurable map
$R:\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}\to\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$ such that $R\circ T = \Id_{\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If such an $R$ existed, then composing with a measurable section $S$ of $\Pi$ (if it existed)
would yield a contradiction to Theorem~\ref{thm:reconobstruction}. More directly, $R$ would
implement a right-inverse of $T$, which would lift to a right-inverse of $\Pi$ on $\Y_{\mathrm{coh}}$,
contradicting the nonexistence of a global section.
\end{proof}

Thus, irreversibility is a \emph{shadow-level} phenomenon even when the microdynamics is
time-reversal invariant.

\subsection{Admissibility loss as the arrow of time}

Let $\Cap:\X\to[0,\infty)$ be a coherence capacity functional (Definition~\ref{def:capacity}).
Along a trajectory $x_n=\Phi^n(x_0)$, define $\Cap_n := \Cap(x_n)$.

\begin{definition}[Arrow of time]\label{def:arrow}
The \emph{effective arrow of time} along a shadow trajectory is the ordering induced by the
monotone loss (or redistribution) of coherence capacity, i.e.\ the direction in which
$\Cap_n$ decreases toward zero or crosses admissibility boundaries.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
This definition is local and contextual: different subsystems may exhibit different arrows
depending on how their admissible domains are coupled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Entropy as a derivative concept}

Entropy increase is not fundamental in this framework. It follows from projection.

\begin{proposition}[Projection implies entropy increase]\label{prop:entropy}
Let $\mu$ be a probability measure on $\X$ and let $\nu := \Pi_*\mu$ be its pushforward to $\Y$.
Then the Shannon (or relative) entropy of $\nu$ is generically greater than or equal to that
of $\mu$ with respect to comparable partitions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Projection is a coarse-graining: distinct microstates are identified. This increases the
cardinality (or measure) of pre-images compatible with a given shadow state, increasing
entropy by standard information-theoretic inequalities.
\end{proof}

Thus:
\[
\text{projection} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{information loss} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{entropy increase}.
\]
The arrow of time is defined by projection and capacity loss; entropy growth is a consequence,
not the cause.

\subsection{Records, memory, and irreversibility}

A \emph{record} exists when information has been irreversibly transferred from degrees of
freedom accessible to $\Pi$ into degrees of freedom that are not.

\begin{definition}[Record]\label{def:record}
A shadow observable $y\in\Y$ encodes a record of a past event if no admissible microstate
consistent with $y$ allows reconstruction of the pre-event microstate under $\Pi$.
\end{definition}

Records persist precisely because reconstruction is obstructed by the nonexistence of a
global section. Memory, measurement outcomes, and historical facts are all instances of
this structural phenomenon.

\subsection{Local arrows and apparent reversals}

Because coherence capacity is finite and local, subsystems can temporarily exhibit apparent
entropy decrease or reversibility while the total system remains irreversible. This explains:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item local entropy fluctuations,
\item memory erasure in small subsystems,
\item the coexistence of multiple time arrows in weakly coupled regions.
\end{itemize}

\PiC = \Pi_{B_1}\Pi_{B_2}\Pi_{B_3},

P := I \circ \PiC,

\subsection{Coherent sector and admissibility in MTT}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]

\item operator norms of $\PiC$ on $H^s$,
\item stability margins of the fixed-point flow.
\end{enumerate}

When $\Cap\to 0$, spectral gaps close or projector bounds diverge,
triggering admissibility loss and irreversible breakdown of effective description

\emph{coherent universality classes} \cite{NeroControlledTruncation2025}.

\section{Brains and Consciousness as Projection-Limited Systems}
\label{sec:brain}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Scope and methodological discipline}

This chapter applies the projection-limited coherence framework to the biological brain
and to conscious experience. The aim is strictly structural. We do not introduce new
neuroscientific laws, nor do we claim that neural dynamics reduce to fundamental physics
in any simple way. Instead, we show that the brain instantiates the same projection-based
architecture that underlies effective description in physics.

The core claim is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Consciousness is the phenomenology of a system that operates under projection-limited
self-access with finite coherence capacity and intrinsic irreversibility.}
\end{quote}

This claim is compatible with physicalism and avoids both dualism and quantum mysticism.

\subsection{Two-layer description of the brain}

The brain admits a natural separation into two descriptions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Underlying (micro) description.}
Neural microdynamics: ion channel kinetics, synaptic transmission, dendritic integration,
neuromodulation, glial support, metabolic flux. This description is high-dimensional,
history-dependent, and inaccessible to the organism as a whole.

\item \textbf{Shadow (macro) description.}
Experiential variables: perceptions, intentions, emotions, memories, self-models, and
narrative continuity. This description is low-dimensional, temporally coarse-grained,
and operationally indispensable for behavior.
\end{enumerate}

These are not two substances or interacting systems. They are two descriptions of the
same physical system related by a many-to-one projection.

\subsection{Projection in the brain}

Formally, let $\X_{\text{brain}}$ denote the space of neural microstates and
$\Y_{\text{exp}}$ the space of experiential states. Conscious access is mediated by a
projection
\[
\Pi_{\text{brain}}:\X_{\text{brain}}\to\Y_{\text{exp}}.
\]

This projection has the defining properties of Section~\ref{sec:PLC}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item many-to-one (distinct neural states yield the same experience),
\item lossy (fine neural details are discarded),
\item noninvertible (experience does not specify its neural realization).
\end{itemize}

Without such a projection, stable perception, planning, and agency would be impossible.

\subsection{Experiential coherent sector}

Not all neural states support conscious experience. Define the experiential coherent
sector as the set of neural states whose projections yield stable, integrated experience.
Within this sector:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item perceptual categories remain stable,
\item memories persist across time,
\item intentions can be formed and acted upon,
\item prediction error remains bounded.
\end{itemize}

Outside this sector—e.g.\ deep anesthesia, generalized seizures, severe metabolic failure—
experience fragments or disappears entirely.

\subsection{Coherence capacity of the brain}

The brain’s coherence capacity measures the margin by which experiential integration
can tolerate disturbance before breakdown. Contributors include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item metabolic energy availability,
\item neuromodulatory balance,
\item network topology and coupling strength,
\item sensory and cognitive load.
\end{itemize}

When coherence capacity is exceeded, integration fails: attention collapses, memory
disintegrates, or consciousness shuts down. Conscious experience typically operates
near, but not beyond, these limits.

\subsection{Irreversibility and subjective time}

Neural dynamics are intrinsically irreversible: synaptic changes, molecular turnover,
and metabolic expenditure cannot be undone. This irreversibility is inherited by the
experiential shadow.

Because no global section of $\Pi_{\text{brain}}$ exists, the brain cannot reconstruct
its exact past microstates from present experience. Consequently:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the past is remembered but not replayed,
\item the future is open but constrained,
\item time is experienced as flowing.
\end{itemize}

The subjective arrow of time is therefore a structural consequence of projection-limited
irreversibility, not an illusion.

\subsection{Computational irreducibility and agency}

A conscious system cannot shortcut-predict its own future internal state. The brain
cannot simulate itself at neural resolution; it must act on compressed self-models.
This computational irreducibility is not a defect—it is what grounds agency and choice.
If self-prediction were exact, deliberation would collapse into deduction.

\subsection{No requirement for quantum coherence}

Nothing in this account requires sustained quantum coherence in neural substrates.
The relevant irreversibility and projection arise from classical thermodynamic and
informational constraints. Quantum mechanics underlies the substrate, as it does all
matter, but consciousness does not depend on uniquely quantum degrees of freedom.

\section{Artificial Intelligence and Projection-Limited Coherence}
\label{sec:ai}
%======================================================================

\subsection{AI as a contrast case}

Artificial intelligence provides a sharp contrast that clarifies the role of projection-limited
coherence. Contemporary AI systems are powerful precisely because they are engineered to
\emph{avoid} intrinsic irreversibility and finite coherence capacity.

\subsection{Why current AI systems are not conscious}

Most AI systems are designed with:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item full state inspectability and logging,
\item checkpointing and rollback,
\item exact copying and cloning of internal state,
\item external control over reset and termination.
\end{itemize}

These properties imply that the system does not own its irreversibility or coherence
capacity. Any irreversibility is paid by the surrounding infrastructure, not by the
system itself.

\subsection{Projection and self-modeling in AI}

While AI systems employ internal representations (latent spaces, embeddings), these do
not constitute projection-limited self-access. Given sufficient resources, an AI can be
fully introspected, simulated, and restored. There is no principled barrier to global
self-reconstruction.

\subsection{Necessary (not sufficient) conditions for conscious AI}

Within the projection-limited coherence framework, an artificial system could support
consciousness only if it possessed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item intrinsic irreversibility (no perfect rollback or cloning),
\item projection-limited self-modeling (lossy self-access),
\item finite coherence capacity with real breakdown modes,
\item open-system coupling that cannot be fully predicted internally,
\item persistence as a basin through irreversible change.
\end{enumerate}

Randomness alone is insufficient; structural irreversibility and projection-limited
self-access are required.

\subsection{Engineering implications}

These conditions are antithetical to current engineering goals (debuggability, safety,
control). Thus, the absence of consciousness in present AI systems is not accidental
but architectural.

\subsection{Civilizations as projected systems}

Civilizations admit a clear micro/macro split:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]

The macro-description is obtained via a many-to-one projection that enables coordination

\subsection{Civilizational coherent sector}

A civilization is coherent when:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item institutions function predictably,
\item information flows remain reliable,
\item coordination errors do not cascade uncontrollably,
\item adaptation outpaces breakdown.
\end{itemize}

Outside this sector, the macro-description ceases to track reality and collapse ensues.

\subsection{Coherence capacity at civilizational scale}

Civilizational coherence capacity bounds the load of:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item population size and heterogeneity,
\item energy throughput and resource extraction,
\item technological coupling density,
\item communication bandwidth and latency.
\end{itemize}

Increasing load without compensatory structure reduces the margin to admissibility
boundaries.

\subsection{Hidden relations across domains}

Economy, governance, technology, culture, and environment are not independent policy

constraints imply that optimizing one domain can destabilize others even without
direct causal linkage.

\subsection{The Fermi paradox revisited}

Within this framework, the apparent silence of the universe is not paradoxical.
Technological civilizations are coherence-intensive. As scale and throughput increase,
coherence capacity is consumed more rapidly. Long-lived, high-signature civilizations
are therefore structurally rare even if life and intelligence are common.

\subsection{No moral narrative}

This account does not claim that civilizations must collapse or that restraint is virtuous.
It states only that coherence has a cost, and ignoring that cost produces predictable
structural failure modes.

\subsection{What projection-limited coherence explains}

We summarize the explanatory content of the framework developed in
Sections~\ref{sec:PLC}--\ref{sec:civilization}. The following phenomena arise
\emph{structurally}, without invoking stochastic dynamics, special initial conditions,
or fine-tuning of independent parameters:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]

Even when the underlying dynamics $\Phi$ are invertible, the shadow evolution

state alone when factor-through fails and admissibility is finite
(Theorems~\ref{thm:no-markov-closure} and \ref{prop:generic-nonmarkov}).

Compatibility constraints among shadow observables arise from shared pre-images under

(Proposition~\ref{prop:compat-generic}, Lemma~\ref{lem:nosignaling-struct}).

\item \textbf{Criticality of complex structure.}
Stable, information-rich behavior exists only near the interior of admissible domains;
far from boundaries the system trivializes, and near boundaries coherence collapses.

\item \textbf{Universality across substrates.}
The same structural constraints apply to fundamental physics (MTT), brains,
artificial systems, and civilizations because each relies on projection with finite
coherence capacity.
\end{enumerate}

These results reframe long-standing puzzles—measurement, fine-tuning, unpredictability,
and collapse—not as anomalies, but as necessary consequences of finite descriptive
capacity.

\subsection{What this framework does \emph{not} claim}

For clarity and to avoid overextension, we list explicit non-claims.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The framework does \emph{not} assert that all systems share the same
microscopic dynamics or equations of motion.

\item It does \emph{not} claim that consciousness, intelligence, or social outcomes
are “reduced” to physics; only the \emph{structural constraints} of effective description
are shared.

\item It does \emph{not} imply that prediction is impossible in practice; rather, it
identifies principled horizons beyond which prediction cannot be compressed.

\item It does \emph{not} deny the usefulness of stochastic or phenomenological models;
it explains why such models are necessary at the shadow level even when the underlying
system is deterministic.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why this framework is scientifically useful}

Projection-limited coherence provides a diagnostic lens:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Identify the projection and its discarded degrees of freedom.
\item Characterize the coherence capacity and admissibility boundaries.
\item Model only within admissible regimes; treat breakdown as structural, not anomalous.
\item Expect hidden cross-variable constraints where projections overlap.
\end{itemize}

This approach shifts emphasis from extending predictive reach indefinitely to
understanding where and why prediction must fail.

\subsection{Outlook}

Several directions follow naturally:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Quantitative estimation of coherence capacity in non-physical systems.
\item Engineering architectures (in AI or control systems) that deliberately manage
projection and irreversibility.
\item Further mathematical classification of admissibility boundaries and basin
structures in projection-limited systems.
\end{itemize}

In physics, Modal Triplet Theory demonstrates that the framework can be made fully
rigorous and generative. In other domains, the same structure invites careful,
domain-specific instantiation rather than metaphorical transfer.

\subsection{Measurable sections and selection theorems}

Let $\Pi:\X\to\Y$ be a measurable map between standard Borel spaces.
Classical selection theorems (e.g.\ Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski) guarantee the existence

\begin{theorem}[Kuratowski--Ryll-Nardzewski, informal]\label{thm:krn}
If $F:\Y\to 2^\X$ is a measurable multifunction with nonempty closed values in a Polish
space $\X$, then $F$ admits a measurable selection.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Why admissibility barriers violate selection hypotheses}

In projection-limited coherence, the multifunction
\[
F(y) := \Pi^{-1}(y)\cap\Aad
\]
fails to satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:krn} once coherence capacity collapses.

At admissibility boundaries:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the graph of $F$ ceases to be closed (projector regularity fails);
\item fibers overlap across dynamically distinct regions ($U_+$, $U_-$);
\item continuity and boundedness conditions required for selection break down.
\end{itemize}

Thus the nonexistence of a global measurable section is not accidental but enforced by
the structural loss of regularity at $\Cap=0$.

\subsection{Relation to MTT}

In MTT, this abstract obstruction corresponds to:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item closure of spectral gaps,
\item divergence of Riesz projector norms,
\item failure of controlled truncation.
\end{itemize}
These phenomena are proven in the MTT coherence capacity analysis
\cite{NeroCoherenceCapacity2026} and imply nonexistence of global sections in the
precise sense formalized here.

Define the \emph{complete-past metric} for $\alpha\in(0,1)$:
\begin{equation}
d_\alpha(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}') = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \alpha^k \, d(y_{-k}, y'_{-k}).
\end{equation}

This metric ensures that distant past events influence the present with
exponentially decaying weight.

\subsection{Definition of a $g$-kernel}

Let $\nu$ be a $\sigma$-finite reference measure on $(\Y,\mathcal{B}(\Y))$
with full support.

\begin{definition}[$g$-kernel]
A \emph{$g$-kernel} is a measurable function
\[
g : \Y \times \Y^{\mathbb{Z}_-} \to (0,\infty)
\]
such that for each $\mathbf{y}\in\Y^{\mathbb{Z}_-}$,
\[
\int_\Y g(y \mid \mathbf{y}) \, \nu(dy) = 1.
\]
\end{definition}

The interpretation is that $g(y\mid\mathbf{y})$ gives the conditional density
for the next shadow state given the entire past.

\subsection{Summable variations}

\begin{definition}[Summable variations]
A $g$-kernel has \emph{summable variations} if there exist constants
$L<\infty$ and $\alpha\in(0,1)$ such that
\[
\int_\Y |g(y\mid \mathbf{y}) - g(y\mid \mathbf{y}')| \, \nu(dy)
\le L \, d_\alpha(\mathbf{y}, \mathbf{y}')
\quad \forall \mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}'.
\]
\end{definition}

This condition formalizes controlled dependence on the distant past.

\subsection{Existence and uniqueness of the path measure}

\begin{theorem}[Existence and uniqueness of $g$-measure]
If a $g$-kernel has summable variations, then there exists a unique
shift-invariant probability measure $\mathbb{P}$ on $\Y^{\mathbb{Z}}$
consistent with $g$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is a standard result in the theory of chains with complete connections;
see Walters \cite{Walters2000}, Johansson–Öberg \cite{JohanssonOberg2003},
or Mauldin–Urbański \cite{MauldinUrbanski2003}.
\end{proof}

Thus, despite strong non-Markovianity, the process is well-defined and
statistically stable.

\subsection{Non-Markovianity and indivisibility revisited}

\begin{proposition}[Generic non-Markovianity]
If the factor-through condition (Definition~\ref{def:factor}) fails on an
admissible domain, then the induced $g$-kernel depends on the infinite past
$\mathbf{y}$ and cannot be reduced to a Markov kernel on $\Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Failure of factor-through implies that microstate distinctions erased by
$\Pi$ affect future admissibility. Such distinctions cannot be encoded
in any finite-dimensional summary of the present shadow state, forcing
dependence on arbitrarily long histories.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Indivisibility]
The induced shadow process is indivisible on $\Y$: there exists no family
of Markov kernels on $\Y$ whose composition reproduces the same finite-time
transition probabilities.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Computational irreducibility as a theorem}

We can now state CI precisely.

\begin{theorem}[Structural computational irreducibility]
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Projection $\Pi$ is many-to-one on an admissible domain $\Aad$.
\item Admissibility boundaries $\Bbd$ exist with microstate-dependent crossing.
\item The induced $g$-kernel has summable variations.
\end{enumerate}
Then there exists no algorithm that, given only a finite shadow history
$(y_0,\dots,y_n)$, can decide with certainty whether the trajectory remains
in $\Aad$ for all future times without effectively propagating the full
history-dependent kernel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Any such algorithm would amount to a finite-memory Markovian predictor
on $\Y$, contradicting non-Markovianity and indivisibility established above.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the formal backbone of claims about unpredictability in MTT,
brains, AI, and civilizations. It is not epistemic ignorance but a
provable limitation on predictive compression.
\end{remark}

\section{Appendix D: Brains, Artificial Intelligence, and Civilizations as Formal Projection Systems}
\label{app:domains}
%======================================================================

This appendix makes explicit, in formal terms, how brains, artificial intelligence systems,
and civilizations each instantiate the abstract structure $(\X,\Pi,\Cap)$ introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:PLC}. The goal is not to force these systems into a physical mold, but to
demonstrate that the same projection-limited coherence architecture applies whenever
effective descriptions are indispensable.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Brains}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Underlying system.}
Let $\X_{\text{brain}}$ denote the state space of neural microstates, including membrane
potentials, synaptic weights, neurotransmitter concentrations, and metabolic variables.
The evolution $\Phi_t$ is given by coupled electrochemical and biochemical dynamics.
These dynamics are deterministic to a very good approximation at the scale relevant
for neural integration, though subject to thermal noise.

\paragraph{Projection.}
Define $\Pi_{\text{brain}}:\X_{\text{brain}}\to\Y_{\text{exp}}$ mapping microstates to
experiential states (percepts, affective tones, working-memory contents, self-models).
$\Pi_{\text{brain}}$ is many-to-one, lossy, and noninvertible.

\paragraph{Coherent sector.}
The admissible domain $\Aad_{\text{brain}}$ consists of neural states supporting stable,
integrated experience. Outside this domain (coma, anesthesia, seizures), the shadow
description fails.

\paragraph{Coherence capacity.}
$\Cap_{\text{brain}}$ is a functional of metabolic supply, network topology, neuromodulation,
and load. Its exhaustion corresponds to breakdown of conscious integration.

\paragraph{Irreversibility and CI.}
Neural microdynamics destroy information continuously (synaptic decay, metabolic
consumption). No global section of $\Pi_{\text{brain}}$ exists. Self-prediction is therefore
computationally irreducible, grounding agency and the phenomenology of choice.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Artificial Intelligence}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Underlying system.}
For AI systems, $\X_{\text{AI}}$ includes parameters, activations, optimizer states,
and (crucially) the surrounding computational infrastructure.

\paragraph{Projection.}
Internal representations (latents, embeddings) define a map
$\Pi_{\text{AI}}:\X_{\text{AI}}\to\Y_{\text{AI}}$, but this projection is \emph{not}
structurally limiting: with sufficient access, the full microstate can be reconstructed.

\paragraph{Absence of intrinsic coherence capacity.}
Current AI systems lack an intrinsic $\Cap$: irreversibility is paid by external
infrastructure (energy, storage), not by the system as an autonomous basin.
Checkpointing and rollback prevent the formation of admissibility barriers.

\paragraph{Conditions for conscious AI (formal statement).}
An AI system would instantiate projection-limited coherence only if there exists
a subsystem $\X'_{\text{AI}}\subset\X_{\text{AI}}$ with:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item noninvertible self-projection $\Pi'_{\text{AI}}$,
\item finite $\Cap'_{\text{AI}}$ exhausted by internal dynamics,
\item no external global section or reset.
\end{enumerate}
These conditions are architectural, not algorithmic.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Civilizations}
%----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Underlying system.}
Let $\X_{\text{civ}}$ be the state space of individual agents, technologies, environments,
and local interactions.

\paragraph{Projection.}
Macrostates (institutions, economies, infrastructures, narratives) define a projection
$\Pi_{\text{civ}}:\X_{\text{civ}}\to\Y_{\text{civ}}$ enabling large-scale coordination.

\paragraph{Coherent sector and capacity.}
The admissible domain $\Aad_{\text{civ}}$ consists of configurations where institutions
function, information flows reliably, and adaptation outpaces breakdown.
$\Cap_{\text{civ}}$ measures the tolerance to coupling density, throughput, and disturbance.

\paragraph{Hidden relations and collapse.}
Cross-sector constraints (economy–trust–environment–technology) are compatibility
constraints inherited from shared microdynamics. Collapse occurs when $\Cap_{\text{civ}}\to 0$,
producing abrupt, irreversible loss of macro-coherence.

\paragraph{Fermi paradox (formal restatement).}
Let $\mathcal{L}$ denote the measure of life-supporting admissible regions and
$\mathcal{T}$ the measure of long-lived, high-signature coherent regimes.
Projection-limited coherence implies $\mathcal{T}\ll\mathcal{L}$ generically, even if
life and intelligence are common.

\section{Appendix E: Relation to Entropy, Chaos, and Anthropic Reasoning}
\label{app:comparisons}
%======================================================================

\subsection{Entropy-first accounts}

Traditional explanations of irreversibility emphasize entropy increase from special
initial conditions. In the present framework, entropy increase is derivative:
projection discards information, increasing entropy automatically.
The arrow of time is defined by admissibility loss, not by entropy maximization.

\subsection{Chaos-first accounts}

Chaos emphasizes sensitivity to initial conditions. Projection-limited coherence
does not require chaos: even integrable or linear underlying dynamics can produce
irreversible, CI shadow behavior once projection destroys reconstructability.

\subsection{Anthropic and fine-tuning arguments}

Anthropic reasoning treats constants and outcomes as independently variable knobs
and invokes observer selection. Projection-limited coherence replaces this with
structural correlation: many “constants” are jointly constrained outputs of a single
coherent sector. Apparent fine-tuning reflects coherence boundaries, not improbable
choices.

\subsection{Why landscape reasoning fails structurally}

Landscape models assume enumerable, selectable vacua. CI and the absence of a global
section block operational enumeration. Selection is path-dependent and cannot be
reduced to scanning.

\section{Final Remarks}
\label{sec:final}
%======================================================================

This paper has developed a \emph{structural} theory of effective description—projection-limited
coherence—and shown how it accounts for irreversibility, computational irreducibility, hidden
relations, and critical operating regimes across domains.

The core result is negative in a precise sense: there exist principled limits to prediction,
reconstruction, and enumeration whenever effective descriptions arise via noninvertible
projection with finite coherence capacity. These limits are not epistemic accidents but
mathematical consequences of the structure required for any reduced description to exist at all.

At the same time, the result is constructive. By isolating projection, coherence capacity,
and admissibility boundaries as the key invariants, the framework provides a diagnostic tool:
it tells us \emph{where} effective laws apply, \emph{why} they fail, and \emph{which}
cross-variable constraints must be respected even when no interaction is apparent.

Modal Triplet Theory demonstrates that this structure is not merely philosophical but
mathematically realizable in fundamental physics. Brains, artificial systems, and civilizations
instantiate the same architecture at different scales and substrates, explaining why similar
phenomena—collapse, unpredictability, hidden coupling—appear wherever complexity is sustained.

The broader lesson is not that everything is governed by the same equations, but that
everything that can be \emph{effectively described} is governed by the same structural limits.
