## Why a rewrite was required

Version 1 combined several distinct implications:

1. many-to-one projection;
2. nonexistence of a section;
3. noninvertible effective dynamics;
4. non-Markovianity;
5. entropy growth;
6. computational irreducibility;
7. cross-domain physical universality.

These are not equivalent. The old manuscript also repeated material in
multiple appendices and included compilation notes in the paper body.

## Exact corrections

| Previous claim | Version 2 resolution |
|---|---|
| Projection generically forces irreversible shadow evolution. | Replaced by the exact fiber criterion for deterministic descent and explicit examples with invertible shadows. |
| Failure of a global section proves irreversibility. | Replaced by a double-cover counterexample: no continuous section, but an invertible descended rotation. |
| A right inverse reconstructs the source. | Distinguished representative selection from recovery of the actual source point. Exact recovery on all source states requires injectivity. |
| Projection generically produces non-Markovianity. | Replaced by the projected-kernel/lumpability criterion. |
| Non-Markovianity proves computational irreducibility. | Withdrawn. A no-shortcut claim now requires a typed complexity theorem. |
| Projection implies entropy increase. | Replaced by conditional entropy and data-processing statements. Physical entropy production requires a dynamics and entropy law. |
| A finite capacity is a conserved cross-domain resource. | Withdrawn. Capacity is a declared control margin until a constitutive or conservation law is supplied. |
| Hidden relations follow from projection alone. | Replaced by a direct realizable-image recombination test and separate joint-measure criterion. |
| Brains, AI, and civilizations instantiate the same law. | Reclassified as domain-model research programs with explicit validation obligations. |

## Retained mathematical contribution

- A typed source/projection/image/margin framework.
- Exact deterministic descent criterion.
- Exact stochastic lumpability criterion.
- Static representative-selection versus actual-recovery distinction.
- Direct support-compatibility test.
- First-exit lower bound from a declared margin-rate estimate.
- A seven-part validation certificate for applications.

## MTT scope

The paper uses current revised MTT sources only to illustrate how the
abstract slots may be filled. It does not claim a universal MTT projector,
universal capacity law, full quantum derivation, or cross-domain physical
identity.
