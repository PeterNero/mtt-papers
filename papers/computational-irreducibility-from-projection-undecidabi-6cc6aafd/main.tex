\title{\bf Computational Irreducibility from Projection:\\
Undecidability of Selection Events in Coherent Quantum Dynamics}

\author{Peter Nero\\
{\small Independent Researcher}}

\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove that any effective physical description in the coherent universality class capable
of reproducing irreversible measurement, stable classical records, relativistic locality,
and finite predictivity necessarily exhibits algorithmic undecidability at the level of
observable dynamics.
Within the coherent universality class realized explicitly by Modal Triplet Theory (MTT),
noninvertible projection together with admissible basin structure forces the existence of
well-posed physical decision problems that no algorithm can decide. The result is formulated
without reference to external time: the relevant bound is instead a coherence budget
determined by basin geometry, spectral gaps, stability margins, and gravity. The proof
constructs a uniform many-one reduction from the halting problem for universal two-counter
machines to robust selection-event occurrence under admissibility. Computational
irreducibility is thus shown to be a structural consequence of viable physical theories,
distinct from chaos or stochasticity, and explains the absence of globally valid effective
laws across measurement, spacetime, and ultraviolet completion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A widespread assumption in fundamental physics is that, although prediction may be
practically difficult, the effective laws governing observable phenomena are in principle
computable. Classical chaos, quantum uncertainty, and statistical mechanics are typically
understood as limits of prediction, not as failures of computability itself.

In this paper we show that this assumption is false for any theory that successfully
accounts for irreversible measurement, stable macroscopic records, relativistic locality,
and finite predictivity. The obstruction is not merely complexity but \emph{undecidability}:
there exist well-defined physical questions whose answers cannot be decided by any
algorithm.

The result follows from three structural features that recur across successful approaches
to measurement and quantum gravity:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item effective noninvertible projection,
\item dynamically stable admissible basins with finite margins,
\item truncation enforced by spectral gaps and admissibility.
\end{enumerate}

These features define what we call the \emph{coherent universality class}. Modal Triplet
Theory provides a minimal explicit realization of this class, but the undecidability result
does not depend on MTT-specific constructions.

\section{Effective Projection and Admissibility}

\subsection{Reduced description}

Let $\Hilb_{\mathrm{ext}}$ denote an extended Hilbert space describing microscopic degrees
of freedom, and let $\Hilb_4$ denote the reduced Hilbert space supporting observable
degrees of freedom. Effective states are trace-class operators
$\rho \in \Dens(\Hilb_4)$.

\begin{definition}[Noninvertible projection]
A projection $\Proj:\Hilb_{\mathrm{ext}}\to\Hilb_4$ is \emph{noninvertible} if distinct
microscopic configurations map to the same reduced state and no physically admissible
operation reconstructs the preimage.
\end{definition}

Effective evolution is described by protocol-dependent maps
\begin{equation}
\rho_{n+1} = M_{\pi(n)}(\rho_n),
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ labels a protocol cycle. No global, state-independent generator is assumed.

\subsection{Admissible basins}

\begin{definition}[Admissible basin]
A subset $\Bas_\alpha \subset \Dens(\Hilb_4)$ is an admissible basin if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item it is invariant under $M_\pi$,
\item it is contractive with a finite stability margin,
\item stability persists under admissible protocol variations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Admissible basins represent dynamically stable macrostates such as measurement outcomes,
classical records, or geometric encodings.

\section{Selection Events and Records}

\begin{definition}[Selection event]
A \emph{selection event} is an irreversible transition
\[
e_{\alpha\to\beta}:\ \rho\in\Bas_\alpha \longrightarrow \rho'\in\Bas_\beta,
\]
occurring when admissibility fails at a basin boundary and the trajectory is captured into
a new stable basin.
\end{definition}

Selection events write persistent information into a record
$\Rec = (\Rec^{(1)},\Rec^{(2)},\Rec^{(\mathrm{aux})})$.

\begin{remark}
Selection events unify measurement outcomes, collapse transitions, and causal-set events as
the same physical process viewed through different diagnostics.
\end{remark}

\section{Coherence Budget and Admissible Horizon}

\subsection{Selection potential and stability margins}

Let $\Sel(\rho)$ denote a scalar admissibility or selection functional on
$\Dens(\Hilb_4)$ whose value controls basin stability. For each admissible basin
$\Bas_\alpha$ there exists a critical value $\Sel_{\mathrm{crit}}$ such that
$\rho\in\Bas_\alpha$ is stable if and only if $\Sel(\rho)<\Sel_{\mathrm{crit}}$.

Define the initial margin
\begin{equation}
m(\rho_0) := \Sel_{\mathrm{crit}} - \Sel(\rho_0)\,.
\end{equation}

The evolution of $\Sel(\rho_n)$ along admissible trajectories is influenced by
disturbance, protocol-dependent driving, and discarded degrees of freedom.
On bounded-geometry slabs, these effects admit uniform control in terms of
the bottleneck data $\Theta$ (spectral gap $\lambda_*$, projector norm,
Lipschitz constants, damping rates).

\subsection{Coherence budget}

\begin{definition}[Coherence budget]
Let $\sigma^2(\Theta,\pi)$ be a variance proxy bounding fluctuations of
$\Sel(\rho_n)$ along admissible trajectories for protocol $\pi$. The
\emph{coherence budget} is defined as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}(\rho_0;\Theta,\pi) :=
\frac{m(\rho_0)^2}{\sigma^2(\Theta,\pi)}\,.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Large $\mathcal{B}$ corresponds to exponentially stable evolution with
negligible probability of basin exit, while $\mathcal{B}\sim O(1)$ marks
near-critical regimes exhibiting threshold (“knee”) behavior.

\subsection{Admissible horizon}

The validity of the reduced description is limited not by an external time
parameter but by admissibility itself.

\begin{definition}[Admissible horizon]
Fix a truncation tolerance $\eps>0$. The \emph{admissible horizon}
$N_{\max}(\rho_0;\Theta,\pi,\eps)$ is the largest integer such that for all
$n\le N_{\max}$ the reduced trajectory
\[
\rho_{n+1} = M_{\pi(n)}(\rho_n)
\]
remains within the admissible domain at tolerance $\eps$.
\end{definition}

The horizon $N_{\max}$ is determined by basin geometry and gravity-dependent
stability encoded in $\Theta$; it is not an externally chosen clock time.
The spectral gap $\lambda_*$ enters only by setting the encoding resolution
and step granularity.

% ======================================================
\section{Effective Encodings and Physical Decision Problems}

\subsection{Finite encodings}

All undecidability statements are made with respect to finite descriptions.

\begin{definition}[Effective instance]
An effective instance is specified by a finite string encoding:
\begin{itemize}
\item an initial reduced state $\rho_0$ to tolerance $\eps$ (e.g.\ a rational
density matrix in a fixed basis),
\item bottleneck data $\Theta$ given by finite rational approximations,
\item a protocol schedule $\pi$, specified as a finite word over a fixed
finite alphabet of admissible protocol primitives,
\item a designated selection event $e_\star$ (e.g.\ entry into a specified basin).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

No noncomputable real numbers or infinite precision inputs are assumed.

\subsection{Robust selection language}

Because physical admissibility requires robustness under small preparation
errors, event occurrence must be formulated robustly.

\begin{definition}[Robust selection language]
Define
\[
\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps :=
\Big\{(\rho_0,\Theta,\pi,\eps,e_\star)\ \Big|\ 
\exists n\le N_{\max}(\rho_0;\Theta,\pi,\eps)\ \text{s.t. } e_\star \text{ occurs for all }
\tilde\rho_0\in B_\eps(\rho_0)\Big\},
\]
where $B_\eps(\rho_0)$ is the trace-norm $\eps$-ball around $\rho_0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
$\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps$ asks whether a selection event occurs
\emph{before admissibility is lost}, independently of small preparation
errors. This matches the operational meaning of events in MTT.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bounded horizon and decidability}

Although $N_{\max}$ is finite for each instance, it is instance-dependent,
not known a priori, and determined by basin geometry rather than by an
external clock.

Moreover, membership in $\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps$ is not a simple
pointwise reachability question. It requires event occurrence for all
perturbations within $B_\eps(\rho_0)$, a robust reachability property that
is strictly stronger than simulating a single trajectory. Finite horizons
therefore do not restore decidability.

\section{Encoding Universal Computation in Event Logs}

\subsection{Two-counter machines}

A two-counter (Minsky) machine $\mathcal{M}$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item a finite set of control states $Q$,
\item two nonnegative integer counters $c_1,c_2$,
\item instructions of the form \textsc{INC}$(i)$, \textsc{DECJZ}$(i)$,
and \textsc{HALT}.
\end{itemize}

Two-counter machines are computationally universal; the halting problem
for such machines is undecidable.

\subsection{Encoding into basins and records}

Each control state $q\in Q$ is associated with a basin $\Bas_q$. Two
counters are encoded by persistent record registers
$\Rec^{(1)},\Rec^{(2)}$.

Define the token-count functionals
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{N}_i(\rho) := |\Rec^{(i)}|,\qquad i=1,2,
\end{equation}
measured at tolerance $\eps$.

An encoding of a machine configuration $(q,c_1,c_2)$ is any
$\rho\in\Bas_q$ satisfying $\mathsf{N}_1(\rho)=c_1$ and
$\mathsf{N}_2(\rho)=c_2$.

\section{Uniform Instruction Simulation}

We now show that admissible projection dynamics can simulate a universal
two-counter machine using selection events as irreversible computational
tokens, without embedding computation in the control protocol itself.

\subsection{Uniform protocol family}

Fix once and for all a finite set of admissible protocol primitives
\[
\mathcal{P}=\{\Pi_1,\ldots,\Pi_k\},
\]
each corresponding to a physically admissible protocol cycle (e.g.\ fixed
measurement strength, probing duration, and control configuration). The
protocol alphabet $\mathcal{P}$ is independent of the encoded program and
input.

The protocol schedule $\pi$ is universal: at each step the choice of
primitive depends only on the current basin label, not on the encoded
machine or input. Thus all program and input data are encoded solely in
the initial reduced state $\rho_0$.

\subsection{Instruction simulation lemma}

\begin{proof}
We give an explicit construction.

\paragraph{Control basins.}
Fix a universal two-counter machine $\mathcal{M}$ with control states
$Q=\{q_1,\dots,q_m\}$ and a distinguished halting state $q_{\mathrm{halt}}$.
By admissibility, there exists a finite family of dynamically stable basins
$\{\Bas_q\}_{q\in Q\cup\{q_{\mathrm{halt}}\}}$ such that:
(i) each $\Bas_q$ is invariant under admissible intra-basin dynamics,
(ii) each basin has a finite stability margin,
(iii) $\Bas_{q_{\mathrm{halt}}}$ is absorbing on the slab.

Membership in $\Bas_q$ encodes the current control state.

\paragraph{Record registers.}
Let $\Rec^{(1)}$ and $\Rec^{(2)}$ be persistent record registers, each consisting
of a countable collection of stable record sites. Define the token-count
functionals
\[
\mathsf{N}_i(\rho) := |\Rec^{(i)}| , \qquad i=1,2,
\]
measured at truncation tolerance $\varepsilon$. By record stability, tokens
cannot be erased by any admissible operation on the slab.

\paragraph{Encoding.}
A machine configuration $(q,c_1,c_2)$ is encoded by any reduced state
$\rho\in\Bas_q$ such that $\mathsf{N}_1(\rho)=c_1$ and
$\mathsf{N}_2(\rho)=c_2$. Finite stability margins guarantee robustness of this
encoding under perturbations of size $\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{Protocol primitives.}
Fix once and for all a finite protocol alphabet
$\mathcal{P}=\{\Pi_1,\dots,\Pi_k\}$, independent of $\mathcal{M}$ and its input.
Each primitive $\Pi_j$ corresponds to a physically admissible protocol cycle
(measurement strength, probing duration, and control configuration fixed).

The interpreter rule is as follows: when the reduced state lies in basin
$\Bas_q$, apply the unique protocol primitive $\Pi(q)\in\mathcal{P}$ assigned
to $q$. This rule depends only on the basin label and is fixed globally.

\paragraph{Increment.}
If the instruction at $q$ is $\textsc{INC}(i)$ with next state $q'$, the protocol
primitive $\Pi(q)$ is chosen so that the reduced dynamics induces a selection
event appending exactly one new stable token to $\Rec^{(i)}$, followed by capture
into $\Bas_{q'}$. This is realized by a controlled basin exit whose post-capture
record includes the new token. Irreversibility follows from basin capture.

\paragraph{Conditional decrement and branch.}
If the instruction at $q$ is $\textsc{DECJZ}(i)$ with targets $q_0,q_1$, the
protocol primitive $\Pi(q)$ is chosen so that its effect depends on the stability
margin associated with $\Rec^{(i)}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $\mathsf{N}_i(\rho)=0$, the basin boundary corresponding to token
presence is absent, and the trajectory is captured into $\Bas_{q_0}$.
\item If $\mathsf{N}_i(\rho)>0$, the presence of a token shifts the admissibility
functional $\Xi$ so that a knee transition occurs, inducing a selection event
that marks one token inactive (reducing the effective count by one) and captures
the trajectory into $\Bas_{q_1}$.
\end{itemize}
This branching uses only basin geometry and finite stability margins.

\paragraph{Halting.}
If the instruction at $q$ is $\textsc{HALT}$, the corresponding protocol primitive
induces capture into the absorbing basin $\Bas_{q_{\mathrm{halt}}}$, producing a
distinguished selection event $e_{\mathrm{halt}}$. By admissibility, return from
$\Bas_{q_{\mathrm{halt}}}$ is excluded on the slab.

\paragraph{Uniformity.}
The protocol alphabet $\mathcal{P}$ and interpreter rule $\Pi(q)$ are fixed
independently of the machine $\mathcal{M}$ and input $w$. All program and input
information is encoded solely in the initial reduced state $\rho_0$ via basin
membership and record tokens. Thus the construction is uniform.

\paragraph{Correctness.}
By induction on the number of protocol cycles, the reduced dynamics simulates the
step-by-step evolution of $\mathcal{M}$. Each instruction is faithfully realized,
and halting occurs if and only if the reduced trajectory enters
$\Bas_{q_{\mathrm{halt}}}$.

This completes the construction.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}
Increment operations correspond to controlled selection events that append
a stable token to the appropriate record register. Conditional
decrement-and-branch operations are realized by threshold (knee) behavior
at basin boundaries: the presence of a token shifts the stability margin so
that the same protocol primitive induces capture into different basins.
Halting corresponds to capture into a designated absorbing basin. Uniformity
follows because the protocol family and interpreter rule are fixed once and
for all.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
No computation is hidden in the controller. Universality arises from basin
structure and record encoding under noninvertible projection.
\end{remark}

\section{Undecidability of Selection under Coherence Bounds}

We now state and prove the main result.

\begin{theorem}[Undecidability of robust selection]
The protocol alphabet $\mathcal{P}$ and the interpreter rule are fixed independently of the
machine $\mathcal{M}$ and input $w$; universality arises solely from state encoding and basin
dynamics under projection.

\[
\mathrm{HALT} \;\le_m\; \mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps .
\]
Consequently, there exists no algorithm that decides membership in
$\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a two-counter machine and $w$ an input. Define a
computable mapping
\[
F:(\mathcal{M},w)\mapsto(\rho_0,\Theta,\pi,\eps,e_\star),
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\rho_0$ encodes the initial control state and counter values via basin
membership and record tokens;
\item $\Theta$ is fixed bottleneck data ensuring admissibility;
\item $\pi$ is the fixed universal protocol schedule;
\item $e_\star$ denotes entry into the halting basin $\Bas_{\mathrm{halt}}$.
\end{itemize}

By Lemma~7.1, the reduced dynamics simulates $\mathcal{M}$ step by step.
Finite stability margins ensure robustness: the occurrence or non-occurrence
of $e_\star$ is invariant under all perturbations in $B_\eps(\rho_0)$.
Therefore,
\[
\mathcal{M}\ \text{halts on } w
\iff
(\rho_0,\Theta,\pi,\eps,e_\star)\in\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps .
\]
Since the halting problem is undecidable, so is
$\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps$.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Computability limitation]
There exists no algorithm that decides robust selection-event occurrence uniformly over all
admissible instances $(\rho_0,\Theta,\pi,\varepsilon,e_\star)$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Gravity, Measurement, and the Origin of Irreducibility}

Undecidability in this framework is not an artifact of exotic microscopic
dynamics but a consequence of projection and admissibility.

\subsection{Role of gravity}

Gravity enters through the bottleneck vector $\Theta$. Curvature and geometry
control stability margins, damping rates, and thus the coherence budget
$\mathcal{B}$. Gravity therefore sets the \emph{resource bound} for effective
predictability: how long a configuration remains admissible and whether
selection events can occur.

\subsection{Measurement and records}

Selection events coincide with irreversible measurement outcomes. The
existence of stable records requires finite stability margins, which in turn
force noninvertible projection. Computational irreducibility thus arises
directly from the physical requirements of measurement and record stability.

\subsection{Beyond chaos}

Chaos concerns sensitivity to initial conditions under a fixed effective law.
Here the obstruction is stronger: no single effective law exists that applies
across admissible regimes. The unpredictability is therefore algorithmic,
not merely practical or probabilistic.

\section{Ornstein--Uhlenbeck and Kramers Bounds}
\label{app:OU}

The coherence budget introduced in Sec.~4 is justified by standard
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck and Kramers-type estimates for barrier crossing in
stochastic systems with drift and damping.

Let $u_n$ denote the least-stable coordinate normal to a basin boundary,
with effective evolution
\begin{equation}
u_{n+1} = (1-\gamma)u_n + \eta_n ,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma>0$ is the stability margin and $\eta_n$ is a disturbance term
with bounded variance determined by $\Theta$ and the protocol $\pi$.
Standard results imply
\begin{equation}
\Var[u_n] \le \frac{\sigma^2(\Theta,\pi)}{2\gamma},
\end{equation}
uniformly on admissible slabs.

Barrier crossing probabilities admit exponential bounds of the form
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}(\text{exit}) \;\lesssim\;
\exp\!\left(-\frac{m(\rho_0)^2}{\sigma^2(\Theta,\pi)}\right)
= \exp(-\mathcal{B}),
\end{equation}
justifying the interpretation of $\mathcal{B}$ as a physical coherence
budget and $N_{\max}$ as a stability-limited horizon.

\section{Two-Counter Machines and Universality}
\label{app:counter}

Two-counter (Minsky) machines consist of a finite control and two
unbounded nonnegative integer registers with instructions
\textsc{INC}, \textsc{DECJZ}, and \textsc{HALT}. It is a classical result
that such machines are computationally universal and that their halting
problem is undecidable.

The use of two counters is minimal: one-counter machines are decidable,
while two counters suffice to simulate arbitrary Turing machines. This
minimality aligns with the present construction, which uses only two
record registers $\Rec^{(1)},\Rec^{(2)}$ and a finite basin structure.

\section{Chaos versus Undecidability}
\label{app:chaos}

It is important to distinguish chaos from undecidability.

Chaotic systems exhibit sensitive dependence on initial conditions but
are governed by a fixed, computable evolution law. In contrast, the
undecidability established here concerns the absence of any algorithmic
law that decides selection-event occurrence across admissible regimes.
The obstruction is structural and persists even when individual
trajectories are computable.

\section{Relation to Undecidability in Hybrid Systems}
\label{app:hybrid}

Undecidability is known to arise in robust reachability problems for
hybrid systems combining continuous dynamics with discrete transitions
and threshold guards. Classic results show that requiring correctness
under perturbations can render reachability undecidable even for systems
with computable local flows.

The present work is not a reformulation of those results. Instead, it
identifies a physical mechanism—noninvertible projection with
admissibility and basin structure—that forces effective dynamics into a
similar undecidable class. The robustness condition in
$\mathrm{SEL}^{\mathrm{rob}}_\eps$ is not a technical convenience but a
physical requirement reflecting stability of records under small
preparation errors.

Thus, while consonant with known results on hybrid systems, the
undecidability here arises from empirical requirements alone, without
assuming infinite precision, exotic real parameters, or ad hoc discrete
control.

\section{Relation to Other Shadow Constructions}
\label{app:shadows}

The undecidability result integrates naturally with other shadow
constructions within the coherent universality class:
\begin{itemize}
\item collapse and measurement-induced thresholds arise from basin exits;
\item causal-set event structure corresponds to the log of selection
events;
\item asymptotic safety fixed points appear as truncation shadows of a
UV endpoint;
\item loop quantum gravity discreteness arises as an encoding artifact;
\item noncommutative geometry and the spectral action encode admissible
overlap structure.
\end{itemize}
Computational irreducibility explains why these distinct frameworks
capture consistent partial shadows without yielding a single globally
computable effective law.
