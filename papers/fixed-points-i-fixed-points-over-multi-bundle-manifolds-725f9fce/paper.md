abstract: |
  We develop a conditional fixed-point framework for gradient flows on internal fiber products of bundles over a base manifold $`Y`$ of dimension $`d := \dim Y`$. Given smooth bundles $`\pi_n : B_n \to Y`$ with compact fibers $`F_n`$ of uniformly bounded geometry, we construct spectral-bundle projections onto the joint harmonic sector and study gradient flows generated by coercive analytic energies $`C : H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})\to \mathbb{R}`$ on the internal product
  ``` math
  B_{\mathrm{int}}:= B_1 \times_Y \cdots \times_Y B_k .
  ```
  We establish from first principles: (i) precise geometry and anisotropic Sobolev structure; (ii) uniform $`H^1`$ bounds for the coherent projector via Riesz–spectral calculus under bounded geometry; (iii) analytic-semigroup well-posedness and smoothing for $`\Phi_t`$; (iv) existence of projected fixed points by a variational method on the coherent sector; alternatively by Schauder (compact base with base-regularized smoothing) or by Darbo using the Kuratowski measure under a confining base potential; (v) uniqueness either from strong convexity on the coherent sector or via an inertial-manifold reduction under a clean spectral gap condition; (vi) quantitative Galerkin approximation for the uniformly elliptic steady problem with Céa-type quasi-optimal error; (vii) examples (with explicit constants) and Łojasiewicz–Simon convergence. All results are analytic/control statements under the displayed hypotheses. The Riemannian base is not automatically physical Lorentzian spacetime, the flow parameter is not automatically physical time, and the abstract fiber product does not by itself select the canonical MTT internal topology.

# Standing Series Axiom (shared across the FP series)

<div class="assumption">

**Assumption 1** (Series Axiom: bounded geometry + uniform spectral gap + coherent projection).

1.  **Base geometry.** $`(Y,g_Y)`$ is a smooth, complete Riemannian manifold. When uniform constants involving base derivatives are required, we additionally assume $`Y`$ has bounded geometry (uniform bounds on curvature and injectivity radius on the region of interest).

2.  **Fiber bounded geometry.** Each bundle $`\pi_n:B_n\to Y`$ has compact fibers $`F_n(y)`$ whose Riemannian geometry is uniformly bounded in $`y`$ (Assumption <a href="#ass:bounded-geom" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:bounded-geom">4</a> below).

3.  **Uniform fiber spectral gap.** The nonnegative fiber Laplacians $`\Delta_{F_n(y)}`$ have a uniform first positive eigenvalue bound $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}>0`$ (Assumption <a href="#ass:gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:gap">5</a>).

4.  **Coherent projector.** The fiberwise Riesz projector $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ onto the joint harmonic sector is well-defined and bounded on the Sobolev scales used (Section <a href="#sec:projectors" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:projectors">2</a>).

5.  **Coherence invariance (when invoked).** For equilibrium identification and coherent-sector variational arguments we assume $`N(\mathrm{Ran}\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\subset \mathrm{Ran}\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ (Assumption <a href="#ass:coh-inv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coh-inv">18</a>).

# Geometry of the internal product and standing hypotheses

## Internal fiber product

Let $`Y`$ be a smooth $`d`$-dimensional manifold with complete Riemannian metric $`g_Y`$. Let $`\pi_n : B_n \to Y`$ be smooth bundles with compact fibers $`F_n(y)`$.

<div class="remark">

*Remark 2* (Control geometry versus physical spacetime). The base $`(Y,g_Y)`$ is the Riemannian geometry used by the elliptic and parabolic estimates in this paper. Identifying it with a Cauchy slice, Euclideanized background, or control geometry associated with a Lorentzian spacetime requires a separate realization theorem. No Lorentzian causal or dynamical conclusion is inferred here.

</div>

The *internal bundle product* (fiber product over $`Y`$) is
``` math
B_{\mathrm{int}}:= B_1 \times_Y \cdots \times_Y B_k
:= \{(b_1,\dots,b_k): \pi_1(b_1)=\cdots=\pi_k(b_k)\in Y\},
```
with projection $`\pi:B_{\mathrm{int}}\to Y`$, $`\pi(b_1,\dots,b_k)=y`$.

#### Universal property.

$`B_{\mathrm{int}}`$ comes with projections $`\mathrm{pr}_n:B_{\mathrm{int}}\to B_n`$ such that $`\pi_n\circ \mathrm{pr}_n=\pi_m\circ \mathrm{pr}_m`$ for all $`n,m`$. For any manifold $`M`$ with maps $`f_n:M\to B_n`$ satisfying $`\pi_1\circ f_1=\cdots=\pi_k\circ f_k`$, there is a unique $`f:M\to B_{\mathrm{int}}`$ with $`\mathrm{pr}_n\circ f=f_n`$.

We equip each fiber
``` math
F(y):=\pi^{-1}(y)\cong F_1(y)\times \cdots \times F_k(y)
```
with a smooth product-type Riemannian metric $`g_{F(y)}`$ varying smoothly with $`y`$ under bounded-geometry assumptions below, and equip $`B_{\mathrm{int}}`$ with the induced product-type metric.

<div class="remark">

*Remark 3* (Scope of the fiber-product model). The displayed product is an abstract analytic construction. In an MTT realization with overlapping vertical structures, or nested structures after explicit inclusion maps have been supplied—in particular when there is a shared central circle—one must work on the selected six-dimensional internal space with the corresponding joint vertical operators; the shared structure must not be counted as an additional independent factor. The theorems below apply after that model supplies the required bounded geometry, joint projector, and gap hypotheses.

</div>

## Bounded geometry and uniform spectral gap

<div id="ass:bounded-geom" class="assumption">

**Assumption 4** (Bounded geometry of fibers). There exist $`K_0,\iota_0>0`$ and an integer $`m\ge 2`$ such that for all $`y\in Y`$ and each $`n=1,\dots,k`$, the fiber $`(F_n(y),g_{F_n(y)})`$ has $`|\sec|\le K_0`$, $`\mathrm{injrad}\ge \iota_0`$, and the fiber metric depends $`C^m`$-smoothly on $`y`$ with all $`y`$-derivatives up to order $`m`$ bounded uniformly on $`Y`$ (in bounded-geometry coordinate charts on the base, when needed).

</div>

#### Nonnegative Laplacians.

Let $`\Delta_{F_n(y)}`$ denote the *nonnegative* Laplace–Beltrami operator on $`F_n(y)`$ (so $`\Delta\ge 0`$ and $`\ker\Delta`$ are the fiberwise harmonic modes). Define the fiber operator on the product fiber $`F(y)`$ by
``` math
\Delta_F(y) := \sum_{n=1}^k \Delta_{F_n(y)}.
```

<div id="ass:gap" class="assumption">

**Assumption 5** (Uniform fiber spectral gap). There exists $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}>0`$ such that
``` math
\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}\le \inf_{y\in Y}\ \min_{1\le n\le k}\ \lambda_1\!\big(\Delta_{F_n(y)}\big),
```
where $`\lambda_1(\Delta_{F_n(y)})`$ is the first *positive* eigenvalue of the nonnegative fiber Laplacian. Moreover, $`\dim\ker\Delta_{F_n(y)}`$ is constant in $`y`$ for each $`n`$ (hence the harmonic bundle ranks are constant).

</div>

## Function spaces and anisotropic norms

Let $`L^2 := L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$ with the product volume measure. Let $`\nabla_F`$ denote the gradient along fibers. Define the anisotropic (fiber) Sobolev space
``` math
H^1_F := \{\Psi\in L^2:\ \nabla_F\Psi\in L^2\},
\qquad
\|\Psi\|_{H^1_F}^2 := \|\Psi\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla_F\Psi\|_{L^2}^2.
```
We use $`H_0 := L^2`$ and $`H_F := H^1_F`$ as the primary Banach spaces for mapping properties.

<div class="definition">

**Definition 6** (Full Sobolev space). We write
``` math
H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})
:=
\big\{\Psi\in L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}}):\ \nabla_Y\Psi\in L^2,\ \nabla_F\Psi\in L^2\big\},
```
with norm
``` math
\|\Psi\|_{H^1}^2 := \|\Psi\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla_Y\Psi\|_{L^2}^2 + \|\nabla_F\Psi\|_{L^2}^2.
```

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 7* (Why $`H^1_F`$ is the correct space for fiber coercivity). The fiber operator $`\Delta_F`$ controls $`\|\nabla_F\Psi\|_{L^2}`$ but does not control $`\|\nabla_Y\Psi\|_{L^2}`$. Thus a Poincaré-type inequality driven solely by $`\Delta_F`$ fails on the full $`H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$, while it holds naturally in $`H^1_F`$. This is the key coercivity correction used throughout the series.

</div>

#### Coherent sector notation.

Let $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ be the coherent projector (defined in Section <a href="#sec:projectors" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:projectors">2</a>) and set
``` math
H_{\mathrm{coh}}:= \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\cap H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}}),\qquad Q:= \mathrm{Id}- \Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}.
```

# Projectors via fiberwise spectral calculus

For each $`y\in Y`$ define the self-adjoint nonnegative operator
``` math
A(y) := \Delta_F(y)\quad \text{on } L^2(F(y)),\qquad \mathrm{dom}(A(y))=H^2(F(y)).
```
By Assumption <a href="#ass:gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:gap">5</a>, $`\sigma(A(y))\cap (0,\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}})\!=\!\varnothing`$ uniformly in $`y`$.

Let $`\Gamma`$ be the positively oriented circle of radius $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}/2`$ centered at $`0`$. Define the Riesz projector onto $`\ker A(y)`$ by
``` math
\Pi(y) := \frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma} (z-A(y))^{-1}\,dz.
```
Define $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ on $`L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$ fiberwise by
``` math
(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi)(\cdot,y) := \Pi(y)\Psi(\cdot,y).
```

<div class="lemma">

**Lemma 8** (Uniform boundedness of $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ on $`L^2`$ and $`H^1_F`$). *Under Assumptions <a href="#ass:bounded-geom" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:bounded-geom">4</a>–<a href="#ass:gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:gap">5</a>, there exists $`C_\Pi\ge 1`$ such that
``` math
\|\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\|_{L^2\to L^2}\le 1,\qquad \|\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\|_{H^1_F\to H^1_F}\le C_\Pi.
```
In the $`y`$-independent flat case one may take $`C_\Pi=1`$.*

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 9* (On $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ for scalar fields). If the fibers are connected and $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ projects scalar functions onto $`\ker\Delta_F`$, then $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ consists of fiberwise constants and $`\nabla_F(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi)=0`$, so $`\|\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi\|_{H^1_F}=\|\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi\|_{L^2}\le \|\Psi\|_{L^2}`$ (hence $`C_\Pi=1`$). The general $`C_\Pi\ge 1`$ form is kept for vector-valued settings (e.g. bundles of forms/spinors) where $`\ker\Delta`$ may have rank $`>1`$.

</div>

<div id="lem:PiH1" class="lemma">

**Lemma 10** (Boundedness on full $`H^1`$). *Under Assumptions <a href="#ass:bounded-geom" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:bounded-geom">4</a>–<a href="#ass:gap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:gap">5</a> (and base bounded-geometry control when $`y`$-derivatives are invoked), $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ extends to a bounded map $`H^1\to H^1`$, hence $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ is closed in $`H^1`$.*

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 11* (Proof references for spectral projectors). Uniform control of the Riesz–spectral projector on Sobolev scales follows from the Riesz–Dunford formula together with parameter-dependent elliptic regularity on bounded-geometry families; see, for example, .

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 12* (Automatic commutation on the domain). Since $`\Pi(y)`$ projects onto $`\ker A(y)`$, we have $`A(y)\Pi(y)=0`$ and $`\Pi(y)A(y)=0`$ on $`\mathrm{dom}(A(y))`$. Thus $`[A,\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}]=0`$ on $`\mathrm{dom}(A)`$, and by density/continuity on $`L^2`$. Since $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ is a Riesz projector defined via the functional calculus of $`A`$, it commutes with $`A`$ and hence with $`e^{-tA}`$.

</div>

# Gradient flow: well-posedness and smoothing

## Energy, decomposition, and basic assumptions

Let $`C:H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})\to \mathbb{R}`$ be a real-analytic functional such that its $`L^2`$-gradient decomposes as
``` math
\nabla C(\Psi) = A\Psi + N(\Psi),
```
where $`A=\Delta_F`$ acts fiberwise (nonnegative, self-adjoint) and
``` math
N: H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})\to L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}})
```
is locally Lipschitz on bounded sets (with Lipschitz constant $`L`$ on the relevant sublevel set).

The gradient flow is
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flow}
\partial_t\Psi(t) = -A\Psi(t) - N(\Psi(t)),\qquad \Psi(0)=\Psi_0\in L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}}).
\end{equation}
```

<div class="remark">

*Remark 13* (Stabilization parameter). Here $`t`$ parametrizes the analytic gradient/stabilization flow. It is not identified with physical Lorentzian time. A physical evolution operator $`U(t_2,t_1)`$ and its relation, if any, to this flow must be defined separately.

</div>

<div id="ass:lwp" class="assumption">

**Assumption 14** (Well-posedness and smoothing). For every $`\Psi_0\in L^2`$, <a href="#eq:flow" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow">[eq:flow]</a> has a unique global mild solution $`\Psi\in C([0,\infty);L^2)\cap C((0,\infty);H^1)`$. For each $`t>0`$, the time-$`t`$ map $`\Phi_t:\Psi_0\mapsto \Psi(t)`$ maps $`L^2`$ into $`H^1_F`$ and is locally Lipschitz as a map $`L^2\to H^1_F`$; in particular, for some $`M_1(t)`$,
``` math
\|\Phi_t(\Psi_1)-\Phi_t(\Psi_2)\|_{H^1_F}
\le M_1(t)e^{Lt}\|\Psi_1-\Psi_2\|_{L^2}.
```

</div>

<div id="prop:energydec" class="proposition">

**Proposition 15** (Energy dissipation and forward invariance of sublevels). *Along sufficiently regular solutions of the $`L^2`$-gradient flow <a href="#eq:flow" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow">[eq:flow]</a>, the energy is nonincreasing and
``` math
C[\Psi(t)] + \int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla C(\Psi(s))\|_{L^2}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s = C[\Psi_0]\qquad (t\ge 0).
```
In particular, every energy sublevel $`\{\Psi:\ C[\Psi]\le M\}`$ is forward invariant. For semilinear analytic-semigroup flows this identity can be justified by standard approximation arguments; see, e.g., .*

</div>

``` math
D_M:= \{\Psi\in H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}}):\ C[\Psi]\le M\}.
```

<div id="ass:sublevel-inv" class="assumption">

**Assumption 16** (Forward invariance of energy sublevels). For each $`M>0`$, the energy sublevel $`D_M=\{\Psi\in H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}}): C[\Psi]\le M\}`$ is forward invariant under the flow $`\Phi_t`$ used in the Darbo argument (in particular, under the base-regularized flow if $`\varepsilon>0`$ is employed).

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 17* (Semigroup/ODE splitting). Decompose $`L^2=\mathrm{Ran}(Q)\oplus \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$. On $`\mathrm{Ran}(Q)`$ the sectorial operator $`A|_{\mathrm{Ran}(Q)}\ge \ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$ generates an analytic semigroup with the standard $`t^{-1/2}e^{-\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}t}`$ bound for $`A^{1/2}e^{-tA}`$. On $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ the flow reduces to an ODE $`\partial_t u=-\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}N(u)`$ (when coherence invariance holds).

</div>

<div id="ass:coh-inv" class="assumption">

**Assumption 18** (Coherence invariance). We say coherence invariance holds if
``` math
N(\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}))\subset \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}).
```
Under this condition the coherent sector is invariant under the flow.

</div>

## Projected map and identification of equilibria

Define
``` math
T_t := \Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\circ \Phi_t:\ L^2\to \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}).
```

<div id="prop:projfix" class="proposition">

**Proposition 19** (Projected fixed points are equilibria). *Assume coherence invariance (Assumption <a href="#ass:coh-inv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coh-inv">18</a>). If $`\Psi^\ast\in \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ satisfies $`T_t(\Psi^\ast)=\Psi^\ast`$ for some $`t>0`$, then $`\Phi_s(\Psi^\ast)=\Psi^\ast`$ for all $`s\ge 0`$, and $`\nabla C(\Psi^\ast)=0`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Coherence invariance implies $`\Phi_s(\Psi^\ast)\in\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ for all $`s\ge0`$, hence $`T_t(\Psi^\ast)=\Phi_t(\Psi^\ast)=\Psi^\ast`$. Applying the energy identity of Proposition <a href="#prop:energydec" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:energydec">15</a> on $`[0,t]`$ gives
``` math
0=C[\Phi_t(\Psi^\ast)]-C[\Psi^\ast]
=-\int_0^t\|\nabla C(\Phi_s(\Psi^\ast))\|_{L^2}^2\,\mathrm{d}s.
```
The integrand therefore vanishes, so the orbit is stationary and $`\nabla C(\Psi^\ast)=0`$. ◻

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 20* (Without coherence invariance). If $`N(\mathrm{Ran}\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\not\subset \mathrm{Ran}\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$, then a fixed point of $`T_t=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Phi_t`$ need not be a true equilibrium of $`\Phi_t`$. In that case $`T_t`$ can still be used to build invariant sets, but not to identify equilibria.

</div>

## Detailed semigroup bound underpinning $`M_1(t)`$

<div id="lem:semigroup" class="lemma">

**Lemma 21** (Semigroup bound underpinning $`M_1(t)`$). *Let $`Q:=\mathrm{Id}-\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$. Then for all $`t>0`$,
``` math
\|A^{1/2}e^{-tA}Q\|_{L^2\to L^2}
\le C\,t^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda^\discretionary{}{}{} t},
\qquad
\|e^{-tA}\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\|_{L^2\to L^2}=1.
```
Consequently, for all $`\Psi_1,\Psi_2\in L^2`$,
``` math
\|\Phi_t(\Psi_1)-\Phi_t(\Psi_2)\|_{H^1_F}
\le C\bigl(1+t^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda^\discretionary{}{}{} t}\bigr)e^{Lt}\,
\|\Psi_1-\Psi_2\|_{L^2}.
```
In particular, one may take
``` math
M_1(t):=C\bigl(1+t^{-1/2}e^{-\lambda^\discretionary{}{}{} t}\bigr).
```*

</div>

<div id="lem:Tlip" class="lemma">

**Lemma 22** (Lipschitz continuity of $`T_t`$). *For each $`t>0`$ there exists $`K(t)`$ such that for all $`\Psi_{1,2}\in L^2`$,
``` math
\|T_t(\Psi_1)-T_t(\Psi_2)\|_{H^1_F}
\le C_\Pi K(t)e^{Lt}\|\Psi_1-\Psi_2\|_{L^2}.
```
Moreover, *compactness* of $`T_t`$ requires base regularization (see Section <a href="#sec:schauder" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="sec:schauder">4.2</a>) or another mechanism that provides compactness in the base variable.*

</div>

# Existence of equilibria and projected fixed points

## Variational existence on the coherent sector

<div id="ass:coercive" class="assumption">

**Assumption 23** (Coercivity and weak lower semicontinuity). $`C:H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})\to\mathbb{R}`$ is bounded below, coercive on $`H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$, and sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous.

</div>

<div id="thm:variational" class="theorem">

**Theorem 24** (Variational existence). *Assume coherence invariance (Assumption <a href="#ass:coh-inv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coh-inv">18</a>) and Assumption <a href="#ass:coercive" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coercive">23</a>. Then there exists $`\Psi^\ast\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ such that $`\nabla C(\Psi^\ast)=0`$. In particular, $`T_t(\Psi^\ast)=\Psi^\ast`$ for all $`t\ge 0`$.*

</div>

## Alternative route: Schauder (compact base) and base-regularization

To obtain compactness of the time-$`t`$ map in the *base* variable, introduce a base-regularized generator
``` math
\ensuremath{A_{\varepsilon}}:= \Delta_F + \varepsilon \Delta_Y,\qquad \varepsilon\in(0,1],
```
and consider the corresponding flow
``` math
\partial_t\Psi = -\ensuremath{A_{\varepsilon}}\Psi - N(\Psi).
```

<div id="ass:base-reg" class="assumption">

**Assumption 25** (Base-regularized well-posedness). For some $`\varepsilon\in(0,1]`$ and $`\delta>0`$, the flow with generator $`\ensuremath{A_{\varepsilon}}`$ is globally well-posed and, for every $`t>0`$, maps bounded subsets of $`L^2`$ into bounded subsets of $`H^{1+\delta}(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$. This is a standing regularity hypothesis to be verified for each nonlinearity and model.

</div>

<div id="lem:compact" class="lemma">

**Lemma 26** (Compactness for compact bases). *If $`Y`$ is compact and Assumption <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a> holds, then for each $`t>0`$, $`\Phi_t:L^2\to H^1`$ is compact on bounded sets. Consequently $`T_t=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Phi_t`$ is compact on bounded sets in $`H^1`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The total fibered space is compact when $`Y`$ and the fibers are compact. The Rellich embedding $`H^{1+\delta}\hookrightarrow\!\hookrightarrow H^1`$ is then compact. Compose this embedding with the smoothing in Assumption <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a> and the bounded map $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}:H^1\to H^1`$ from Lemma <a href="#lem:PiH1" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:PiH1">10</a>. ◻

</div>

<div id="thm:schauder" class="theorem">

**Theorem 27** (Schauder fixed point). *Assume $`Y`$ is compact and Assumption <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a> holds. Let $`D\subset H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ be nonempty, closed, bounded, and convex, and assume $`T_t(D)\subset D`$ for some $`t>0`$. Then $`T_t`$ has a fixed point in $`D`$.*

</div>

## Darbo condensing on noncompact bases

<div class="definition">

**Definition 28** (Kuratowski measure of noncompactness). For a bounded set $`B`$ in a metric space, $`\alpha(B)`$ is the infimum of $`r>0`$ such that $`B`$ can be covered by finitely many sets of diameter $`<r`$.

</div>

<div id="ass:confinement" class="assumption">

**Assumption 29** (Confinement). There exists a smooth $`V:Y\to[0,\infty)`$ with $`V(y)\to\infty`$ as $`r(y):=\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}_Y(y,y_0)\to\infty`$ for some basepoint $`y_0\in Y`$, and a constant $`K\ge 0`$ such that
``` math
C[\Psi]\ge \int_Y V(y)\,\|\Psi(\cdot,y)\|_{L^2(F(y))}^2\,\mathrm{d}y - K.
```

</div>

<div id="lem:tails" class="lemma">

**Lemma 30** (Uniform tail smallness). *Under Assumption <a href="#ass:confinement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:confinement">29</a>, for each $`M>0`$ and $`\epsilon>0`$ there exists $`R>0`$ such that
``` math
\int_{\{r(y)>R\}} \|\Psi(\cdot,y)\|_{L^2(F(y))}^2\,\mathrm{d}y \le \epsilon^2
\quad\text{whenever } \Psi\in H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}})\ \text{and}\ C[\Psi]\le M.
```*

</div>

<div id="lem:KR" class="lemma">

**Lemma 31** (Compact-local / small-tail decomposition). *Assume Assumptions <a href="#ass:coercive" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coercive">23</a>, <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a>, and <a href="#ass:confinement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:confinement">29</a>, and fix $`t>0`$. Let $`\chi_R\in C_c^\infty(Y)`$ satisfy $`0\le \chi_R\le 1`$, $`\chi_R\equiv 1`$ on $`\{r\le R\}`$ and $`\chi_R\equiv 0`$ on $`\{r\ge R+1\}`$, and write $`\pi:B_{\mathrm{int}}\to Y`$ for the base projection.*

*Define the *output cutoff* maps
``` math
K_R(\Psi_0):=(\chi_R\circ\pi)\,\Phi_t(\Psi_0),
\qquad
S_R(\Psi_0):=(1-\chi_R\circ\pi)\,\Phi_t(\Psi_0).
```
Then $`\Phi_t=K_R+S_R`$. Moreover:*

1.  *For each $`M>0`$, the map $`K_R:D_M\to L^2(B_{\mathrm{int}})`$ is compact.*

2.  *If $`D_M`$ is forward invariant under $`\Phi_t`$, then for each $`M>0`$,
    ``` math
    \sup_{\Psi_0\in D_M}\|S_R(\Psi_0)\|_{L^2}\to 0
    \qquad\text{as }R\to\infty.
    ```*

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 32* (Why this fixes the “tail linearity” issue). Tail smallness follows either from Proposition 3.2 for the original gradient flow, or from Assumption 3.3 when working with the base–regularized flow used in the Darbo argument.

</div>

<div id="prop:condensing" class="proposition">

**Proposition 33** (Condensing property on energy sublevels). *Assume Assumptions <a href="#ass:coercive" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coercive">23</a>, <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a>, and <a href="#ass:confinement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:confinement">29</a>, and fix $`t>0`$ and an energy level $`M>0`$. On the invariant sublevel $`D_M:=\{\Psi:\ C[\Psi]\le M\}`$, the map $`\Phi_t`$ is *Kuratowski-condensing* in the sense of Sadovskiı̆: for every bounded set $`B\subset D_M`$ with $`\alpha(B)>0`$ one has $`\alpha(\Phi_t(B))<\alpha(B)`$. Consequently, $`T_t=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\circ\Phi_t`$ is condensing in $`L^2`$ on any $`T_t`$-invariant subset of $`D_M\cap \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$, because $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ is an $`L^2`$-orthogonal projection and hence has norm one.*

</div>

<div id="thm:darbo" class="theorem">

**Theorem 34** (Darbo–Sadovskiı̆ fixed point). *Assume Assumptions <a href="#ass:coh-inv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coh-inv">18</a>, <a href="#ass:coercive" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coercive">23</a>, <a href="#ass:base-reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:base-reg">25</a>, and <a href="#ass:confinement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:confinement">29</a>. Fix $`t>0`$ and an energy level $`M>0`$. Let $`D\subset D_M\cap\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ be nonempty, $`L^2`$-closed, bounded, convex, and invariant under $`T_t`$. Then $`T_t:D\to D`$ has a fixed point. By Proposition <a href="#prop:projfix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projfix">19</a>, that fixed point is an equilibrium of the gradient flow.*

</div>

# Uniqueness of equilibria

## Route (A): strong convexity / strong monotonicity on the coherent sector

<div id="ass:strongmono" class="assumption">

**Assumption 35** (Strong monotonicity on $`H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$). There exists $`\mu>0`$ such that for all $`u,v\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$,
``` math
\left\langle \nabla C(u)-\nabla C(v),\,u-v \right\rangle_{L^2}\ge \mu \|u-v\|_{L^2}^2.
```

</div>

<div id="thm:uniqueH" class="theorem">

**Theorem 36** (Uniqueness on the coherent sector). *Under Assumptions <a href="#ass:coh-inv" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coh-inv">18</a> and <a href="#ass:strongmono" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:strongmono">35</a>, $`C|_{H_{\mathrm{coh}}}`$ has a unique minimizer $`\Psi^\ast`$, which is the unique equilibrium in $`H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$. Moreover,
``` math
\|\Phi_t(u)-\Phi_t(v)\|_{L^2}\le e^{-\mu t}\|u-v\|_{L^2}\qquad \forall\,u,v\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}.
```*

</div>

## Route (B): inertial manifold reduction

Let $`P_0`$ be a finite-rank spectral-bundle projection and $`Q_0:=\mathrm{Id}-P_0`$.

<div id="ass:IMgap" class="assumption">

**Assumption 37** (Spectral gap and Lipschitz nonlinearity). Assume there exists $`\gamma>0`$ such that
``` math
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\big(\sigma(A|_{\mathrm{Ran}(Q_0)}),\,\sigma(A|_{\mathrm{Ran}(P_0)})\big)\ge \gamma,
\qquad
\|N(u)-N(v)\|_{L^2}\le L\|u-v\|_{L^2}.
```

</div>

<div id="thm:IM" class="theorem">

**Theorem 38** (Inertial manifold). *If $`L<\gamma/4`$, there exists a finite-dimensional $`C^1`$ inertial manifold $`M=\{\xi+G(\xi):\ \xi\in\mathrm{Ran}(P_0)\}`$ that is invariant and exponentially attracting.*

</div>

*Remark 39* (References). Inertial manifold constructions for semilinear parabolic equations under a spectral-gap condition are classical; see, for example, .

</div>

<div id="ass:redmono" class="assumption">

**Assumption 40** (Reduced strong monotonicity). There exists $`\mu>0`$ such that the reduced vector field on $`\mathrm{Ran}(P_0)`$ is strongly monotone with constant $`\mu`$.

</div>

<div id="thm:IMunique" class="theorem">

**Theorem 41** (Uniqueness via inertial manifold). *Under Assumptions <a href="#ass:IMgap" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:IMgap">37</a> and <a href="#ass:redmono" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:redmono">40</a>, the flow has a unique equilibrium $`\Psi^\ast\in M`$, exponentially attracting on $`M`$. If additionally coherence invariance holds and $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\subset \mathrm{Ran}(P_0)`$, then $`\Psi^\ast\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ and agrees with the coherent-sector variational minimizer.*

</div>

<div class="example">

**Example 42** (IM works while convexity fails). Let $`F=S^1_R`$ and $`N(u)=u^3-u`$ (Allen–Cahn). $`N`$ is not convex so Assumption <a href="#ass:strongmono" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:strongmono">35</a> fails. On $`\{\|u\|_{H^1}\le \rho\}`$ one has $`\|N'(u)\|\le 3\rho^2+1=:L(\rho)`$, and $`\gamma`$ is comparable to the spectral gap $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}=R^{-2}`$. Choosing $`\rho`$ so that $`L(\rho)<\gamma/4`$ yields the inertial manifold and uniqueness on it.

</div>

# Galerkin approximation for the steady state

We approximate equilibria by discretizing a uniformly elliptic steady problem. To ensure coercivity (and uniqueness) we assume the elliptic part includes a strictly positive zeroth-order term (mass or confinement).

<div id="ass:coerciveAeps" class="assumption">

**Assumption 43** (Coercive elliptic steady operator). Let $`\varepsilon>0`$ and assume the steady operator has the form
``` math
\widetilde A_\varepsilon := \Delta_F + \varepsilon\Delta_Y + W,
```
where $`W\ge w_0>0`$ is a measurable multiplication operator and the associated closed quadratic form has domain
``` math
V_\varepsilon:=\{w\in H^1(B_{\mathrm{int}}):W^{1/2}w\in L^2\}.
```
Examples include $`W\equiv m_0^2`$ on compact $`Y`$ and a confining potential $`W=V(y)\to\infty`$ on a noncompact base.

</div>

We consider the steady equation
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:steady}
\widetilde A_\varepsilon \Psi + N(\Psi)=0.
\end{equation}
```

<div class="definition">

**Definition 44** (Energy norm for $`\widetilde A_\varepsilon`$). Under Assumption <a href="#ass:coerciveAeps" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coerciveAeps">43</a>, define for $`w\in V_\varepsilon`$
``` math
\|w\|_{\varepsilon}^2
:= \|\nabla_F w\|_{L^2}^2 + \varepsilon\|\nabla_Y w\|_{L^2}^2
+\int_{B_{\mathrm{int}}}W|w|^2.
```

</div>

<div id="lem:strongmonoF" class="lemma">

**Lemma 45** (Strong monotonicity in the energy norm). *Assume Assumption <a href="#ass:coerciveAeps" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:coerciveAeps">43</a>. Suppose $`N`$ is $`L`$-Lipschitz in $`L^2`$ on the set of interest and $`L<w_0`$. Let $`F:=\widetilde A_\varepsilon+N:V_\varepsilon\to
V_\varepsilon^\ast`$. Then for all $`u,v`$ in that set,
``` math
\langle F(u)-F(v),u-v\rangle_{V_\varepsilon^\ast,V_\varepsilon}
\ge \|u-v\|_\varepsilon^2-L\|u-v\|_{L^2}^2
\ge \mu_\varepsilon \|u-v\|_{\varepsilon}^2,
```
where $`\mu_\varepsilon := 1-L/w_0>0`$.*

</div>

<div id="thm:cea" class="theorem">

**Theorem 46** (Céa-type quasi-optimal error in the energy norm). *Assume Lemma <a href="#lem:strongmonoF" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:strongmonoF">45</a>. Let $`V_N\subset V_\varepsilon`$ be a finite-dimensional Galerkin space and let $`\Psi^\ast`$ solve $`\widetilde A_\varepsilon\Psi + N(\Psi)=0`$. Let $`\Psi_N\in V_N`$ satisfy the Galerkin condition
``` math
\langle \widetilde A_\varepsilon\Psi_N+N(\Psi_N),v
\rangle_{V_\varepsilon^\ast,V_\varepsilon}=0
\qquad\forall\,v\in V_N.
```
Then $`\Psi_N`$ is unique (for each $`N`$) and
``` math
\|\Psi^\ast-\Psi_N\|_{\varepsilon}
\le
\frac{1+L/w_0}{1-L/w_0}\,
\inf_{v\in V_N}\|\Psi^\ast-v\|_{\varepsilon}.
```
If $`W`$ is also bounded above, the norm $`\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}`$ is equivalent to $`H^1`$, hence this yields an $`H^1`$ error bound with an additional explicit norm-equivalence constant. For a confining unbounded $`W`$, the result is instead an estimate in the weighted form space $`V_\varepsilon`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The quadratic-form part has continuity constant one in $`\|\cdot\|_\varepsilon`$. Moreover,
``` math
|\langle N(u)-N(v),w\rangle|
\le L\|u-v\|_{L^2}\|w\|_{L^2}
\le \frac{L}{w_0}\|u-v\|_\varepsilon\|w\|_\varepsilon.
```
Thus $`F`$ has continuity constant $`1+L/w_0`$ and strong-monotonicity constant $`1-L/w_0`$. Galerkin orthogonality and the standard nonlinear Céa argument give the displayed ratio. Strong monotonicity also gives uniqueness of the finite-dimensional solution. ◻

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 47*. In Theorem 6.4 the Lipschitz constant $`L`$ is taken with respect to the $`L^2`$ norm. The conversion to the energy norm $`\|\cdot\|_\varepsilon`$ uses the inequality
``` math
\|u\|_{L^2}\le w_0^{-1/2}\|u\|_\varepsilon,
```
which explains the appearance of the factor $`(1+L/w_0)`$ in the Céa constant.

</div>

# Examples and counterexamples

## Compact fibers with fixed geometry: circles and tori have $`\ensuremath{\lambda_{1}}>0`$

<div class="proposition">

**Proposition 48**. *If $`F`$ is compact, then the first positive Laplace eigenvalue $`\lambda_1(\Delta_F)>0`$.*

</div>

<div class="corollary">

**Corollary 49** (Circle/torus examples). *On $`S^1_R`$, $`\sigma(\Delta)=\{(k/R)^2:\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}`$, so $`\lambda_1=R^{-2}>0`$. Similarly for flat tori $`T^m`$ one has $`\lambda_1>0`$.*

</div>

## How the uniform gap $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$ can fail

If radii degenerate (e.g. $`R(y)\to\infty`$) or fibers become noncompact, then $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$ can drop to $`0`$, destroying uniform estimates.

## Effect of base potentials

A smooth confining potential $`V:Y\to[0,\infty)`$ added to $`C`$ improves tail coercivity (Assumption <a href="#ass:confinement" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:confinement">29</a>) but does not change the fiber spectra.

## Worked example with explicit constants: $`Y=\ensuremath{S^{1}}`$, $`F(y)=\ensuremath{S^{1}}_{R(y)}`$

Assume $`R\in C^1(S^1)`$ with $`0<R_{\min}\le R(y)\le R_{\max}`$ and $`|R'(y)|\le \rho`$.

1.  **First eigenvalue:** $`\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}=\inf_y \lambda_1(\Delta_{F(y)}) = 1/R_{\max}^2`$.

2.  **$`H^1`$ projector bound (one explicit model):**

    with $`A(y)=\Delta_{F(y)}=-R(y)^{-2}\partial_\theta^2`$ one computes $`\partial_y A(y)= 2R'(y)R(y)^{-3}\partial_\theta^2`$ and hence $`\|\partial_y A(y)\|_{H^2\to L^2}\le 2\rho R_{\min}^{-3}`$. Using the Riesz formula and $`\|(z-A)^{-1}\|\le 2/\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$ for $`|z|=\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}/2`$ yields a concrete
    ``` math
    \|\Pi\|_{H^1\to H^1}\le 1+\sup_y \|\partial_y\Pi(y)\|_{L^2\to L^2}
    \le 1+\frac{4\rho R_{\max}^2}{R_{\min}^3}.
    ```

3.  **IM threshold:** taking $`P_0=\Pi`$ gives $`\gamma=\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}=R_{\max}^{-2}`$, and the IM criterion is $`L<\gamma/4 = 1/(4R_{\max}^2)`$.

such that $`\|\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\|_{H^1_F\to H^1_F}\le C_\Pi`$. If the fibers are $`y`$-independent and flat, $`C_\Pi=1`$.*

**Lemma 51** (Norm equivalence on the harmonic bundle). *On $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ one has $`\nabla_F\Psi=0`$ in the scalar connected-fiber case, hence $`\|\Psi\|_{H^1_F}=\|\Psi\|_{L^2}`$. More generally, on $`\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$ all Sobolev norms are uniformly equivalent by elliptic regularity on the harmonic bundle.*

</div>

# Łojasiewicz–Simon convergence of the gradient flow

<div id="ass:PS" class="assumption">

**Assumption 52** (Analyticity and Palais–Smale). $`C`$ is real-analytic and satisfies a Palais–Smale condition on energy sublevels: if $`C[\Psi_n]`$ is bounded and $`\|\nabla C(\Psi_n)\|_{L^2}\to 0`$, then $`(\Psi_n)`$ has a convergent subsequence in $`H^1`$. We also assume the orbit under consideration remains in a bounded energy sublevel on which the gradient flow is precompact in $`H^1`$ and the local Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality is valid near every cluster point.

</div>

<div class="theorem">

**Theorem 53** (Łojasiewicz–Simon convergence). *Assume Assumption <a href="#ass:PS" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:PS">52</a>. For any solution $`\Psi(t)`$ of <a href="#eq:flow" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:flow">[eq:flow]</a> with initial data in an energy sublevel, there exists $`\Psi_\infty`$ with $`\nabla C(\Psi_\infty)=0`$ and
``` math
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\Psi(t)-\Psi_\infty\|_{H^1}=0.
```
If coherence invariance holds and $`\Psi(0)\in \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$, then $`\Psi_\infty\in \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$.*

</div>

*Remark 54* (Reference). The underlying Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality and convergence mechanism originate with .

</div>

<div class="corollary">

**Corollary 55** (Exponential rate under nondegeneracy). *If, in addition, the limiting equilibrium is nondegenerate in the relevant phase space—equivalently, the local Łojasiewicz–Simon exponent is $`1/2`$, for example because the linearized Hessian has a positive spectral gap after quotienting exact symmetries—then $`\|\Psi(t)-\Psi_\infty\|_{H^1}\le Ce^{-\eta t}`$ for some $`\eta>0`$. Uniqueness by itself does not imply an exponential rate.*

# Singular perturbation and the limit $`\ensuremath{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}`$

Let $`\Psi_\varepsilon`$ solve the base-regularized steady equation
``` math
(\Delta_F+\varepsilon\Delta_Y)\Psi_\varepsilon + N(\Psi_\varepsilon)=0.
```

<div id="ass:unifbds" class="assumption">

**Assumption 56** (Uniform bounds). Assume coherence invariance and $`\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\|\Psi_\varepsilon\|_{H^1}<\infty`$. Assume either $`Y`$ is compact or a confining potential provides compactness on energy sublevels. Assume also that $`N`$ is sequentially demicontinuous along this compactness class: whenever $`u_j\rightharpoonup u`$ in $`H^1`$ and $`u_j\to u`$ in $`L^2`$, then $`N(u_j)\rightharpoonup N(u)`$ in $`H^{-1}`$. This holds for the standard subcritical Nemytskii nonlinearities but must be checked in each model.

</div>

<div class="theorem">

**Theorem 57** (Convergence as $`\varepsilon\downarrow 0`$). *Under Assumption <a href="#ass:unifbds" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:unifbds">56</a> and, in addition, either*

1.  *the $`Q`$-sector Lipschitz constant satisfies $`L<\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$, or*

2.  *$`N`$ is weakly monotone on the $`Q`$-sector: $`\left\langle QN(u)-QN(v),\,Q(u-v) \right\rangle_{L^2}\ge 0`$ for all $`u,v`$,*

*there exists a subsequence $`\varepsilon_j\downarrow 0`$ with $`\Psi_{\varepsilon_j}\to \Psi^\ast`$ in $`L^2`$ and weakly in $`H^1`$, where
``` math
\Delta_F\Psi^\ast + N(\Psi^\ast)=0,\qquad \text{and necessarily }\Psi^\ast\in \mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}).
```*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The uniform $`H^1`$ bound and the compactness hypothesis give a subsequence with the stated weak-$`H^1`$/strong-$`L^2`$ convergence. The term $`\varepsilon_j\Delta_Y\Psi_{\varepsilon_j}`$ tends to zero in $`H^{-1}`$, while the fiber form passes to the limit weakly and demicontinuity identifies the nonlinear limit. This yields the limiting steady equation.

Let $`p=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi^\ast`$ and $`q=Q\Psi^\ast`$. Coherence invariance gives $`QN(p)=0`$. Testing the $`Q`$ equation against $`q`$ gives
``` math
\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}\|q\|_{L^2}^2
\le -\langle Q(N(p+q)-N(p)),q\rangle.
```
Under alternative (i), the right side has absolute value at most $`L\|q\|_{L^2}^2`$, so $`L<\ensuremath{\lambda^{\ast}}`$ forces $`q=0`$. Under alternative (ii), the nonlinear term is nonnegative on the left-hand side and again $`q=0`$. Thus $`\Psi^\ast\in\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})`$. ◻

</div>

# Related work and novelty

Our existence and regularity statements are classical in spirit, but three aspects appear new or at least not treated in this precise combination: (i) spectral-bundle projections with uniform constants on fibered products with bounded geometry (Theorem <a href="#thm:PiBound" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:PiBound">50</a>), including explicit constants in a worked model; (ii) a condensing proof on noncompact bases via a compact-local/small-tail decomposition (Lemma <a href="#lem:KR" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="lem:KR">31</a>) tied directly to confinement; (iii) a clean link between projected fixed points and true equilibria (Proposition <a href="#prop:projfix" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:projfix">19</a>) via coherence invariance.
