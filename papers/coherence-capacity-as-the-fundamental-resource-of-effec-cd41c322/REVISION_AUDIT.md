## Revision purpose

Version 3 treated an arbitrary positive coherence-capacity scalar as the
fundamental resource of effective physics. It then inferred failure of a
right inverse, irreversible effective evolution, measurement collapse,
thermalization, black-hole information loss, entropy, an arrow of time,
and Einstein gravity from exhaustion or variation of that scalar.

Those conclusions do not follow from a zero set or robustness margin.
Version 4 replaces the central claim with a narrower and operationally
meaningful one: after an allowed perturbation family, action, and cost are
specified, coherence capacity can define a model-specific control budget.
A genuine resource theory additionally requires free operations,
composition, and conversion rules. A physical resource additionally
requires dynamics, units, sources, and observables.

The revised paper is a standalone explanatory development, not merely a
list of corrections.

## Current version delta

Version 4 introduces four explicitly separated levels:

1. a diagnostic normalized capacity record;
2. an operational robustness budget;
3. a resource theory with free operations and conversion order; and
4. a dynamical or physical resource with its own law and observables.

The operational exit budget is the infimum cost of an allowed perturbation
that leaves the admissible set. In a metric model it is the distance to the
complement and therefore the maximal guaranteed open perturbation-ball
radius. The paper proves controlled transport under a bi-Lipschitz
representation change, a cumulative rowwise consumption theorem, and a
minimum law for independent parallel systems in the max-product metric.

The paper also gives an explicit counterexample showing that a capacity
budget is not automatically monotone under all admissibility-preserving
operations: a repair map can increase it and a degrading map can decrease
it. Resource language becomes valid only after the free operations and
allowed monotonicity direction are declared.

## Claim-by-claim audit

### Capacity is the fundamental resource of all effective physics

Version 3 claim:
One positive scalar with the correct failure boundary is the universal
resource underlying effective descriptions.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
The scalar depends on normalization, perturbation structure, and cost.
Different effective tasks can have different distances to failure.

Version 4 resolution:
Use a model-specific control budget and publish the complete perturbation
record. Promotion to a resource theory or physical resource is an
additional construction.

### Exhaustion forbids a right inverse

Version 3 claim:
Noninjective projection and capacity exhaustion imply that no global
right inverse exists.

Decision:
Withdraw as false.

Reason:
For an orthogonal projection `P:H -> P H`, inclusion
`P H -> H` is a bounded measurable right inverse. Noninjectivity prevents
a left inverse that recovers every original upper state; it does not
prevent a section that chooses one representative.

Version 4 resolution:
Decoder existence, dynamic commutation, and information recovery are
separate theorem obligations.

### Exhaustion forces irreversible effective evolution

Version 3 claim:
A first capacity exit necessarily makes the effective dynamics
irreversible.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
A first exit ends one certificate. The upper evolution can remain
invertible, another lower chart may exist, or the lower description may
simply stop. Irreversibility requires a semigroup, coarse-graining,
record-stability, reset, or thermodynamic theorem.

### Capacity supplies measurement probabilities or collapse

Version 3 claim:
Capacity exhaustion supplies an outcome-selection event shared by quantum
measurement.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
A probability law requires a state, observable algebra, and instrument or
a same-source probability theorem. A reset requires explicit hybrid
dynamics. Capacity can delimit their controlled domain but cannot replace
them.

### Capacity is entropy or a thermodynamic resource

Version 3 claim:
Capacity exhaustion unifies thermalization and entropy growth.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
Entropy requires state counting, a measure, a density operator,
coarse-graining, or a thermodynamic potential. Capacity is not generally
conserved and repair can increase it.

### Capacity gradients are forces

Version 3 claim:
Spatial variation of the capacity scalar directly generates force.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
A gradient is a force only when a supplied action, Hamiltonian, or
constitutive law gives it that role and fixes units and sign.

### Capacity variation derives Einstein gravity and Newton's constant

Version 3 claim:
The scalar selects a curvature action and gives an effective Newton
constant.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
A term such as `f(C) R` is an added scalar-tensor coupling. The capacity
record does not select `f`, the Einstein-Hilbert normalization, or the
measured value of `G`.

Version 4 resolution:
Gravity remains a downstream geometry/action theorem. No gravitational
result is promoted here.

### Capacity bottlenecks are horizons or black-hole information loss

Version 3 claim:
A capacity boundary is a horizon and its exhaustion explains black-hole
information loss.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
Horizons require Lorentzian causal geometry and field equations.
Information-recovery claims require a specified channel, algebra, and
decoder.

### Exhaustion events define the arrow of time

Version 3 claim:
Ordering capacity-exhaustion events is sufficient to derive time's arrow.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
An arrow of time requires asymmetric dynamics, boundary conditions,
stable records, or thermodynamic structure.
