\begin{abstract}
Modal Triplet Theory repeatedly uses fixed points, positive Hessians, heat
kernels, coherent projectors, and perturbative graphs.  These objects have
often appeared as neighboring constructions without one theorem stating
their exact common source.  This paper supplies that theorem at the
structural tier.  Begin with a nonlinear defect section \(\Phi\) on a metric
configuration space and the positive repair cost
\(\calC=\frac12\norm{\Phi}^{2}\).  Its negative gradient is closure repair.
At a zero-defect fixed point the Hessian is exactly the normal operator
\(D\Phi^{\dagger}D\Phi\); its heat semigroup, Green operator, spectral
projectors, local kernel, and complete finite repair-vertex jet are then
different mathematical shadows of the same linearized source.  At a
nonzero-defect critical point an additional covariant residual term is
present, so the normal-square formula cannot be used without qualification.

The construction is extended to local Cech descent and to an
\(\alpha\)-twisted cohesive module.  The projective twist cancels in the
endomorphism algebra, the Maurer--Cartan curvature
\(F(a)=da+a^{2}\) gives a canonical nonlinear repair residual, and a chosen
Hermitian structure yields the Hodge operator and tangent heat flow.  An
exact stratified example shows simultaneously that the full cohesive
operator may remain regular while every uniform fiberwise Green operator
diverges across a cohomology-rank jump.  Compatible isometric intertwiners
transport the repair flow, functional calculus, projectors, kernels, and
formal graph coefficients.

The paper also proves the necessary boundary.  A positive repair square is
not a signed physical action.  It loses first-order sign, phase, Morse index,
causal support, and generally the physical interaction vertices.  A direct
action requires a variational anchor satisfying Helmholtz conditions; a
universal multiplier lift always exists; and a cyclic Maurer--Cartan source
admits a Chern--Simons-type signed action.  For the q79 program, the cohesive
Hartshorne--Serre benchmark closes the structural chain exactly after a
Hermitian choice.  Selection of the physical \(V_3/W_9\) endpoint, its HYM
metric, full Hull--Strominger action, Lorentzian domains, and the
continuum-to-finite intertwiner remain open.  Thus closure repair explains
why the operator and kernel language should recur, without promoting that
recurrence into a completed theory of dynamics or quantization.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question before the operator}

Many physical formalisms begin with an operator.  One specifies a
Hamiltonian, a Laplacian, a Dirac operator, a Hessian, or a propagator and
then asks what it does.  MTT begins one question earlier:
\begin{quote}
What is being repaired, and why should its first observable shadow be an
operator at all?
\end{quote}

The answer developed here is deliberately modest.  Suppose a state is
admissible when a nonlinear defect vanishes.  The squared size of the defect
is then a natural nonnegative cost.  Its gradient points in the direction in
which inadmissibility grows most rapidly; its negative gradient therefore
defines a repair flow.  Linearizing that flow at an exact admissible state
produces a positive normal operator.  Once that operator exists, the heat
semigroup, Green operator, spectral projectors, and local kernels belong to
ordinary functional analysis.

This does not make every physical operator a theorem of MTT.  It identifies
one precise mechanism by which a large and important class of operators can
arise.  The mechanism has hypotheses, and several of them carry physical
content: the configuration space, defect bundle, metric, connection, domain,
background, and interpretation must all be selected.  Hiding any one of
these choices inside the word ``closure'' would merely relocate the input.

The central distinction of this paper is therefore:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{The repair operator is derived from a selected defect and metric.
The selected defect and metric are not derived by notation.}
\end{quote}

\section{Residual geometry and the repair Hessian}
\label{sec:residual-hessian}

\subsection{The nonlinear source}

Let \((\calM,g)\) be a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold.  The same
formulas extend to a Hilbert manifold when the derivatives, adjoints, and
domains used below exist.  Let
\[
(\calE,h,\nabla^{\calE})\longrightarrow \calM
\]
be a real metric or complex Hermitian vector bundle with metric connection,
and let
\[
\Phi\in\Gamma(\calE)
\]
be a twice differentiable defect section.  In the complex case every scalar
pairing below means its real part.  Define
\begin{equation}
\calC(p)=\frac12 h\bigl(\Phi(p),\Phi(p)\bigr).
\label{eq:repair-cost}
\end{equation}

At \(p\in\calM\), write
\begin{align}
r_p&=\Phi(p),\\
J_pX&=\nabla_X^{\calE}\Phi,\\
B_p(X,Y)&=
\nabla_X^{\calE}\nabla_Y^{\calE}\Phi
-\nabla_{\nabla_X^{\calM}Y}^{\calE}\Phi.
\end{align}
The tensor \(B_p\) is the covariant second derivative of the defect.

\begin{theorem}[Covariant residual-Hessian identity]
\label{thm:residual-hessian}
For every \(X,Y\in T_p\calM\),
\begin{align}
\dd\calC_p(X)&=h(r_p,J_pX),
\label{eq:first-variation}\\
\Hess_p\calC(X,Y)&=
h(J_pX,J_pY)+h(r_p,B_p(X,Y)).
\label{eq:full-hessian}
\end{align}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\grad\calC(p)=J_p^{\dagger}r_p,
\qquad
H_p=J_p^{\dagger}J_p+S_{r_p},
\label{eq:operator-hessian}
\end{equation}
where
\[
g(S_{r_p}X,Y)=h(r_p,B_p(X,Y)).
\]
The residual form is symmetric, including when \(\nabla^{\calE}\) has
curvature.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Metric compatibility gives
\[
X[\calC]=h(\nabla_X^{\calE}\Phi,\Phi),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:first-variation}.  Differentiate once more and
subtract the Levi--Civita correction:
\begin{align*}
\Hess\calC(X,Y)
&=X[\dd\calC(Y)]-\dd\calC(\nabla_X^{\calM}Y)\\
&=h(\nabla_X^{\calE}\Phi,\nabla_Y^{\calE}\Phi)
+h\!\left(\Phi,
\nabla_X^{\calE}\nabla_Y^{\calE}\Phi
-\nabla_{\nabla_X^{\calM}Y}^{\calE}\Phi\right).
\end{align*}
This is \eqref{eq:full-hessian}.  The antisymmetric part of \(B\) is
\(R^{\calE}(X,Y)\Phi\).  Curvature of a metric or Hermitian connection is
skew-adjoint, so
\(\operatorname{Re}h(\Phi,R^{\calE}(X,Y)\Phi)=0\).  The residual term is
therefore symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact-zero normal square]
\label{cor:zero-normal-square}
If \(p_*\) is an exact closure state, \(\Phi(p_*)=0\), then
\begin{equation}
\grad\calC(p_*)=0,
\qquad
\Hess_{p_*}\calC=J_*^{\dagger}J_*\geq0.
\label{eq:normal-square}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\ker\Hess_{p_*}\calC=\ker J_*.
\]
\end{corollary}

This is the exact source of the positive repair Hessian used later.  It is
also the point at which a common shortcut must stop.  Equation
\eqref{eq:normal-square} is not the general Hessian formula.

\begin{corollary}[Nonzero-defect correction]
\label{cor:nonzero-correction}
A state \(p\) is critical precisely when
\[
J_p^{\dagger}r_p=0.
\]
If \(r_p\neq0\), the Hessian is
\[
J_p^{\dagger}J_p+S_{r_p},
\]
and the second term generally cannot be reconstructed from \(r_p\) and
\(J_p\) alone.  On \(\ker J_p\), local stability is controlled entirely by
\(S_{r_p}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{example}[Why the correction cannot be dropped]
\label{ex:nonzero-witness}
Let \(\Phi_a:\bbR\to\bbR^2\) be
\[
\Phi_a(x)=(x,x^2+a),
\qquad a=1.
\]
At \(x=0\), the residual is \((0,1)\), the derivative is \((1,0)\), and
\(J^{\dagger}r=0\).  Thus \(x=0\) is critical although the defect is
nonzero.  The Gram term is \(J^{\dagger}J=1\), the residual correction is
\(2\), and
\[
\Hess_0\calC=3.
\]
Using only the normal square would give the wrong local stiffness by a
factor of three.
\end{example}

\subsection{Symmetry and physical zero modes}

Let a Lie group \(G\) act isometrically on \(\calM\) and fiberwise
isometrically on \(\calE\), and suppose \(\Phi\) is equivariant.

\begin{proposition}[Orbit zero modes]
\label{prop:orbit-zero}
If \(p\) is critical and \(\xi_{\calM}(p)\) is tangent to its \(G\)-orbit,
then
\[
\Hess_p\calC\bigl(\xi_{\calM}(p),Y\bigr)=0
\]
for every \(Y\in T_p\calM\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Invariance gives \(\dd\calC(\xi_{\calM})=0\) identically.  Differentiate
in the direction \(Y\).  The term containing
\(\dd\calC(\nabla_Y\xi_{\calM})\) vanishes at a critical point, leaving the
claimed Hessian identity.
\end{proof}

Consequently, positivity in a gauge or phase-symmetric system means
positivity on a slice normal to the symmetry orbit.  Requiring strict
positivity on the unreduced space would incorrectly reject the zero modes
that encode the symmetry.

\section{Repair as a flow}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{Dissipative and reversible parts}

Pure repair is the negative-gradient equation
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd u}{\dd s}=-\grad\calC(u).
\label{eq:gradient-repair}
\end{equation}
The parameter \(s\) orders stabilization.  It is not automatically
Lorentzian time.  A more general reversible--dissipative split is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd u}{\dd s}=(K-\Gamma)\grad\calC(u),
\label{eq:reversible-dissipative}
\end{equation}
where \(K\) is skew-adjoint and \(\Gamma\) is self-adjoint and positive
semidefinite.

\begin{theorem}[Repair monotonicity and tangent generator]
\label{thm:repair-flow}
Along every solution of \eqref{eq:reversible-dissipative},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd}{\dd s}\calC(u(s))
=-g\bigl(\grad\calC,\Gamma\grad\calC\bigr)\leq0.
\label{eq:energy-monotone}
\end{equation}
At a critical point \(p\), the linearized generator is
\begin{equation}
D\bigl[(K-\Gamma)\grad\calC\bigr]_p
=(K_p-\Gamma_p)H_p^{\sharp}.
\label{eq:linearized-general}
\end{equation}
At an exact-zero state and for pure repair, the tangent equation is
\begin{equation}
\partial_s v=-J_*^{\dagger}J_*v,
\qquad
v(s)=e^{-sJ_*^{\dagger}J_*}v(0).
\label{eq:tangent-heat}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pair \eqref{eq:reversible-dissipative} with \(\grad\calC\).  The
\(K\)-term vanishes by skew-adjointness, and positivity of \(\Gamma\)
gives \eqref{eq:energy-monotone}.  At a critical point the derivatives of
\(K\) and \(\Gamma\) multiply \(\grad\calC(p)=0\), so only the Hessian
term remains.  The exact-zero specialization follows from
\cref{cor:zero-normal-square}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict-dissipation circulation no-go]
\label{cor:circulation-no-go}
If \(\Gamma\) is positive definite along a periodic orbit of
\eqref{eq:reversible-dissipative}, that orbit is constant.  A persistent
circulation therefore requires a null-dissipation sector, a conservative
sector, a relative equilibrium generated by symmetry, or external driving.
\end{corollary}

This small result matters conceptually.  A particle-like phase circulation
cannot be obtained merely by drawing a loop around a strictly dissipative
minimum.  Repair explains stabilization.  Reversible phase transport
requires additional selected structure.

This also separates repair from stationary noise. Fixed Points III owns
the stationary-ensemble obstruction for strict Lyapunov repair: under its
integrability and invariance hypotheses, a stationary ensemble has zero
dissipation almost everywhere. A closed finite-spatial-mode unitary model
with compact resolvent supplies a different obstruction, namely recurrence
rather than irreversible mixing. Removing only an occupation-number
cutoff does not remove that finite-spatial-mode recurrence.

Thus a stationary diffusion limit needs an independently justified
recurrent fast driver and a mixing/scaling argument, a continuum or
thermodynamic limit, or a declared open system. Toral-chaos examples remain
valid examples of separately supplied drivers; they are not flows of a
strictly decreasing repair cost. These distinctions preserve the repair
Hessian theorem and locate the extra dynamical input precisely. The
owning discussion and certificates are in Fixed Points III and the
\href{https://github.com/PeterNero/mtt-results-repro}{curated reproduction
repository}, under the finite-mode recurrence and fixed-point noise
results; their proofs are not duplicated here.

\subsection{Fixed points and basins}

The flow may possess many fixed points or many attraction basins.  Their
existence, uniqueness, regularity, and convergence require the familiar
compactness, coercivity, analytic-semigroup, or
\L{}ojasiewicz--Simon hypotheses developed in Fixed Points I
\cite{MTTFixedPointsI2026}.  The present paper does not duplicate those

If a compact connected sector contains no zero of \(\Phi\), the continuous

modulo symmetry, reversible transport, and the map to observables.

\section{The cohesive source}
\label{sec:cohesive}

\subsection{Why a complex rather than only a bundle}

The q79 source program contains an \(\alpha\)-twisted Hartshorne--Serre
object
\[
\calS_{\HS}\in D^b(J,\alpha)
\]
with a bounded local perfect atlas.  Its open-stratum contraction uses an
inverse transverse coordinate and cannot pass through the exceptional
stratum.  Discarding the full complex would therefore erase precisely the
place at which its cohomology rank changes.

Cohesive modules provide a lawful alternative.  A bounded
\(\alpha\)-twisted perfect atlas is represented by an
\(\alpha\)-twisted graded smooth module \(E^\bullet\) with an integrable
antiholomorphic superconnection
\begin{equation}
\Ebar=\Ebar_0+\Ebar_1+\Ebar_2+\cdots,
\qquad
\Ebar^2=0.
\label{eq:cohesive-superconnection}
\end{equation}
This is standard cohesive and twisted-complex mathematics
\cite{Quillen1985,Block2005,Wei2015,Wei2018,BismutShenWei2021,
BresslerEtAl2007}.  The q79-specific statement is that the certified local
atlas satisfies its hypotheses.

\begin{theorem}[Endomorphism untwisting]
\label{thm:end-untwist}
Let the local projective transitions of \(E^\bullet\) satisfy
\[
G_{ij}G_{jk}G_{ki}=\alpha_{ijk}\Id.
\]
Then \(\End(E^\bullet)\) glues as an ordinary global graded algebra by
conjugation.  The commutator differential
\begin{equation}
d_{\End}(T)=[\Ebar,T]
\label{eq:end-differential}
\end{equation}
satisfies \(d_{\End}^{2}=0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On a triple overlap,
\[
\ad(G_{ij})\ad(G_{jk})\ad(G_{ki})
=\ad(\alpha_{ijk}\Id)=\Id.
\]
Thus the scalar gerbe cocycle disappears from the endomorphism transition.
The graded Jacobi identity and \(\Ebar^2=0\) give
\[
d_{\End}^{2}(T)=[\Ebar^2,T]=0.
\]
\end{proof}

The twist has not vanished from the object.  It has become invisible to the
adjoint algebra.  This is exactly what is needed for a global deformation
complex without falsely trivializing the original gerbe.

\subsection{The canonical nonlinear residual}

Set
\[
\calA=\Omega^{0,*}(\End E),
\qquad d=[\Ebar,-].
\]
For a degree-one perturbation \(a\in\calA^1\), define
\begin{equation}
F(a)=da+a^2=(\Ebar+a)^2.
\label{eq:mc-residual}
\end{equation}
Then \(F(a)=0\) precisely when the perturbed superconnection remains
integrable.  This is not an arbitrary polynomial chosen to resemble an
interaction.  It is the curvature of the perturbed cohesive source.

Choose a Hermitian structure and a gauge row.  On degree one, let
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\MC}(a)=\bigl(F(a),d_0^{\dagger}a\bigr),
\qquad
\calC_{\MC}(a)=\frac12\norm{F(a)}^2
+\frac12\norm{d_0^{\dagger}a}^2.
\label{eq:mc-repair}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cohesive Maurer--Cartan repair]
\label{thm:mc-repair}
At the integrable background \(a=0\),
\begin{equation}
D\Phi_{\MC}(0)=(d_1,d_0^{\dagger}),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\Hess_0\calC_{\MC}
=d_1^{\dagger}d_1+d_0d_0^{\dagger}
=\Delta_1.
\label{eq:mc-hodge}
\end{equation}
The tangent negative-gradient flow is
\[
\partial_s a=-\Delta_1a,
\qquad
a(s)=e^{-s\Delta_1}a(0).
\]
The nonlinear curvature also satisfies the Bianchi identity
\[
d_aF(a)=0,
\qquad
d_a=d+[a,-].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate \eqref{eq:mc-residual} at zero.  The quadratic term has zero
first derivative, so \(DF(0)=d_1\).  Apply
\cref{cor:zero-normal-square} to the two residual rows in
\eqref{eq:mc-repair}.  The Bianchi identity follows by expanding
\(dF+[a,F]\), using \(d^2=0\) and the graded Leibniz rule.
\end{proof}

The algebraic residual is canonical once the cohesive object is fixed.  The
Hermitian metric, adjoint, normalization, and physical moment-map rows are
not.  In particular, \(d_0^{\dagger}a=0\) is a gauge slice and must not be
renamed the physical HYM equation.

\section{One charge, complementary curvature readouts}
\label{sec:charge-curvature}

The preceding construction starts from a defect and a metric. The next
question is whether its reversible, dissipative and curvature operators
can come from fewer independent choices. Six finite/local source records
give a precise partial answer. Here \emph{charge} means an algebraic
operator, as in a differential or supersymmetry charge, not an electric
charge or a selected particle quantum number. The Hodge-square mechanism
has established precedents in supersymmetric quantum mechanics
\cite{WittenMorse1982}. Its use here supplies a constrained source
architecture, not a claim that the mechanism itself is new.

\subsection{The shared center must be specified as a representation}
\label{sec:central-qutrit-character}
On a qutrit basis \(e_j\), \(j\in\mathbb Z_3\), put
\[
 Xe_j=e_{j+1},\qquad Ze_j=\omega^j e_j,\qquad \omega^3=1,
 \quad \omega\ne1.
\]
Then \(XZ=\omega^{-1}ZX\) and
\(XZX^{-1}Z^{-1}=\omega^{-1}I\). The Fourier matrix with entries
\(\omega^{jk}\) conjugates \(X\) to \(Z\) and \(Z\) to \(X^{-1}\).
On \((\mathbb C^3)^{\otimes3}\), placing \(X,Z\) separately in each
factor gives three commuting lanes with the same scalar commutator
\(\omega^{-1}I_{27}\) \cite{PreprojectionCentralQutrit2026}.

The representation qualification matters. These are independent lane
actions with their scalar centers identified, a central-product
construction. They are not the diagonal tensor action of one qutrit group.
For that diagonal action the two operators \(X^{\otimes3},Z^{\otimes3}\)
commute, since their commutator is \(\omega^{-3}I_{27}=I_{27}\).
Thus even the same tensor dimension does not specify the central character.
The witness does not identify this carrier with CLN, the physical finite
27-dimensional carrier, or an exceptional Jordan algebra. Nor does an
order-three scalar action alone construct the shared differential line
with its continuum connection and holonomy.

\subsection{Nilpotence links reversible transport and damping}
\label{sec:single-charge-factorization}
Let \(d\) act on a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, with
\(d^2=0\). For a unit phase \(\zeta\), define
\[
 B_\zeta=\zeta d+\bar\zeta d^\dagger,\qquad
 \Delta=dd^\dagger+d^\dagger d.
\]
The square expansion gives \(B_\zeta^\dagger=B_\zeta\),
\(B_\zeta^2=\Delta\geq0\), and
\(\ker\Delta=\ker d\cap\ker d^\dagger\). Let \(P_h\) project onto
this harmonic space. If \(Z_h\) is skew-adjoint and commutes with \(d\)
and \(d^\dagger\), then for \(\gamma\geq0\) the generator
\[
 \mathcal L=-iB_\zeta-\gamma\Delta+Z_hP_h
\]
has mutually commuting summands. Consequently
\[
 e^{s\mathcal L}=e^{sZ_hP_h}e^{-isB_\zeta}e^{-\gamma s\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac{d}{ds}\norm{\psi}^2=-2\gamma\langle\psi,\Delta\psi\rangle.
\]
This is the single-charge factorization result
\cite{PreprojectionSingleCharge2026}. It derives compatibility between
the reversible operator and damping from \(d\), while leaving \(d\), its
metric, \(\zeta\), \(\gamma\), and \(Z_h\) as supplied data. The
parameter \(s\) is not automatically physical time. Continuum extensions
need domains and strong commutation, not just formal commutator identities.

For a small example take \(d=E_{12}\) on \(\mathbb C^3\). Then
\(\Delta=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)\). The first two modes decay for
\(\gamma>0\); on the third mode both \(B_\zeta\) and \(\Delta\)
vanish. Persistent harmonic phase motion must therefore be supplied by
\(Z_hP_h\), not by the Hodge operator. This is compatible with the
earlier strict-gradient circulation obstruction: the undamped motion
belongs to the extra skew generator, not to gradient repair.

Changing \(\zeta\) changes the unsquared operator in a fixed frame, but
that is not by itself an observable distinction. When \(d\) raises an
integer grading \(N\) by one,
\[
 U_\theta=e^{i\theta N},\qquad
 B_{e^{i\theta}}=U_\theta B_1U_\theta^\dagger.
\]
If states and observables are transported as well, these are unitarily
equivalent descriptions. A physical relative phase requires fixed
comparison data. Squaring alone selects neither that data nor an arrow
of time.

\subsection{Curved squares can see phase without identifying it}
\label{sec:curved-phase-boundary}
For an arbitrary finite operator \(d\), put
\(\Delta_0=dd^\dagger+d^\dagger d\). The same expansion gives
\[
 B_\zeta^2=\Delta_0+\zeta^2d^2+
                  \bar\zeta^2(d^\dagger)^2.
\]
The family is independent of \emph{every} unit phase exactly when
\(d^2=0\). Its two nonconstant Fourier coefficients are \(d^2\) and
\((d^\dagger)^2\); both must vanish for constancy. But the sign pair
\(\zeta,-\zeta\) always gives the same square
\cite{PreprojectionCurvedCharge2026}.

There can be further ambiguities. For
\[
 d=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 d^2=2I,\qquad \Delta_0=5I,
\]
one obtains \(B_{e^{i\theta}}^2=(5+4\cos2\theta)I\). The squares
at \(\theta=0\) and \(\pi/2\) are \(9I\) and \(I\), but
\(\theta\) and \(-\theta\) give the same square and need not differ
by a sign phase. Phase dependence is therefore not unique recovery even
modulo sign.

For the graded Maurer--Cartan source the related identity is
\[
 d_a=d+[a,-],\qquad d_a^2=[F(a),-],\qquad F(a)=da+a^2,
\]
with graded brackets \cite{DotsenkoShadrinVallette2022}. Here the operator
curvature is the \emph{action} of \(F\), not necessarily the element
\(F\) itself. Central curvature can be nonzero while its adjoint action
vanishes. Ordinary cohomology is unavailable when \(d_a^2\ne0\), but
nonzero curvature does not forbid it in a representation where that
curvature acts trivially. The curved-square correction is also not
automatically the residual-Hessian correction of
\cref{thm:residual-hessian}; relating them requires their common source,
pairing and admissible variations.

\subsection{Jordan and Lie readouts lose different information}
\label{sec:jordan-lie-center}
In a finite unital matrix star algebra with positive cyclic trace
\(\tau\), take \(F=F^\dagger\) and define
\[
 C_F(X)=FX+XF,\qquad A_F(X)=FX-XF.
\]
Together they reconstruct \(FX=(C_FX+A_FX)/2\) and
\(XF=(C_FX-A_FX)/2\). The Jordan map \(C_F\) is self-adjoint on
the real Hermitian slice. The commutator is a derivation; \(-iA_F\),
not \(A_F\) itself, maps Hermitian observables to Hermitian observables
and generates their unitary conjugation \cite{PreprojectionJordanLie2026}.

For a residual whose second derivative is \(XY+YX\), the trace identity
\[
 \tau\bigl(F(XY+YX)\bigr)=\tau\bigl((FX+XF)Y\bigr)
\]
identifies the ambient residual-Hessian correction with \(C_F\) on
this real slice. For instance \(F=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\) and
\(X=E_{12}+E_{21}\) give \(C_FX=3X\),
\(A_FX=E_{12}-E_{21}\) and a Jordan quadratic pairing of six.
This finite trace model does not automatically supply the graded signs,
positive real slice or cyclic pairing of a physical q79 theory. A gauge
row, a curved admissible stratum or additional physical residuals can
also change its Hessian.

The information loss is exact in
\(\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^r M_{n_\alpha}(\mathbb C)\).
Write
\[
 F=F_0+z,\qquad z=\bigoplus_\alpha\lambda_\alpha I_{n_\alpha},
 \qquad\lambda_\alpha=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}F_\alpha}{n_\alpha},
\]
where each block of \(F_0\) is traceless. The split is orthogonal for
a positive weighted block trace. Knowing \([F,X]\) for \emph{all}
\(X\in\mathcal A\) determines \(F\) only modulo this center, whereas
\(F=\frac12 C_F(1)\) recovers it from the complete Jordan map
\cite{PreprojectionCentralCurvature2026}.

This last qualification is essential. A Hessian known only on an
admissible tangent space may not include the unit or determine \(C_F(1)\);
restricted commutator observations can likewise leave more ambiguity
than the center. Complete ambient readouts cannot be assumed from a
few projected rows.

An exact group action permuting isomorphic blocks leaves one invariant
real central coordinate per block orbit. For \(M_2\oplus M_2\), the
two center coordinates reduce to one under an imposed block exchange.
That counts the remaining \emph{central ambiguity}, not all source
parameters. The algebra, symmetry, trace, admissible variations and
traceless data remain inputs until selected. A one-dimensional center of
\(M_{27}\) is not a theorem that the physical 27-carrier model has one
free constant, or that the unresolved scalar rows are central curvature.

\subsection{Admissible geometry comes before physical stability}
\label{sec:admissible-radial-circle}
For an odd self-adjoint finite charge \(Q\), consider the specific
homogeneous cost
\[
 F=Q^2,\qquad E(Q)=\tfrac12\norm{Q^2}^2,
 \qquad L_Q(a)=Qa+aQ.
\]
With a fixed positive trace pairing, \(dE_Q(a)=\langle F,L_Q(a)\rangle\).
If the admissible tangent space \(V\) contains the radial direction
\(Q\), stationarity would require
\[
 0=dE_Q(Q)=2\norm{F}^2,
\]
so nonzero curvature cannot be stationary in that radial direction
\cite{PreprojectionAdmissibleTangent2026}. This is a no-go for this
homogeneous cost and variation space, not for every nonlinear defect.
Fixing a differential's principal symbol, a normalization stratum or
boundary data may remove the radial variation, but that restriction is
source data and must be justified.

On a smooth admissible submanifold \(S\), tangent restriction of the
ambient Hessian alone is insufficient. With \(\mathrm{II}\) its normal
second fundamental form,
\[
 \Hess_S(E|_S)(a,b)=\Hess_{\rm amb}E(a,b)
            +\langle\grad_{\rm amb}E,\mathrm{II}(a,b)\rangle.
\]
This is the same covariant chain rule as
\cref{thm:residual-hessian}, now applied to the constrained source geometry.
The extra term may remain nonzero at a critical point of \(E|_S\),
because that point need not be ambient-critical.

The finite circle witness takes \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and
\[
 Q_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\I_2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&J\\-J&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both are self-adjoint and odd for grading \(\operatorname{diag}(I_2,-I_2)\);
\(Q_0^2=Q_1^2=I_4\) and \(Q_0Q_1+Q_1Q_0=0\). Thus
\(Q_\theta=\cos\theta Q_0+\sin\theta Q_1\) has constant square
\(I_4\) and cost two under the ordinary trace. At zero, the ambient
Hessian along \(Q_1\) is eight, while the normal-acceleration term is
minus eight. The intrinsic circle Hessian is zero.

The circle therefore carries a protected first-order coordinate, not a
damped mode proved by its positive ambient entry. This also does not
select its traversal speed, identify it with the physical shared line,
or turn its parameter into Lorentzian time. The useful synthesis is a
source with an operator, metric, admissible variation geometry and shared
representation, from which compatible readouts can follow. None of those
source data can be reconstructed merely by renaming their projections.

\section{Hodge, Green, and kernel shadows}
\label{sec:hodge}

\subsection{The total-space Hodge package}

Choose a smooth Hermitian metric on \(E^\bullet\), a Hermitian metric on the
compact boundaryless base \(J\), and the associated density.  Define
\begin{equation}
B_E=\Ebar+\Ebar^{\dagger},
\qquad
\Delta_E=B_E^2.
\label{eq:super-hodge}
\end{equation}
Its principal symbol is the Dolbeault symbol.  Standard elliptic theory then
gives a self-adjoint closure with compact resolvent
\cite{BerlineGetzlerVergne2004}.

\begin{theorem}[Hodge and kernel package]
\label{thm:hodge-kernel}
Under the preceding compactness, ellipticity, metric, and domain hypotheses,
there exist:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a finite-dimensional harmonic space \(\ker\Delta_E\) and orthogonal
projector \(P_0\);
\item a smoothing heat semigroup \(e^{-s\Delta_E}\) for \(s>0\);
\item a reduced Green operator
\begin{equation}
G_E=(\Delta_E|_{\ker\Delta_E^{\perp}})^{-1}(I-P_0)
=\int_0^\infty\bigl(e^{-s\Delta_E}-P_0\bigr)\,\dd s;
\label{eq:green-heat}
\end{equation}
\item finite-rank spectral projectors \(\chi_I(\Delta_E)\) for bounded
spectral intervals \(I\); and
\item local Schwartz kernels for the smoothing functional calculus.
\end{enumerate}
Every item is determined by the same selected \(\Delta_E\).
\end{theorem}

The word ``shadow'' is literal but not mystical.  The spectral resolution
is the wave-like normal-mode description.  The local kernel
\[
K_s(x,y)=\langle x|e^{-s\Delta_E}|y\rangle
\]
is the localized propagation-of-repair description.  They are two
representations of one operator.  Neither representation, by itself, makes
the semigroup a physical quantum evolution.

\subsection{The wave--particle operator obtains a source candidate}

The conditional wave--particle paper begins with a supplied positive
operator \(A\) and forms
\[
B_{\mathrm{adm}}
=P\chi(A)e^{-\tau_{\mathrm{adm}}A}\chi(A)P
\cite{MTTWaveParticle2026}.
\]
At the present structural tier, \cref{thm:mc-repair,thm:hodge-kernel}
provide the exact candidate
\begin{equation}
A=\Delta_1=D\Phi_{\MC}(0)^{\dagger}D\Phi_{\MC}(0)
\label{eq:wave-source-candidate}
\end{equation}
on the cohesive benchmark.  This closes the formerly missing mathematical
arrow from nonlinear repair to the positive filtered operator on that
benchmark.

It does not close the physical common-source theorem of the wave--particle
paper.  That stronger theorem must show that the state, Hamiltonian or
generator, effects, instrument, damping channel, and selected MTT source all
belong to one physical construction.  Equation
\eqref{eq:wave-source-candidate} supplies the positive repair operator, not
the rest of quantum mechanics.

\subsection{A stratified warning}

The existence of a regular total-space operator does not imply a uniform
family of fiberwise inverses.  The following exact example is the useful
model.

\begin{proposition}[Regular complex, divergent fiberwise Green operator]
\label{prop:stratified-green}
Let
\[
d_r=\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)&rI_3
\end{bmatrix}:\bbC^6\longrightarrow\bbC^3
\]
and let
\[
B_r=\begin{bmatrix}0&d_r^{\dagger}\\d_r&0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\Delta_r=B_r^2.
\]
The entries of \(B_r\) and \(\Delta_r\) are polynomial in \(r\) and remain
finite at \(r=0\).  For \(r\neq0\),
\[
\spec(\Delta_r)=
\{0^{(3)},(1+r^2)^{(2)},(r^2)^{(4)}\}.
\]
At \(r=0\),
\[
\spec(\Delta_0)=\{0^{(7)},1^{(2)}\}.
\]
Hence the smallest positive fiberwise eigenvalue is \(r^2\) and the reduced
fiberwise Green norm grows as \(r^{-2}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The three rows of \(d_r\) are orthogonal with squared norms
\(1+r^2,r^2,r^2\).  These are the nonzero eigenvalues of both
\(d_rd_r^{\dagger}\) and \(d_r^{\dagger}d_r\), with each nonzero singular
value appearing in both blocks of \(\Delta_r\).  The stated multiplicities
and inverse norm follow directly.
\end{proof}

At \(r=2\), the spectrum is
\(\{0^{(3)},4^{(4)},5^{(2)}\}\); at \(r=0\), the kernel jumps from dimension
three to seven.  The full complex is regular while the open-stratum
contraction using \(1/r\) is not.  This is why the cohesive object cannot be
replaced globally by its rank-three open-stratum cohomology.

\section{Descent, holonomy, and compatible projection}
\label{sec:descent-projection}

\subsection{Nonlinear Cech descent}

Let \(\{\calM_i\}\) be local Riemannian configuration charts with local
defect spaces \(\calE_i\).  Suppose the overlap maps
\[
U_{ij}:\calM_i\to\calM_j,
\qquad
V_{ij}:\calE_i\to\calE_j
\]
are respectively Riemannian isometries and unitary maps, and
\begin{equation}
\Phi_j\circ U_{ij}=V_{ij}\circ\Phi_i.
\label{eq:defect-descent}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Nonlinear repair descent]
\label{thm:repair-descent}
The local costs \(\calC_i=\frac12\norm{\Phi_i}^2\), negative-gradient
fields \(X_i=-\grad\calC_i\), and their local flows \(R_i(s)\) obey
\begin{align}
\calC_j\circ U_{ij}&=\calC_i,\\
TU_{ij}X_i&=X_j\circ U_{ij},\\
U_{ij}\circ R_i(s)&=R_j(s)\circ U_{ij}.
\end{align}
Therefore the nonlinear Cech equalizer
\[
\Gamma(\calM)=
\{(x_i):x_j=U_{ij}x_i\text{ on every overlap}\}
\]
is preserved.  Compatible fixed points, basins, and existing asymptotic
limits descend.  A single-valued global basin label must lie in
\[
\bigcap_{\gamma}\Fix(\Hol_{\gamma})
\]
for the induced label local system.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:defect-descent} and unitarity give equality of the local
costs.  Differentiating that equality and using that \(U_{ij}\) is an
isometry intertwines the gradient fields.  Uniqueness for the local
initial-value problem then intertwines the flows.  Passing to an existing
limit commutes with the continuous overlap maps.  Transport around a cycle
gives the holonomy-fixed condition on a global label.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not select one basin.  It says exactly when local repair is
one global process.  Unique actualization requires an additional theorem
that one allowed global basin is selected, or an explicit probability law
when several remain.  Measurement need not be a privileged primitive here;
it is one physical process capable of changing the local defect map and its
basins.

\subsection{Tangent and spectral projection}

For a finite connected cover with unitary local-system transitions, the
linear equalizer is concretely
\[
\Gamma(\mathcal H)=\ker d_0,\qquad
(d_0\psi)_{ij}=\psi_j-U_{ij}\psi_i.
\]
Transport along a spanning tree determines all components from one root
vector; the remaining edges impose the cycle-holonomy fixed equations.
Thus the global space is the intersection of those fixed spaces, not a
tensor product over overlapping charts. Projectors descend only when
\(U_{ij}P_i=P_jU_{ij}\). These are the finite linear conclusions of the
local Cech--Hilbert record cited in the evidence section.

Differentiate \eqref{eq:defect-descent} at a compatible zero-defect fixed
point.  The Jacobians and Hessians intertwine, so the tangent heat semigroup
preserves the linear Cech equalizer.  The Hilbert-space description is thus
the linearization of nonlinear descent, not its replacement.

\begin{theorem}[Compatible projection of the repair calculus]
\label{thm:projection-calculus}
Let \(H\geq0\) be a self-adjoint repair Hessian on a Hilbert space and let
\(P\) be an orthogonal projector reducing \(H\).  Put
\(H_P=H|_{\Ran P}\).  Then, on their declared domains,
\begin{equation}
Pe^{-sH}=e^{-sH_P}P,
\qquad
P\chi_I(H)=\chi_I(H_P)P,
\qquad
PG=G_PP,
\label{eq:functional-intertwining}
\end{equation}
where the Green identity is taken on the corresponding harmonic
complements. A unitary equivalence \(T\) of configuration spaces and a
unitary equivalence \(U\) of residual spaces, satisfying
\(\Phi'(Ta)=U\Phi(a)\), transport the nonlinear cost jet and the tangent
functional calculus at compatible zero-defect backgrounds.

For an isometric embedding rather than an onto equivalence, the cost jet
is transported only as a pullback. Full tangent transport additionally
requires, on the common operator domains,
\[
J'T=UJ,\qquad J'^\dagger U=TJ^\dagger.
\]
These identities make the embedded tangent range reducing for the
self-adjoint Hessian. Without the second identity only
\(T^\dagger H'T=H\) is implied, not \(H'T=TH\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first three identities are consequences of the spectral theorem for a
reducing subspace. The residual identity gives the pullback equality
\(\calC'(Ta)=\calC(a)\) and hence equality of all pulled-back cost
derivatives. At a zero-defect background the two displayed derivative
identities give
\(H'T=J'^\dagger J'T=J'^\dagger UJ=TJ^\dagger J=TH\).
For onto unitary maps the adjoint identity follows from the chain identity.
For an embedding it is an extra hypothesis. The common-domain self-adjoint
intertwining then gives the claimed functional calculus.
\end{proof}

For example, let \(\Phi(x)=x\), \(\Phi'(x,y)=x+y\),
\(Tx=(x,0)\), and \(U=1\). The residual and cost pull back exactly, but
\[
H=1,\qquad H'=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H'Tx=(x,x)\ne(x,0)=THx .
\]
The transverse response prevents reduction. This explains why a
dimension match, an isometric compression or a compatible cost restricted
to a subspace cannot replace a full operator intertwiner.

\paragraph{Retained projectors are not the whole ambient projector.}
There is a second, independent completeness requirement in the older
continuum-to-finite cutset record \cite{Q79IntertwinerCutset2026}.
Even exact reducing intertwining gives
\(\Pi'T=T\Pi\), not necessarily \(\Pi'=T\Pi T^\dagger\).
For a concrete counterexample, take
\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),\qquad
H'=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,0),\qquad T(x,y)=(x,y,0).
\]
The intertwining residual is zero, both positive spectral gaps are one,
and the range of \(T\) is reducing. Nevertheless,
\[
\Pi'-T\Pi T^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1),\qquad
\norm{\Pi'-T\Pi T^\dagger}=1.
\]
Thus the packet's unrestricted whole-projector estimate
\(\norm{\Pi'-T\Pi T^\dagger}\leq2\epsilon_H/g\), based only on
\(\epsilon_H=\norm{H'T-TH}\) and a gap \(g\), is false.
It must not be used to promote the ambient kernel. In the exact reducing
case the precise identity is
\[
\Pi'=T\Pi T^\dagger+\Pi'_\perp,
\]
where \(\Pi'_\perp\) projects onto the zero modes in
\((\Ran T)^\perp\). Equality of the whole projectors requires
\(\Pi'_\perp=0\); the retained intertwining identity needs no such assumption.

For approximate comparisons, the spectral object must also be fixed
correctly. A small positive perturbation of a zero eigenvalue changes its
literal kernel, although an isolated spectral cluster can persist. On a
common Hilbert space let \(A,B\) be self-adjoint, let \(A-B\) extend to a
bounded operator on their common operator domain, and let a common rectifiable
resolvent contour \(\Gamma\) isolate the two clusters being compared.
Writing \(M_A=\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\norm{(z-A)^{-1}}\), and similarly
\(M_B\), the resolvent identity and contour integral give
\[
\norm{\Pi_A-\Pi_B}
 \leq \frac{\operatorname{length}(\Gamma)}{2\pi}
 M_A M_B\norm{A-B}.
\]
Indeed, subtract the two Riesz integrals and insert
\((z-A)^{-1}-(z-B)^{-1}=(z-A)^{-1}(A-B)(z-B)^{-1}\).
This is a cluster-comparison estimate, not a bound derived from an
embedding residual alone. Identifying either cluster with the full
physical harmonic sector requires a separate completeness certificate.

For a non-isometric Fourier--Mukai or BHT transform, derived equivalence alone
is insufficient.  It may preserve Ext groups, Yoneda products, and the formal
Maurer--Cartan deformation problem after dg enhancement
\cite{LuntsOrlov2009,Wei2015,BrinzanescuHalanayTrautmann2010}.  It does not
automatically preserve the Hermitian metric, adjoint, Hodge spectrum, or
numerical kernel.  Those require a transported metric theorem or a quantified
comparison defect.

The transported-metric result makes the latter task concrete. For a bounded
invertible chain/product map \(T\), with domains also carried by \(T\), set
\[
g_{\rm tr}=(T^{-1})^\dagger g\,T^{-1}.
\]
Then \(T\) is unitary for this transported metric. If a separately selected
physical metric is \(g_{\rm phys}(x,y)=g_{\rm tr}(x,Ey)\), its positive
distortion \(E\) changes the degree-\(k\) adjoint by
\[
D_k^{\dagger_{\rm phys}}
 =E_k^{-1}D_k^{\dagger_{\rm tr}}E_{k+1},\qquad
C_k=E_{k+1}D_k-D_kE_k .
\]
Thus \(C_k=0\) is the metric-chain compatibility test. The exact witness
in the transported-metric record has transported Hodge matrix \(I_2\)
but physical matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(4,1/9)\). Polar normalization
does not automatically repair the product: if \(T=US\), its positive
factor must itself respect the differential and product for \(U\) to be
a dg-algebra map. These are explicit comparison tests, not a construction
of the physical BHT operator or its HYM metric.

\section{What survives a projection: compression, elimination and transfer}
\label{sec:compression-transfer}

A retained space can inherit an operator without inheriting its entire
algebra. Agreement of quadratic forms, products, Green operators and

\([Q_x,-]^2=[\Phi(x),-]\) can be nonzero while the outer differential is
nilpotent. These operators act on different spaces; neither identity
repairs the other by changing its meaning.

For a closed compact \(M\), the usual elliptic realization gives the
positive operator
\[
 H_{\beta,V}=d_{\beta,V}d_{\beta,V}^{\dagger}
            +d_{\beta,V}^{\dagger}d_{\beta,V}
\]
and its contractive heat semigroup. Boundary or noncompact settings need
compatible adjoints and self-adjoint domains; the polynomial computations
below are formal differential-operator identities, not domain certificates.
On the configuration line let \(A_V=\partial_x+\beta V'\). Then
\begin{align*}
 H_0=A_V^\dagger A_V&=-\partial_x^2+\beta^2(V')^2-\beta V'',\\
 H_1=A_V A_V^\dagger&=-\partial_x^2+\beta^2(V')^2+\beta V''.
\end{align*}
At a critical point, \(V''\) contains the full residual-Hessian term from
\cref{thm:residual-hessian}, not just the Gram term. For
\(\Phi(x)=1+x^2\), one has \(V=\frac12(1+x^2)^2\) and, at zero,
\[
 \Phi=1,\quad \Phi'=0,\quad V'=0,\qquad
 |\Phi'|^2=0,\quad \Phi\Phi''=2,\quad V''=2.
\]
The opposite potential terms \(-2\beta\) and \(2\beta\) are not opposite
spectral-positivity claims: positivity belongs to the factorized
differential operators, including their kinetic terms.

This construction retains the defect's full second jet, but it does not
make the Witten operator equal to the internal curvature or to the
finite-dimensional Hessian matrix. On a closed compact \(M\), multiplication
by \(e^{\beta V}\) identifies its cohomology with de Rham cohomology.
Nilpotence alone therefore does not select zero defect or a unique
physical configuration. The supplied \(\beta\) is a localization parameter,
not Lorentzian time or a derived thermodynamic temperature.

\subsection{What the eliminated pair leaves behind}
\label{sec:nil-minimal-model}

 pi=I_H,\quad dh+hd=I-ip,\quad h^2=ph=hi=0.

Under the transfer convention already used in

 m_3(a,a,b)=ac,\qquad m_3(a,b,b)=-bc

With weights \(w(a)=w(b)=1\), \(w(c)=2\), every source operation and

modulo three. This is a representation-level phase statement, not a

The top-form functional \(\tau_A(u)=[abc]u\) is graded cyclic but is not a

neither supplies that additional scalar trivialization.

\subsection{Which data a nested contraction must preserve}
\label{sec:restricted-nested-arrow}
A quasi-isomorphism is insufficient for this purpose. The restricted
transfer record requires the same admissible base, typing and boundary
data; maps \(p,i,h\) satisfying the contraction and side identities;
first-order and adjoint intertwiners; compatible cyclic and Hilbert

equivariance \cite{PreprojectionRestrictedNested2026}. The maps must be
parallel over the configuration base, with a common \(\beta\), to
intertwine the outer Witten operators. Transfer trees also need a
terminating filtration or a proved convergence domain.

For two algebraically compatible contractions the composite is

 p=p_2p_1,\qquad i=i_1i_2,\qquad h=h_1+i_1h_2p_1.
\]
These formulas preserve the normalized contraction identities. Analytic
admissibility of the composite still needs a common transfer-control
class; convergence on unrelated domains is not automatically enough.
Likewise, local arrows alone do not prove effective overlap descent.

Here is the same-source central example. Let \(\Gamma_A\) be form parity,
take \(S=\mathbb R^{2|2}\), and define

 R(x)=\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\-x&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad

Since \(C(x)^2=(1+x^2)I_4\) and \(d\Gamma_A=-\Gamma_A d\),

 Q_A^2=(1+x^2)I_{32},\qquad

\]
Tensor the preceding contraction with \(I_4\). It intertwines \(Q\),
its declared adjoint and curvature. Rank-normalized trace gives
\(V=\frac12(1+x^2)^2\) on both fibers; raw traces would differ with rank.
Because \(p\) is independent of \(x\), it also intertwines the formal
outer Witten blocks. The same \(p,i,h\) produce the nonzero \(m_3\).

Strict intertwining and transfer remain different claims: the source

one. Equality of normalized costs is verified for this source, not
inferred from any arbitrary rank-reducing map.

\subsection{Two ways to retain noncentral curvature}

The positive route instead takes \(S=\mathbb R^{1|2}\) and

 V(x)=\tfrac13(1+x^2)^2,\qquad

 \quad\cite{PreprojectionPositiveNested2026}.
\]
The omitted half would be a genuine error in a Gram-only Hessian.
The positive norm uses ordinary trace; graded cyclicity uses supertrace.
They are separate pairings, not one automatically positive cyclic form.

A wording correction is necessary here. The self-adjoint coefficient

 Q_A-Q_A^\dagger=(d-d^\dagger)\otimes I_3\ne0.

Indeed \(d(c)=ab\) while \(d^\dagger(c)=0\) in the monomial metric.

The ordered coefficient product is essential; \(B\) is not commutative.

 M_1^2(X)+M_2(M_0,X)-M_2(X,M_0)=0.

Thus \(M_0=-F\) is a sign convention, not a claim that positive

\section{Global descent and the actual rational topology}
\label{sec:morita-hirsch}

The local tensor examples answer how a coherent algebra can retain

\subsection{What descends through projective overlaps}
\label{sec:morita-curved-descent}
Keep the projective atlas of \cref{thm:end-untwist}. On a good cover,
let \(G_{ij}\) be even unitary transitions satisfying
\(G_{ij}G_{jk}G_{ki}=\alpha_{ijk}I\), and suppose odd coefficient charges
obey \(q_i=G_{ij}q_jG_{ij}^{-1}\). Set
\[
 B_i=\End(E_i),\qquad F_i=q_i^2,\qquad d_i=[q_i,-].
\]
Conjugation is an honest algebra cocycle even though the module cocycle
is projective. Squaring and taking graded commutators give
\[
 F_i=\operatorname{Ad}(G_{ij})F_j,\qquad
 d_i\operatorname{Ad}(G_{ij})=\operatorname{Ad}(G_{ij})d_j .
\]
Thus the earlier endomorphism untwisting extends to a curved
differential, not just to the zero-curvature case
\cite{PreprojectionMoritaDescent2026}. The coefficient charge need not
commute with the transitions; it need only transform correctly.

Suppose the form-factor contractions have honest overlaps \(T_{ij}\)
preserving the unit and intertwining their transferred \(m_n\). Then
\[
 R_{ij}=T_{ij}\widehat\otimes\operatorname{Ad}(G_{ij})
\]
has an honest triple law. For the coefficient operations of
\cref{sec:unital-curved-coefficients},
\[
 R_{ij}M_{n,j}=M_{n,i}R_{ij}^{\otimes n},\qquad n\geq0.
\]
For \(n=0\) this means transport of curvature. For \(n\geq2\) it follows
from transport of \(m_n\) and the ordered coefficient product; even
transitions preserve the Koszul signs. Hence the local curved identities
glue without introducing another curvature-insertion series. This imports
the overlap extension; it does not duplicate the untwisting proof.

An ordinary endomorphism sheaf is not necessarily a globally split matrix
algebra. In the locally free case it can be an Azumaya algebra with
nontrivial Brauer class \cite{CaldararuTwisted2000}. The scalar twist
cancels from conjugation, not from the original module. For the same reason,
central shared-line holonomy cannot be reconstructed from this adjoint
action alone. The Hermitian differential line remains separate source data.

\subsection{A noncommuting witness and its metric boundary}

It is odd and self-adjoint, and a clock overlap moves it nontrivially.
The transformed charges glue, their \(F_i=q_i^\dagger q_i=q_i^2\)

Here self-adjointness concerns the displayed coefficient \(q_i\), not

Likewise, coordinate-dependent transitions require the connection law,

 {\cal A}_i=G_{ij}{\cal A}_jG_{ij}^{-1}
                 -(dG_{ij})G_{ij}^{-1},

up to central terms invisible on endomorphisms. Parallel overlap maps,

\section{Higher repair jets and graph shadows}
\label{sec:graphs}

The operator is only the quadratic shadow of nonlinear repair.  Higher
derivatives of the same cost provide its formal interaction tensors.

Let \(E\) and \(F\) be finite-dimensional inner-product spaces, let
\(\Phi:E\to F\), and allow a positive residual metric field \(W(a)\).  At a
background \(a_*\), write
\[
R_m=D^m\Phi(a_*),
\qquad
W_m=D^mW(a_*),
\qquad
V_n=D^n\calC(a_*),
\]
where
\[
\calC(a)=\frac12\ip{\Phi(a)}{W(a)\Phi(a)}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Complete repair-jet formula]
\label{thm:repair-jet}
For every ordered three-color partition
\([n]=A\sqcup B\sqcup C\), preserving the original argument order within
each block,
\begin{equation}
V_n(v_1,\ldots,v_n)
=\frac12\sum_{A\sqcup B\sqcup C=[n]}
\ip{R_{|A|}(v_A)}{W_{|B|}(v_B)R_{|C|}(v_C)}.
\label{eq:three-color-jet}
\end{equation}
At a zero-defect background,
\[
V_2(v,w)=\ip{R_1(v)}{W_0R_1(w)}.
\]
If \(W\) is constant and \(\Phi\) is polynomial of degree \(r\), then
\(V_n=0\) for \(n>2r\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the multivariable Leibniz rule to the three factors
\(\Phi,W,\Phi\).  Each derivative label is assigned exactly once to the
left residual, metric, or right residual.  Summing over all assignments
gives \eqref{eq:three-color-jet}.  If \(R_0=0\), the terms with an empty
left or right residual block vanish at order two.  Polynomial termination
is immediate from the maximum derivative order of the two residual factors.
\end{proof}

For the Maurer--Cartan residual \(F(a)=da+a^2\) with constant metric, the
repair cost therefore has only quadratic, cubic, and quartic vertices.  They
are not independent knobs: they are derivatives of one residual and one
metric.

Once the reduced inverse \(G\) of \(V_2\) is declared, Wick contraction
organizes the formal coefficients by graphs \cite{Wick1950}.  The modal
diagrammatics paper proves this graph expansion and its compatible
projection theorem \cite{MTTDiagrammatics2026}.  The present theorem adds the
missing upstream arrow:
\[
(\Phi,W,a_*)
\longmapsto
\bigl(V_2,G,\{V_n\}_{n\geq3}\bigr)
\longmapsto
\text{formal repair graphs}.
\]

This explains why Gaussian and graph structures recur.  Every sufficiently
smooth local repair process has a quadratic tangent shadow, and every formal
quadratic expansion admits Wick organization.  What is universal is the
grammar.  A physical Feynman rule additionally needs a signed action,
grading, gauge complex, state, causal prescription, and renormalization.

\section{Why repair is not automatically action}
\label{sec:action-boundary}

This section is not a caveat added after the fact.  It is a theorem-level
separation.  Without it, the preceding construction would overclaim exactly
where it becomes most physically interesting.

\subsection{The variational-anchor test}

Let \(E,F\) be finite-dimensional real Hilbert spaces and
\(\Phi:E\to F\).  A constant variational anchor is a map
\(A:F\to E^*\) defining the one-form
\[
\alpha_a(v)=(A\Phi(a))(v).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite Helmholtz gate]
\label{thm:helmholtz}
On a star-shaped domain, a scalar functional \(S_{\mathrm{var}}\) with
\(\dd S_{\mathrm{var}}=\alpha\) exists if and only if \(\alpha\) is closed,
equivalently
\begin{equation}
D(A\Phi)(a)=D(A\Phi)(a)^{T}
\label{eq:helmholtz}
\end{equation}
for every \(a\).  When it exists, one reconstruction is
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{var}}(a)=S_{\mathrm{var}}(0)
+\int_0^1\alpha_{ta}(a)\,\dd t.
\label{eq:radial-action}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact one-forms are closed.  Conversely,
\eqref{eq:radial-action} is the Poincare radial homotopy on a star-shaped
domain; differentiation and \(\dd\alpha=0\) give
\(\dd S_{\mathrm{var}}=\alpha\).  The coordinate form of closedness is
\eqref{eq:helmholtz}.
\end{proof}

In local field theory the same question becomes the graded formal-adjoint
Helmholtz problem on jet bundles, with boundary terms and operator domains
retained explicitly \cite{Voronov2003}.  A positive metric on residual space
does not supply this anchor.

\subsection{The universal first-order lift}

Even when a direct anchor is absent, introduce a multiplier
\(\lambda\in F\) and define
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{mult}}(a,\lambda)=\ip{\lambda}{\Phi(a)}.
\label{eq:multiplier-action}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplier lift and normal square]
\label{thm:multiplier}
Every exact closure state \(a_*\) gives a lifted critical point
\((a_*,0)\).  If \(J=D\Phi(a_*)\), the Hessian is
\begin{equation}
\calD_J=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&J^{\dagger}\\
J&0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\calD_J^2=
\begin{bmatrix}
J^{\dagger}J&0\\
0&JJ^{\dagger}
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:multiplier-square}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
\ker\calD_J=\ker J\oplus\ker J^{\dagger}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Varying \eqref{eq:multiplier-action} gives
\(\Phi(a)=0\) and \(D\Phi(a)^{\dagger}\lambda=0\).  Differentiating again
at \((a_*,0)\) gives the off-diagonal block operator.  Direct multiplication
gives its square and kernel.
\end{proof}

The multiplier lift is a genuine first-order square root of the repair
normal operator.  It does not decide whether the multiplier is an ordinary
field, response field, antifield, or unphysical auxiliary variable.  That
typing belongs to a later physical construction, such as a selected BV
complex \cite{PTVV2011}.

\subsection{Information erased by the square}

\begin{proposition}[Normal-square nonselection]
\label{prop:square-no-go}
The positive operator \(J^{\dagger}J\) does not determine the sign or phase
of \(J\), the signed action Hessian, a causal inverse, or the first-order
response.  In particular,
\[
J_+=\operatorname{diag}(2,3),
\qquad
J_- =\operatorname{diag}(-2,3)
\]
have the same normal square \(\operatorname{diag}(4,9)\), but their inverse
responses differ in the sign of the first channel.
\end{proposition}

If a signed self-adjoint action Hessian \(H_{\mathrm{var}}\) is identified
with \(J\), then the repair Hessian is \(H_{\mathrm{var}}^2\) in the simplest
equal-metric case.  Squaring erases the Morse index.  A saddle direction of
the physical action becomes a positive repair direction.

The interaction vertices also differ.  If
\[
S_{\mathrm{var}}(x)=\frac m2x^2+\frac g6x^3,
\qquad
\Phi(x)=S_{\mathrm{var}}'(x)=mx+\frac g2x^2,
\]
then
\[
S_{\mathrm{rep}}(x)=\frac12\Phi(x)^2
=\frac{m^2}{2}x^2+\frac{mg}{2}x^3+\frac{g^2}{8}x^4.
\]
The signed-action rows \((V_2,V_3,V_4)=(m,g,0)\) become the repair rows
\((m^2,3mg,3g^2)\).  Repair graphs are therefore not physical Feynman graphs
unless a separate theorem identifies the physical perturbative datum with
the repair functional.

\subsection{When a signed action does exist}

Let \(\calA\) be a differential graded associative algebra with a degree-three
cyclic trace \(\tau\) satisfying
\[
\tau(dx)=0,
\qquad
\tau(xy)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\tau(yx).
\]
For \(a\in\calA^1\), define
\begin{equation}
S_{\MC}(a)=\frac12\tau(a\,da)+\frac13\tau(a^3).
\label{eq:mc-action}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Cyclic Maurer--Cartan action]
\label{thm:cyclic-action}
For every degree-one variation \(u\),
\begin{equation}
DS_{\MC}(a)[u]=\tau\bigl(u(da+a^2)\bigr).
\label{eq:mc-variation}
\end{equation}
If the pairing between degrees one and two is nondegenerate, the critical
locus is exactly the Maurer--Cartan locus.  The action is infinitesimally
gauge invariant under
\(\delta_\epsilon a=d\epsilon+[a,\epsilon]\).  It descends through even
projective overlaps because the scalar cocycle cancels under conjugation and
the cyclic trace is conjugation invariant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the quadratic term.  The Stokes identity moves \(d\) from the
variation to \(a\), making the two contributions equal.  Cyclicity makes the
three cubic contributions equal.  This gives \eqref{eq:mc-variation}.
Pairing a gauge variation with the residual and integrating by parts yields
the Bianchi identity.  On overlaps, each monomial is conjugated by the same
even transition, and the trace removes that conjugation.
\end{proof}

On a compact complex threefold with holomorphic volume form \(\Omega\), the
candidate trace is
\[
\tau(x)=\int_X\Omega\wedge\Str(x),
\]
and \eqref{eq:mc-action} becomes holomorphic Chern--Simons theory
\cite{Witten1992,Costello2004}.  This supplies a signed action for the
integrability lane.  It does not automatically supply nondegenerate cyclic
pairings for the HYM, balanced, anomaly, coframe, and Lorentzian lanes.

\section{From anomaly data to a constrained repair problem}
\label{sec:string-repair}

There is a useful intermediate answer to the action question. The anomaly
equation need not be an unrelated extra row appended to a bundle complex.
It can help define the complex itself. Three existing source results make
this precise: a string-algebroid constructor, a Bott--Chern lift of bundle
deformations, and a moment-map interpretation of the remaining equations
\cite{PreprojectionStringConstructor2026,PreprojectionBCLift2026,
PreprojectionCalabiRoles2026}. Their order matters. Constructing the complex
does not solve its moment-map equations, and solving a form equation over
real or complex coefficients does not select an integral flux sector.

\subsection{The rank-102 carrier acquires structure}
\label{sec:string-constructor}

Let \(P\) be the holomorphic product principal bundle for the tangent,
visible and projective hidden factors, and let \(c\) be a specified
nondegenerate invariant bilinear pairing on its Lie algebra. Given a
compatible connection \(\theta\) and a form
\(H\in\Omega^{3,0}\oplus\Omega^{2,1}\) satisfying
\[
 F_\theta^{0,2}=0,\qquad dH+c(F_\theta\wedge F_\theta)=0,
 \tag{S1}\label{eq:string-anomaly}
\]
the standard string-algebroid construction supplies the holomorphic
Courant structure, including its anchor, pairing and bracket
\cite[Proposition 2.4]{GarciaRubioTiplerString2018}. It fits into
\[
 0\longrightarrow T^*X\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow A_P
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad
 0\longrightarrow\ad P\longrightarrow A_P\longrightarrow TX
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Its smooth splitting has complex rank \(3+8+8+80+3=102\).
The finite central quotient of the hidden group leaves its rank-80 adjoint
honest. It does not trivialize the hidden fundamental gerbe. Thus rank
matching identifies the carrier type; the anomaly triple supplies its
operations. Once that triple is given, a bracket is not another free choice.

The q79 promotion uses more than an invariant-form computation. The full
real map \(H^{2,2}_{BC}(X)\to H^4_{\rm dR}(X;\mathbb R)\) is injective on
the specified q79 geometry. For supplied holomorphic product bundles with
the required total characteristic cancellation, choose Chern connections.
Their real, closed, pure-type form
\(\Xi=c(F_\theta\wedge F_\theta)\) is de Rham exact. Consequently one may
write, in the source's convention,
\[
 \Xi=2i\partial\bar\partial\tau,\qquad
 H=2i\partial\tau,\qquad dH=-\Xi .
 \tag{S2}\label{eq:string-bc-primitive}
\]
The sign follows from \(\bar\partial\partial=-\partial\bar\partial\).
This is a conditional existence construction for the complex anomaly data,
not a numerical solution of the HYM equations. Changing the primitive can
change the string extension class. Nor is this complex truncated form
automatically the real torsion \(H_{\mathbb R}=d^c\omega\): reality, trace
coefficients, integral refinements and the tangent-connection convention
must still match. Existing existential hidden-bundle/HYM results are not
reopened by these additional requirements on a common physical source.

A small exact witness checks the mechanism, not the continuum existence
premises. Take \(\partial\tau=h\), \(\bar\partial\tau=k\),
\(\partial k=v\), \(\bar\partial h=-v\), with all further maps zero.
Three closed curvature labels with squares \(2v\), weighted by
\((3/2,-1/2,-1/2)\), give \(\Xi=v\). Then \(H=h\) solves
\(dH+\Xi=0\). This rational form complex verifies the transgression sign
and product-gauge accounting; it is not a selected curvature table.

\subsection{What the Bott--Chern lift actually lifts}
\label{sec:string-mc-lift}

The form lane remains visible in the deformation algebra. Write
\(\mathfrak g^k=\Omega^{0,k}(\ad P)\) and
\(\mathfrak F^k=\bigoplus_{j=0}^k\Omega^{j+2,k-j}\), omitting types outside
the dimension of \(X\). In nonnegative degrees the string differential
graded Lie algebra \(\mathfrak L_Q\) is an extension of \(\mathfrak g\)
by this abelian form complex. In the constructor's convention,
\[
 d_Q(a,b)=\bigl(\bar\partial_\theta a,
             db-2c(\partial_\theta a\wedge F_\theta)\bigr).
\]
The bracket also has a quadratic form component. In particular, for a
bundle Maurer--Cartan solution
\(\bar\partial_\theta a+\tfrac12[a,a]=0\), define
\[
 \Delta(a)=2c(\partial_\theta a\wedge F_\theta)
                 +c(\partial_\theta a\wedge\partial_\theta a).
 \tag{S3}\label{eq:string-deformation}
\]
The full equation is \(db=\Delta(a)\), and the deformed anomaly triple is
\((\theta+a,H-b)\), not \((\theta+a,H+b)\). On the fixed smooth bundle,
\(\Delta(a)\) is closed, pure \((2,2)\) and de Rham exact. Complexifying
the full q79 Bott--Chern injectivity result gives
\(\Delta(a)=2i\partial\bar\partial\tau_a\); hence
\(b=-2i\partial\tau_a\) solves it exactly. The connection \(\theta+a\)
need not be the Chern connection of the old Hermitian metric.

An auxiliary Hermitian elliptic solution operator \(\mathcal K_{BC}\)
can select a primitive on this exact-form subspace. It defines a section
of Maurer--Cartan \emph{sets},
\[
 s_{\mathcal K}(a)=(a,\mathcal K_{BC}\Delta(a)),\qquad
 \pi s_{\mathcal K}(a)=a.
 \tag{S4}\label{eq:string-mc-section}
\]
For each \(a\), its unquotiented fiber is affine over the closed degree-one
form lane. The auxiliary metric chooses a representative, not a physical
flux. The section is generally nonlinear, and gauge-equivariance,
integral refinement or an \(L_\infty\) equivalence do not follow from
\(\pi s=1\). The representable product-adjoint subcomplex embeds as a
Lie dg algebra in the commutator algebra of cohesive endomorphisms. It is
not an associative dg subalgebra: traceless matrices are closed under
commutators, but \(E_{12}E_{21}=E_{11}\) has nonzero trace. Arbitrary
off-diagonal or higher cohesive deformations are not covered by this lift.

The finite source example makes the distinction tangible. In degree one
use \((e,b,z)\), in degree two \((c,v)\), and set
\[
 de=c-2v,\quad db=v,\quad dz=0,\quad [e,e]=2c-2v,
\]
with all other brackets and degree-two differentials zero. Its
Maurer--Cartan residual at \(xe+yb+\lambda z\) is
\[
 (x+x^2)c+(y-2x-x^2)v.
\]
Thus \(s(x)=(x,2x+x^2,0)\) lifts precisely the two base roots \(x=0,-1\),
and \(\lambda z\) remains free. The polynomial extension obeys
\(s(2)-2s(1)=2b\), so it is not a linear splitting. A source section has
been constructed; uniqueness of a complete physical deformation has not.

\subsection{One moment map, with distinct volume-form roles}
\label{sec:string-calabi}

On a compact real form, the integrable positive horizontal-lift space has
the Calabi moment-map description. For fixed positive volume \(\mu\), put
\(f_\omega=\tfrac12\log((\omega^n/n!)/\mu)\). Its zero locus consists of
\[
 F_\theta\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0,\qquad
 d(e^{-\ell f_\omega}\omega^{n-1})=0,
\]
together with the integrability equations \(F^{0,2}=0\) and
\(dd^c\omega+c(F\wedge F)=0\). For \(n=3\), \(\ell=1\), and the
holomorphic volume convention, \(e^{-f_\omega}=\|\Omega_{\rm hol}\|_\omega\).
This recovers the Hull--Strominger equations
\cite[Proposition 5.14 and Corollary 5.15]{GarciaRubioTiplerGauge2020}.
For the MTT product group, the three HYM rows and the balanced row are
components of one moment map; the integrability and anomaly equations
instead define its configuration stratum. Matching the selected physical
trace and real torsion conventions is required for this interpretation.

The normalization row has a different role again. If
\(\mu_{\Omega_{\rm hol}}\) is the positive volume of the holomorphic form,
define
\[
 \Omega_{\rm unit}=e^{f_\omega}\Omega_{\rm hol},\qquad
 \mu_{\Omega_{\rm unit}}
 =e^{2f_\omega}\mu_{\Omega_{\rm hol}}=\omega^3/3!.
 \tag{S5}\label{eq:string-unit-volume}
\]
This is a coordinate identity. In general
\(d\Omega_{\rm unit}=e^{f_\omega}df_\omega\wedge\Omega_{\rm hol}\ne0\).
Requiring the same form to be both holomorphic and unit normalized adds
a constant-norm restriction. The conformally balanced equation must use
\(\Omega_{\rm hol}\); replacing it by the unit representative would
wrongly force \(d\omega^2=0\). Thus the two uses of the symbol \(\Omega\)
in an ambient residual compiler must be separated before physical use.

\subsection{Restrict the Hessian, not the ambient proof}
\label{sec:string-constrained-hessian}

Suppose an actual stratum inclusion \(\iota:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal C\)
implements the constructor, normalization chart and same-complex
identification just described. At an exact solution, let \(j=D\iota\).
If \(\mathcal A\circ\iota=\mathcal N\circ\iota=0\), then
\[
 D\mathcal A\,j=D\mathcal N\,j=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Hess}(\calC\circ\iota)
       =j^\dagger D\Phi^\dagger W D\Phi j .
 \tag{S6}\label{eq:string-restricted-gram}
\]
Here \(W\) is any chosen positive residual metric. All terms involving
the vanished rows, including cross terms, vanish on this tangent space.
The proof is the chain rule at \(D\calC=0\), not cancellation of positive
terms in the ambient Hessian. The normal and obstruction complex is still
needed. Gauge/diffeomorphism slices concern the quotient analysis; they
are not additional physical residual equations.

For example, take an ambient \(\mathbb R^{11}\) with coordinates
\((\phi,a,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_4,n,g_1,\ldots,g_4)\). Let
\(J=(I_7\ 0)\). Its Euclidean Gram matrix has rank seven. Inclusion of
\((\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_4,g_1,\ldots,g_4)\), setting \(\phi=a=n=0\),
pulls it back to \(\operatorname{diag}(I_4,0_4)\), of rank four. The three
removed directions are transverse, not three newly discovered physical
gauge modes. This conditional restriction does not contradict the ambient
25-block support in \cref{sec:q79}, and is not proof of a reducing or
isometric spectral intertwiner. Without the inclusion and convention
match, deleting the anomaly row remains unjustified.

\subsection{The action bridge and its positivity boundary}
\label{sec:string-positivity}

With convention \(d\langle\boldsymbol\mu,\xi\rangle
=\iota_{\rho_\xi}\varpi\) and \(g(u,v)=\varpi(u,Iv)\), one has
\(\operatorname{grad}_g\langle\boldsymbol\mu,\xi\rangle=I\rho_\xi\)
where the metric is nondegenerate. This ties the moment-map components to
infinitesimal complexified gauge motion. The rational witness on
\(\mathbb R^4_x\oplus\mathbb R^4_y\) has standard \(\varpi,I\),
\(g=I_8\), \(\rho_i=-\partial_{y_i}\) and \(\mu_i=x_i\).
Its orbit Jacobian is \(I_4\). It illustrates a variational anchor but
does not establish positivity in the geometric moduli problem.

In particular, the cited Calabi construction is generally
\emph{pseudo-Kahler}, possibly degenerate, not automatically a positive
repair metric. A positive horizontal lift means that its underlying
Hermitian form is positive; it does not give positivity of every field-space
metric. The anomaly pairing has signed factor weights. Nondegeneracy,
positive directions, convexity and coercive estimates need their own
hypotheses; at degeneracy an inverse-metric gradient is not available.
Neither a local orbit-potential argument nor the finite positive witness
establishes global minimization or a Lorentzian physical action.

The later cyclic-cotangent result is already incorporated in the action
discussion: the uncompleted string form lane does not automatically inherit
a nondegenerate cyclic trace, whereas a shifted cotangent completion supplies
dual variables. Shared-line compatibility on the neutral product-adjoint
sector likewise does not identify every fundamental charged field.
These three imports therefore remove independent bracket and form-lift
searches on their stated domain, organize the residual roles, and feed the
existing BV construction. They do not select the common physical endpoint,
the remaining flux data or the four-dimensional action.

\section{From a cotangent lift to a four-dimensional BV theory}
\label{sec:bv-reduction}

The action boundary has a constructive continuation. A multiplier pairs an
equation with a dual variable; Batalin--Vilkovisky (BV) geometry organizes
fields, ghosts and antifields with an odd symplectic pairing. Antifields are
dual bookkeeping variables for the gauge equations, not additional observed
particles. With consistent ghost degrees, the classical master equation
\(\{S,S\}=0\) expresses compatibility of the action and gauge differential.
A volume element, integration prescription and quantum master equation are
further data \cite{SchwarzBV1992}. The following two source results explain
what this construction preserves under reduction
\cite{PreprojectionCotangentReduction2026,PreprojectionOrientationBV2026}.

\subsection{Retain fields and their correct duals together}
\label{sec:bv-cotangent-retract}

Let \((L,d_L)\) retract onto \((H,d_H)\) by cochain maps \(p,i\) and a
degree-minus-one map \(h\), with
\[
 pi=1,\qquad d_Lh+hd_L=1-ip,\qquad h^2=ph=hi=0.
\]
Assume finite-dimensional complexes, or locally perfect complexes with a
specified density-valued dual and nondegenerate evaluation. A functional
analytic realization must additionally specify domains and continuous duals.
Write \(p^!,i^!\) for the graded density transposes. In the cyclic DGLA
convention used by the internal source, the cotangent complexes and maps are
\begin{align}
 \widehat L&=L\oplus L^![-3],&
 \widehat H&=H\oplus H^![-3], \notag\\
 \widehat i&=i\oplus p^!,&
 \widehat p&=p\oplus i^!.
 \label{eq:bv-cotangent-lift}
\end{align}
The suspension to field coordinates gives the degree-minus-one BV pairing;
the bracket notation in \eqref{eq:bv-cotangent-lift} is not itself a
spacetime dimension. Transpose the homotopy with the same graded convention.
For the ordinary block-matrix check, the differential and homotopy are
\[
 \widehat d=\operatorname{diag}(d_L,-d_L^T),\qquad
 \widehat h=\operatorname{diag}(h,-h^T).
\]
Then
\[
 \widehat p\widehat i=1,\qquad
 \widehat d\widehat h+\widehat h\widehat d
   =1-\widehat i\widehat p,\qquad
 \widehat i^*\omega_L=\omega_H .
\]
The pairing identity follows from
\((p^!\eta)(iy)=\eta(piy)=\eta(y)\). Thus it is the \emph{inclusion of the
retained field--dual sector}, not the projection on the entire upper space,
that preserves the nondegenerate pairing.

For example, retain \(r_1,r_2\) from a complex with basis
\((r_1,r_2,u,v)\), \(du=v\), and \(h(v)=u\). The cotangent lift has eight
coordinates above and four below. A discarded pair \(u,u^*\) has pairing
one above and zero after projection. This elementary example explains why
deleting modes is not automatically a symplectic equivalence. Restricting
to the retained symplectic sector is exact; integrating the complement
instead requires a suitable BV pushforward. Such effective-action
constructions are established mathematics
\cite{CattaneoMnev2008}; the existing downstream q79 results apply after
their four-dimensional BV complex and compatible split have been supplied.

A cochain contraction alone does not transport an arbitrary nonlinear
action. If the retained nonlinear maps obey \(Q_L i=iQ_H\), then
\[
 S_L(\widehat i(x,\eta))
 =\langle p^!\eta,Q_L(ix)\rangle
 =\langle\eta,Q_H(x)\rangle .
\]
For a BV interpretation \(Q\) must have the appropriate degree and satisfy
\(Q^2=0\); an arbitrary residual only defines a multiplier action. With
non-strict transfer, all required higher brackets and the compatible
cyclic map must be retained. Neither a bare projection nor a truncated
residual proves that action identity.

\subsection{Why the field-only action cannot be obtained by relabeling}
\label{sec:bv-zero-section}

The internal multiplier has \(S_{\mathrm{cot}}(x,0)=0\). A supplied
four-dimensional BV theory generally has
\[
 S_{\mathrm{BV}}(z,z^*)=S_0(z)+\sum_i z_i^*Q^i(z),
 \qquad S_{\mathrm{BV}}(z,0)=S_0(z)\not\equiv0.
\]
Consequently a zero-section-preserving pullback cannot identify these
actions without a field-only upper term that reduces to \(S_0\).
This is a restricted obstruction, not a theorem against every canonical
transformation: a transformation mixing fields and antifields would need
its own generating data and proof.

The cyclic integrability action in the preceding section supplies one
signed field-only functional. It is not thereby the complete Standard
Model action on a Lorentzian four-manifold. In particular, matching the
abstract cotangent degree does not identify internal adjoint/form fields
with gauge potentials, charged fermions, ghosts and the Higgs. Their
representations, density, real structure, statistics and operator domains
must be carried by the same reduction.

\subsection{The exact unit--orientation construction}
\label{sec:bv-orientation-profile}

There is nevertheless a simple exact reduction mechanism. Use the rational
q79 algebra of Section~\ref{sec:q79-hirsch-model}, and let
\(\nu=utv\) be its degree-six top class, with \(\tau(\nu)=1\).
The two-dimensional subalgebra
\[
 R=\operatorname{span}\{1,\nu\},\qquad
 1\nu=\nu1=\nu,\qquad \nu^2=0
\]
has nondegenerate cyclic pairing
\[
 B_R=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These are two complementary \emph{internal profiles}, not two physical
fields or two universes. The raw topological trace has \(\tau(1)=0\).
A normalized density is therefore essential for a field-only term:
\(\tau(\nu S_0)=S_0\). Choosing an algebraic map
\(\star1=\nu,\star\nu=1\) gives \(B_R\star=I_2\), but does not select
the physical Hodge star or volume. For a Riemannian metric with volume
\(V\) and \(\nu=\mathrm{vol}/V\), instead
\(\star1=V\nu\), \(\star\nu=1/V\); the pairing matrix is
\(\operatorname{diag}(V,1/V)\). Topological normalization does not determine
\(V\).

Now supply an antifield-linear classical BV action with
\(Q^2=0\) and \(Q(S_0)=0\), and assign profiles
\[
 z_i\longmapsto z_i\otimes1,\qquad
 z_i^*\longmapsto z_i^*\otimes\nu .
\]
Declare the degree-minus-six fiber-integration shift, as well as the
field/ghost gradings. Then both the action and odd pairing reduce exactly:
\begin{align}
 \tau\!\left(\nu S_0(z\otimes1)
       +\sum_i(z_i^*\otimes\nu)Q^i(z\otimes1)\right)
   &=S_{\mathrm{BV}}(z,z^*), \label{eq:bv-orientation-action}\\
 \omega_{\mathrm{red}}(z\otimes1,z^*\otimes\nu)
   &=\omega_4(z,z^*). \label{eq:bv-orientation-pairing}
\end{align}
Without the degree shift these equations establish coefficient agreement,
not agreement of BV degrees. The restriction to antifield-linear actions
also matters: a term with two such antifield profiles would contain
\(\nu^2=0\). General open gauge algebras require additional structure.

A small example has even fields \(x,z\), odd ghost \(c\), and
\(Qx=c,\ Qc=Qz=0\). Choose
\[
 S_0(z)=\tfrac12z^2+\tfrac13z^3,\qquad
 S_{\mathrm{BV}}=S_0+x^*c .
\]
Here \(Q^2=0\) and \(Q(S_0)=0\), so the classical master equation holds.
Both terms return unchanged under \eqref{eq:bv-orientation-action}. The
ghost is a Grassmann variable, not a freely substituted ordinary number;
rational coefficient checks are not a replacement for that grading.

The construction has the precise direction
\(\mathrm{Red}\circ\mathrm{Lift}=1\). It reconstructs a theory already
specified below and demonstrates that the reduction rules can fit together.
It does not prove that the selected upper MTT source is this lift, or derive
the chosen coefficients of \(S_0\).

\subsection{A consistent sector is not all low-energy modes}
\label{sec:bv-mode-completeness}

Tensoring the full internal contraction by an external cochain complex
\((E,d_E)\) is exact. The total differential and homotopy are
\[
 D(e\otimes a)=d_Ee\otimes a+(-1)^{|e|}e\otimes d_Aa,\qquad
 K(e\otimes a)=(-1)^{|e|}e\otimes h(a).
\]
With \(I=1\otimes i\), \(P=1\otimes p\), the signs cancel the mixed
terms and give \(DK+KD=1-IP\) and the normalized side conditions. For
the archived three-state external example this is a \(288\)-to-\(264\)
contraction, retaining all \(88\) internal cohomology profiles.

By contrast, the orientation choice keeps only two of them. The cyclic
pairing splits
\[
 H(A)=R\oplus R^\perp,\qquad \dim R^\perp=86,
\]
and its restriction to \(R^\perp\) remains nondegenerate. These classes
cannot be contracted away by the bare topology differential. Nor need
\(R^\perp\) be an ideal: the degree-one class \(v\) and degree-five class
\(ut\) lie in the complement but \(v(ut)=-\nu\). Thus the two-profile
projection is not an algebra quotient of the full cohomology.

A consistent classical restriction is still possible when the full
Hamiltonian vector field is tangent to the retained symplectic sector.
Cyclicity, orthogonality and preservation by all source operations are
sufficient for the usual cyclic construction; a newly added field-only
term must satisfy the same tangency condition. This is stronger than
having a subalgebra. It is weaker than proving that the discarded modes
are absent or massive. A physical bundle/flux/Hessian operator would have
to constrain or lift the extra profiles and certify the relevant energy
scale. The number \(86\) counts bare rational cohomology classes, not
86 predicted particles.

\subsection{The topology-only lifting question is already decided}
\label{sec:bv-euler-lifting}

A stronger follow-on theorem sharpens the preceding boundary
\cite{PreprojectionTopologyLifting2026}. On the \emph{unchanged graded}
88-dimensional carrier,
\[
 \chi(H)=1-1+21-42+21-1+1=0,\qquad
 \chi(R)=1+1=2.
\]
Euler characteristic is invariant under replacing a finite cochain complex
by its cohomology. Hence no degree-one square-zero differential on this
carrier can leave exactly \(R\) as cohomology. If it preserves
\(H=R\oplus R^\perp\) and is zero on \(R\), then
\[
 \chi(R^\perp)=-2,\qquad
 \dim H(R^\perp,d_{\mathrm{eff}})\geq2.
\]
Thus at most \(84\) of the \(86\) complement profiles can be lifted within
this specified cochain model. This is not an unsolved numerical search.

The bound is attained, including cyclic compatibility. Choose rational
Poincare-dual bases \(b_i,b_i^\vee\) in degrees two and four and symplectic
pairs \(w_i,z_i\) in degree three, \(0\leq i\leq20\). Set

\subsection{Keep the grading before taking the square}
\label{sec:matter-index-first}

 H_+=D_+^\dagger D_+,\qquad H_-=D_+D_+^\dagger,\qquad
 \operatorname{ind}D_+=\dim\ker H_+-\dim\ker H_- .
\]
The nonzero spectra pair; the kernels need not. Replacing \(D_+\) by its
adjoint reverses the index and exchanges the two positive blocks.
Consequently the \emph{ungraded} direct sum \(H_+\oplus H_-\) does not
determine the sign of chirality. This is not a claim that a positive
operator loses its index when its grading has actually been retained.

For an exact finite example take \(D_+=(1,0,0,0):\mathbb Q^4\to\mathbb Q\).

have diagonals
\[
 (1,0,0,0,1),\qquad (1,1,0,0,0),

on the target, however,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\Gamma f(H_+\oplus H_-)\right)

\]
This identity holds for every function on the finite spectrum.
In an infinite-dimensional realization the trace must exist; for
heat kernels this is the graded heat-trace/index mechanism
\cite{McKeanSinger1967,BerlineGetzlerVergne2004}. Compact ellipticity
supplies the usual trace-class setting. A general noncompact Fredholm
operator alone does not.

in every character block, giving
\[

An inverse-character pairing requires the \emph{equivariant} duality
data. Serre duality pairs \(F\) with \(F^\vee\otimes K_X\);

\(I_{\chi^{-1}}=-I_\chi\). If the canonical line has a nontrivial

\section{Falsifiers and acceptance tests}

The structural theorem is falsified in an application if any required map
or domain fails.  The physical interpretation fails if a stronger claim is
made without its bridge.  A candidate promotion should pass the following
tests.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Defect source:} specify every residual row, bundle, connection,
domain, and source hash.
\item \textbf{Background:} prove whether the selected state has zero defect.
If it does not, compute the residual Hessian correction.
\item \textbf{Metric:} derive or explicitly declare the field and residual
metrics; do not infer them from a categorical equivalence.
\item \textbf{Descent:} verify the literal overlap maps, cocycles, and
isometric/unitary compatibility.
\item \textbf{Hodge domain:} prove self-adjointness, ellipticity or the
appropriate hyperbolic replacement, and boundary conditions.
\item \textbf{Projection:} verify reduction of the operator and preservation
of the nonlinear residual jet, not only matching dimensions.
\item \textbf{Action:} provide a Helmholtz/cyclic anchor or a fully typed
multiplier/BV lift.  Do not call the positive square the physical action.
\item \textbf{Causality:} derive retarded, advanced, or Feynman prescriptions
from the signed gauge-fixed operator and state.
\item \textbf{Strata:} test Green and contraction bounds near every
cohomology-rank jump.
\item \textbf{Numerics:} evaluate endpoint coefficients only after the same
source has passed the structural tests.
\end{enumerate}

These tests turn the paper into a research instrument.  A failure identifies
which arrow is missing; it does not send the program back to the beginning.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What has genuinely been gained}

The main gain is upstream organization.  The heat kernel is no longer merely
an attractive analogy for coherence.  Under the theorem's hypotheses it is
the exact tangent propagator of nonlinear defect repair.  The harmonic sector
is the kernel of the same Hessian.  The spectral projector and local kernel
come from its functional calculus.  The formal repair vertices are higher
derivatives of the same cost.  Compatible projection preserves the whole
package.

This makes several previously separate papers part of one coherent story:
\[

\text{closure defect and metric}
\longrightarrow
\text{repair flow},\\
\text{repair flow}
\longrightarrow
\text{Hodge Hessian},\\
\text{Hodge Hessian}

\subsection{What has not been gained}

The same clarity exposes the missing physics.  A positive normal square
cannot tell us which first-order sign or phase nature uses.  It cannot choose
a Lorentzian causal prescription.  It cannot distinguish a physical saddle
from a repair minimum.  It cannot supply a quantum state, a Born rule, or a
renormalization scheme.  These are not small numerical corrections; they are
different typed structures.

The result is therefore neither ``only mathematics'' nor a completed theory
of everything.  It is a rigorous mathematical bridge that narrows the
physical frontier.  It tells us which downstream constructions are already
forced once a selected source exists, and which ones still require genuinely
new input.

\subsection{Why the cohesive route matters}

The cohesive route is especially useful because it remains regular where an
open-stratum bundle contraction fails.  It allows the full derived object to
carry a global differential, deformation algebra, and Hodge calculus without
first pretending that the cohomology rank is constant.  This may be the right
preprojection language for MTT.  It may also turn out to be only an auxiliary
resolution of a physical pure-bundle endpoint.  The present theorem permits
both outcomes and gives a concrete test between them.

\section{Conclusion}

Closure is not represented here first as a static operator.  It is
represented by a nonlinear defect and the process that reduces its cost.
The operator appears as the derivative of that process at an admissible
state.  Hodge theory, heat flow, Green response, spectral projection, local
kernels, and formal repair graphs then follow as exact shadows of one source.

The result is strongest precisely because its boundary is explicit.  At a
nonzero-defect critical point the Hessian contains a residual correction.  A
derived equivalence is not automatically isometric.  A global cohesive
operator does not guarantee a uniform fiberwise Green family.  A positive
repair functional is not a signed action.  A kernel is not automatically a
quantum propagator.  A descended basin map is not automatically unique
actualization.

For q79, the cohesive Hartshorne--Serre benchmark now realizes the structural
chain exactly after a Hermitian choice.  The next leap is no longer to guess
another operator.  It is to select the physical endpoint, metric, cyclic or
BV action, Lorentzian domains, and continuum-to-finite intertwiner from one
source.  If that is achieved, much of the downstream operator language will
already be waiting as theorem rather than assumption.
