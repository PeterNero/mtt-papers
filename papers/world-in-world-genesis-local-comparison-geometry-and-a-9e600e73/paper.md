# How to Read World-in-World Geometry

The closest standard mathematical picture is the deformation gradient in continuum mechanics. A three-dimensional body can carry a $`3\times3`$ linear map comparing two local frames without becoming nine-dimensional. The nine numbers describe how one three-dimensional fiber is compared with another at the same base point. Polar decomposition then separates a local rotation from a positive strain.

World-in-world geometry uses this comparison structure as a candidate pre-projection language. The local linear algebra is exact. Its proposed relation to a four-dimensional Lorentzian base and six-dimensional q79 internal carrier is a separate globalization problem.

## A concrete strain split

For example, consider the symmetric matrix
``` math
S=

 5&6&2
 \end{pmatrix}.
```
Its trace is $`3`$, so the scalar part is
``` math
P_1S=I_3.
```
Relative to the displayed ordered frame, the traceless diagonal and off-diagonal parts are

They add back to $`S`$ and are mutually orthogonal in the Frobenius inner product. The spaces from which these pieces are drawn have dimensions $`1`$, $`2`$, and $`3`$. The numerical entries are not those dimensions, and the three subspaces are not three separate manifolds.

# Physical emergence gates

The original narrative can now be expressed as separate targets.

## Time

A noncompact parameter, physical propagator, and arrow-producing condition are required. Bookkeeping order or phase winding alone is insufficient.

## Gravity

A Lorentzian metric or tetrad, connection, action or equations, constraints, and a stress tensor are required. The local polar factor $`R`$ does not derive the Einstein–Hilbert action.

## Matter

A spin bundle, finite representation data, Dirac operator, chirality and real structure, and a particle/observable interpretation are required. The local $`1+2+3`$ strain split alone does not select Standard Model representations.

## Quantization

A complex state space, observable algebra, dynamics, probability functional, and gauge and constraint treatment are required. Discrete topology or a compact resolvent can produce discrete spectra but not the Born rule by itself.

## Mass and scalar relaxation

Mass requires a canonically normalized pole or dispersion relation. A Higgs mode requires a selected scalar representation and couplings. Positivity of a six-dimensional strain Hessian does not imply a unique radial Higgs.
