\begin{abstract}
This paper examines a concrete MTT proposal: localized persistent structures
may be represented as coherence basins, while their effective motion may be
described by capacity-dependent forces.  The basin part is viable only after a
map from effective state space to spatial observables is supplied; distinct
basins need not have distinct spatial densities.  The force part requires still
more structure.  A gradient of a diagnostic admissibility margin is not a force
until a selected action, Hamiltonian, stress law, or constitutive equation
couples that margin to motion.  We derive the exact centroid balance law for a
localized density and show how Newtonian and Lorentz equations follow
conditionally from a declared effective action.  The integrated basin weight
equals inertial mass only when the kinetic term is normalized accordingly.
Gauge charge conservation requires a symmetry or transported representation
label, not basin persistence alone.  Confinement follows from a capacity
interpretation only after a coercive separation energy is proved; an area law
does not follow from qualitative ``strain.''  The result is a rigorous
effective-particle and force-encoding framework.  It preserves coherence basins
as a useful model proposal while leaving the selected MTT action, constitutive
map, physical masses, interaction laws, and gravitational coupling open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The proposal and its logical layers}

The intuitive proposal is attractive.  A physical particle is not observed as
an abstract point with no internal structure; it is recognized through
persistence, localization, charges, and reproducible responses.  A stable
region of an effective state space can model persistence.  A localized density
can model position.  An internal representation can model charge.  A selected
action can model response to fields.  Taken together, these ingredients form a
credible effective-particle record.

They do not follow from one another automatically.  In particular:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a basin in state space is not yet a region of physical space;
\item a spatial density is not yet a mass density;
\item a scalar margin is not yet an energy;
\item a gradient is not yet a force;
\item a persistent label is not automatically a conserved Noether charge;
\item a growing diagnostic cost is not yet confinement or a Wilson-loop area
law.
\end{enumerate}

The purpose of this revision is to keep these types separate and then prove the
bridges that can actually be proved.  The final result is conditional, but it
is operational: every missing physical statement is attached to a specific
source row rather than absorbed into the word ``coherence.''

\subsection{Status language}

\begin{definition}[Encoding, dynamics, and selection]\label{def:status}
An \emph{encoding} is a typed representation of one effective object by
another.  A \emph{dynamical model} supplies an action, generator, balance law,
or constitutive evolution.  A \emph{selected MTT derivation} additionally
proves that those data descend from one accepted upper source without fitting
the target behavior.
\end{definition}

This paper closes several encoding-level and conditional dynamical statements.
It does not claim the final selected derivation.

\section{From an effective basin to a spatial particle}

\subsection{Invariant basins}

Let $\cY$ be an effective state space on a declared time slab, with evolution
$\Phi_{t,s}$ wherever that evolution is defined.

\begin{definition}[Stable coherence basin]\label{def:basin}
A set $\cB_\alpha\subset\cY$ is a stable coherence basin on $[t_0,t_1]$ if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item it is invariant on the slab:
$\Phi_{t,s}(\cB_\alpha)\subseteq\cB_\alpha$;
\item nearby allowed states are attracted or remain controlled according to a
specified stability estimate;
\item the basin remains separated from competing basins by a declared
positive margin on the controlled domain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

This definition captures persistence in state space.  It says nothing yet
about spatial localization, spin, statistics, energy, or particle detection.

\subsection{The observation map}

Let $\Sigma$ be a physical or effective spatial slice.  A spatial
representation requires a map
\[
 \cO_t:\cY\longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_+(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{J}(\Sigma),
\]
where $\mathcal{M}_+(\Sigma)$ is a class of positive finite measures or
densities and $\mathcal{J}(\Sigma)$ is a class of currents.  Write
\[
 \cO_t(y)=(\rho_y(\,\cdot\,,t),j_y(\,\cdot\,,t)).
\]
The map, its regularity, and its physical meaning are additional data.

\begin{proposition}[Spatial identity does not follow from basin identity]
\label{prop:noninjective}
Distinct invariant basins can have identical spatial density histories.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\cY=\mathcal{D}\times\{-1,+1\}$, let the evolution preserve the second
coordinate, and let
\[
 \cB_\pm=\mathcal{D}\times\{\pm1\}.
\]
Define $\cO_t(\rho,\sigma)=(\rho,j_\rho)$ independently of $\sigma$.  Then
$\cB_+$ and $\cB_-$ are distinct invariant basins but have the same image
under $\cO_t$.  Thus the spatial observation map need not be injective.
\end{proof}

The proposition withdraws the earlier claim that an effective density
uniquely tracks basin identity.  Hidden spin, flavor, gauge, or topological
labels may be invisible to a chosen density observable.  A complete particle
record must carry them separately.

\begin{definition}[Effective particle record]\label{def:particle-record}
An effective particle record on a slab is
\[
 \mathfrak{P}_\alpha
 =
 (\cB_\alpha,\cO,\rho_\alpha,j_\alpha,
  \Pi_\alpha,b_\alpha,S_{\mathrm{eff}},
  \mathcal{R}_\alpha,\varepsilon_\alpha),
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\cB_\alpha$ is a stable basin;
\item $(\rho_\alpha,j_\alpha)$ is its selected spatial image;
\item $\Pi_\alpha$ is a momentum-flux or stress tensor;
\item $b_\alpha$ is a body-force density or source term;
\item $S_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is an effective action, or a declared constitutive
replacement sufficient to determine $\Pi_\alpha$ and $b_\alpha$;
\item $\mathcal{R}_\alpha$ contains internal representation and charge data;
\item $\varepsilon_\alpha$ records approximation and slab errors.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A basin is therefore the persistence component of a particle model, not the
entire particle theorem.

\section{Exact centroid mechanics}

\subsection{Balance assumptions}

Assume $\rho\geq0$, $j=\rho v$, and sufficient integrability on a fixed region
$\Omega\subset\Sigma$.  Define
\[
 M(t)=\int_\Omega \rho\,\dd x,\qquad
 X^i(t)=\frac{1}{M(t)}\int_\Omega x^i\rho\,\dd x,\qquad
 P^i(t)=\int_\Omega \rho v^i\,\dd x.
\]
Suppose
\begin{align}
 \partial_t\rho+\partial_j(\rho v^j)&=0,
 \label{eq:continuity}\\
 \partial_t(\rho v^i)+\partial_j\Pi^{ij}&=b^i.
 \label{eq:momentum}
\end{align}
These are dynamical or constitutive assumptions.  A smooth density
representation alone does not imply them.

\begin{theorem}[Exact centroid balance]\label{thm:centroid}
If the mass flux through $\partial\Omega$ vanishes and $M>0$, then $M$ is
constant and
\begin{align}
 M\dot X^i&=P^i,\label{eq:first-moment}\\
 M\ddot X^i
 &=\int_\Omega b^i\,\dd x
   -\int_{\partial\Omega}\Pi^{ij}n_j\,\dd S.
 \label{eq:centroid}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrating \cref{eq:continuity} gives
$\dot M=-\int_{\partial\Omega}\rho v\cdot n\,\dd S=0$.
Multiplying \cref{eq:continuity} by $x^i$, integrating by parts, and using the
same boundary condition gives \cref{eq:first-moment}.  Integrating
\cref{eq:momentum} and applying the divergence theorem gives
\[
 \dot P^i
 =\int_\Omega b^i\,\dd x
  -\int_{\partial\Omega}\Pi^{ij}n_j\,\dd S.
\]
Differentiating \cref{eq:first-moment} completes the proof.
\end{proof}

This theorem is stronger and clearer than the former narrow-basin argument.
It identifies the force on the centroid as the integrated body source plus
the boundary traction.  The equation does not say where those terms come
from.

\begin{corollary}[Controlled point-particle approximation]
\label{cor:narrow}
Suppose the boundary traction vanishes and
$b^i(x,t)=\rho(x,t)a^i(x,t)$, with $a(\,\cdot\,,t)$ Lipschitz on the basin
support with constant $L_a(t)$.  Then
\[
 \left|
 M\ddot X^i-Ma^i(X,t)
 \right|
 \leq
 L_a(t)\int_\Omega |x-X|\,\rho(x,t)\,\dd x.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Subtract $Ma^i(X,t)$ from the volume term in
\cref{eq:centroid} and apply the Lipschitz bound.
\end{proof}

Newtonian form is therefore a controlled localized limit of an already
specified momentum law.  It is not generated by localization alone.

\section{Why a capacity gradient is not yet a force}

Let $C:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}$ be a normalized diagnostic margin.  Its zero set
may mark loss of admissibility, and its sign may rank distance from a chosen
boundary.  Neither fact supplies dimensions of energy or a coupling to
motion.

\begin{proposition}[Reparameterization obstruction]\label{prop:reparam}
Let $\phi$ be strictly increasing with $\phi(0)=0$.  Then $C$ and
$\widetilde C=\phi\circ C$ have the same sign and zero set, but
\[
 \nabla\widetilde C=\phi'(C)\nabla C.
\]
Consequently a force rule $F=-\nabla C$ is not invariant under admissibility-
preserving reparameterization of the diagnostic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity preserves the sign and zero set.  The gradient identity is the
chain rule.  Unless $\phi'(C)=1$, the proposed force changes.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is decisive because capacity diagnostics often admit
equivalent monotone normalizations.  A physical force can still depend on
capacity, but the theory must select both a representative and a coupling law.

\begin{definition}[Capacity-to-dynamics promotion]\label{def:promotion}
A capacity-to-dynamics promotion consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a fixed normalized capacity representative $C$;
\item an effective action, Hamiltonian, stress law, or mobility law;
\item a dimensionful constitutive map, for example $V_C=G(C)$;
\item boundary conditions and a solution concept;
\item a source theorem or calibration rule for every coefficient;
\item an error budget and declared domain of validity.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

With $V_C=G(C)$ selected, the conservative capacity contribution becomes
\[
 F_C=-\nabla V_C=-G'(C)\nabla C.
\]
Under $C\mapsto\phi(C)$, the same physical potential is preserved only if the
constitutive function transforms to $G\circ\phi^{-1}$.  The invariant object
is the selected potential, not the bare diagnostic gradient.

\section{Conditional Newton and Lorentz equations}

\subsection{A declared effective action}

Consider one localized basin with centroid $X(t)$ on a Riemannian spatial
chart, inertial coefficient $m_I>0$, scalar potential $\Phi$, connection
$A_i$, representation label $q$, and capacity potential $V_C$.  Take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lagrangian}
 L_{\mathrm{eff}}
 =
 \frac12m_I g_{ij}(X)\dot X^i\dot X^j
 +qA_i(X,t)\dot X^i
 -q\Phi(X,t)-V_C(X,t).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional force law]\label{thm:force}
The Euler--Lagrange equations of \cref{eq:lagrangian} give
\[
 m_I\frac{D\dot X^i}{\dd t}
 =
 q\bigl(E^i+F^i{}_j\dot X^j\bigr)
 -\nabla^iV_C,
\]
where
\[
 F_{ij}=\partial_iA_j-\partial_jA_i,
 \qquad
 E_i=-\partial_i\Phi-\partial_tA_i.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $\partial L/\partial\dot X^i$, subtract
$\partial L/\partial X^i$, and collect the Levi--Civita terms into the
covariant acceleration.  The antisymmetric derivative of $A$ gives $F_{ij}$,
the time derivative gives $E_i$, and the remaining scalar term is
$-\nabla_iV_C$.
\end{proof}

The theorem shows exactly what survives from the earlier argument.  The
Lorentz force follows from gauge-covariant minimal coupling in the effective
action.  If MTT derives that action and its $U(1)$ connection from the same
source, the result becomes an MTT force law.  Phase coherence by itself does
not select the kinetic term, charge, connection, or mass.

\subsection{Three distinct notions of mass}

The previous paper used
\[
 M_\rho=\int\rho\,\dd x
\]
as both normalization and mass.  Three quantities must instead be separated:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M_\rho$, the conserved weight of the chosen density;
\item $m_I$, the coefficient of the kinetic term and hence inertial response;
\item $m_{\mathrm{spec}}$, a rest-energy or spectral mass extracted from a
physical operator.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\begin{proposition}[Mass-identification condition]\label{prop:mass}
The equality $M_\rho=m_I$ is a convention or a constitutive theorem, not a
consequence of the continuity equation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any constant $c>0$, replacing $\rho$ by $c\rho$ leaves the normalized
centroid $X$ unchanged and preserves the homogeneous continuity equation, but
changes $M_\rho$ by $c$.  The kinetic coefficient $m_I$ in
\cref{eq:lagrangian} is unchanged unless a separate rule ties it to $\rho$.
\end{proof}

A selected theory may prove $M_\rho=m_I=m_{\mathrm{spec}}$ after fixing
normalization.  Until then, ``mass is integrated coherence weight'' is only a
model ansatz.

\section{Charge and particle number}

\subsection{Charge conservation}

A representation label can remain constant under parallel transport, but
that statement requires a fixed representation bundle and compatible
connection.  In an action formulation, continuous charge conservation is
normally tied to a symmetry and a current identity.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional charge conservation]\label{thm:charge}
Assume an effective action invariant under a $U(1)$ gauge symmetry, a
well-defined gauge current $J^\mu$, and equations of motion for which the
Noether identity is valid.  Then
\[
 \nabla_\mu J^\mu=0.
\]
If boundary flux vanishes, the total charge on a spatial slice is conserved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard Noether identity for the declared gauge symmetry,
followed by integration of the continuity equation.
\end{proof}

Basin persistence alone is insufficient.  A persistent basin may contain a
nonconserved internal coordinate, while a conserved charge may be shared
across several changing quasiparticle basins.

\subsection{Particle number}

Basin merge, split, creation, or dissolution are possible descriptions of
particle-number change, but possibility is not dynamics.  A physical claim
requires a transition kernel, field equation, interaction vertex, or
singular-limit rule that determines rates and preserves the required charges.
Version 3 therefore retains basin recombination as an interpretation and
withdraws automatic particle creation or annihilation from barrier crossing.

\section{Confinement requires a coercive energy law}

Qualitative non-Abelian ``strain'' does not prove confinement.  A useful
capacity model must produce a gauge-invariant energy or action whose cost
grows with separation.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional separation bound]\label{thm:confinement}
Let $R\geq0$ be a gauge-invariant separation coordinate for a nonsinglet
configuration.  If a selected effective energy satisfies
\[
 V_{\mathrm{sep}}(R)\geq\sigma R-c,
 \qquad \sigma>0,
\]
then every configuration in the energy sublevel
$V_{\mathrm{sep}}\leq E$ obeys
\[
 R\leq\frac{E+c}{\sigma}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Combine the lower bound with $V_{\mathrm{sep}}(R)\leq E$ and solve for $R$.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine confinement-type statement for finite-energy sublevels.  To
connect it to Yang--Mills confinement one still needs the physical gauge
field, Hilbert space or path-integral measure, static-source limit,
renormalization, and a Wilson-loop or equivalent observable.  A Wilson-loop
area law cannot be inferred merely from a capacity bottleneck.  Capacity may
help generate $V_{\mathrm{sep}}$, but the coercive estimate is the theorem that
must be derived.

\section{Gravity and universality}

The universality of gravity does not follow from the fact that every basin has
a capacity record.  A gravitational model needs Lorentzian geometry, a
stress-energy source, field equations or an action, and a rule connecting the
particle record to geodesic or forced motion.

At the effective level one may append
\[
 S_{\mathrm{grav}}[g]
 +S_{\mathrm{matter}}[g,\Psi]
\]
and derive both the gravitational field equation and covariant matter
response.  Equality of inertial and gravitational mass, universal free fall,
and backreaction then become testable properties of the common action.  They
are not consequences of a scalar capacity gradient.

The basin proposal remains compatible with gravity: a localized solution of a
matter theory may define a quasiparticle whose centroid follows a controlled
worldline limit.  The missing step is the same-source action and limit theorem.

\section{The MTT source contract}

\begin{definition}[Selected basin-particle source contract]
\label{def:source}
A selected MTT basin-particle derivation must provide:
\begin{enumerate}
\item one upper state complex and selected action or generator;
\item a coherent projector and a proved stable-basin sector;
\item a spatial observation map with localization and approximation bounds;
\item a momentum or stress balance derived from the same action;
\item kinetic normalization and a source-derived mass observable;
\item gauge representation, connection, current, and charge identity;
\item any capacity-to-potential or capacity-to-stress constitutive map;
\item transition dynamics for merge, split, creation, and annihilation;
\item for confinement, a gauge-invariant coercive energy or area-law theorem;
\item for gravity, a common metric coupling and controlled worldline limit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional basin-particle encoding]\label{thm:encoding}
If the rows of Definition~\ref{def:source} are supplied by one selected upper
MTT object and commute with projection to the effective record, then each
stable localized basin defines a controlled effective particle model.  Its
centroid obeys \cref{thm:centroid}; its force law is determined by the
projected action or constitutive stress; and its masses and charges have the
status proved by their source rows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The basin and observation rows construct
Definition~\ref{def:particle-record}.  The common action supplies the balance
law and internal symmetry.  The centroid theorem transfers the distributed
dynamics to a worldline equation with an explicit localization error.
Because the remaining quantities are carried by the same commuting source
maps, no force, mass, or charge is introduced by relabeling a diagnostic.
\end{proof}

Current MTT work has not instantiated the full contract.  In particular, the
live upper-action blocker remains open.  The theorem is therefore a precise
conditional target, not a claim that MTT has already replaced quantum field
theory's particle and interaction structure.

\section{What the basin picture achieves}

The corrected proposal still has real value.
\begin{enumerate}
\item It gives a common effective vocabulary for persistence, localization,
internal labels, and transition events.
\item It replaces an ideal point particle by a controlled localized record,
which is often the natural object in soliton, quasiparticle, and wave-packet
limits.
\item It supplies an exact route from distributed balance laws to centroid
mechanics.
\item It identifies how a capacity diagnostic may enter physics without
confusing admissibility with energy: through a selected potential, action, or
stress law.
\item It turns broad claims about mass, charge, confinement, and gravity into
separate falsifiable source obligations.
\end{enumerate}

The proposal is not a proof that all elementary particles are classical
attractors.  Fermionic statistics, spin, relativistic localization, quantum
superselection, scattering, and particle creation require the appropriate
quantum field-theoretic carrier.  A basin may encode a sector or stable
excitation in that carrier; it need not be a literal classical region in
three-space.

\section{Falsifiability and next steps}

A concrete basin-particle model should publish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the state space, evolution, basin, and stability estimate;
\item the spatial observation map and localization error;
\item the action or constitutive laws producing $\Pi$ and $b$;
\item independent definitions of density weight, inertial mass, spectral
mass, and gravitational coupling;
\item the gauge bundle, current, and conservation proof;
\item the capacity normalization and selected coupling function;
\item held-out predictions for motion, scattering, spectra, or transitions.
\end{enumerate}

The immediate MTT frontier is to derive one nontrivial entry of this list from
the selected upper action.  A useful first target would be a localized mode of
the selected operator whose effective kinetic term, mass coefficient, and one
gauge coupling are all obtained by the same projection.  That would establish
more than a visual resemblance to a particle: it would connect persistence,
inertia, and interaction in one source-preserving calculation.

\section{Conclusion}

Stable coherence basins remain plausible effective encodings of persistent
localized excitations.  The mathematical content begins only after the basin
is connected to spatial observables and dynamics.  Under declared continuity
and momentum balances, the exact centroid equation follows.  Under a declared
gauge-covariant action, Newtonian and Lorentz force laws follow.  Under a
coercive separation energy, a confinement-type finite-energy bound follows.

None of these laws follows from a diagnostic capacity gradient alone.
Version 3 therefore replaces the claim that particles and forces have already
been derived with a more useful result: a complete typed particle record,
exact centroid mechanics, conditional force theorems, no-go results for
untyped identifications, and a sharply stated same-source action target.
