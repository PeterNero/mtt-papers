\title{Finite-Memory Noise and the White-Noise/Markov Limit\\
\large Exact Colored-OU Convergence and Its MTT Scope}

\author{Peter Nero}

\begin{abstract}
A delta-correlated covariance is the distributional limit of many
finite-memory kernels, but covariance convergence alone does not imply
convergence of stochastic processes, Gaussianity, or the Markov property. We
separate these statements and prove an exact process-level model. Nonnegative
approximate-identity kernels with total mass \(2D\) converge to
\(2D\delta_0\) as distributions. A valid stationary covariance must
additionally be of positive type. For the explicit stationary colored
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck source
\[
d\xi_\tau=-\tau^{-1}\xi_\tau\,dt
 +\sqrt{2D}\,\tau^{-1}dW_t,
\]
the integrated disturbance
\(B_\tau(t)=\int_0^t\xi_\tau(s)\,ds\) converges weakly in
\(C([0,T])\) to \(\sqrt{2D}\,W\). The damped response therefore converges to
the white-noise Ornstein--Uhlenbeck equation. Its exact finite-memory
stationary variance is
\[
\Var(a_\tau)=\frac{D}{\gamma(1+\gamma\tau)},
\]
which tends to \(D/\gamma\). This supplies a rigorous effective Markov limit,
not a derivation of fundamental randomness. A general MTT source theorem
would still need a selected preparation measure or invariant state, positivity
of the covariance, mixing or a functional limit theorem, closure of the
resolved variables, and a derivation of the finite-memory coefficients from
the physical carrier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Five different limits}

The notation
\[
\E[\xi(t)\xi(t')]=2D\,\delta(t-t')
\]
is compact, but it can conceal several independent claims:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a family of ordinary kernels converges to a Dirac distribution;
\item those kernels are valid covariances of actual processes;
\item the finite-dimensional laws of those processes converge;
\item the full paths converge in a specified topology; and
\item the limiting resolved dynamics is Markov.
\end{enumerate}

Only the first statement follows from the approximate-identity calculation.
The other four require extra hypotheses. This paper proves all five for one
explicit Gaussian colored-OU model and keeps the general case conditional.

\midrule
Kernel & positivity as a measure, normalization, concentration\\
Covariance & positive type or nonnegative spectral measure\\
Finite-dimensional law & distributional information beyond second moments,
unless Gaussian\\
Path law & tightness plus finite-dimensional convergence\\
Markov limit & closure of the limiting state and transition law\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\section{Approximate identities}

Let \(C_\tau\in L^1(\mathbb{R})\), with \(\tau>0\), model a memory kernel.

\begin{theorem}[Delta-correlation limit]
\label{thm:delta}
Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(C_\tau(t)\geq0\);
\item \(\int_{\mathbb R}C_\tau(t)\,dt=2D\); and
\item for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}C_\tau(t)\,dt\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{as }\tau\downarrow0.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Then \(C_\tau\to2D\delta_0\) in the sense of distributions. Equivalently,
for every \(f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R})\),
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}C_\tau(t)f(t)\,dt\longrightarrow2Df(0).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\int C_\tau(t)f(t)\,dt-2Df(0)
=\int C_\tau(t)\bigl(f(t)-f(0)\bigr)\,dt.
\]
For a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), the contribution from
\(|t|<\varepsilon\) is bounded by
\[
2D\sup_{|t|<\varepsilon}|f(t)-f(0)|.
\]
The complementary contribution is bounded by
\[
2\|f\|_\infty
\int_{|t|>\varepsilon}C_\tau(t)\,dt.
\]
First take \(\tau\downarrow0\), then
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).
\end{proof}

Two standard examples are
\begin{align}
C_\tau^{\mathrm{exp}}(t)
&=\frac{D}{\tau}e^{-|t|/\tau},
\label{eq:exp-cov}\\
C_\tau^{\mathrm{gau}}(t)
&=\frac{2D}{\sqrt{2\pi}\tau}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\tau^2}\right).
\label{eq:gauss-cov}
\end{align}
Both have total mass \(2D\), and both concentrate at the origin.

\section{A kernel is not automatically a covariance}

Pointwise nonnegativity is enough for
Theorem~\ref{thm:delta}, but not enough for a stationary covariance.

\begin{definition}[Positive-type covariance]
An even function \(C:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) is of positive type if,
for every finite set \(t_1,\ldots,t_n\) and
\(z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb{C}\),
\[
\sum_{j,k=1}^n
z_j\overline{z_k}\,C(t_j-t_k)\geq0.
\]
\end{definition}

Every covariance of a second-order stationary process has this property,
because the displayed sum equals
\[
\E\left|
\sum_j z_j\xi(t_j)
\right|^2.
\]
Conversely, under the usual continuity assumptions, Bochner's theorem
represents a positive-type function as the Fourier transform of a finite
nonnegative spectral measure.

The two kernels \eqref{eq:exp-cov}--\eqref{eq:gauss-cov} are valid:
\[
\widehat C_\tau^{\mathrm{exp}}(\omega)
=\frac{2D}{1+\omega^2\tau^2}\geq0,
\qquad
\widehat C_\tau^{\mathrm{gau}}(\omega)
=2D e^{-\omega^2\tau^2/2}\geq0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Covariance does not determine process law]
\label{prop:covariance}
Two processes can have identical means and covariances but different laws.
Consequently, covariance convergence alone does not imply process convergence
or Gaussian white noise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) and let \(R\) be Rademacher, with values
\(\pm1\) of equal probability. The constant processes
\(X_t=Z\) and \(Y_t=R\) both have mean zero and covariance one at every
pair of times. Their one-time distributions are different.
\end{proof}

Gaussianity removes this particular ambiguity because a Gaussian process is
determined by its mean and covariance. The exact model below uses that fact
and separately proves path tightness.

\section{An exact finite-memory source}

Fix \(D>0\). For every \(\tau>0\), let \(\xi_\tau\) be the stationary
solution of
\begin{equation}
d\xi_\tau(t)
=-\frac{1}{\tau}\xi_\tau(t)\,dt
+\frac{\sqrt{2D}}{\tau}\,dW_t,
\label{eq:colored-ou}
\end{equation}
initialized with
\(\xi_\tau(0)\sim N(0,D/\tau)\), independent of the future Wiener
increments. Then
\begin{equation}
\E[\xi_\tau(t)\xi_\tau(s)]
=\frac{D}{\tau}e^{-|t-s|/\tau}.
\label{eq:colored-cov}
\end{equation}
The source is Markov and Gaussian on the augmented variable
\(\xi_\tau\), has finite memory time \(\tau\), and has diverging pointwise
variance \(D/\tau\). Its integrated power remains finite.

Define
\[
B_\tau(t)=\int_0^t\xi_\tau(u)\,du.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Colored noise converges to Brownian forcing]
\label{thm:functional}
For every finite \(T>0\),
\[
B_\tau\Rightarrow\sqrt{2D}\,W
\quad\text{in }C([0,T])
\]
as \(\tau\downarrow0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each \(B_\tau\) is a centered continuous Gaussian process. For
\(0\leq s\leq t\), direct integration of
\eqref{eq:colored-cov} gives
\begin{align}
\Cov(B_\tau(t),B_\tau(s))
={}&2Ds
-D\tau(1-e^{-s/\tau})\notag\\
&-D\tau
\left(e^{-(t-s)/\tau}-e^{-t/\tau}\right).
\label{eq:integrated-cov}
\end{align}
This converges to \(2D\min(s,t)\), the covariance of
\(\sqrt{2D}\,W\). Gaussianity therefore gives convergence of every
finite-dimensional distribution.

For \(0\leq s\leq t\leq T\),
\[
\E|B_\tau(t)-B_\tau(s)|^2
=2D(t-s)-2D\tau(1-e^{-(t-s)/\tau})
\leq2D|t-s|.
\]
The increment is Gaussian, so
\[
\E|B_\tau(t)-B_\tau(s)|^4
=3\left(\E|B_\tau(t)-B_\tau(s)|^2\right)^2
\leq12D^2|t-s|^2.
\]
The Kolmogorov tightness criterion gives tightness in \(C([0,T])\).
Together with finite-dimensional convergence, this proves the claim.
\end{proof}

This theorem is stronger than
\[
C_\tau\to2D\delta_0.
\]
It establishes convergence of complete continuous paths for an explicit
finite-memory process.

\section{The damped response and its exact floor}

Let \(a_\tau\) solve the ordinary random differential equation
\begin{equation}
\dot a_\tau(t)
=-\gamma a_\tau(t)+\xi_\tau(t),
\qquad \gamma>0.
\label{eq:colored-response}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
a_\tau(t)
=a_0-\gamma\int_0^t a_\tau(s)\,ds+B_\tau(t).
\]

\begin{corollary}[White-noise OU limit]
\label{cor:ou-limit}
On every finite interval,
\[
a_\tau\Rightarrow a
\quad\text{in }C([0,T]),
\]
where
\begin{equation}
da(t)=-\gamma a(t)\,dt+\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t.
\label{eq:white-ou}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a continuous driver \(b\), the integral equation
\[
x(t)=a_0-\gamma\int_0^t x(s)\,ds+b(t)
\]
has a unique solution, and the solution map is continuous in the uniform
norm by Gronwall's inequality. Apply the continuous mapping theorem to
Theorem~\ref{thm:functional}.
\end{proof}

The finite-memory stationary variance can also be computed exactly without
taking the limit.

\begin{theorem}[Exact colored-OU response variance]
\label{thm:finite-floor}
For the stationary joint system
\eqref{eq:colored-ou}--\eqref{eq:colored-response},
\[
\Var(\xi_\tau)=\frac{D}{\tau},
\qquad
\Cov(a_\tau,\xi_\tau)=\frac{D}{1+\gamma\tau},
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\Var(a_\tau)
=\frac{D}{\gamma(1+\gamma\tau)}.
\label{eq:finite-variance}
\end{equation}
Hence
\[
\Var(a_\tau)\longrightarrow\frac{D}{\gamma},
\]
the stationary variance of equation \eqref{eq:white-ou}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Stationarity of \(\xi_\tau\) gives
\(\Var(\xi_\tau)=D/\tau\). Differentiating the stationary second
moments, or equivalently solving the \(2\times2\) Lyapunov equation,
gives
\[
0=-(\gamma+\tau^{-1})\Cov(a_\tau,\xi_\tau)
+\frac{D}{\tau},
\]
and
\[
0=-2\gamma\Var(a_\tau)
+2\Cov(a_\tau,\xi_\tau).
\]
Solving these equations yields the result.
\end{proof}

The quantity in \eqref{eq:finite-variance} is an effective response width.
It is not, without further operator and normalization theorems, the
Heisenberg uncertainty relation.

\section{What is genuinely Markov}

At finite \(\tau\), the augmented pair
\[
(a_\tau,\xi_\tau)
\]
is Markov. The resolved coordinate \(a_\tau\) alone is not closed: its
instantaneous derivative depends on the hidden memory coordinate
\(\xi_\tau\). In the limit, Corollary~\ref{cor:ou-limit} yields the closed
Markov diffusion \(a\).

Thus a correct statement for this model is
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite Markov augmentation}
\longrightarrow
\text{nonclosed resolved memory}
\longrightarrow
\text{closed Markov limit}.
}
\]

For a general colored disturbance, none of these arrows is automatic.
One needs:
\begin{itemize}
\item a normalized invariant law or preparation ensemble;
\item a valid covariance or transition kernel;
\item mixing, martingale, or other functional-limit hypotheses;
\item tightness in a declared path topology;
\item and closure of the limiting resolved variables.
\end{itemize}

A deterministic upstream flow can satisfy a functional central limit theorem
under suitable invariant-measure and mixing assumptions. That is a
deterministic source of effective Brownian behavior, but the invariant measure
and hypotheses remain part of the theorem. Determinism alone does not imply a
diffusive limit.

\section{Nonlinear limits and stochastic calculus}

For the additive equation \eqref{eq:white-ou}, Ito and Stratonovich forms
coincide. For nonlinear multiplicative noise, the limiting calculus can depend
on how the finite-memory or smooth forcing is taken to zero memory. Classical
Wong--Zakai results show why smooth-noise approximations often produce a
Stratonovich correction under their stated hypotheses
\cite{WongZakai1965}.

Therefore a bare covariance
\[
2D\delta(t-t')
\]
does not encode the eliminated approximation scheme. An MTT derivation would
have to retain enough source geometry to decide the limiting drift correction,
not choose Ito or Stratonovich by convention after the fact.

\section{MTT interpretation and status}

Projection can hide distinctions among upper configurations. If an ensemble
or invariant state is supplied, those hidden distinctions can induce an
effective stochastic kernel on the reduced variables. Three cautions are
essential:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Noninjective projection does not create a probability measure.
\item A deterministic upper state with fully known initial data remains
deterministic unless an ensemble, coarse-graining, or limit theorem is added.
\item Finite memory can survive projection; white noise is a further scaling
limit, not the definition of projected stochasticity.
\end{enumerate}

The exact results in this paper establish a mathematically complete effective
model:
\[
\text{colored OU source}
\longrightarrow
\text{Brownian path limit}
\longrightarrow
\text{white-noise OU response}.
\]
They do not yet establish that the selected q79 or other physical MTT carrier
emits equation \eqref{eq:colored-ou}.

\section{Completion program}

To promote the effective construction into an MTT source theorem:
\begin{enumerate}
\item select an upper state or preparation measure from the physical branch;
\item derive a centered finite-memory observable \(\xi_\tau\);
\item prove positivity and normalization of its covariance;
\item prove quantitative mixing or a martingale approximation;
\item establish a functional limit with a computable error bound;
\item prove closure of the intended resolved state;
\item derive \(D\), \(\tau\), and \(\gamma\) from the same source; and
\item compare finite-memory corrections against an observable protocol.
\end{enumerate}

This route can falsify the white-noise approximation. If the selected source
has long memory, heavy tails, anomalous scaling, or no closed resolved state,
the correct effective theory will not be the Markov OU model.

\section{Conclusion}

White noise can be a rigorous zero-memory limit, but three levels must be kept
apart. Normalized kernels may converge to a Dirac distribution. Valid
covariances require positive type. Process and Markov convergence require
law-level and path-level control.

The stationary colored-OU source supplies all of that control in one exact
example. Its integrated forcing converges to Brownian motion in
\(C([0,T])\); its damped response converges to the standard OU diffusion; and
its finite-memory variance tends monotonically to the white-noise floor. This
shows precisely how finite memory can be compressed into delta correlation.

For MTT, the result is a reusable target, not a completed ontology. Projection
may motivate unresolved effective forcing, but the source measure, memory
kernel, mixing theorem, Markov closure, and coefficients still have to be
selected from geometry. The credible claim is therefore not that randomness
has been eliminated. It is that one exact route from finite-memory physics to
an effective white-noise model has been constructed, with every additional
MTT obligation visible.
