## Revision purpose

Version 3 preserved a useful intuition: an effective construction can be
summarized by its weakest remaining admissibility reserve. Its definition
was nevertheless too weak to support a quantitative or physical invariant.
It allowed any positive scalar with the same zero set and then inferred a
global inverse obstruction, irreversibility, gravity, time, entropy,
measurement, and undecidability from that scalar.

Version 4 retains this paper as the primary normalization and compression
paper for coherence capacity. It makes the full margin vector primary,
requires units, scales, metrics, norms, tolerances, and provenance, and
defines invariance only as controlled comparison within a capacity class.
It is a standalone explanatory paper, not a correction list.

## Claim-by-claim audit

### Any scalar with the correct zero set is sufficient

Version 3 claim:
Any positive scalar that vanishes precisely when controlled truncation fails
defines the same invariant resource.

Decision:
Withdraw quantitatively.

Reason:
The maps `C`, `C^3`, and `sqrt(C)` can share a zero set while having
different boundary rates, gradients, and comparison behavior.

Version 4 resolution:
Retain the full normalized reserve vector and define scalar representatives
only after scales or a metric are declared. Use capacity equivalence up to
explicit positive comparison constants.

### All MTT conditions fail simultaneously

Version 3 claim:
Different technical constructions realize the same margin and fail
simultaneously.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
Admissibility is the intersection of declared conditions. The bottleneck is
zero when at least one active row reaches its boundary. Other rows may
retain large reserves.

### Existence from compactness and continuity

Version 3 claim:
Compact admissible slabs and continuous spectral data automatically produce
the desired capacity functional.

Decision:
Replace.

Version 4 resolution:
The bottleneck exists once a finite sourced margin record and positive
normalizations are supplied. Metric clearance exists after a metric and an
open admissible set are supplied. Quantitative equivalence requires an
additional error-bound or transversality estimate.

### Capacity collapse implies no global section

Version 3 claim:
If capacity vanishes, the coherent projection has no global measurable
section.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Noninjectivity does not obstruct a right inverse. For an orthogonal
projection `P:H -> P H`, the inclusion `P H -> H` is a bounded measurable
section. What noninjectivity forbids is a left inverse recovering every
upper state.

Version 4 resolution:
State decoder existence, dynamic commutation, and information recovery as
separate questions. Capacity exhaustion alone decides none of them.

### Effective irreversibility and arrow of time

Version 3 claim:
Admissibility failure necessarily yields structural irreversibility and the
ordering of capacity-exhaustion events defines time's arrow.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
A zero crossing is a first exit from one declared certificate domain.
Irreversibility requires a lower semigroup, coarse-graining theorem, reset
law, stable records, or thermodynamic limit.

### Gravity and Newton's constant

Version 3 claim:
The unique two-derivative bookkeeping action is `C R`, and
`G_eff = 1/C`.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
The action is an additional constitutive choice. A varying coefficient of
`R` generally introduces scalar-tensor terms, and arbitrary capacity
reparameterization would arbitrarily change the claimed coupling.

Version 4 resolution:
No gravitational action or normalization is derived. A downstream theory
must source its action and coupling independently.

### Horizons, entropy, and measurement

Version 3 claim:
Capacity bottlenecks are horizons, imply area-scaling entropy, and force
measurement reset.

Decision:
Withdraw as general theorems.

Version 4 resolution:
Horizon claims require Lorentzian geometry and field equations; area laws
require state counting and a coefficient; measurement requires
system-apparatus dynamics, records, and an instrument. A reset is a new
hybrid-system rule.

### Probability and undecidability

Version 3 claim:
Capacity exhaustion supplies selection behavior and is undecidable as a
consistency requirement.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 4 resolution:
A boundary supplies neither a probability measure nor a universal-machine
embedding. Those are independent theorem obligations.
