\begin{abstract}
We show how quantum field theory arises as an effective statistical representation of
coherence basin ensembles, under the same admissibility, regularity, and coarse-graining
assumptions used elsewhere in the coherence-capacity framework.
 We show that
quantum fields arise as collective variables encoding statistics of
localized coherence basins, while quantum states correspond to ensemble
distributions over admissible basins. Correlation functions, operators,
and Hilbert-space structure emerge as bookkeeping devices for basin
statistics, rather than fundamental ontological entities. This
reconstruction explains the origin of operator algebras, superposition,
and probabilistic measurement without postulating fundamental wave
functions or fields.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Quantum field theory (QFT) is the most successful predictive framework in
physics, yet its conceptual foundations remain unsettled. Fields are
treated as fundamental objects, operators act on abstract Hilbert spaces,
and infinities are handled by renormalization procedures whose physical
interpretation is often opaque.

In the preceding papers we showed that particles and forces arise from
stable coherence basins and coherence-capacity transport. In this work we
take the next step: we show that quantum field theory itself emerges as a
\emph{statistical effective theory of coherence basin ensembles}.

The central claim is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{Quantum fields do not describe fundamental physical substances; they
encode statistical correlations among large ensembles of coherence
basins.}
\end{quote}

From this perspective, quantum superposition, operator algebras, and
correlation functions are not mysterious axioms but inevitable
bookkeeping tools. 
\paragraph{Claim discipline.}
All ``reconstruction'' statements in this paper are representation statements: they assert that
basin-ensemble statistics admit standard QFT encodings under stated regularity and linearized
assumptions. No claim is made that QFT emerges uniquely or globally outside admissible regimes.


\section{Framework and Assumptions}

We adopt the minimal axioms used previously.

\begin{description}
\item[Axiom A1.] Fundamental dynamics on a configuration space $X$ is
invertible.
\item[Axiom A2.] Observable physics arises via a noninjective projection
$P:X\to Y$.
\item[Axiom A3.] Effective descriptions are valid only within admissible
basins characterized by positive coherence capacity.
\item[Axiom A4.] Coherence basins are stable, contractive regions of
effective state space.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Scope note.}
In this paper, coherence capacity is used only as an admissibility label imported from prior
work; no independent capacity dynamics, transport, or flux assumptions are introduced here.
No assumption of fundamental quantum fields, Hilbert spaces, or operator
algebras is made.

\section{Coherence Basin Ensembles}

\subsection{Single-basin description}

A single coherence basin corresponds to what is ordinarily called a
particle. Its centroid follows classical equations of motion, as shown
previously.

However, experimental preparations rarely isolate a single basin. They
produce ensembles of admissible basins with varying internal structure
and trajectories.

\subsection{Ensembles as physical states}

\begin{definition}[Basin ensemble]
A basin ensemble is a normalized measure $\mu$ on the space of admissible
coherence basins.
\end{definition}
\paragraph{Measure-theoretic assumptions.}
The basin ensemble measure $\mu$ is assumed to be a normalized Borel measure on the space of
admissible coherence basins, equipped with the minimal $\sigma$-algebra required to define
expectation values of basin observables. No claim is made that this space admits a canonical
or unique measure; all results below are invariant under admissible reparameterizations of
$\mu$ that preserve observable statistics.

Physically, $\mu$ encodes the preparation conditions of an experiment.
Different microconfigurations projecting to the same effective
description correspond to different points in the ensemble.

\begin{definition}[Effective state]
An effective state is an equivalence class of basin ensembles yielding
the same observable statistics.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Preservation of basin identity under ensemble statistics]
    Within admissible regimes, ensemble statistics uniquely determine the identity of the
underlying coherence basin up to admissible equivalence. Distinct coherence basins cannot
yield identical ensemble statistics on overlapping admissible domains, except at
merge--split boundaries where basin identity ceases to be well-defined.
\end{lemma}



This replaces the notion of a fundamental wave function.

\section{Observables and Expectation Values}

\subsection{Observables as basin functionals}

An observable $\mathcal O$ is defined as a functional on coherence basins,
\[
\mathcal O : B_\alpha \mapsto \mathbb R.
\]

The expectation value of $\mathcal O$ in an ensemble $\mu$ is
\[
\langle \mathcal O \rangle_\mu
= \int \mathcal O(B)\, d\mu(B).
\]

This definition is purely classical at the ensemble level.

\subsection{Emergence of linearity}

Linearity arises because expectation values depend linearly on $\mu$.
Convex combinations of ensembles produce convex combinations of
expectation values.

This is the origin of the linear structure of quantum states.

\section{Hilbert Space as Statistical Bookkeeping}

\subsection{Representation theorem}

We now show that ensemble statistics admit a Hilbert-space
representation.

\begin{theorem}[Hilbert representation under ensemble regularity]
There exists a Hilbert space $\mathcal H$ and a mapping
\[
\mu \mapsto |\psi_\mu\rangle \in \mathcal H
\]
such that expectation values can be written as
\[
\langle \mathcal O \rangle_\mu
= \langle \psi_\mu | \hat{\mathcal O} | \psi_\mu \rangle
\]
for a suitable operator $\hat{\mathcal O}$ associated with $\mathcal O$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume the algebra of basin observables admits a $C^*$-algebra completion and that the
ensemble expectation $\langle \cdot \rangle_\mu$ defines a positive linear functional on this
algebra.
This follows from the Gelfand--Naimark--Segal construction applied to the
commutative algebra of basin observables, extended to include noncommuting
operators generated by incompatible basin partitions.
\end{proof}

Thus the Hilbert space is not fundamental; it is a representation of
ensemble statistics.

\section{Field Operators from Basin Statistics}

\subsection{Local observables}

Local observables correspond to functionals of basin density in spacetime
regions. Define the basin density operator
\[
\hat{\phi}(x)
\]
as the operator encoding fluctuations of basin density near $x$.

\subsection{Field interpretation}

\begin{theorem}[Emergent quantum fields in the linearized ensemble regime   ]
Quantum fields arise as operator-valued distributions encoding
correlations of basin ensembles.
\end{theorem}
This representation holds in regimes where basin-density fluctuations admit a linearized,
Gaussian description.

Field operators do not represent fundamental degrees of freedom but
statistical correlations among many coherence basins.

\section{Superposition and Interference}

Superposition arises because different basin ensembles may produce
identical marginal statistics but differ in higher-order correlations.
Interference reflects overlap of basin-support measures, not simultaneous
existence of incompatible classical realities.

\section{Discussion}

This reconstruction demystifies several features of quantum theory:

\begin{itemize}
\item Quantum states are statistical objects, not ontological waves.
\item Operators encode ensemble correlations.
\item Superposition reflects ensemble structure.
\item Measurement collapse corresponds to capacity-induced basin
selection.
\end{itemize}

Quantum field theory emerges as the optimal statistical language for
describing coherence basin ensembles.
\section{Propagators as Basin Correlation Kernels}

\subsection{Two-point correlations}

In quantum field theory, propagators encode correlations between field
values at different spacetime points. In the basin framework, these
correlations arise naturally from statistics of basin ensembles.

Let $\rho_B(x)$ denote the basin density associated with a coherence basin
$B$. For an ensemble $\mu$, define the two-point correlation function
\[
G(x,y)
:= \int \rho_B(x)\,\rho_B(y)\, d\mu(B)
- \int \rho_B(x)\,d\mu(B)\int \rho_B(y)\,d\mu(B).
\]

This quantity measures correlated support of coherence basins at $x$ and
$y$.

\begin{theorem}[Propagators as basin correlation kernels]
In the linearized ensemble regime, the two-point correlation function $G(x,y)$ admits a
representation equivalent to the quantum field propagator associated with the corresponding
effective field operator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the Hilbert representation of ensemble statistics, $G(x,y)$ is
represented by a state-dependent expectation value (e.g. vacuum in a chosen representation) of the time-ordered product
of field operators. Its functional form is determined by the linearized
dynamics of basin fluctuations.
\end{proof}

Thus propagators are not fundamental Green's functions but correlation
kernels encoding basin statistics.

\section{Path Integrals as Ensemble Generating Functionals}

\subsection{Generating functionals}

Consider the generating functional
\[
Z[J] := \int \exp\!\left( i \int J(x)\,\rho_B(x)\,dx \right) d\mu(B),
\]
where $J(x)$ is an external source.

\begin{theorem}[Path integrals as ensemble generating functionals]
The functional $Z[J]$ serves as an effective generating functional for correlation functions,
analogous to the quantum field theoretic path integral.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exponential weighting generates moments of basin density
distributions. In the Hilbert representation, this reproduces the
functional integral over field configurations weighted by the effective
action, which encodes basin statistics.
\end{proof}

The path integral therefore serves as a compact encoding of ensemble
statistics rather than a sum over ontological field histories.

\section{Renormalization as Capacity Coarse-Graining}

\subsection{Scale dependence}

Quantum field theories exhibit scale dependence and require
renormalization. In the present framework, this arises from coarse-graining
of coherence basins under limited capacity.

\begin{definition}[Capacity coarse-graining]
Capacity coarse-graining is the systematic elimination of basin features
that cannot be stably maintained under reduced coherence capacity at a
given scale.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Renormalization group flow}

\begin{theorem}[Renormalization from basin statistics, effective]
Renormalization group flow corresponds to evolution of effective ensemble
descriptions under capacity coarse-graining.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This correspondence holds at the level of effective descriptions under systematic
coarse-graining of basin ensembles.
As observational resolution decreases, fine basin distinctions are erased
by projection. Coupling constants flow because they encode statistical
properties of basin ensembles at different scales.
\end{proof}

This interpretation explains why renormalization is necessary and why
physical predictions remain finite despite formal divergences.

\section{Perturbation Theory and Feynman Diagrams}

\subsection{Perturbative expansion}

Perturbation theory arises when interactions between basins are weak.
Correlation functions can then be expanded in powers of interaction
strength.

\begin{theorem}[Diagrammatic expansion]
Feynman diagrams enumerate contributions to basin correlation functions
under perturbative expansion of ensemble statistics.
\end{theorem}

Vertices represent interaction-induced basin correlations; propagators
represent basin correlation kernels.

This explains why diagrammatic perturbation theory is effective and why
its combinatorial structure matches experimental observations.

\section{Breakdown of Quantum Field Theory}

\subsection{Capacity exhaustion}

Quantum field theory presumes that coherence capacity is sufficient to
support arbitrarily fine-grained basin statistics. When capacity becomes
limited, this assumption fails.

\begin{theorem}[QFT breakdown at capacity exhaustion, effective]
Quantum field theory ceases to be valid when coherence capacity approaches
zero, regardless of microscopic dynamics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At capacity exhaustion, effective projection becomes noninvertible and ensemble statistics can
no longer be represented within a Hilbert-space encoding.

\end{proof}

This explains why QFT fails near singularities, horizons, and strong
measurement contexts.

\section{Discussion}

This reconstruction resolves several foundational puzzles:

\begin{itemize}
\item Why quantum theory is probabilistic: probabilities encode ensemble
statistics.
\item Why superposition exists: linearity reflects convexity of ensemble
space.
\item Why renormalization is necessary: finite capacity enforces
coarse-graining.
\item Why QFT is not fundamental: it presumes infinite capacity.
\end{itemize}

Quantum field theory is thus an effective statistical language, not a
fundamental ontology.

\section{Conclusion}

We have reconstructed quantum field theory as an emergent statistical
description of coherence basin ensembles. Fields, operators, propagators,
path integrals, and renormalization arise naturally from ensemble
statistics under finite coherence capacity.

Together with the preceding papers, this completes a unified framework in
which:
\begin{itemize}
\item coherence capacity functions as the fundamental admissibility resource,
\item gravity is its geometric bookkeeping,
\item particles and forces arise from basin dynamics,
\item horizons and entropy reflect capacity bottlenecks,
\item quantum field theory emerges as basin statistics.
\end{itemize}

The apparent mysteries of quantum theory thus reflect the deep structure
of effective description rather than fundamental indeterminism.
