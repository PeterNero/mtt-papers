abstract: |
  A recurring objection to emergent approaches is that if the underlying theory is deterministic, then the emergent effective theory must also be deterministic; conversely, if the effective theory is stochastic, it must be merely “random.” We show that both inferences fail in Modal Triplet Theory (MTT). In MTT, the coherent projector is non-invertible and stabilization is contractive on admissible domains. We prove: (i) determinism does not descend to the 4D coherent effective theory unless a stringent fiber-invariance condition holds; (ii) the induced 4D stochastic evolution is not “random” but is geometrically biased and bounded by admissibility through a selection potential $`\Xi`$ and an OU-type damping–disturbance balance; and (iii) once an outcome/commitment is selected, coupled coherent subsystems undergo cascading basin reconfiguration that quenches downstream variability. This yields a third category of dynamics: lawful stochasticity generated by deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization. We clarify why this differs from superdeterminism and why many other theories do not naturally realize the same trichotomy.

# Scope and standing MTT inputs

We work in the standard MTT setting: a modal field $`\Psi`$ on the structured arena with commuting modal filters; a coherent projector $`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ onto the joint harmonic sector; slab-local bounded geometry; and an admissible regime in which a Fundamental Contractivity Condition (FCC) ensures contraction of the evolve–project map. No global-in-time regularity or censorship assumption is made; all results are local to a bounded-geometry time slab.

The purpose of this paper is structural. We formalize two points that are used implicitly across the MTT corpus:

1.  *Determinism does not automatically descend* from the 10D modal dynamics to the 4D coherent effective theory because coherent projection is generally information-erasing.

2.  The resulting 4D stochasticity is *not arbitrary randomness*: it is biased and bounded by the same geometric and stability structures that select and maintain coherence.

We then add a third structural statement needed for a complete account of measurement/action: *post-selection cascading stabilization* across coupled coherent subsystems.

# Formal setup: micro evolution, coherent projection, and effective states

## Micro state space and deterministic evolution

Let $`\mathcal H_{10}`$ be the (Sobolev/Hilbert) state space of the full modal field $`\Psi`$ on the 10D arena. Fix a discrete time step $`\Delta t>0`$ (or sample a continuous flow at step $`\Delta t`$). Assume the micro evolution is deterministic:
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:microdet}
\Psi_{n+1}=\Phi(\Psi_n),
\end{equation}
```
where $`\Phi:\mathcal H_{10}\to\mathcal H_{10}`$ is a well-posed map on the slab.

## Coherent projector and effective 4D state

Let
``` math
\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}:\mathcal H_{10}\to\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}
```
be the coherent projector (bounded on the relevant Sobolev scale on admissible slabs). Define the effective 4D state variable
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Pdef}
X := P(\Psi):=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi \in \mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}.
\end{equation}
```

<div class="remark">

*Remark 1* (Non-invertibility is structural, not epistemic). The map $`P`$ is typically many-to-one: different microstates can share the same coherent component while differing in discarded noncoherent content. The results below quantify the consequences of this non-invertibility for effective determinism and effective stochasticity.

</div>

# Main result I: determinism does not descend through coherent projection

## Factorization criterion

<div class="definition">

**Definition 2** (Deterministic induced dynamics on the effective state). We say the effective dynamics is deterministic if there exists a map $`F:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}\to\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$ such that
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:factor}
P(\Phi(\Psi)) = F(P(\Psi)) \qquad \forall\, \Psi\in\mathcal H_{10}.
\end{equation}
```
Equivalently, $`P\circ\Phi`$ factors through $`P`$.

</div>

<div id="prop:descend" class="proposition">

**Proposition 3** (Necessary and sufficient condition for induced determinism). *A deterministic induced map $`F`$ satisfying <a href="#eq:factor" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:factor">[eq:factor]</a> exists if and only if
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fiber_condition}
P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)\ \Longrightarrow\ P(\Phi(\Psi_1))=P(\Phi(\Psi_2)).
\end{equation}
```*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* ($`\Rightarrow`$) If $`P(\Phi(\Psi))=F(P(\Psi))`$ holds, then for any $`\Psi_1,\Psi_2`$ with $`P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)=x`$,
``` math
P(\Phi(\Psi_1))=F(x)=P(\Phi(\Psi_2)).
```
($`\Leftarrow`$) Assume <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a>. Define $`F(x):=P(\Phi(\Psi))`$ for any $`\Psi`$ with $`P(\Psi)=x`$. Condition <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a> ensures this is well-defined. Then <a href="#eq:factor" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:factor">[eq:factor]</a> follows. ◻

</div>

<div id="cor:nodeterminism" class="corollary">

**Corollary 4** (Generic effective non-determinism). *If there exist $`\Psi_1\neq \Psi_2`$ such that
``` math
P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)\quad\text{but}\quad P(\Phi(\Psi_1))\neq P(\Phi(\Psi_2)),
```
then *no* deterministic induced law $`X_{n+1}=F(X_n)`$ exists on $`\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$.*

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 5* (MTT interpretation). Condition <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a> states: all microstates with the same coherent component must have the same next-step coherent component. In MTT this generally fails because discarded noncoherent content can influence the next coherent image before being damped/filtered. Thus even when <a href="#eq:microdet" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:microdet">[eq:microdet]</a> is deterministic, the effective 4D description need not be.

</div>

# Main result II: induced stochasticity is structured and non-random

A critic might accept Corollary <a href="#cor:nodeterminism" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:nodeterminism">4</a> and then claim: “fine, the effective 4D dynamics is just random.” We now show the induced stochasticity is constrained by coherence geometry, damping margins, and the selection potential.

## The correct induced object: a transition kernel

To describe the effective evolution, introduce a physically relevant measure on microstates.

<div id="ass:measure" class="assumption">

**Assumption 6** (Reference measure / stationary ensemble on the slab). Let $`\mu`$ be a physically relevant probability measure on $`\mathcal H_{10}`$ on the slab, such that conditioning on $`X=P(\Psi)`$ is meaningful. (For example: a stationary ensemble for the damped microdynamics on the slab, or a slab-restricted invariant/physical measure for the admissible regime.)

</div>

For $`x\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$, let $`\mu_x`$ denote a conditional measure supported on the fiber $`P^{-1}(x)`$.

<div id="def:kernel" class="definition">

**Definition 7** (Induced transition kernel). For measurable $`A\subseteq\mathcal H_{\mathrm{coh}}`$, define
``` math
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kernel}
K(x,A) := \mu_x\big(\{\Psi\in P^{-1}(x):\ P(\Phi(\Psi))\in A\}\big).
\end{equation}
```

</div>

<div id="prop:dirac" class="proposition">

**Proposition 8** (Kernel reduces to deterministic map iff fiber invariance holds). *The kernel $`K(x,\cdot)`$ is a Dirac delta for every $`x`$ (hence induces a deterministic map) if and only if <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a> holds.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* If <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a> holds, then $`P(\Phi(\Psi))`$ is constant over each fiber $`P^{-1}(x)`$, so $`K(x,\cdot)`$ is Dirac. Conversely, if $`K(x,\cdot)`$ is Dirac for all $`x`$, then for any $`\Psi_1,\Psi_2`$ in the same fiber, $`P(\Phi(\Psi_1))`$ and $`P(\Phi(\Psi_2))`$ must coincide (otherwise the conditional pushforward would have support at more than one point), which is <a href="#eq:fiber_condition" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:fiber_condition">[eq:fiber_condition]</a>. ◻

</div>

## Quantitative non-randomness via selection potential $`\Xi`$ and OU balance

<div id="ass:OU" class="assumption">

**Assumption 9** (Linearized OU balance for discarded modes). On the slab, assume that near an admissible coherent configuration $`\Psi^\ast`$, the discarded (noncoherent) amplitudes $`a(t)\in (I-\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\mathcal H_{10}`$ admit a Gaussian linearized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck approximation
``` math
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \xi(t),\qquad \Gamma\ge \gamma_0 \mathrm{Id},
```
with mean-zero Gaussian forcing $`\xi(t)`$ of covariance operator $`Q`$, and stationary covariance $`\Sigma`$ solving the Lyapunov equation
``` math
\Gamma\Sigma+\Sigma\Gamma^\top = Q,
\qquad
\|\Sigma\|\le \frac{\|Q\|}{2\gamma_0}.
```

</div>

<div id="ass:MD" class="assumption">

**Assumption 10** (Mixing / short-memory regime). On the admissible slab and for the chosen sampling interval $`\Delta t`$, correlations of the discarded sector decay on a timescale $`t_{\mathrm{mix}} \ll \Delta t`$. In this regime, the effective forcing entering the reduced dynamics may be approximated by a martingale increment (white-noise closure) to the accuracy required in subsequent bounds.

</div>

<div id="ass:XiLip" class="assumption">

**Assumption 11** (Centroid response and Lipschitz selection potential). Assume the effective centroid/state displacement obeys
``` math
\|X(t)-X^\ast\|\le C_X\|a(t)\|,
```
and the selection potential $`\Xi`$ (as defined in the FP/MTT corpus) is Lipschitz on the admissible set, with constant $`L_\Xi`$, with respect to the same metric:
``` math
|\Xi(X)-\Xi(Y)|\le L_\Xi\|X-Y\|.
```
Let $`\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}`$ denote the critical value associated with the zero-margin boundary.

</div>

<div id="prop:nonrandomXi" class="proposition">

**Proposition 12** (Non-randomness via OU control and the selection potential). *Under Assumptions <a href="#ass:measure" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:measure">6</a>–<a href="#ass:XiLip" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:XiLip">11</a>, the process $`\Xi(X(t))`$ is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
``` math
\sigma^2 \le L_\Xi^2 C_X^2 \|\Sigma\| \le \frac{L_\Xi^2 C_X^2\|Q\|}{2\gamma_0}.
```
Consequently, barrier/exit events $`\{\Xi(X(t))>\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}\}`$ are exponentially suppressed on the slab, and the induced kernel $`K(X,\cdot)`$ concentrates inside admissible basins with tails quantitatively controlled by the damping margin $`\gamma_0`$ and disturbance scale $`\|Q\|`$.*

*In particular, the effective stochasticity is *not arbitrary*: it is biased by $`\Xi`$ and bounded by the damping–disturbance balance in discarded modes.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof sketch.* By Lipschitzness and the centroid response,
``` math
|\Xi(X(t))-\Xi(X^\ast)| \le L_\Xi\|X(t)-X^\ast\| \le L_\Xi C_X\|a(t)\|.
```
Under the stationary OU law, $`\|a(t)\|`$ has Gaussian tails controlled by $`\Sigma`$, yielding the stated variance proxy for the centered process. Standard Gaussian tail bounds (e.g. Borell–TIS for $`\sup_{t\in[0,T]}`$) then give exponential suppression of barrier crossings. Since inadmissible excursions correspond to $`\Xi`$ exceeding $`\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}`$, the kernel $`K(X,\cdot)`$ inherits concentration toward admissible basins. ◻

</div>

<div id="cor:third" class="corollary">

**Corollary 13** (Third category: lawful stochasticity). *Under the standing MTT assumptions, the induced 4D effective evolution is generically:
``` math
\text{not deterministic (Prop.~\ref{prop:descend})}\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{not random (Prop.~\ref{prop:nonrandomXi})},
```
but a *lawful stochastic* process generated by deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization and admissibility bias.*

</div>

# Cascading stabilization after commitment (action and measurement)

The preceding results explain why the effective theory is neither deterministic nor arbitrary noise. A further structural question is essential for applications to agency and measurement: *why do selected outcomes persist and propagate robustly downstream?* The answer is cascading basin reconfiguration across coupled coherent subsystems.

<div id="cor:cascading" class="corollary">

**Corollary 14** (Cascading stabilization after commitment). *Let $`X_{t_0}`$ lie in a near-threshold region where multiple admissible basins are accessible, and suppose the induced kernel $`K(X_{t_0},\cdot)`$ selects (with nonzero probability) a basin $`\mathcal{B}`$ at time $`t_0`$. Then, for $`t>t_0`$, the induced evolution generically exhibits *cascading stabilization*:*

1.  *The selection of $`\mathcal{B}`$ modifies the effective state $`X_t`$ so as to reconfigure the admissible basin structure of multiple coupled coherent subsystems.*

2.  *For each such subsystem, the FCC holds with strict margin inside the reconfigured basin, yielding exponential suppression of further branching.*

3.  *Consequently, the induced kernel $`K(X_t,\cdot)`$ becomes sharply concentrated along the selected trajectory, and stochastic variability is quenched downstream.*

*Thus stochasticity is *gated*: it is active primarily at upstream decision/threshold regions and is suppressed once a coherent commitment is made.*

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 15* (Upstream slack and downstream execution). Corollary <a href="#cor:cascading" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:cascading">14</a> formalizes the physical fact that a committed action is not merely the resolution of a local stochastic gate but a coordinated reconfiguration of admissible basins across many coupled coherent subsystems.

For example, initiating a hand movement simultaneously constrains motor, proprioceptive, visual, and environmental coherent sectors. Each sector has its own admissibility domain and damping margin, yet the action aligns them into a mutually reinforcing basin structure. Noise remains present but is orthogonal to the dominant contractive directions and cannot generically reverse the action without a new threshold crossing.

The same description applies to measurement: pre-measurement there may be multiple admissible basin-level outcomes (pointer configurations and correlated records). The measurement interaction triggers basin capture, and the resulting record propagates downstream by reconfiguring many coupled subsystems (apparatus, environment, memory) into a high-margin admissible configuration, quenching re-randomization.

</div>

# Why this is not superdeterminism

Superdeterminism is a specific thesis about conspiratorial correlations between hidden variables and future measurement settings (failure of statistical independence). The results above require none of that. They are purely structural:

- Effective non-determinism (Cor. <a href="#cor:nodeterminism" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:nodeterminism">4</a>) arises from non-invertibility of $`P=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$, not from a hidden-variable completion of the 4D effective state.

- The induced kernel $`K`$ (Def. <a href="#def:kernel" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="def:kernel">7</a>) is defined by conditional pushforward of a stationary/admissible ensemble, not by fine-tuned correlations with future settings.

- Cascading stabilization (Cor. <a href="#cor:cascading" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:cascading">14</a>) depends on local contractivity and admissibility margins and is compatible with locality/correlation control; it does not require global “conspiracy” constraints.

# Why many other theories do not naturally realize this trichotomy

MTT’s “deterministic upstairs, lawful stochastic downstairs” structure is not unique in spirit (coarse-graining is universal), but MTT is unusual in making it *native* and *quantitative* via projector geometry + FCC + admissibility margins + controlled stochastic closure.

## Classical deterministic theories

Classical mechanics is deterministic at the micro-level and is often treated as deterministic at the macro-level because macro variables are chosen as sufficient statistics or because coarse-graining is treated phenomenologically. Stochastic closures (turbulence, kinetic theory) exist but are typically model-dependent. MTT differs by building a bounded, non-invertible projector into the ontology and controlling its stability via spectral gaps and FCC.

## Standard quantum mechanics

Standard QM is stochastic in measurement outcomes, but the stochasticity is postulated (Born rule + measurement postulate) rather than derived from a geometric stabilization map. Decoherence explains suppression of interference but does not by itself select a single outcome without additional interpretive commitments.

## Many-worlds (unitary-only)

Many-worlds keeps determinism at the universal state level and treats randomness as epistemic (self-locating uncertainty). MTT differs because the effective 4D evolution itself is non-deterministic due to the non-invertible coherent projection, and post-selection trajectories are stabilized by basin reconfiguration rather than by branching persistence.

## Hidden-variable completions (e.g. Bohmian)

Hidden-variable theories restore determinism at the level of a complete state and treat probabilities as ignorance about hidden variables. MTT differs because the effective 4D state is not the projection of a persistent hidden-variable completion that could be conditioned on; discarded modes are continually damped, re-excited, and erased by stabilization.

## Objective collapse models

Collapse models add fundamental stochastic terms. MTT adds no new stochastic law: it derives effective stochasticity from deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization with quantitative dependence on damping margins.

## Superdeterminism

Superdeterminism preserves determinism by rejecting statistical independence via global correlations. MTT does not require such correlations; its stochasticity arises from non-invertible projection and is constrained locally by admissibility and damping.

## Why MTT can do it

MTT combines three ingredients rarely unified in one framework:

1.  **A geometrically defined, bounded, non-invertible projection** ($`\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}`$) with physical meaning (coherent sector selection).

2.  **A quantitative stability theory** (FCC/admissibility) controlling persistence, exits, localization, and post-selection quenching.

3.  **A controlled stochastic closure** (OU balance / covariance control) for discarded modes that produces structured kernels rather than arbitrary noise.

This yields Corollaries <a href="#cor:third" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:third">13</a> and <a href="#cor:cascading" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="cor:cascading">14</a> as structural results rather than interpretive stances.

# Appendix: OU regime and emergence of the induced kernel

This appendix summarizes how the abstract kernel $`K`$ reduces to an OU-driven effective description in the linearized Gaussian regime used across FP-series analyses.

Let $`\Psi^{\ast}`$ be an admissible coherent fixed point on a bounded-geometry slab. Decompose
``` math
\Psi=\Psi_{\mathrm{coh}}\oplus a,
\qquad
\Psi_{\mathrm{coh}}=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\Psi,
\qquad
a=(I-\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}})\Psi.
```
Linearizing the damped modal dynamics about $`\Psi^{\ast}`$ yields
``` math
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \text{(higher order)} + \text{(disturbance)}.
```
In the Gaussian linearized regime (Assumption <a href="#ass:OU" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:OU">9</a>), the disturbance is modeled as mean-zero Gaussian forcing $`\xi(t)`$ with covariance $`Q`$, giving the OU closure
``` math
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \xi(t),\qquad \Gamma\ge \gamma_0 \mathrm{Id}.
```
The stationary covariance $`\Sigma`$ solves the Lyapunov equation $`\Gamma\Sigma+\Sigma\Gamma^\top=Q`$, implying $`\|\Sigma\|\le \|Q\|/(2\gamma_0)`$.

The effective state $`X(t)=P(\Psi(t))`$ depends on $`a(t)`$ through centroid response and projection geometry; thus $`X(t)`$ inherits a controlled stochastic component from $`a(t)`$. The induced Markov kernel $`K(X,\cdot)`$ is precisely the conditional pushforward of the OU-driven microstate ensemble through $`P\circ\Phi_{\Delta t}`$. Near a stable basin, FCC contraction yields concentration (quenching); near basin boundaries OU fluctuations control threshold crossings, producing gated branching behavior.

*Clarification.* Here “Markov kernel” is meant strictly in the discrete-time, one-step transition sense associated with the sampled map $`P \circ \Phi_{\Delta t}`$. No claim is made that the induced dynamics forms a continuous-time semigroup, nor that it is CP-divisible, without an additional mixing or weak-coupling limit.

# Conclusion

We proved the precise condition under which determinism descends from deterministic 10D modal evolution to the 4D coherent effective state. Because the coherent projection is generally many-to-one, this condition fails generically and the 4D effective evolution is non-deterministic even when the micro evolution is deterministic. We then showed that the induced stochasticity is not “mere randomness”: it is quantitatively biased and bounded by the selection potential $`\Xi`$ and the OU damping–disturbance balance. Finally, we established that once an outcome/commitment is selected, coupled coherent subsystems undergo cascading stabilization that quenches downstream variability—capturing the observed robustness of both actions and measurements. This yields a third category of dynamics: lawful stochasticity produced by deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization.
