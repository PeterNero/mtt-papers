\title{\bf Determinism Without Superdeterminism:\\
Projection-Induced Stochasticity, Non-Randomness, and Cascading Stabilization\\
in Modal Triplet Theory}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{January, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A recurring objection to emergent approaches is that if the underlying theory is deterministic,
then the emergent effective theory must also be deterministic; conversely, if the effective theory
is stochastic, it must be merely ``random.'' We show that both inferences fail in Modal Triplet
Theory (MTT). In MTT, the coherent projector is non-invertible and stabilization is contractive on
admissible domains. We prove: (i) determinism does not descend to the 4D coherent effective theory
unless a stringent fiber-invariance condition holds; (ii) the induced 4D stochastic evolution is not
``random'' but is geometrically biased and bounded by admissibility through a selection potential
$\Xi$ and an OU-type damping--disturbance balance; and (iii) once an outcome/commitment is selected,
coupled coherent subsystems undergo cascading basin reconfiguration that quenches downstream
variability. This yields a third category of dynamics: lawful stochasticity generated by deterministic
micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization. We clarify why this differs from
superdeterminism and why many other theories do not naturally realize the same trichotomy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and standing MTT inputs}
\label{sec:scope}

We work in the standard MTT setting: a modal field $\Psi$ on the structured arena with commuting
modal filters; a coherent projector $\Pcoh$ onto the joint harmonic sector; slab-local bounded geometry;
and an admissible regime in which a Fundamental Contractivity Condition (FCC) ensures contraction of
the evolve--project map. No global-in-time regularity or censorship assumption is made; all results are
local to a bounded-geometry time slab.

The purpose of this paper is structural. We formalize two points that are used implicitly across the
MTT corpus:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Determinism does not automatically descend} from the 10D modal dynamics to the 4D coherent
effective theory because coherent projection is generally information-erasing.
\item The resulting 4D stochasticity is \emph{not arbitrary randomness}: it is biased and bounded by
the same geometric and stability structures that select and maintain coherence.
\end{enumerate}
We then add a third structural statement needed for a complete account of measurement/action:
\emph{post-selection cascading stabilization} across coupled coherent subsystems.

\section{Formal setup: micro evolution, coherent projection, and effective states}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Micro state space and deterministic evolution}

Let $\Hten$ be the (Sobolev/Hilbert) state space of the full modal field $\Psi$ on the 10D arena.
Fix a discrete time step $\Delta t>0$ (or sample a continuous flow at step $\Delta t$). Assume the
micro evolution is deterministic:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:microdet}
\Psi_{n+1}=\Phi(\Psi_n),
\end{equation}
where $\Phi:\Hten\to\Hten$ is a well-posed map on the slab.

\subsection{Coherent projector and effective 4D state}

Let
\[
\Pcoh:\Hten\to\Hcoh
\]
be the coherent projector (bounded on the relevant Sobolev scale on admissible slabs). Define the
effective 4D state variable
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pdef}
X := P(\Psi):=\Pcoh\Psi \in \Hcoh.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Non-invertibility is structural, not epistemic]
The map $P$ is typically many-to-one: different microstates can share the same coherent component
while differing in discarded noncoherent content. The results below quantify the consequences of
this non-invertibility for effective determinism and effective stochasticity.
\end{remark}

%========================================================
\section{Main result I: determinism does not descend through coherent projection}
\label{sec:nodescent}

\subsection{Factorization criterion}

\begin{definition}[Deterministic induced dynamics on the effective state]
We say the effective dynamics is deterministic if there exists a map
$F:\Hcoh\to\Hcoh$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
P(\Phi(\Psi)) = F(P(\Psi)) \qquad \forall\, \Psi\in\Hten.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $P\circ\Phi$ factors through $P$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Necessary and sufficient condition for induced determinism]
\label{prop:descend}
A deterministic induced map $F$ satisfying \eqref{eq:factor} exists if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber_condition}
P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)\ \Longrightarrow\ P(\Phi(\Psi_1))=P(\Phi(\Psi_2)).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) If $P(\Phi(\Psi))=F(P(\Psi))$ holds, then for any $\Psi_1,\Psi_2$ with
$P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)=x$,
\[
P(\Phi(\Psi_1))=F(x)=P(\Phi(\Psi_2)).
\]
($\Leftarrow$) Assume \eqref{eq:fiber_condition}. Define $F(x):=P(\Phi(\Psi))$ for any $\Psi$ with
$P(\Psi)=x$. Condition \eqref{eq:fiber_condition} ensures this is well-defined. Then
\eqref{eq:factor} follows. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Generic effective non-determinism]
\label{cor:nodeterminism}
If there exist $\Psi_1\neq \Psi_2$ such that
\[
P(\Psi_1)=P(\Psi_2)\quad\text{but}\quad P(\Phi(\Psi_1))\neq P(\Phi(\Psi_2)),
\]
then \emph{no} deterministic induced law $X_{n+1}=F(X_n)$ exists on $\Hcoh$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[MTT interpretation]
Condition \eqref{eq:fiber_condition} states: all microstates with the same coherent component must
have the same next-step coherent component. In MTT this generally fails because discarded noncoherent
content can influence the next coherent image before being damped/filtered. Thus even when \eqref{eq:microdet}
is deterministic, the effective 4D description need not be.
\end{remark}

\section{Main result II: induced stochasticity is structured and non-random}
\label{sec:nonrandom}

A critic might accept Corollary~\ref{cor:nodeterminism} and then claim: ``fine, the effective 4D
dynamics is just random.'' We now show the induced stochasticity is constrained by coherence geometry,
damping margins, and the selection potential.

\subsection{The correct induced object: a transition kernel}

To describe the effective evolution, introduce a physically relevant measure on microstates.

\begin{assumption}[Reference measure / stationary ensemble on the slab]
\label{ass:measure}
Let $\mu$ be a physically relevant probability measure on $\Hten$ on the slab, such that conditioning
on $X=P(\Psi)$ is meaningful. (For example: a stationary ensemble for the damped microdynamics on the slab,
or a slab-restricted invariant/physical measure for the admissible regime.)
\end{assumption}

For $x\in\Hcoh$, let $\mu_x$ denote a conditional measure supported on the fiber $P^{-1}(x)$.

\begin{definition}[Induced transition kernel]
\label{def:kernel}
For measurable $A\subseteq\Hcoh$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel}
K(x,A) := \mu_x\big(\{\Psi\in P^{-1}(x):\ P(\Phi(\Psi))\in A\}\big).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Kernel reduces to deterministic map iff fiber invariance holds]
\label{prop:dirac}
The kernel $K(x,\cdot)$ is a Dirac delta for every $x$ (hence induces a deterministic map) if and only
if \eqref{eq:fiber_condition} holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \eqref{eq:fiber_condition} holds, then $P(\Phi(\Psi))$ is constant over each fiber $P^{-1}(x)$, so
$K(x,\cdot)$ is Dirac. Conversely, if $K(x,\cdot)$ is Dirac for all $x$, then for any $\Psi_1,\Psi_2$ in
the same fiber, $P(\Phi(\Psi_1))$ and $P(\Phi(\Psi_2))$ must coincide (otherwise the conditional pushforward
would have support at more than one point), which is \eqref{eq:fiber_condition}. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\subsection{Quantitative non-randomness via selection potential $\Xi$ and OU balance}

\begin{assumption}[Linearized OU balance for discarded modes]
\label{ass:OU}
On the slab, assume that near an admissible coherent configuration $\Psi^\ast$, the discarded (noncoherent)
amplitudes $a(t)\in (I-\Pcoh)\Hten$ admit a Gaussian linearized Ornstein--Uhlenbeck approximation
\[
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \xi(t),\qquad \Gamma\ge \gamma_0 \Id,
\]
with mean-zero Gaussian forcing $\xi(t)$ of covariance operator $Q$, and stationary covariance
$\Sigma$ solving the Lyapunov equation
\[
\Gamma\Sigma+\Sigma\Gamma^\top = Q,
\qquad
\|\Sigma\|\le \frac{\|Q\|}{2\gamma_0}.
\]
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Mixing / short-memory regime]\label{ass:MD}
On the admissible slab and for the chosen sampling interval \(\Delta t\),
correlations of the discarded sector decay on a timescale
\(t_{\mathrm{mix}} \ll \Delta t\).
In this regime, the effective forcing entering the reduced dynamics
may be approximated by a martingale increment (white-noise closure)
to the accuracy required in subsequent bounds.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Centroid response and Lipschitz selection potential]
\label{ass:XiLip}
Assume the effective centroid/state displacement obeys
\[
\|X(t)-X^\ast\|\le C_X\|a(t)\|,
\]
and the selection potential $\Xi$ (as defined in the FP/MTT corpus) is Lipschitz on the admissible set,
with constant $L_\Xi$, with respect to the same metric:
\[
|\Xi(X)-\Xi(Y)|\le L_\Xi\|X-Y\|.
\]
Let $\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}$ denote the critical value associated with the zero-margin boundary.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Non-randomness via OU control and the selection potential]
\label{prop:nonrandomXi}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:measure}--\ref{ass:XiLip}, the process $\Xi(X(t))$ is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
\[
\sigma^2 \le L_\Xi^2 C_X^2 \|\Sigma\| \le \frac{L_\Xi^2 C_X^2\|Q\|}{2\gamma_0}.
\]
Consequently, barrier/exit events $\{\Xi(X(t))>\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}\}$ are exponentially suppressed on the slab,
and the induced kernel $K(X,\cdot)$ concentrates inside admissible basins with tails quantitatively controlled by
the damping margin $\gamma_0$ and disturbance scale $\|Q\|$.

In particular, the effective stochasticity is \emph{not arbitrary}: it is biased by $\Xi$ and bounded by the
damping--disturbance balance in discarded modes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lipschitzness and the centroid response,
\[
|\Xi(X(t))-\Xi(X^\ast)| \le L_\Xi\|X(t)-X^\ast\| \le L_\Xi C_X\|a(t)\|.
\]
Under the stationary OU law, $\|a(t)\|$ has Gaussian tails controlled by $\Sigma$, yielding the stated variance proxy
for the centered process. Standard Gaussian tail bounds (e.g.\ Borell--TIS for $\sup_{t\in[0,T]}$) then give exponential
suppression of barrier crossings. Since inadmissible excursions correspond to $\Xi$ exceeding $\Xi_{\mathrm{crit}}$, the
kernel $K(X,\cdot)$ inherits concentration toward admissible basins. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Third category: lawful stochasticity]
\label{cor:third}
Under the standing MTT assumptions, the induced 4D effective evolution is generically:
\[
\text{not deterministic (Prop.~\ref{prop:descend})}\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{not random (Prop.~\ref{prop:nonrandomXi})},
\]
but a \emph{lawful stochastic} process generated by deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing
stabilization and admissibility bias.
\end{corollary}

\section{Cascading stabilization after commitment (action and measurement)}
\label{sec:cascading}

The preceding results explain why the effective theory is neither deterministic nor arbitrary noise.
A further structural question is essential for applications to agency and measurement:
\emph{why do selected outcomes persist and propagate robustly downstream?}
The answer is cascading basin reconfiguration across coupled coherent subsystems.

\begin{corollary}[Cascading stabilization after commitment]
\label{cor:cascading}
Let $X_{t_0}$ lie in a near-threshold region where multiple admissible basins are accessible, and suppose the induced
kernel $K(X_{t_0},\cdot)$ selects (with nonzero probability) a basin $\mathcal{B}$ at time $t_0$.
Then, for $t>t_0$, the induced evolution generically exhibits \emph{cascading stabilization}:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item The selection of $\mathcal{B}$ modifies the effective state $X_t$ so as to reconfigure the admissible basin
structure of multiple coupled coherent subsystems.
\item For each such subsystem, the FCC holds with strict margin inside the reconfigured basin, yielding exponential
suppression of further branching.
\item Consequently, the induced kernel $K(X_t,\cdot)$ becomes sharply concentrated along the selected trajectory,
and stochastic variability is quenched downstream.
\end{enumerate}

Thus stochasticity is \emph{gated}: it is active primarily at upstream decision/threshold regions and is suppressed
once a coherent commitment is made.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Upstream slack and downstream execution]
Corollary~\ref{cor:cascading} formalizes the physical fact that a committed action is not merely the resolution of a
local stochastic gate but a coordinated reconfiguration of admissible basins across many coupled coherent subsystems.

For example, initiating a hand movement simultaneously constrains motor, proprioceptive, visual, and environmental
coherent sectors. Each sector has its own admissibility domain and damping margin, yet the action aligns them into a
mutually reinforcing basin structure. Noise remains present but is orthogonal to the dominant contractive directions
and cannot generically reverse the action without a new threshold crossing.

The same description applies to measurement: pre-measurement there may be multiple admissible basin-level outcomes
(pointer configurations and correlated records). The measurement interaction triggers basin capture, and the resulting
record propagates downstream by reconfiguring many coupled subsystems (apparatus, environment, memory) into a high-margin
admissible configuration, quenching re-randomization.
\end{remark}

\section{Why this is not superdeterminism}
\label{sec:notsuperdet}

Superdeterminism is a specific thesis about conspiratorial correlations between hidden variables and future measurement
settings (failure of statistical independence). The results above require none of that. They are purely structural:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Effective non-determinism (Cor.~\ref{cor:nodeterminism}) arises from non-invertibility of $P=\Pcoh$, not from a
hidden-variable completion of the 4D effective state.
\item The induced kernel $K$ (Def.~\ref{def:kernel}) is defined by conditional pushforward of a stationary/admissible
ensemble, not by fine-tuned correlations with future settings.
\item Cascading stabilization (Cor.~\ref{cor:cascading}) depends on local contractivity and admissibility margins and
is compatible with locality/correlation control; it does not require global ``conspiracy'' constraints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why many other theories do not naturally realize this trichotomy}
\label{sec:compare}

MTT's ``deterministic upstairs, lawful stochastic downstairs'' structure is not unique in spirit (coarse-graining is
universal), but MTT is unusual in making it \emph{native} and \emph{quantitative} via projector geometry + FCC +
admissibility margins + controlled stochastic closure.

\subsection{Classical deterministic theories}
Classical mechanics is deterministic at the micro-level and is often treated as deterministic at the macro-level because
macro variables are chosen as sufficient statistics or because coarse-graining is treated phenomenologically.
Stochastic closures (turbulence, kinetic theory) exist but are typically model-dependent. MTT differs by building a
bounded, non-invertible projector into the ontology and controlling its stability via spectral gaps and FCC.

\subsection{Standard quantum mechanics}
Standard QM is stochastic in measurement outcomes, but the stochasticity is postulated (Born rule + measurement postulate)
rather than derived from a geometric stabilization map. Decoherence explains suppression of interference but does not
by itself select a single outcome without additional interpretive commitments.

\subsection{Many-worlds (unitary-only)}
Many-worlds keeps determinism at the universal state level and treats randomness as epistemic (self-locating uncertainty).
MTT differs because the effective 4D evolution itself is non-deterministic due to the non-invertible coherent projection,
and post-selection trajectories are stabilized by basin reconfiguration rather than by branching persistence.

\subsection{Hidden-variable completions (e.g.\ Bohmian)}
Hidden-variable theories restore determinism at the level of a complete state and treat probabilities as ignorance about
hidden variables. MTT differs because the effective 4D state is not the projection of a persistent hidden-variable
completion that could be conditioned on; discarded modes are continually damped, re-excited, and erased by stabilization.

\subsection{Objective collapse models}
Collapse models add fundamental stochastic terms. MTT adds no new stochastic law: it derives effective stochasticity
from deterministic micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization with quantitative dependence on damping margins.

\subsection{Superdeterminism}
Superdeterminism preserves determinism by rejecting statistical independence via global correlations. MTT does not require
such correlations; its stochasticity arises from non-invertible projection and is constrained locally by admissibility and
damping.

\subsection{Why MTT can do it}
MTT combines three ingredients rarely unified in one framework:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A geometrically defined, bounded, non-invertible projection} ($\Pcoh$) with physical meaning (coherent
sector selection).
\item \textbf{A quantitative stability theory} (FCC/admissibility) controlling persistence, exits, localization, and
post-selection quenching.
\item \textbf{A controlled stochastic closure} (OU balance / covariance control) for discarded modes that produces
structured kernels rather than arbitrary noise.
\end{enumerate}
This yields Corollaries~\ref{cor:third} and \ref{cor:cascading} as structural results rather than interpretive stances.

\section{Appendix: OU regime and emergence of the induced kernel}
\label{sec:appendixOU}

This appendix summarizes how the abstract kernel $K$ reduces to an OU-driven effective description in the linearized
Gaussian regime used across FP-series analyses.

Let $\Psi^{\ast}$ be an admissible coherent fixed point on a bounded-geometry slab. Decompose
\[
\Psi=\Psi_{\mathrm{coh}}\oplus a,
\qquad
\Psi_{\mathrm{coh}}=\Pcoh\Psi,
\qquad
a=(I-\Pcoh)\Psi.
\]
Linearizing the damped modal dynamics about $\Psi^{\ast}$ yields
\[
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \text{(higher order)} + \text{(disturbance)}.
\]
In the Gaussian linearized regime (Assumption~\ref{ass:OU}), the disturbance is modeled as mean-zero Gaussian forcing
$\xi(t)$ with covariance $Q$, giving the OU closure
\[
\dot a = -\Gamma a + \xi(t),\qquad \Gamma\ge \gamma_0 \Id.
\]
The stationary covariance $\Sigma$ solves the Lyapunov equation
$\Gamma\Sigma+\Sigma\Gamma^\top=Q$, implying $\|\Sigma\|\le \|Q\|/(2\gamma_0)$.

The effective state $X(t)=P(\Psi(t))$ depends on $a(t)$ through centroid response and projection geometry; thus $X(t)$
inherits a controlled stochastic component from $a(t)$. The induced Markov kernel $K(X,\cdot)$ is precisely the
conditional pushforward of the OU-driven microstate ensemble through $P\circ\Phi_{\Delta t}$. Near a stable basin,
FCC contraction yields concentration (quenching); near basin boundaries OU fluctuations control threshold crossings,
producing gated branching behavior.

\noindent\emph{Clarification.}
Here “Markov kernel” is meant strictly in the discrete-time, one-step transition sense
associated with the sampled map \(P \circ \Phi_{\Delta t}\).
No claim is made that the induced dynamics forms a continuous-time semigroup,
nor that it is CP-divisible, without an additional mixing or weak-coupling limit.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proved the precise condition under which determinism descends from deterministic 10D modal evolution to the 4D coherent
effective state. Because the coherent projection is generally many-to-one, this condition fails generically and the 4D
effective evolution is non-deterministic even when the micro evolution is deterministic. We then showed that the induced
stochasticity is not ``mere randomness'': it is quantitatively biased and bounded by the selection potential $\Xi$ and
the OU damping--disturbance balance. Finally, we established that once an outcome/commitment is selected, coupled coherent
subsystems undergo cascading stabilization that quenches downstream variability---capturing the observed robustness of both
actions and measurements. This yields a third category of dynamics: lawful stochasticity produced by deterministic
micro-evolution plus information-erasing stabilization.
