\begin{abstract}
This paper corrects the first edition's claim that a full nonperturbative,
unitary gravity sector follows from separately filtered graviton and ghost
covariances, termwise BRST identities, and reflection positivity of the
transverse-traceless sector.  Those implications are not valid.  We replace
them by a typed compatibility contract.  First, a common bounded functional
calculus preserves a linear BRST complex when the kinetic Laplacians, spectral
projections, domains, and boundary conditions intertwine the differential;
independently chosen filters do not ensure this.  Second, an exact
finite-cutoff quantum BRST measure gives Ward identities and gauge-fixing
independence by super-Stokes, while Borel summation preserves a linear Ward
identity only under a common analytic domain and uniform Gevrey-one remainder
bounds.  Third, BRST cohomology yields a physical pre-Hilbert space only when
reflection positivity is proved on the full BRST-closed positive-time
observable algebra and exact classes lie in the reflection null radical.
Positivity on the TT subspace alone is insufficient.  We also prove that a
nonzero positive Kallen--Lehmann measure cannot have permanent Gaussian decay
in the same Euclidean spectral variable, so the old SPT positivity argument
cannot be used.  The result is a rigorous conditional theorem and an explicit
exit certificate.  Current MTT data provide useful finite classical and
free-field inputs, but not yet the selected gravitational quantum measure,
quantum master equation, constructive Borel theorem, or physical
reflection-positivity theorem required by that certificate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and revision statement}

The first edition attempted to lift a conditional SPT-filtered Euclidean TT
model to a complete gauge theory.  Its central chain was
\[
 \text{filtered covariances}
 \Longrightarrow \text{Borel-summed BRST identities}
 \Longrightarrow \text{TT positivity}
 \Longrightarrow \Hphys .
\]
Each arrow needs an additional theorem.  In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item a regulator must be a chain map for the BRST/BV complex, including its
domains and boundary conditions;
\item the regularized measure must satisfy the quantum master equation, or an
equivalent anomaly-free quantum BRST invariance statement;
\item the constructive expansion must exist for a precisely specified stable
interaction and have uniform bounds strong enough to pass the identity to its
Borel sum; and
\item reflection positivity must hold for the physical gauge-invariant
Schwinger functional, not merely for a selected free TT block.
\end{enumerate}

Version 2 withdraws the claim that these four steps have been completed for
MTT quantum gravity.  It proves the implications that are valid once their
hypotheses are supplied and identifies which hypotheses remain open.  This is
a theorem-level correction, not a change of notation or a boundary disclaimer.

\subsection{Typed claim levels}

We use three levels throughout:
\begin{description}
\item[Classical algebraic level.] A nilpotent BRST differential and a classical
master action are defined after a gauge symmetry and action are supplied.
\item[Regularized quantum level.] A regulator, measure, integration cycle, and
renormalized action satisfy a quantum master equation with controlled
boundaries and anomalies.
\item[Physical reconstruction level.] Gauge-invariant Euclidean correlators
satisfy the relevant OS axioms and reconstruct a positive Lorentzian theory.
\end{description}
The first level does not imply the second, and the second does not imply the
third.

\section{Current MTT input and its boundary}

The current quantum-gravity audit records exact finite representation data, a
finite q79 TT Hessian proportional to the identity, a two-derivative
TEGR/Einstein action shape on the explicitly selected branch, and a positive
two-helicity free graviton sector.  At fixed low-energy order, standard
background-field BRST/BV quantum-GR effective-field-theory machinery can be
composed with that action.  This is an imported parity construction, not an
MTT derivation of the quantum measure or UV completion
\cite{MTTQGAudit,Donoghue2012}.

There is also an exact classical BRST result for the selected finite Standard
Model carrier: the quotient gauge algebra, its Chevalley--Eilenberg ghost
differential, and its sixteen-state matter representation close exactly.  That
result is a classical gauge-stack theorem.  It does not construct the
gravitational BV measure or prove a quantum master equation
\cite{MTTSMBRST}.

The revised fixed-point series is deliberately classical.  Its damping,
covariance, and equilibrium results do not select a quantum covariance or a
BRST regulator \cite{FPVI}.  Consequently, neither fixed-point damping nor the
finite TT Hessian can silently supply the missing quantum hypotheses below.

\section{Classical gravitational BRST data}

Let $g$ be a background metric on a bounded Euclidean domain $\Om$ and let
$\gamma=g+h$ be the total metric.  The standard gauge-fixed multiplet is
\[
 \Phi=(h_{\mu\nu},c^\mu,\bar c_\mu,b_\mu),
\]
with ghost, antighost, and Nakanishi--Lautrup field.  In a consistent graded
sign convention, the diffeomorphism BRST differential has the schematic form
\[
 \sop\gamma=\mathcal L_c\gamma,
 \qquad
 \sop c=\tfrac12[c,c]_{\mathrm{gr}},
 \qquad
 \sop\bar c=b,
 \qquad
 \sop b=0.
\]
The graded Jacobi identity and the representation of vector fields by Lie
derivatives give $\sop^2=0$.  A gauge-fixing fermion $\Psi$ adds an
$\sop$-exact gauge-fixing and ghost term to a diffeomorphism-invariant
classical action.  The BV extension adds antifields and packages these
relations in the classical master equation
\[
 (S_{\BV},S_{\BV})=0.
\]
These are standard consequences of a supplied diffeomorphism-invariant action
\cite{BatalinVilkovisky1981,HenneauxTeitelboim1992,BarnichBrandtHenneaux2000}.

\begin{proposition}[Classical lift does not select a quantum theory]
A nilpotent classical differential and a solution of the classical master
equation do not determine a regularized measure, a BV Laplacian, a quantum
master action, an integration cycle, or a physical positive inner product.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The classical data depend only on the gauge orbits and the classical action.
Different regularizations can have different Jacobians and anomaly
counterterms, and an indefinite gauge-fixed state space can admit inequivalent
or nonexistent positive physical completions.  These objects are additional
data in the BV and OS formalisms.  The quantum master equation explicitly
contains the measure-dependent BV Laplacian, which is absent from the classical
master equation \cite{Schwarz1993,Costello2007}.
\end{proof}

\section{When an SPT filter preserves a BRST complex}

The first edition applied separate scalar functions to the graviton and ghost
kinetic operators.  The correct requirement is an intertwining theorem.

\subsection{A shared functional-calculus theorem}

Let
\[
 \cdots\longrightarrow E^r
 \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{Q_r} E^{r+1}
 \mathop{\longrightarrow}^{Q_{r+1}} E^{r+2}
 \longrightarrow\cdots,
 \qquad Q_{r+1}Q_r=0,
\]
be a Hilbert complex.  Let $\Delta_r\ge0$ be self-adjoint kinetic Laplacians on
$E^r$, with spectral measures $P_r(B)$ for Borel sets
$B\subset[0,\infty)$.  Boundary conditions are part of the domains of $Q_r$
and $\Delta_r$.

\begin{assumption}[Spectral chain compatibility]\label{ass:chain}
For every Borel set $B$ and every $u$ in the domain of $Q_r$,
\[
 Q_rP_r(B)u=P_{r+1}(B)Q_ru.
\]
In addition, the selected boundary domains are mapped by $Q_r$ and no boundary
term appears in the corresponding Green identity.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Shared-filter chain theorem]\label{thm:filter}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:chain}, every bounded Borel function $f$ satisfies
\[
 Q_rf(\Delta_r)=f(\Delta_{r+1})Q_r
\]
on the domain of $Q_r$.  Hence, for $\lambda>0$, the common filtered covariance
\[
 C_r=f(\Delta_r)(\Delta_r+\lambda)^{-1}
\]
is a chain map.  The same statement holds for common spectral cutoffs
$P_r([0,\Lambda])$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The spectral theorem gives
\[
 f(\Delta_r)=\int_{[0,\infty)}f(x)\,dP_r(x).
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:chain} permits $Q_r$ to pass through the spectral integral,
which gives the displayed identity.  The resolvent factor is another bounded
Borel function of the same spectral variable.  Taking
$f=\mathbf 1_{[0,\Lambda]}$ gives the cutoff statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independent filters are insufficient]\label{prop:independent}
Even when the unfiltered kinetic operators form a complex, independently
chosen filters need not preserve it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $E^0=E^1=\mathbb C$, $Q_0=1$, and
$\Delta_0=\Delta_1=0$.  If the two filters act by constants $a$ and $b$, then
chain compatibility requires $aQ_0=bQ_0$, hence $a=b$.  Independent choices
with $a\ne b$ violate BRST compatibility in this one-dimensional example.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:filter} is useful but limited.  It controls the linearized
complex and its Gaussian covariance.  Nonlinear BRST transformations,
interaction vertices, antifields, counterterms, and the measure Jacobian must
still satisfy the quantum master equation.  A smoothing operator can be an
excellent regulator while breaking the unmodified Slavnov--Taylor identity;
then one needs a modified identity and a proved restoration limit, not an
assertion of exact invariance \cite{Lavrov2020}.

\subsection{Boundary conditions are structural data}

Support of observables away from $\partial\Om$ does not by itself make the
integration domain BRST invariant.  One must select relative, absolute, or
other elliptic boundary conditions for the full complex and prove that $Q_r$
maps each domain to the next.  Boundary ghosts and possible edge modes must be
included whenever gauge transformations act nontrivially at the boundary.

\section{The finite-cutoff quantum Ward theorem}

The clean finite-dimensional statement is a super-Stokes theorem.  It shows
exactly which premise the first edition assumed rather than proved.

Let $\mathcal M_N$ be a finite-dimensional graded field space with Berezin
measure $d\mu_N$, integration cycle $\Gamma_N$, odd vector field $s_N$, and
Euclidean action $S_{N,\alpha}$.  The parameter $\alpha$ labels a family of
gauge-fixing choices.

\begin{assumption}[Quantum BRST measure]\label{ass:qmeasure}
The following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $s_N^2=0$ and $\Gamma_N$ is invariant or has vanishing super-boundary;
\item the weighted measure is invariant,
\[
 \operatorname{div}_{\mu_N}\!\left(s_Ne^{-S_{N,\alpha}/\hbar}\right)=0;
\]
\item the gauge variation is exact,
$\partial_\alpha S_{N,\alpha}=s_NK_{N,\alpha}$; and
\item all displayed integrals and differentiations are absolutely controlled.
\end{enumerate}
In BV language, item (b) is the finite-cutoff quantum master equation with the
chosen measure and anomaly counterterms.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-cutoff Ward and gauge-fixing theorem]\label{thm:ward}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:qmeasure},
\[
 \int_{\Gamma_N}s_NG\,e^{-S_{N,\alpha}/\hbar}d\mu_N=0.
\]
If $s_NO=0$ and $O$ has no explicit $\alpha$ dependence, then its normalized
expectation is gauge-fixing independent:
\[
 \partial_\alpha\bbE_{N,\alpha}[O]=0.
\]
Expectations therefore depend only on the $s_N$-cohomology class of $O$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is super-Stokes applied to
$G e^{-S_{N,\alpha}/\hbar}d\mu_N$.  For a closed $O$, the graded Leibniz rule
turns $O\,s_NK_{N,\alpha}$ into an $s_N$-exact insertion.  Differentiating the
numerator and denominator of the normalized expectation therefore gives zero
for each derivative term.  Replacing $O$ by $O+s_NH$ changes no expectation.
\end{proof}

This theorem is nonperturbative at a fixed finite cutoff, but only after the
quantum measure premise has been constructed.  Classical nilpotency does not
prove item (b).  Neither does formal cancellation of bosonic and ghost
Jacobians, because a regulator, boundary, chiral matter content, or global
gauge orbit can change the Jacobian.  BV gauge independence is precisely a
consequence of the quantum master and integration-cycle hypotheses
\cite{Schwarz1993,Costello2007}.

\section{What Borel summation can and cannot preserve}

Suppose a constructive model has already produced Banach-valued correlators
$F(g)$ with formal series
\[
 F(g)\sim\sum_{n\ge0}F_ng^n.
\]
Let $W$ be a continuous linear Ward operator on the correlator space.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Borel inheritance of a linear Ward identity]
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $WF_n=0$ for every $n$;
\item the Borel transform
$\widehat F(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}F_nt^n/n!$ has a common analytic continuation along
the summation ray;
\item $\widehat F$ has a uniform exponential bound that permits the Laplace
integral and permits $W$ to pass through it; and
\item the resulting Laplace integral is the unique Borel sum of $F$ in the
declared Nevanlinna--Sokal domain.
\end{enumerate}
Then $WF(g)=0$ throughout that domain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Continuity and item (i) give
$W\widehat F(t)=\sum_n(WF_n)t^n/n!=0$ near the origin.  Analytic continuation
keeps it zero along the summation ray.  By item (iii), $W$ commutes with the
Laplace integral, so the Borel sum is also annihilated by $W$.  Uniqueness
prevents a second analytic function with the same controlled asymptotic series
from being substituted \cite{Sokal1980}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is an inheritance result, not a constructive existence theorem.
It does not derive the loop-vertex expansion, stability, the common analytic
domain, cutoff-uniform constants, or convergence as $N\to\infty$.  A nonlinear
QME additionally requires a Banach algebra in which products and Borel
convolutions are controlled.  The first edition supplied none of these uniform
estimates for the full gravity/ghost interaction.  The corresponding claim is
therefore conditional.

The antecedent QG-I paper also requires its own repair: Hilbert--Schmidt control
alone is not a construction of a countably additive Gaussian measure on the
same Hilbert space, and generic local analyticity plus a lower bound is not a
complete mixed boson--ghost Borel theorem.  QG-II cannot inherit a conclusion
that its antecedent has not established.

\section{Physical cohomology and reflection positivity}

Let $\mathcal A$ be a Euclidean observable algebra, $\Aplus$ its positive-time
subalgebra, $\Theta$ the antilinear time reflection, and $\mathcal S$ the
Euclidean expectation functional.  Let $s$ be an odd derivation preserving
support and compatible with reflection.  Set
\[
 Z_+=\ker(s|_{\Aplus}),\qquad B_+=\operatorname{im}(s|_{\Aplus})\cap Z_+.
\]

\begin{assumption}[Physical reflection package]\label{ass:physicalOS}
For all $F,G\in Z_+$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item the form $(F,G)_{\mathrm{OS}}=\mathcal S(\Theta F\,G)$ is Hermitian and
positive semidefinite;
\item BRST-exact vectors are in its radical,
$\mathcal S(\Theta F\,sH)=\mathcal S(\Theta(sH)\,F)=0$; and
\item positive Euclidean-time translations preserve $Z_+$, $B_+$, and the null
space of the form.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional cohomological OS reconstruction]\label{thm:os}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:physicalOS}, the quotient
\[
 \mathcal H_{0}=
 \frac{Z_+}{B_++\mathcal N_{\mathrm{OS}}}
\]
has a positive definite inner product.  Its completion is a Hilbert space
$\Hphys$.  The Euclidean translation maps descend to contractions whenever
the usual OS semigroup hypotheses hold.  A global positive-energy Lorentzian
unitary group follows only after the full OS reconstruction hypotheses,
including a suitable all-time or infinite-volume completion, are proved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item (b) makes the form independent of the representative of a BRST
cohomology class.  Item (a) makes the descended form positive semidefinite, and
quotienting by its null space makes it positive definite.  Completion gives
$\Hphys$.  Item (c) permits the OS semigroup construction.  The final
Lorentzian statement is exactly the additional content of the OS
reconstruction theorem, not a consequence of a bounded slab alone
\cite{OsterwalderSchrader1973,OsterwalderSchrader1975}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[TT positivity does not imply physical positivity]
Positivity of the OS form on a TT subspace $T\subset Z_+$ does not imply
Assumption~\ref{ass:physicalOS}(a) unless every physical cohomology class has a
TT representative and the representative map preserves the OS form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $Z_+=\mathbb C^2$, $s=0$, and the Hermitian form
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.  Its restriction to
$T=\operatorname{span}\{(1,0)\}$ is positive, but the full cohomology contains
the negative vector $(0,1)$.  Thus positivity on a subspace has no such logical
consequence.  The missing representative theorem is exactly what would rule
out this counterexample.
\end{proof}

Kugo--Ojima positivity similarly requires a representation of the BRST charge,
the quartet mechanism, and spectral/asymptotic assumptions; it is not a formal
consequence of writing $\ker Q/\operatorname{im}Q$
\cite{KugoOjima1979,KalauVanHolten1991}.

\section{Why permanent Gaussian damping cannot prove standard positivity}

The earlier SPT program tried to combine a positive spectral representation
with permanent Gaussian ultraviolet damping.  The following elementary no-go
is independent of the BRST issue.

\begin{theorem}[Positive spectral measure versus Gaussian decay]
Let
\[
 D(x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{\rho(ds)}{x+s},\qquad x>0,
\]
where $\rho$ is a nonzero positive measure and $D(x)$ is finite.  Then there
are $R,m>0$ such that
\[
 D(x)\ge\frac{m}{x+R}.
\]
Consequently, for no $C,\tau,\lambda>0$ can
\[
 D(x)\le \frac{C e^{-\tau x}}{x+\lambda}
\]
hold for all sufficiently large $x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\rho$ is nonzero, some bounded interval $[0,R]$ has mass
$m=\rho([0,R])>0$.  Restricting the integral to that interval gives the lower
bound.  Combining both inequalities would imply
\[
 \frac{m}{C}\le e^{-\tau x}\frac{x+R}{x+\lambda},
\]
whose right-hand side tends to zero.
\end{proof}

Thus a nontrivial standard positive Kallen--Lehmann measure and permanent
Gaussian decay cannot hold in the same Euclidean spectral variable.  This does
not prove that every nonlocal model is nonunitary; it proves that the old
Stieltjes/OS argument cannot establish the desired result.  Nonlocal theories
require a separate, internally consistent spectral and positivity framework
\cite{AsoreyRachwalShapiro2018,MTTSPTCorrection}.

\section{The corrected conditional theorem}

We can now state the strongest result supported by the present paper.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional SPT--BRST reconstruction contract]\label{thm:main}
Consider an SPT-filtered Euclidean gravity model.  Suppose all of the following
are supplied independently:
\begin{description}
\item[F1.] A selected gauge-invariant classical action and a closed BRST/BV
complex with compatible boundary domains.
\item[F2.] A shared spectral filter satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:chain}, together with a controlled finite-cutoff measure
space.
\item[F3.] An anomaly-free quantum measure satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:qmeasure} at every cutoff, with a proved removal limit.
\item[F4.] A constructive existence and Borel theorem with a common analytic
domain, uniform Gevrey-one remainders, and convergence of the Borel sums as the
cutoff and volume are removed.
\item[F5.] The physical gauge-invariant Schwinger functional satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:physicalOS} and the remaining OS reconstruction axioms.
\end{description}
Then BRST-exact insertions vanish, BRST-closed expectations are independent of
the admitted gauge-fixing homotopy, the linear Ward identities survive Borel
summation, and the BRST cohomology reconstructs a positive physical Hilbert
space.  If the global OS, continuation, and time-translation hypotheses are
also met, the reconstructed Lorentzian time evolution is unitary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
F1 and F2 give Theorem~\ref{thm:filter}.  F3 gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:ward} at finite cutoff.  F4 permits the uniform Borel
inheritance theorem and the declared removal limits.  F5 gives
Theorem~\ref{thm:os} and, with the remaining OS axioms, Lorentzian
reconstruction.  No step uses TT positivity as a substitute for physical
positivity or classical nilpotency as a substitute for the QME.
\end{proof}

The theorem is rigorous as an implication.  It is not a proof that current MTT
satisfies F2--F5.

\section{Conclusion}

SPT filtering, BRST cohomology, Borel summation, and OS reconstruction are
compatible only after their interfaces are proved.  The shared-filter theorem
gives the correct linear interface; the finite quantum-measure theorem gives
the correct Ward interface; the uniform Borel theorem gives the correct
summation interface; and the cohomological OS theorem gives the correct
physical-state interface.  The previous edition assumed these interfaces and
therefore overstated its conclusion.

The present MTT program has meaningful ingredients on both sides of this
contract: finite q79 gravity data, a classical gravitational action at a
declared branch tier, a free helicity sector, and exact classical finite gauge
BRST data.  What remains is sharply localized in F2--F5.  Until those objects
are constructed, this paper establishes a rigorous dependency theorem and
research target, not a nonperturbative unitary theory of quantum gravity.
