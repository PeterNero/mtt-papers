\title{\vspace{-1em}
Modal Triplet Theory and Calabi--Yau Compactification:\\[0.25em]
\large A Conditional Realization Map and Its Selection Boundary}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{Version 2, July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A Calabi--Yau compactification is not specified by a six-dimensional metric
alone.  It also requires topology, complex and K\"ahler data, gauge bundles
and connections, anomaly cancellation, a supersymmetry convention, and a
declared worldsheet or effective-field-theory approximation.  This paper asks
what Modal Triplet Theory (MTT) presently establishes about that complete
object.  We define a typed realization map from an upper MTT configuration to
a lower Calabi--Yau compactification record and prove the corresponding
conditional realization theorem: if one MTT source emits every required row
and its reduction factors through the standard compactification functional,
then both descriptions give the same declared lower data.  This is an exact
embedding statement, not a derivation or selection of a unique Calabi--Yau
vacuum.  We also isolate the analytic conditions under which spectral gaps,
harmonic projectors, and commuting modal operators are available.  A positive
gap and bounded harmonic projector hold for each fixed compact elliptic
problem; uniformity over moduli and operator commutation require additional
non-collapsing or product hypotheses.  A flat six-torus gives an elementary
compatibility witness but not realistic four-dimensional phenomenology.  The
selected \(q=79\) Fu--Yau program is a distinct non-K\"ahler torsional branch
and cannot serve as a proof of a strict Calabi--Yau corner.  The outcome is a
precise and reusable realization contract, with unique-vacuum selection,
moduli stabilization, and phenomenological prediction retained as
independent open problems.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and the corrected answer}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Why Calabi--Yau geometry is a natural target}

Compact Calabi--Yau threefolds organize a large and mathematically controlled
class of ten-to-four-dimensional string compactifications.  Their complex
geometry supports holomorphic bundles and cohomological spectrum
calculations; Yau's theorem supplies a Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric in each
chosen K\"ahler class; and dimensional reduction turns the chosen geometric
record into a four-dimensional effective theory
\cite{Yau1978,CandelasEtAl1985}.

MTT is organized differently.  It begins with an upper carrier, admissibility
conditions, projection or decoding maps, and fixed-point dynamics.  The
natural question is therefore not whether the word ``Calabi--Yau'' can be
attached to an MTT parameter limit.  It is whether one selected upper object
emits the complete lower record needed by a compactification and whether the
declared reduction respects that record.

\subsection{What this paper establishes}

This paper owns three limited results.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item It defines the complete lower record that an MTT-to-Calabi--Yau map must
      emit.
\item It proves the conditional realization statement obtained when every
      row of that record and the reduction factorization are supplied.
\item It identifies which common MTT analytic requirements are automatic for
      a fixed compact elliptic problem and which require extra uniformity or
      product assumptions.
\end{enumerate}
The result is a compatibility and bookkeeping theorem.  It does not construct
the missing MTT source or choose one point in the Calabi--Yau landscape.

\subsection{Logical dependency map}

The corrected order is
\[
\begin{split}
\text{selected upper MTT state}
&\longrightarrow \text{typed lower geometric record}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{Calabi--Yau and bundle equations}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{worldsheet completion at a declared order}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{four-dimensional reduction}\\
&\longrightarrow \text{stabilized vacuum and observables}.
\end{split}
\]
An implication may be used only after its own hypotheses have been checked.
In particular, a Ricci-flat metric does not choose a bundle, a bundle does not
by itself define an exact conformal field theory, and a massless
four-dimensional spectrum does not stabilize its moduli.

\section{The lower object that must be realized}
\label{sec:lower}

\subsection{Geometric Calabi--Yau datum}

\begin{definition}[Geometric Calabi--Yau record]
\label{def:cy-record}
A geometric Calabi--Yau record in complex dimension three is
\[
\cC_{\mathrm{geom}}
  =(X,I,\Omega,[\omega],g),
\]
where \(X\) is a compact connected smooth six-manifold, \(I\) is an
integrable complex structure, \(\Omega\) is a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
\((3,0)\)-form, \([\omega]\) is a K\"ahler class, and \(g\) is the
Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric in that class.  We use ``Calabi--Yau'' in the
inclusive sense \(\operatorname{Hol}(g)\subseteq SU(3)\); exact
\(SU(3)\)-holonomy is a separate condition when four-dimensional
\(N=1\) supersymmetry rather than an enhanced theory is intended.
\end{definition}

For a fixed compact K\"ahler manifold with \(c_1(X)=0\), Yau's theorem gives a
unique Ricci-flat K\"ahler metric in each fixed K\"ahler class
\cite{Yau1978}.  This is not uniqueness of the manifold, complex structure,
or K\"ahler class.  Those choices vary in families and can change the
resulting physics.

An equivalent differential-geometric presentation uses an \(SU(3)\)
structure \((\omega,\Omega)\).  If
\[
\dd\omega=0,\qquad \dd\Omega=0,
\]
with the usual algebraic compatibility and positivity conditions, then the
structure is torsion-free and the metric has holonomy contained in \(SU(3)\).
The vanishing of intrinsic torsion is a test on a supplied structure; it does
not show that MTT has emitted such a structure.

\subsection{Heterotic data are additional}

For the heterotic interpretation emphasized in the MTT corpus, the lower
record must be enlarged to
\[
\cC_{\mathrm{het}}=
(X,I,\Omega,\omega,g;
 V_{\mathrm{vis}},A_{\mathrm{vis}};
 V_{\mathrm{hid}},A_{\mathrm{hid}};
 \nabla,H,\Phi,\cG_B).
\]
Here \(V_{\mathrm{vis}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{hid}}\) are holomorphic gauge
bundles, \(A_{\mathrm{vis}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{hid}}\) are unitary
connections, \(\nabla\) is a declared connection on \(TX\), \(H\) is the
three-form flux, \(\Phi\) is the dilaton, and \(\cG_B\) denotes the global
\(B\)-field or gerbe data.

In the strict unwarped Calabi--Yau limit one normally imposes
\[
H=0,\qquad \dd\Phi=0,\qquad
F_a^{0,2}=0,\qquad F_a\wedge\omega^2=0
\quad(a=\mathrm{vis},\mathrm{hid}),
\]
together with the anomaly condition
\[
0=\dd H=\frac{\alpha'}4
\left(\tr R_\nabla\wedge R_\nabla
-\tr F_{\mathrm{vis}}\wedge F_{\mathrm{vis}}
-\tr F_{\mathrm{hid}}\wedge F_{\mathrm{hid}}\right)
\]
in one fixed trace convention, possibly modified by a declared five-brane
class.  A topological equality of second Chern classes is necessary in the
usual setting but does not automatically identify the differential
four-form representatives.

The Donaldson--Uhlenbeck--Yau correspondence supplies HYM connections on
slope-polystable holomorphic bundles; it does not turn an arbitrary smooth
bundle into a supersymmetric gauge background
\cite{Donaldson1985,UhlenbeckYau1986}.  The visible and hidden bundles, their
stability chambers, and the tangent-connection convention must therefore be
part of the input or of a separate derivation.

\subsection{Worldsheet and effective-theory rows}

Target-space supergravity equations are not identical to an exact worldsheet
construction.  A heterotic model must additionally specify the sigma-model
field content, gauge bundle, anomaly cancellation, GSO projection, modular
invariance, and the perturbative order at which conformal invariance is
claimed.  The standard embedding has a familiar \((2,2)\) description;
general stable bundles lead instead to \((0,2)\) models.  Equality of target
metrics alone does not prove equality of the complete CFT.

Likewise, a four-dimensional theory requires a declared reduction scheme,
normalizations, quantum corrections, and an energy range.  Its massless
spectrum depends on bundle-valued cohomology, not only on the Hodge numbers
of \(X\).  Its couplings depend on normalized modes and moduli.  Stabilizing
those moduli is a further dynamical problem
\cite{AndersonEtAl2011}.

\section{The MTT realization contract}
\label{sec:contract}

\subsection{Typed source and reduction maps}

Let \(\cU_{\mathrm{MTT}}\) be a declared upper configuration space and let
\[
\cR_{\mathrm{CY}}:\cU_{\mathrm{MTT}}\dashrightarrow
\cM_{\mathrm{CY}}
\]
be a possibly partial map into the space of records listed in
\cref{tab:rows}.  The map is typed: it must say which upper fields produce
the complex structure, K\"ahler form, bundles, connections, flux, dilaton,
and global data.  Isomorphic bundles without an identified
connection-preserving map are not yet the same physical row.

Let \(\cD_{\mathrm{std}}\) denote a standard compactification or
dimensional-reduction functional on its domain, and let
\(\cD_{\mathrm{MTT}}\) be the lower description assigned by MTT.  The
factorization condition is
\[
\cD_{\mathrm{MTT}}
=\cD_{\mathrm{std}}\circ\cR_{\mathrm{CY}}
\quad\text{on a declared domain } \cU_0\subseteq\cU_{\mathrm{MTT}}.
\]
This equation is the precise replacement for the old phrase ``MTT produces
exactly the same physics.''

\begin{definition}[Complete MTT--CY realization]
\label{def:complete-realization}
An upper state \(u_\ast\in\cU_0\) is a complete MTT--CY realization at a
declared perturbative order if:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item \(\cR_{\mathrm{CY}}(u_\ast)\) is defined and supplies every required
      row of \cref{tab:rows};
\item the geometric, HYM, anomaly, and supersymmetry equations hold in one
      common convention on that one record;
\item the worldsheet and effective-theory claims are restricted to the
      orders for which their completion data have been supplied; and
\item the factorization equation for \(\cD_{\mathrm{MTT}}\) holds at
      \(u_\ast\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Calabi--Yau realization]
\label{thm:conditional-realization}
If \(u_\ast\) satisfies \cref{def:complete-realization}, then
\(\cR_{\mathrm{CY}}(u_\ast)\) is a Calabi--Yau compactification record at the
declared order, and every observable in the domain of
\(\cD_{\mathrm{std}}\) satisfies
\[
\cD_{\mathrm{MTT}}(u_\ast)
=\cD_{\mathrm{std}}\!\left(\cR_{\mathrm{CY}}(u_\ast)\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first two hypotheses place the lower record in the domain of the
standard compactification functional.  The third fixes the approximation
level and prevents a target-space solution from being promoted to an exact
worldsheet statement.  The fourth hypothesis is precisely the displayed
identity.  No existence or uniqueness assertion beyond the supplied record
is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the theorem matters]
The theorem is intentionally conditional.  Its value is that it identifies
the exact upstream construction that would turn compatibility into a
derivation.  Merely finding parameters for which a written torsion class
vanishes does not satisfy the source-map or factorization rows.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What realization does not imply}

Even a complete realization does not by itself show:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item that the realized topology or bundle is unique;
\item that the corresponding fixed point attracts generic initial data;
\item that a preparation or branch law selects it physically;
\item that all moduli are stabilized; or
\item that its low-energy parameters agree with experiment.
\end{itemize}
These are higher rungs of the selection ladder developed in the companion
heterotic-selection paper \cite{NeroHeteroticSelection2026}.  They are not
reproved here.

\section{Analytic compatibility: what is automatic and what is not}
\label{sec:analytic}

\subsection{A fixed compact background}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-background spectral admissibility]
\label{prop:fixed-spectrum}
Let \(L\) be a nonnegative self-adjoint elliptic operator of Laplace type on a
Hermitian vector bundle over a fixed compact manifold.  Then \(L\) has
discrete spectrum with finite-dimensional kernel,
\[
\lambda_\ast
=\min\bigl(\Spec(L)\setminus\{0\}\bigr)>0,
\]
and the orthogonal projector \(P_{\Ker L}\) extends boundedly on every Sobolev
space \(H^s\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compact resolvent gives a discrete spectrum of finite multiplicity with no
finite accumulation point.  Elliptic regularity makes the kernel smooth and
finite dimensional.  The spectral projector is therefore smoothing of
finite rank and is bounded on all Sobolev scales.
\end{proof}

This proposition supports a fixed-background MTT gap and projector check.
It does not supply one numerical gap uniformly over all complex structures,
K\"ahler classes, bundle moduli, or degenerating metrics.

\subsection{Uniformity over a family}

For a parameter family \(L_t\), a uniform bound
\(\inf_t\lambda_\ast(L_t)>0\) requires a controlled parameter domain.  Typical
sufficient hypotheses include precompact bounded geometry, uniformly
elliptic coefficients, fixed operator domain, and no jump in kernel
dimension.  Degeneration, collapse, or an eigenvalue crossing zero destroys
the claimed uniform constant.  Thus a ``thick region'' must be specified and
certified; compactness of each fiber separately is not enough.

\subsection{When modal Laplacians commute}

\begin{lemma}[Exact product commutation]
\label{lem:product-commutation}
Let
\[
X=X_1\times X_2\times X_3,\qquad
E=E_1\boxtimes E_2\boxtimes E_3
\]
carry product metrics and product connections.  On the Hilbert tensor
product, define
\[
L_1=L_{E_1}\otimes I\otimes I,\quad
L_2=I\otimes L_{E_2}\otimes I,\quad
L_3=I\otimes I\otimes L_{E_3}.
\]
Then the self-adjoint closures of \(L_i\) strongly commute, their spectral
projectors commute, and the product Laplacian is
\[
L_E=L_1+L_2+L_3.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each operator acts on a different tensor factor.  Their spectral measures
are tensor products with the identity measures on the other factors, so the
spectral projections commute.  The product formula follows on the algebraic
tensor core and extends to the self-adjoint closure.
\end{proof}

This lemma does not apply to three arbitrary connections over the same base.
For a tensor-product bundle on one manifold, covariant derivatives act along
the same tangent directions and the rough Laplacian generally contains mixed
terms.  Commutation must then be calculated, not inferred from notation.
Moreover, resolving an orbifold usually destroys exact product
factorization even when a torus limit had it.

\subsection{HYM compatibility is conditional}

If holomorphic bundles \(E_i\) are HYM in one K\"ahler chamber, their tensor
product connection has curvature
\[
F_{E_1\otimes E_2}
=F_{E_1}\otimes I+I\otimes F_{E_2}.
\]
Its contracted curvature is the sum of the two central HYM constants.  This
is a useful construction, but it does not establish stability, the desired
structure group, anomaly cancellation, or a visible-sector index.  Those
remain separate rows.

\section{An exact compatibility witness and its limits}
\label{sec:witness}

\subsection{The flat six-torus}

Let
\[
X=T^2_1\times T^2_2\times T^2_3
\]
with complex coordinates \(z^1,z^2,z^3\),
\[
\omega=\frac{\ii}{2}\sum_{a=1}^3\dd z^a\wedge\dd\bar z^a,
\qquad
\Omega=\dd z^1\wedge\dd z^2\wedge\dd z^3.
\]
Then \(\dd\omega=\dd\Omega=0\), the product metric is flat, and the scalar
Laplacian splits into three strongly commuting factors.  With trivial flat
visible, hidden, and tangent connections and \(H=0\), the differential
Bianchi identity vanishes term by term.  This gives a completely explicit
lower compatibility witness for the geometric, spectral, product, and
leading-order anomaly rows.

The witness is deliberately modest.  The torus has holonomy strictly smaller
than \(SU(3)\), produces enhanced supersymmetry, and the trivial gauge choice
does not yield a chiral Standard-Model-like spectrum.  It also does not prove
that an MTT source emits the record.  It shows only that the lower target
class is nonempty and that MTT-style analytic conditions are not mutually
inconsistent with a simple Calabi--Yau background.

\subsection{Why the former orbifold example is withdrawn}

The previous version named a \(T^6/\mathbb Z_3\) resolution but omitted the
promised construction.  A valid orbifold model would have to specify the
group action, fixed loci, crepant resolution, K\"ahler chamber, gauge shift
or bundle, twisted sectors, anomaly constraints, and the relation between
orbifold and resolved descriptions \cite{DixonEtAl1985}.  Exact product
commutation need not persist after resolution.  Without those rows there is
no worked compactification and no parameter map to audit.

\section{What Calabi--Yau machinery may be inherited}
\label{sec:inherit}

\subsection{Massless fields}

Once \(X\) and a holomorphic bundle \(V\) are supplied, candidate massless
fields are organized by bundle-valued cohomology groups.  For example, the
multiplicity of a representation associated with a bundle \(U\) is computed
from groups such as \(H^1(X,U)\), subject to the chosen embedding and
conventions.  This is a conditional calculation on \((X,V)\); Hodge numbers
of \(X\) alone do not determine the charged spectrum.

\subsection{Yukawa couplings}

For normalized harmonic representatives \(\psi_i\), a holomorphic Yukawa
coupling has the schematic form
\[
Y_{ijk}\sim
\int_X\Omega\wedge
\psi_i\wedge\psi_j\wedge\psi_k,
\]
with bundle contractions and normalization factors fixed by the model.
Writing \(Y_{ijk}=Y_{ijk}(\Theta_{\mathrm{MTT}})\) is justified only after the
source map derives \(X,\Omega,V,\psi_i\) and their normalization from
\(\Theta_{\mathrm{MTT}}\).  Otherwise the equation is a change of variables,
not a prediction.

\subsection{Gauge and gravitational couplings}

Dimensional reduction similarly yields volume- and dilaton-dependent gauge
and gravitational couplings.  Such formulas become MTT predictions only if
the relevant volume, dilaton, normalization, and threshold data are selected
upstream.  A spectral gap can control a Kaluza--Klein scale on a fixed
background, but it does not by itself fix the overall compactification scale.

\subsection{Moduli and stabilization}

Yau's theorem leaves the complex and K\"ahler moduli unspecified.  Bundle
holomorphy can obstruct some complex-structure deformations through the
Atiyah map, and explicit models can stabilize subsets of moduli
\cite{AndersonEtAl2011}.  Other moduli require additional perturbative or
nonperturbative effects.  Therefore ``the MTT fixed point is stable'' and
``all compactification moduli are stabilized'' are different statements
about different operators.

\subsection{Mirror symmetry and thresholds}

Mirror symmetry, threshold corrections, and special geometry may be studied
after a model and approximation scheme are fixed.  They are not automatic
consequences of a three-block notation.  In particular, an MTT duality map
must be constructed and shown to intertwine the relevant period, bundle, and
quantum data before it can be identified with a mirror map.

\subsection{What the current repository results contribute}

The current MTT program has exact finite \(q=79\) arithmetic, a literal
finite rank-two Cech witness, a certified finite projected HYM approximation,
and a selected rank-two continuum HYM witness with a Wiener contraction
certificate.  These are substantial results at their declared tiers.  They
do not provide a Calabi--Yau topology, a rank-three visible bundle, a hidden
bundle, or an MTT-to-CY source map.  They therefore appear here as contextual
evidence and reusable tools, not as direct proof of
\cref{thm:conditional-realization}.

The companion Hull--Strominger paper states the required flow-intertwining
bridge \cite{NeroMTTStrominger2026}.  The physical visible--hidden endpoint
still requires one common carrier with the required bundles, HYM
connections, anomaly identity, and global flux data.

\section{Selection and completion boundary}
\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{Compatibility, existence, and selection}

Three claims must remain distinct.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.7cm,style=nextline]
\item[Compatibility]
The lower equations and MTT analytic requirements can hold simultaneously.
The torus witness establishes this in a simple non-phenomenological case.

\item[Realization]
One selected upper state emits a complete lower record and the MTT reduction
factors through the standard compactification.  This is the hypothesis of
\cref{thm:conditional-realization}; it has not yet been constructed.

\item[Selection]
An MTT preparation, evolution, or branch law chooses that realization among
alternatives.  This requires more than equation solving or local isolation.
\end{description}

\subsection{A completion contract}

To promote the present paper from a conditional map to a physical
Calabi--Yau result, a future construction must provide:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item a selected compact complex threefold \(X\), not merely a local
      \(SU(3)\)-structure ansatz;
\item an MTT-derived complex structure, K\"ahler class, holomorphic volume
      form, and Ricci-flat metric;
\item explicit visible and hidden holomorphic bundles in a common stability
      chamber, with HYM connections;
\item the differential anomaly identity and global \(B\)-field data in one
      convention;
\item a source map preserving the relevant connections and holonomies;
\item the worldsheet completion and perturbative order actually claimed;
\item a dimensional-reduction factorization with normalized modes; and
\item a stabilization and selection mechanism if a unique physical vacuum is
      claimed.
\end{enumerate}
These rows are deliberately stronger than a list of matching dimensions or
isomorphic abstract groups.  They identify the actual object on which the
calculation is performed.

\subsection{Conclusion}
