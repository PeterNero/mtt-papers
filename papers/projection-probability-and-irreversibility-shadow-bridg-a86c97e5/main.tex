\begin{abstract}
This corrected successor separates three mathematical questions that the earlier
shadow-bridge paper conflated: whether upstairs dynamics descends to a deterministic
shadow map, whether an upstairs state can be recovered from shadow data, and whether
probability laws in measurement, black-hole radiation and cosmology have one selected
common source. We prove the exact fiber-preservation criterion for descent and the
injectivity-on-a-code criterion for exact recovery. These results correct the earlier
right-inverse argument: a noninjective surjection may have a measurable section, while
still failing to recover the lost state. We also prove that noninjective projection
alone does not imply an arrow of time; a quotient is reversible when its fiber
equivalence relation is invariant in both time directions. At the probabilistic level,
one upper measure and three explicit outcome maps do yield three pushforwards, but
arbitrary target weights can always be represented by basin sizes, so basin
representation alone derives neither the Born rule, Hawking weights nor a cosmological
measure. Measurement instruments and island constructions are consequently described
as updates or recovery on restricted codes/algebras, not as global inverses. The three
physical domains remain useful structural shadow bridges. Their theorem-level
unification requires an MTT-selected upper measure, explicit pushforward maps and
independent derivations of all three target laws.
\end{abstract}

\section{Correction and claim tiers}

The paper studies an invertible upstairs flow and a many-to-one shadow description.
It does not claim that the current MTT corpus has derived quantum measurement,
black-hole evaporation or cosmological probabilities from one measure. The corrected
claim tiers are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact mathematics:} descent, reversibility, restricted recovery,
pushforward and nonuniqueness theorems.
\item \textbf{Established domain mathematics:} quantum instruments and code recovery,
semiclassical Hawking radiation, and measures on declared cosmological model spaces.
\item \textbf{MTT shadow bridges:} possible common structural interpretations,
conditional on source maps that have not yet been selected.
\end{enumerate}

The MTT geometry is denoted $Y_4\times X_6$. The local Circle--Lens--Nil filtration
is not used as a literal product topology. No result below depends on identifying
compact phase with physical time.

\section{Descent of upstairs dynamics}

Let $\cX$ and $\cY$ be standard Borel spaces, let $P:\cX\to\cY$ be a measurable
surjection, and let $\Phi_t:\cX\to\cX$ be a measurable bijection. Define the fiber
equivalence relation
\[
 x\sim_P x'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P(x)=P(x').
\]

\begin{theorem}[Descent theorem]\label{thm:descent}
There exists a map $T_t:\cY\to\cY$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 T_t\circ P=P\circ\Phi_t
\label{eq:descent}
\end{equation}
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 P(x)=P(x')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 P(\Phi_t x)=P(\Phi_t x')
\label{eq:fiber}
\end{equation}
for all $x,x'\in\cX$. If it exists, $T_t$ is unique. It is measurable whenever
the quotient map has the corresponding measurable factorization property.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $T_t$ exists, applying it to $P(x)=P(x')$ gives condition
\eqref{eq:fiber}. Conversely, define $T_t(y)=P(\Phi_t x)$ for any $x$ with
$P(x)=y$. Condition \eqref{eq:fiber} makes the definition independent of the
representative. Surjectivity gives uniqueness.
\end{proof}

Thus a many-to-one shadow does not automatically possess autonomous shadow dynamics.
When the fiber-preservation condition fails, two upstairs representatives of one
shadow state have different shadow futures. The correct output is then a relation,
stochastic kernel, memory-dependent evolution, or an enlarged state, not a
single-valued $T_t$.

\begin{theorem}[Reversible quotient criterion]\label{thm:reversible}
Suppose $\{\Phi_t\}_{t\in\mathbb R}$ is a measurable group and the fiber relation is
preserved by every $\Phi_t$, positive and negative. Then the descended maps form a
group,
\[
 T_{t+s}=T_tT_s,\qquad T_t^{-1}=T_{-t}.
\]
Consequently, noninjectivity of $P$ alone does not imply irreversible shadow
dynamics or an arrow of time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Using surjectivity of $P$ and \eqref{eq:descent},
\[
T_{t+s}P=P\Phi_{t+s}=P\Phi_t\Phi_s=T_tP\Phi_s=T_tT_sP.
\]
Therefore $T_{t+s}=T_tT_s$. Taking $s=-t$ gives the inverse.
\end{proof}

Irreversibility can arise if only a forward semigroup descends, if the retained
description is repeatedly coarse-grained, if an environment is traced out, or if a
boundary/initial condition selects one branch. Each requires its own theorem.

\section{Recovery is not a right inverse of a surjection}

The earlier paper argued that noninjectivity forbids a right inverse. This is false.
For example, $P:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$, $P(x)=x^2$, is noninjective but has the
measurable section $S(y)=\sqrt y$ with $P S=\Id$. The section chooses a representative;
it does not recover the sign of the original input.

Let $\cC\subseteq\cX$ be a declared code or admissible subset. At time $t$, exact
state recovery means a map
\[
 R_t:P(\Phi_t\cC)\to\Phi_t\cC
\]
such that
\[
 R_t(P(\Phi_t x))=\Phi_t x\qquad(x\in\cC).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact restricted-recovery theorem]\label{thm:recovery}
An exact recovery map $R_t$ exists if and only if
$P|_{\Phi_t\cC}$ is injective. If $\Phi_t\cC$ is Borel, the spaces are standard
Borel and $P|_{\Phi_t\cC}$ is a Borel injection, then its inverse on the image is
Borel measurable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $R_t$ exists and $P(z)=P(z')$ for $z,z'\in\Phi_t\cC$, then
$z=R_tP(z)=R_tP(z')=z'$. Conversely, injectivity defines the inverse of the
restriction. The measurable statement is the standard Borel injection theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No global recovery after a collision]
If two distinct states in the declared code have the same shadow image, no recovery
map can restore both. A section may still return one representative, but cannot be
an inverse on the original code.
\end{corollary}

This is the correct mathematical form of an admissibility barrier: either descent
fails because a fiber splits into different shadow futures, or recovery fails because
the shadow channel identifies distinct code states. Gap closure or projector
discontinuity may cause such a failure, but the failure must be checked rather than
inferred from terminology.

\section{Quantum channels and restricted codes}

Let $\cN$ be a completely positive trace-preserving channel and let $\cC$ now denote
a set of density operators on a code subspace.

\begin{proposition}[Channel recovery obstruction]\label{prop:channel}
If there are distinct $\rho,\sigma\in\cC$ with
$\cN(\rho)=\cN(\sigma)$, no channel $\cR$ can satisfy
$\cR\cN(\omega)=\omega$ for every $\omega\in\cC$. Exact recovery is therefore a
code-relative property, not a global consequence of projection.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Applying a purported recovery channel to the equality would give $\rho=\sigma$.
\end{proof}

For a finite-dimensional quantum code and noise operators $E_a$, the
Knill--Laflamme conditions~\cite{KnillLaflamme1997}
\[
 P_{\cC}E_a^\dagger E_bP_{\cC}=\alpha_{ab}P_{\cC}
\]
are the standard necessary and sufficient conditions for exact correction of the
declared error set. Island and entanglement-wedge reconstruction should be compared
with this restricted algebra/code language. They are not global inverses from all
radiation states to a unique microscopic bulk state.

\subsection{Measurement instruments are updates}

A quantum instrument is a family of completely positive trace-nonincreasing maps
$\{\cI_i\}$ whose sum is trace preserving. Its probabilities and conditional states
are
\[
 p_i=\Tr\cI_i(\rho),\qquad
 \rho_i=\frac{\cI_i(\rho)}{p_i}\quad(p_i>0).
\]
This is a selected operational law. It is not a right inverse of premeasurement
dynamics. Projection language may encode the outcome partition, but it does not
derive the instrument, the Born functional, or objective single-outcome collapse.

\section{Coarse graining and entropy}

Information loss, entropy growth and dynamical noninvertibility are related but not
identical.

\begin{proposition}[No entropy arrow from noninjectivity alone]\label{prop:entropy}
A noninjective channel or shadow map does not by itself imply monotonic increase of
von Neumann entropy. Unital quantum channels do not decrease entropy in finite
dimension, but general nonunital channels may decrease it. Likewise,
Theorem~\ref{thm:reversible} provides noninjective projections with reversible
quotient dynamics.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unital statement follows from majorization. As a counterexample for general
channels, amplitude damping sends every state asymptotically to a pure ground state,
so some mixed-state entropies decrease. The reversible quotient statement follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:reversible}.
\end{proof}

A physical arrow therefore needs additional input: an initial-condition asymmetry,
a dissipative semigroup, repeated coarse graining, a bath state, or a branch
selection rule. The current MTT branch axiom may serve as such input only after its
physical source and compatibility conditions are proved.

\section{One upper measure and three pushforwards}

Let $(\cX,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a probability space and let
$O_j:\cX\to\cY_j$ be measurable maps for
$j\in\{\mathrm{meas},\mathrm{BH},\mathrm{cos}\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Common-source pushforward theorem]\label{thm:push}
Each domain receives the probability measure
\[
 \nu_j=(O_j)_*\mu,\qquad
 \nu_j(A)=\mu(O_j^{-1}(A)).
\]
For a finite outcome partition
$B_{j,a}=O_j^{-1}(\{a\})$, its weights are
$\nu_j(a)=\mu(B_{j,a})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the definition of pushforward measure. Measurability makes inverse images
events, and countable additivity and normalization are inherited from $\mu$.
\end{proof}

This theorem says exactly what a common basin measure would mean. To use it
physically, one must provide the same explicit $\mu$ and all three selected maps.
Merely writing three unrelated measures with the same symbol does not establish a
common source.

\begin{theorem}[Basin representation is not a derivation]\label{thm:nonunique}
Let $(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ be any probability vector. On the atomless probability space
$([0,1],\mathcal B,dx)$ there exists a measurable partition
$[0,1]=B_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup B_n$ with $dx(B_i)=p_i$. There are generally
infinitely many such partitions. Hence reproducing target probabilities as basin
volumes has no predictive content unless the measure and basins are selected
independently of those target probabilities.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $a_0=0$ and $a_i=\sum_{k\leq i}p_k$, and take
$B_i=[a_{i-1},a_i)$, with the final endpoint included. Measure-preserving
rearrangements generate further partitions with the same weights.
\end{proof}

\section{The three probability laws remain distinct}

\subsection{Born probabilities}

For a density operator $\rho$ and projective outcomes $\Pi_i$, the Born weights are
\[
 p_i=\Tr(\rho\Pi_i).
\]
Gleason's theorem~\cite{Gleason1957} shows, under its dimension and additivity
hypotheses, that
probability measures on the projection lattice have this density-operator form. It
does not select $\rho$, an MTT outcome map, or a physical collapse mechanism. A basin
model derives Born weights only if its independently selected pushforward is proved
equal to this functional for all allowed states and measurement contexts.

\subsection{Hawking weights}

Semiclassical Hawking occupation factors~\cite{Hawking1975} arise from quantum-field mode propagation
and Bogoliubov coefficients on a black-hole background. They are not a probability
measure on the same sample space as a laboratory quantum instrument merely because
both contain normalized weights. A common-source MTT theorem must construct
$O_{\mathrm{BH}}$ and recover the temperature, greybody factors and state dependence,
not insert them as basin labels.

Island formulae and entanglement-wedge reconstruction
~\cite{Penington2019,AEMM2019} provide entropy and recovery
statements in declared gravitational/holographic settings. Their natural comparison
here is restricted recovery on a code algebra. They do not establish the global MTT
projection or a common Born--Hawking measure.

\subsection{Cosmological measures}

A cosmological probability law requires a declared solution/history space, symplectic
or other measure, gauge quotient, cutoff and conditioning prescription
~\cite{GHS1987}. Even a
canonical measure on a finite model can become ambiguous after regularization of an
infinite ensemble. Inflationary attraction can change distributions dynamically but
does not by itself select the initial measure. Therefore a cosmological basin map is
another independent source obligation.

\begin{corollary}[Measure-separation verdict]
The Born, Hawking and cosmological laws may be three pushforwards of one upper MTT
measure, but the current corpus has not proved that statement. Until one
$(\cX,\mu)$ and three explicit maps are supplied, the relationship is a structural
shadow bridge rather than theorem-level probability equivalence.
\end{corollary}

\section{Computational irreducibility}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional undecidability transfer]\label{prop:undecidable}
Suppose an MTT basin-membership problem is computably defined and there is a
computable reduction from the halting problem to it. Then no algorithm decides
membership for all admissible inputs.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such a decision algorithm, composed with the reduction, would decide the halting
problem.
\end{proof}

This conditional proposition is exact but does not establish undecidability for a
specific MTT barrier. That requires an explicit encoding and reduction. Complexity
or sensitive dependence alone is insufficient.

\section{MTT promotion contract}

A theorem unifying the three domains must emit:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a selected upstairs measurable or quantum state space $\cX$;
\item one normalized measure/state $\mu$ selected without using the three target laws;
\item a flow or channel and a proof of the descent condition on each claimed domain;
\item explicit outcome maps $O_{\mathrm{meas}},O_{\mathrm{BH}},O_{\mathrm{cos}}$;
\item a proof that their pushforwards equal the Born, Hawking and cosmological laws,
including domain-specific corrections;
\item declared code algebras and recovery maps for every island or measurement claim;
\item an independent source of any time arrow or entropy monotonicity; and
\item a computable reduction before any MTT undecidability claim is promoted.
\end{enumerate}

The current finite $q=79$ operators, shared-circle candidate and preprojection
language program may help construct these objects. They do not yet provide the
selected measurement capture measure identified as open in the quantization audit.

\section{Version delta}

Relative to the previous version, this successor:
\begin{itemize}
\item replaces the false no-right-inverse argument by the descent and exact-recovery
theorems;
\item distinguishes a measurable section from recovery of the original state;
\item proves that a noninjective shadow can still have reversible quotient dynamics;
\item treats POVM instruments and islands as updates or restricted code recovery;
\item separates structural analogies from a common probability measure;
\item proves the nonuniqueness of basin representations of arbitrary weights;
\item no longer identifies Born, Hawking and cosmological weights without one upper
measure and three derived pushforwards;
\item makes entropy growth and the time arrow independent source obligations; and
\item reduces computational irreducibility to a conditional transfer theorem pending
an explicit MTT reduction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

Projection can hide information, obstruct autonomous descent, or prevent recovery,
but these are different statements. The corrected mathematics identifies exactly
which one holds. Likewise, basin measures can represent probabilities, but
representation is not derivation. Measurement, black-hole reconstruction and
cosmology remain genuinely suggestive MTT shadow bridges because all involve
restricted observables, coarse descriptions and source-selection problems. Their
strong unification is still open: it requires one selected upper measure and three
independently computed pushforwards. This narrower formulation preserves the useful
idea while making every future promotion testable.
