# Introduction

Causal set theory proposes that spacetime is fundamentally discrete, consisting of elementary events partially ordered by causality. Measurement theory and collapse models, by contrast, treat events as irreducible outcomes of quantum dynamics, often without spacetime ontology. These two notions of “event” are typically regarded as independent.

In this paper we show that they are not independent. Within Modal Triplet Theory (MTT), both causal set elements and measurement/collapse events arise as the same objects: *selection events* generated by coherence breakdown under noninvertible projection. Spacetime discreteness, causal order, and irreversibility are therefore different shadows of a single reduced-dynamical mechanism.

This work extends earlier results showing that causal sets arise as an effective limit of MTT. Here we go further by identifying the physical meaning of causal set elements as selection events and by deriving their density, ordering, and statistical symmetries from coherent-sector data.

3.  admissibility and stability-margin parameters.

# Selection Events in the Coherent Sector

<div class="definition">

**Definition 3** (Admissible basins). Let $`\mathcal{C}`$ denote the coherent sector on the slab. Admissible configurations lie in dynamically stable basins $`\{\mathcal{B}_\alpha\}`$ of the projected dynamics.

</div>

<div class="definition">

**Definition 4** (Selection event). A *selection event* is the spacetime-localized occurrence of a trajectory exiting an admissible basin $`\mathcal{B}_\alpha`$ and being irreversibly captured into a different basin $`\mathcal{B}_\beta`$ under projection.

</div>

<div id="prop:irreversible" class="proposition">

**Proposition 5** (Irreversibility of selection events). *Selection events are irreversible on the slab: once capture into $`\mathcal{B}_\beta`$ occurs, return to $`\mathcal{B}_\alpha`$ with nonzero probability is excluded by contractivity in $`\mathcal{B}_\beta`$.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Exit from $`\mathcal{B}_\alpha`$ occurs at loss of a stability margin. After capture into $`\mathcal{B}_\beta`$, all unstable directions are contracted. Standard basin stability arguments imply return probability below truncation tolerance. ◻

</div>

# Emergent Causal Order from Basin Selection

<div class="definition">

**Definition 6** (Event dependence). For selection events $`e_1,e_2`$, write $`e_1\prec e_2`$ if the basin selected by $`e_1`$ is a necessary condition for the admissibility of the trajectory leading to $`e_2`$.

</div>

<div id="thm:order" class="theorem">

**Theorem 7** (Emergent causal order). *The relation $`\prec`$ defines a partial order on selection events.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Transitivity follows from composition of basin dependencies. Antisymmetry follows from irreversibility (Proposition <a href="#prop:irreversible" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="prop:irreversible">5</a>). Cycles would require return to an exited basin, excluded by admissibility. ◻

# Event Density, Lorentz Invariance, and Deviations

<div id="prop:density" class="proposition">

**Proposition 10** (Event density). *The mean density of selection events per unit four-volume is fixed by the bottleneck vector $`\Theta`$, in particular by the spectral gap and stability margins.*

</div>

<div id="thm:lorentz" class="theorem">

**Theorem 11** (Statistical Lorentz invariance). *On scales large compared to the truncation scale, the distribution of selection events is statistically Lorentz invariant.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* The coherent sector is Lorentz covariant. Projection introduces no preferred frame. Event statistics therefore inherit covariance in expectation values. ◻

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 12*. Near coherence-breakdown regions or strong measurement, event clustering and Lorentz-violating correlations are predicted deviations from Poisson sprinkling.

</div>

# Relation to Causal Set Theory and Measurement Physics

Causal set theory postulates discreteness and Poisson sprinkling to preserve Lorentz invariance. Here discreteness and approximate Lorentz invariance are derived, and the sprinkling density is fixed by $`\Theta`$ rather than arbitrary.

In measurement theory, collapse events are postulated as irreducible. Here they are identified with selection events that also generate causal order. Thus measurement events and spacetime events are the same objects viewed through different diagnostics.

# Cross-Sector Closure and Falsifiability

<div id="thm:closure" class="theorem">

**Theorem 13** (Cross-sector closure). *Within the coherent universality class on slabs, the following are fixed functions of the same bottleneck vector $`\Theta`$:*

1.  *event density and causal order;*

2.  *collapse and measurement thresholds;*

3.  *ultraviolet completion data;*

4.  *canonical geometric discreteness.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Each quantity depends only on projection-induced truncation, spectral gaps, and stability margins encoded in $`\Theta`$. Independent tuning would require modifying $`\Theta`$, affecting all sectors simultaneously. ◻

</div>

<div class="corollary">

**Corollary 14** (Falsifiability). *If any sector requires parameter values incompatible with $`\Theta`$ inferred from another, the coherent-sector hypothesis is falsified.*

</div>

# Conclusions

We have shown that causal sets and measurement events arise as the same shadow of coherence-selection events under projection. Causal order, discreteness, and irreversibility are unified as reduced-dynamical consequences of admissibility and stability in the coherent sector.

Causal set discreteness is therefore effective rather than fundamental; Poisson sprinkling is an approximation whose density and deviations are fixed by coherent bottleneck data. Measurement collapse and spacetime events are identical objects viewed through different lenses.

All results are slab-local and admissibility-conditioned. Together with independent shadow constructions for collapse, measurement-induced transitions, ultraviolet completion, and canonical geometry, this provides a tightly overconstrained validation framework for coherence-based fundamental dynamics.
