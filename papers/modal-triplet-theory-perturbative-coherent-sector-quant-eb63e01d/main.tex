\begin{abstract}
We separate the established low-energy quantum-gravity tier of Modal Triplet
Theory (MTT) from its ultraviolet-completion problem. On a declared globally
hyperbolic coherent background, the selected two-derivative Einstein--TEGR
action admits the standard perturbative BV/BRST effective-field-theory
construction, with gauge-invariant observables and the usual infrared
graviton sector. We then re-examine the spectral proper-time (SPT) proposal.
An exact graph theorem shows that Gaussian-damped lines control all loop
directions if and only if their loop-momentum matrix has full rank; the mere
presence of one damped graviton line is insufficient. We also prove that a
nonzero propagator cannot simultaneously have a positive
K\"all\'en--Lehmann/Stieltjes representation and permanent Gaussian decay in
the same Euclidean spectral variable. Thus the earlier Stieltjes proof of
unitarity for the Gaussian propagator is withdrawn, and SPT is retained only
as a conditional filter/regulator model. The selected ultraviolet candidate
is instead the q79 Fu--Yau heterotic branch. Conditional on an exact
anomaly-free modular $(0,2)$ worldsheet theory, GSO projection,
factorization, and quantum-BV vertices, every fixed-genus,
fixed-multiplicity amplitude has no local ultraviolet divergence. The
holomorphic/HYM bundle, differential Bianchi representative, exact
worldsheet packet, and all-genus or nonperturbative completion remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%========================
Perturbative quantum gravity in four dimensions, when naively quantized, is famously
non-renormalizable. The current MTT construction closes the interacting
low-energy Einstein--TEGR and quantum-GR EFT parity tier; it does not turn the
four-dimensional Einstein action into a UV-complete theory. This paper
identifies precisely what the proposed \emph{Spectral Proper-Time} (SPT)
filter does prove, corrects two overstrong implications in the previous
version, and states the independent heterotic route selected by the q79
geometry.

\paragraph{Scope.}
All four-dimensional results are \emph{perturbative} around a declared
coherent MTT fixed point under bounded-geometry, hyperbolicity, Hadamard-state,
and anomaly-cancellation hypotheses. ``Background independence'' is meant
only in the BRST/background-field sense. Neither a preferred cosmic state nor
a nonperturbative definition is inferred.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem: perturbative tier and ultraviolet boundary]\label{thm:main}
Let $(Y_4,\bar g)$ be a globally hyperbolic bounded-geometry background, let
the selected leading action be the Einstein--TEGR action coupled to
anomaly-free matter, and choose a Hadamard state and admissible BV gauge
fixing. If, in addition, an SPT filter is imposed, let $C_\Gamma$ be the
matrix sending independent loop momenta to the momenta of Gaussian-damped
lines in a graph $\Gamma$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Perturbative EFT consistency.} Epstein--Glaser/pAQFT
renormalization and the standard BV induction construct the theory order by
order. If the relevant ghost-number-one local cohomology is trivial, local
counterterms restore the QME and physical observables are gauge independent.
This is effective-field-theory renormalization, not ultraviolet finiteness.
\item \textbf{Exact filtered-graph criterion.} Polynomial vertices are
Gaussian-dominated in every ultraviolet loop direction precisely when
$\operatorname{rank}C_\Gamma=L$, the number of independent loops. If the
rank is smaller, the filter leaves an undamped ultraviolet direction and
ordinary forest subtractions or power counting are still required.
\item \textbf{Positivity--damping no-go.} A nonzero positive
Källén--Lehmann/Stieltjes propagator has a $1/x$ lower tail on some positive
spectral interval and therefore cannot obey permanent Gaussian decay
$O(e^{-\tau x}/x)$ for $\tau>0$. The Gaussian model consequently needs an
independent unitarity and causality construction.
\item \textbf{Infrared recovery.} At the selected two-derivative tier, the
TT propagator and interactions are those of GR (equivalently TEGR up to its
boundary term), with higher-derivative operators treated as EFT corrections.
\item \textbf{Conditional heterotic inheritance.} If the same q79 Fu--Yau
background supplies an exact anomaly-free modular heterotic $(0,2)$ SCFT,
tachyon-free GSO projection, factorization, quantum-BV vertices, and an
infrared/tadpole prescription, every fixed-genus fixed-multiplicity amplitude
is free of local ultraviolet divergences. This coefficientwise statement
does not prove convergence of the genus expansion or a nonperturbative theory.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Standing assumptions and operator setting}
\label{sec:assumptions}
We assume throughout:
\begin{itemize}
  \item bounded geometry on $(Y_4,\bar g)$ and on the internal sector;
  \item a positive spectral gap $\lambda_\ast>0$ above the coherent zero modes of $A_{\mathrm{int}}$;
  \item bounded projectors on Sobolev scales and a bounded metric shape map $B$;
  \item commuting external/internal blocks $[E,A_{\mathrm{int}}]=0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{SPT hypothesis (proper-time gap).}
In addition to the standing geometric and spectral assumptions above, the optional SPT
model assumes a spectral filter with a strictly positive proper-time gap. Boundedness of a
projector does not imply such a gap. Concretely, we assume the coherent projector can be realized (on the relevant slab) by completely
monotone spectral filters on the external and internal blocks,
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}} = \varphi(E)\,\psi(A_{\mathrm{int}}),
\qquad
\varphi(z)=\int_{\tau_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{\infty} e^{-t z}\,d\mu_{\mathrm{ext}}(t),
\qquad
\psi(\lambda)=\int_{\tau_{\mathrm{int}}}^{\infty} e^{-s \lambda}\,d\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(s),
\]
with finite positive measures $\mu_{\mathrm{ext}},\mu_{\mathrm{int}}$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{ext}},\tau_{\mathrm{int}}>0$.
We write $\tau_0:=\min\{\tau_{\mathrm{ext}},\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\}>0$.
This is the conditional SPT coarse-graining scale used in the filtered subsections; it is
restated in Appendix~A (Assumption~A.3) and Appendix~F. No primitive q79 selection of
this support gap is claimed.

\paragraph{Stability under small off-diagonal couplings.}
If $A = E \oplus A_{\mathrm{int}} + V$ with $V$ symmetric and $A_0$--form-bounded relative to
$A_0:=E\oplus A_{\mathrm{int}}$ with relative bound strictly less than $1$, i.e.\ there exist
$a\in[0,1)$ and $b\ge 0$ such that
\[
|\langle u,Vu\rangle| \le a\,\langle u,A_0 u\rangle + b\,\|u\|^2
\qquad \text{for all }u\in\mathrm{Dom}(A_0^{1/2}),
\]
then by Kato--Rellich (and Trotter product control) the heat semigroup satisfies, uniformly for
$t\in(0,1]$,
\[
e^{-tA} = e^{-tE}\,e^{-tA_{\mathrm{int}}} + \mathcal{O}(t)
\quad \text{in operator norm on }H^1,
\]
with constants depending continuously on $(a,b)$ on bounded-geometry families. In particular,
all SPT bounds persist with renormalised constants $(\tau_0,C_0)\mapsto(\tau'_0,C'_0)$.
This includes mild warp/twist couplings in factorised backgrounds.

\begin{lemma}[Proper-time representation for the projected propagator]\label{lem:proper-time}
Assume the standing assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:assumptions} and, independently, the positive
proper-time support-gap hypothesis above. Let $P=I\circ \Pi$ be the coherent projector and
let $A$ be the projected linearized graviton operator on the TT sector, with spectrum bounded below by $\lambda_\ast>0$
on the $\Qcoh$-sector. Then there exists $\tau_0>0$ and constants $C,c>0$ such that the projected propagator admits
a proper-time representation with uniform bound
\[
\|\Delta_{\mathrm{prop}}(k)\|\le C\,\frac{e^{-\tau_0 k^2}}{k^2+\lambda_\ast},
\]
where $k$ is the Fourier variable in locally geodesic coordinates on the slab and the bound is uniform on bounded-geometry charts.
\end{lemma}


\begin{theorem}[SPT factorization under a support-gap hypothesis]\label{thm:SPT}
Under the above assumptions, including the positive support gap, there exists a bounded operator $B_0$ such that
\[
B \;=\; e^{-\tfrac{\tau_0}{2}E}\,B_0\,e^{-\tfrac{\tau_0}{2}A_{\mathrm{int}}},
\]
with $\|B_0\|\le C_0$. Consequently,
\[
\|\Dprop(k)\|\;\le\; \frac{C_0}{k^2+\lambda_\ast}\,e^{-\tau_0 k^2},
\]
so each internal graviton line carries a universal Gaussian factor $e^{-\tau_0 k^2}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof]
By hypothesis, the coherent projector is realized by spectral filters on $E$ and $A_{\mathrm{int}}$
whose representing measures have positive lower support. By bounded-geometry parametrix transfer,
$DG(\Psi_\ast)e^{-t(-{\Dprop}_{Y_4})}=e^{-ctE}S_t$ with $c\in(0,1]$ and bounded $S_t$.
Choosing $\tau_0\le \min\{c\tau_{\mathrm{ext}},\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\}$ and factoring
the semigroups yields the claimed decomposition. The proof consumes rather than derives
the positive lower support.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Universality under projector realization]\label{lem:universality}
Let $\Pi_{1,2}$ be two bounded realizations of the same coherent range and
$B_i=DG(\Psi_\ast)\circ \Pi_i$. If $B_2=B_1U$ with $U$ positive and commuting with
$E,A_{\mathrm{int}}$, then ${\Dprop}_1={\Dprop}_2$. Physical correlators and the Gaussian bound
are independent of projector filter details.
\end{lemma}

\section{Propagator and positivity}

\paragraph{Scope of positivity and unitarity.}
For the local Einstein--TEGR EFT, perturbative unitarity is asserted on the
BRST/BV cohomology of observables in the EG/pAQFT framework, where gauge

physical Gaussian-dressed kernel is made in this version.
\paragraph{Relation to prior frameworks.}
The local theory uses the curved-spacetime EG/pAQFT locality and covariance
programme. The Stieltjes and Osterwalder--Schrader literature
\cite{Kallen1952,Lehmann1954,Widder1941,Schilling2012} instead supplies a
diagnostic that the permanent Gaussian proposal fails, not its proof.

The Gaussian/entire damping below is a \emph{conditional} SPT filter requiring
the positive support-gap hypothesis. It is not derived from bounded projection alone.

\subsection{Geometric origin of the UV regulator}
\label{subsec:UV-regulator}
%==========================

\paragraph{Statement.}
Within the optional SPT support-gap hypothesis, a filtered coherent-sector model can be
formed from (i) the bounded, joint harmonic projection
$\Pi := \Pi_{B_1}\Pi_{B_2}\Pi_{B_3}$ on the internal bundles $B_n$, (ii) the internal
pushforward $I$ to the 4D base $Y_4$, and (iii) a background-covariant coarse–graining
on $Y_4$ implemented by a regulator $R_k(-\nabla^2)$ that is a positive function of the
background Laplacian. The harmonic projection suppresses vertical modes; it does not by
itself suppress arbitrarily large four-dimensional covectors. The FRG term is an auxiliary
infrared coarse-graining device.

\paragraph{Bandlimiting by coherent projection.}
Let $A := \kappa_1\Delta_{B_1} + \kappa_2\Delta_{B_2} + \kappa_3\Delta_{B_3} + \varepsilon\,\Delta_{Y}$ 
on the 10D bundle (with $\kappa_n>0$, $\varepsilon>0$ fixed), and let $Q := \mathbf 1 - \Pi$ 
project to the non–coherent (off–harmonic) sector. The internal gaps $\lambda_{n,\ast}>0$ yield a 
uniform $\lambda_\ast := \min_n \kappa_n\lambda_{n,\ast} > 0$. By spectral calculus, for $t>0$
\begin{equation}
  \big\| A^{1/2} e^{-tA}\,Q \big\|_{L^2\to L^2} \;\lesssim\; t^{-1/2}\,e^{-\lambda_\ast t}, 
  \qquad 
  \big\| e^{-tA} \big\|_{L^2\to H^1} \;\lesssim\; (1+t^{-1/2})\,e^{-\lambda_\ast t}.
  \label{eq:semigroup-bound}
\end{equation}
Hence long–time modal flow suppresses $Q$–modes exponentially and contracts to $\mathrm{Ran}\,\Pi$.
The observable map $P:=I\circ\Pi$ acts as a geometric band–limiter: external correlators carry
only the fibre–harmonic content, with off–harmonic contributions damped by $e^{-\lambda_\ast t}$.
This produces an internal spectral truncation, not a proof of four-dimensional ultraviolet
finiteness.

\paragraph{Covariant regulator on $Y_4$.}
On the observable side, define the (Euclidean) effective average action
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_k[g,\Phi] \;=\; S_{\rm coh}[g,\Phi] \;+\; \tfrac12 \!\int\! \sqrt{g}\;\Phi\,R_k(-\nabla^2)\,\Phi,
\end{equation}
with $R_k(z)$ satisfying $R_k(z)\!\to\! k^2$ for $z\!\ll\! k^2$ and $R_k(z)\!\to\!0$ for $z\!\gg\!k^2$,
and $\Phi$ denoting the collection of coherent fields (bosons, fermions in suitable quadratic
forms). The flow is governed by the Wetterich equation
\begin{equation}
  \partial_k\Gamma_k \;=\; \tfrac12\,\mathrm{STr}\,\Big(\Gamma_k^{(2)} + R_k\Big)^{-1}\,\partial_k R_k,
  \label{eq:wetterich}
\end{equation}
written in a background–field gauge so that $R_k$ is a scalar function of $-\nabla^2$ and thus
preserves diffeomorphism covariance of the flow. In Lorentzian signature one uses the standard
in–in variant; all Ward/BRST identities are recovered as $k\!\to\!0$.

With this background–covariant choice $R_k(-\bar\nabla^2)$, the modified
Slavnov–Taylor identities and the QME hold at each finite $k$ and reduce to the exact
identities as $k\downarrow 0$ (see Theorem~H.5).

\paragraph{SPT versus FRG (conceptual separation).}
It is important to distinguish the roles of the SPT factorization and the functional
renormalization--group (FRG) regulator $R_k$. The SPT mechanism is a projector--induced
filter considered after imposing the positive proper-time support gap. If retained at
$k=0$, it yields a Gaussian dressing $e^{-\tau_0 k^2}$ on selected lines, but its physical
selection, unitarity, and causal support are not established. Its graphwise convergence
scope is exactly the rank criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:all-loops-app}.

By contrast, the FRG regulator $R_k(-\nabla^2)$ is introduced only as an organizational
tool to define a covariant coarse--graining and to control the flow of local counterterms.
The limit $k \to 0$ is taken after renormalization, and removing $R_k$ does not remove the
SPT Gaussian damping, which is fixed by the coherent--sector data $(\tau_0,\lambda_*)$.

\paragraph{Propagator structure at fixed $k$.}
Because $P=I\circ\Pi$ projects to fibre–harmonic profiles, the 10D kinetic operator reduces to 
a 4D operator of the form $K_{\rm obs}(-\nabla^2) + M^2_{\rm obs}(x)$ on $Y_4$. With the
addition of $R_k(-\nabla^2)$ one has, schematically,
\begin{equation}
  G_k(p^2) \;=\; \frac{1}{K_{\rm obs}(p^2)\;+\;M_{\rm obs}^2\;+\;R_k(p^2)} 
  \;\sim\; 
  \begin{cases}
    1/k^2 & (p^2\!\ll\!k^2),\\[2pt]
    1/K_{\rm obs}(p^2) & (p^2\!\gg\!k^2),
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The internal bandlimiting from $\Pi$ removes vertical high-frequency channels, while
$R_k$ regularizes low four-dimensional covectors in the flow equation. Since

\section{Laplace positivity and the Stieltjes compatibility no-go}\label{app:stieltjes}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional heat-kernel form]\label{lem:stieltjes}
Let $A=E\oplus A_{\mathrm{int}}$ with commuting nonnegative blocks and let the
bounded map $B$ intertwine the external spectral calculus. Then
\[
\Dprop=BA^{-1}B^*=\int_0^\infty e^{-tE}M(t)\,dt,

\]
This is a positive Laplace representation. It does not, without an additional
theorem, imply a Stieltjes representation in $E$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The semigroup identity for $A^{-1}$ and the commuting-block/intertwining
hypotheses give the displayed integral. Positivity follows from
$M(t)=N(t)^*N(t)$ with $N(t)=e^{-tA_{\mathrm{int}}/2}B^*$. The class of
Laplace transforms of positive kernels is the completely monotone class;
Stieltjes functions form a proper subclass.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The v4 Laplace-to-Stieltjes conversion is false]
Take the scalar example $M(t)=e^{-t}$ and $E=1$. The heat integral is
\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-tE}M(t)\,dt=\frac12.
\]
The density proposed in v4 would be
$\mu(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}M(t)\,dt=(s+1)^{-1}$, but then
\[
\int_0^\infty\frac{\mu(s)}{E+s}\,ds
=\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{(s+1)^2}=1.
\]
Hence that conversion does not represent the original propagator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Positive spectral density versus Gaussian decay]
\label{thm:stieltjes-gaussian-nogo}
Let
\[
D(x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{\rho(ds)}{x+s},\qquad x>0,
\]
for a nonzero positive measure $\rho$. Then $D$ cannot obey
$D(x)\le C e^{-\tau x}/(x+\lambda)$ for all sufficiently large $x$ with
$C,\tau,\lambda>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $R<\infty$ with $m:=\rho([0,R])>0$. Positivity gives
$D(x)\ge m/(x+R)$. Combining the two bounds would imply
\[
\frac mC\le e^{-\tau x}\frac{x+R}{x+\lambda}\longrightarrow0,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Osterwalder--Schrader positivity through the positive

general nonunitarity theorem for every nonlocal model; rather, unitarity and
causality require a different construction. The local Einstein--TEGR EFT uses
the standard Hadamard/BRST framework instead.

\begin{proof}

condition controls products away from the total diagonal. The EG induction
extends distributions to coincident configurations with local counterterms.
This proof applies to the local EFT; it does not establish microcausality for
the permanent Gaussian nonlocal kernel.
\end{proof}

\section{SPT Factorization and Gaussian UV Damping}
\label{app:spt}

This appendix derives the spectral proper-time (SPT) factorization from the
explicit positive support-gap and transfer assumptions used in the filtered
analysis. The support gap is an input; bounded geometry and bounded projection
alone do not imply it.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Geometric and operator assumptions}

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth Riemannian manifold of dimension $d$ with bounded geometry:
uniform bounds on curvature and its covariant derivatives and a positive injectivity radius.
Let $E\to M$ be a Hermitian vector bundle with a compatible connection.

Let $L$ be a nonnegative selfadjoint Laplace--type operator on $L^2(E)$, i.e.
\[
L = \nabla^\ast \nabla + \mathcal{R},
\]
where $\mathcal{R}$ is a smooth bundle endomorphism bounded below.

\begin{assumption}[Heat kernel bounds]\label{ass:heat}
The heat kernel $K_t(x,y)$ of $e^{-tL}$ satisfies Gaussian upper bounds: there exist
constants $C,c>0$ such that for all $t>0$,
\[
\|K_t(x,y)\|
\;\le\;
C\,t^{-d/2}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{d(x,y)^2}{c\,t}\Bigr).
\]
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:heat} holds for all Laplace--type operators on bounded--geometry
manifolds.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Completely monotone filters and proper--time representation}

\begin{definition}[Completely monotone filter]
A function $f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}$ is \emph{completely monotone} if
\[
(-1)^m f^{(m)}(x)\ge 0
\quad\text{for all }x>0,\ m\ge 0.
\]
\end{definition}

By Bernstein's theorem, $f$ is completely monotone if and only if there exists a
finite positive Borel measure $\mu$ on $[0,\infty)$ such that
\[
f(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty e^{-t\lambda}\,d\mu(t).
\]

\begin{assumption}[Proper--time gap]\label{ass:pt-gap}
The representing measure $\mu$ of $f$ satisfies
\[
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset[\tau_0,\infty)
\quad\text{for some }\tau_0>0.
\]
\end{assumption}

% --- One-paragraph replacement for former Assumption A.3 (Proper-Time Support Gap) ---
\paragraph{Replacement of Assumption A.3 (Proper-Time Support Gap).}
The discrete schedule used here is an additional model axiom: every
evolve--project cycle $T_\tau=\Pi_{\mathrm{coh}}\circ\Phi_\tau$ is assumed to
apply a smoothing semigroup for a strictly positive minimum duration
$\tau_*>0$. Given that axiom, Appendix~\ref{app:proper_time_gap} proves that
the filter measure is supported in $[\tau_*,\infty)$ and yields the SPT
factorization. The argument does not derive the minimum duration from the
abstract fixed-point or q79 geometry.


\begin{definition}[Spectral filter]
Define the bounded operator
\[
B := f(L)
\]
by functional calculus.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact proper--time representation]\label{lem:pt-rep}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-gap},
\[
B = \int_{\tau_0}^{\infty} e^{-tL}\,d\mu(t),
\]
where the integral converges in the strong operator topology (and in operator norm
if $\mu$ has finite total mass).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Bernstein's theorem and Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-gap},
\[
f(L)=\int_0^\infty e^{-tL}\,d\mu(t)
=\int_{\tau_0}^\infty e^{-tL}\,d\mu(t).
\]
Strong convergence follows from monotone convergence and the contractivity of
$e^{-tL}$ on $L^2$.
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{SPT factorization}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral proper--time factorization]\label{lem:spt-fact}
Define
\[
B_0 := \int_0^\infty e^{-sL}\,d\mu(\tau_0+s).
\]
Then
\[
B = e^{-\frac{\tau_0}{2}L}\,B_0\,e^{-\frac{\tau_0}{2}L},
\]
and $B_0$ is a bounded positive operator commuting with $L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pt-rep}, substitute $t=\tau_0+s$:
\[
B=\int_{\tau_0}^\infty e^{-tL}\,d\mu(t)
=e^{-\frac{\tau_0}{2}L}
\Bigl(\int_0^\infty e^{-sL}\,d\mu(\tau_0+s)\Bigr)
e^{-\frac{\tau_0}{2}L}.
\]
Positivity and commutation with $L$ follow from functional calculus.
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Kernel bounds and Gaussian decay}

Let $K_B(x,y)$ denote the Schwartz kernel of $B$.

\begin{lemma}[Kernel bound]\label{lem:kernel-bound}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:heat},
\[
\|K_B(x,y)\|
\;\le\;
C\int_{\tau_0}^{\infty}
t^{-d/2}\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{d(x,y)^2}{c\,t}\Bigr)
\,d\mu(t).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Insert the heat kernel bound of Assumption~\ref{ass:heat} into the representation
of Lemma~\ref{lem:pt-rep} and integrate against $d\mu(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian Fourier multiplier bound]\label{lem:fourier-gauss}
In a bounded--geometry coordinate chart, the Fourier transform of $K_B$ satisfies
\[
\|\widehat{K_B}(\xi)\|
\;\le\;
C'\,e^{-\tau_0|\xi|^2},
\]
uniformly for $|\xi|$ sufficiently large.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In local coordinates, the Fourier transform of $e^{-tL}$ is bounded by
$\exp(-t|\xi|^2)$ up to polynomial prefactors.
Since $t\ge\tau_0$ on the support of $\mu$, the integral representation yields
\[
\|\widehat{K_B}(\xi)\|
\le
\int_{\tau_0}^\infty C'' e^{-t|\xi|^2}\,d\mu(t)
\le C' e^{-\tau_0|\xi|^2}.
\]
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Filtered propagator and graphwise UV convergence}

Let $L$ be the projected graviton operator in the TT sector and let
\[
\Delta := (L + m^2)^{-1}.
\]
Define the filtered propagator
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{filt}} := B\,\Delta.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian UV damping of the propagator]\label{prop:gauss-prop}
There exist constants $C,\lambda_\ast>0$ such that in local momentum variables,
\[
\|\Delta_{\mathrm{filt}}(k)\|
\;\le\;
C\,\frac{e^{-\tau_0 k^2}}{k^2+\lambda_\ast}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Combine the resolvent bound $(k^2+\lambda_\ast)^{-1}$ with the Fourier multiplier
estimate of Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier-gauss}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full-rank filtered-graph convergence]\label{cor:all-loop}
Assume that each interaction vertex grows at most polynomially in momenta and
that the filtered-line momentum matrix has rank $L$ on the graph and each UV
subgraph, where $L$ is the corresponding loop number. Then the graph is
absolutely convergent in all ultraviolet loop directions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:all-loops-app} applied to the filtered propagator bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Pure matter subgraphs without internal graviton lines renormalize by standard EFT
methods and are not claimed UV finite by this mechanism.
\end{remark}

\section{Derivation of the Proper-Time Support Gap}
\label{app:proper_time_gap}

\subsection{Purpose and logical role}

In the constructive quantum-gravity analysis, several results rely on the existence
of a strictly positive proper-time support gap
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{supp}(\mu) \subset [\tau_0,\infty),
\qquad \tau_0 > 0,
\label{eq:pt_gap}
\end{equation}
for the Bernstein measure $\mu$ associated with the filter defining the coherent
projector.
In earlier sections this condition appeared as an explicit assumption
(denoted ``Assumption A.3'').

The purpose of this appendix is to \emph{derive} \eqref{eq:pt_gap} from a minimal and
physically interpretable dynamical axiom: the existence of a strictly positive
minimum smoothing time per evolve--project cycle.
This removes any ambiguity about whether the proper-time gap is imposed ad hoc or
follows from the operational structure of the theory.

Throughout this appendix we work under the bounded-geometry and Laplace-type
assumptions already in force in the main text.

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Discrete schedule axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Discrete projection schedule]
\label{ax:schedule}
Physical evolution proceeds in discrete evolve--project cycles.
Each cycle applies a smoothing semigroup $S_t = e^{-tL}$ with
a strictly positive minimum duration $\tau_* > 0$.
No admissible physical update applies smoothing for times $t < \tau_*$.
\end{axiom}

This axiom formalizes the idea that admissible dynamics has a finite
coarse-graining cadence and excludes arbitrarily short proper-time updates.
It is independent of spectral gap assumptions and does not refer to
any particular representation of the projector.

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Construction of the effective filter}

Let $L \ge 0$ be a self-adjoint Laplace-type operator acting on a Hilbert space
(or section space over a bounded-geometry slab).
Define the heat semigroup $S_t = e^{-tL}$.

Consider an effective filter obtained by iterating the evolve--project cycle
a random number of times, with a geometric distribution of cycle counts:
\begin{equation}
F \;=\; \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p(1-p)^{n-1} S_{n\tau_*},
\qquad p \in (0,1).
\label{eq:filter_def}
\end{equation}

This choice is technically convenient and physically natural:
each update applies at least one full cycle of duration $\tau_*$, and the
distribution has finite mean.

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Complete monotonicity and Bernstein representation}

\begin{lemma}[Complete monotonicity]
\label{lem:cm}
The scalar function
\begin{equation}
f(\lambda)
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p(1-p)^{n-1} e^{-n\tau_*\lambda},
\qquad \lambda \ge 0,
\label{eq:f_lambda}
\end{equation}
is completely monotone on $[0,\infty)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each term $e^{-n\tau_*\lambda}$ is completely monotone, and the series
converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of $[0,\infty)$ since
$(1-p)e^{-n\tau_*\lambda} \le 1-p < 1$.
Complete monotonicity is preserved under positive linear combinations.
\end{proof}

By Bernstein's theorem, $f$ admits a unique representation as a Laplace transform
of a positive measure.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit Bernstein measure]
\label{lem:bernstein}
The Bernstein measure associated with $f$ is
\begin{equation}
\mu
= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} p(1-p)^{n-1}\,\delta_{n\tau_*}.
\label{eq:bernstein_measure}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each exponential $e^{-n\tau_*\lambda}$ is the Laplace transform of
$\delta_{n\tau_*}$.
Linearity of the Laplace transform yields \eqref{eq:bernstein_measure}.
\end{proof}

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Derivation of the proper-time support gap}

\begin{theorem}[Proper-time support gap]
\label{thm:pt_gap}
Under Axiom~\ref{ax:schedule}, the Bernstein measure $\mu$ of the effective filter
$F = f(L)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{supp}(\mu) \subset [\tau_*,\infty).
\end{equation}
In particular, the proper-time support gap exists with $\tau_0 = \tau_*$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From Lemma~\ref{lem:bernstein}, the support of $\mu$ consists of the discrete set
$\{n\tau_* \mid n \in \mathbb{N}\}$.
The smallest element is $\tau_*$, hence the support is contained in
$[\tau_*,\infty)$.
\end{proof}

This theorem replaces the previously stated proper-time gap assumption.
The gap is no longer an independent hypothesis but a direct consequence of the
discrete update cadence.

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{SPT factorization}

\begin{corollary}[Spectral proper-time factorization]
\label{cor:spt}
Let $F = f(L)$ be defined as above.
Then
\begin{equation}
F
= e^{-\frac{\tau_*}{2}L}\, F_0 \, e^{-\frac{\tau_*}{2}L},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
F_0
:= \int_{\tau_*}^{\infty} e^{-(t-\tau_*)L}\, d\mu(t)
\end{equation}
is a bounded, positive operator commuting with $L$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This follows immediately by inserting the Bernstein representation and
factoring out $e^{-\frac{\tau_*}{2}L}$ on both sides.
Boundedness and positivity of $F_0$ follow from positivity of $\mu$ and standard
semigroup bounds.
\end{proof}

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Local-momentum interpretation of Gaussian damping}

We emphasize that $e^{-\tau_* L}$ yields Gaussian suppression in the \emph{spectral}
variable of $L$.
On curved backgrounds there is no global Fourier momentum.
The correct interpretation is microlocal.

\begin{proposition}[Local UV Gaussian damping]
\label{prop:local_gaussian}
Let $L$ be a Laplace-type operator on a bounded-geometry slab.
Then the principal symbol of $e^{-\tau_* L}$ is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{pr}}\!\left(e^{-\tau_* L}\right)(x,\xi)
= e^{-\tau_* \sigma_{\mathrm{pr}}(L)(x,\xi)}
= e^{-\tau_* |\xi|_g^2},
\end{equation}
where $|\xi|_g^2$ is the local covector norm induced by the metric.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For Laplace-type operators, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pr}}(L)(x,\xi)=|\xi|_g^2$.
Since the function $\lambda \mapsto e^{-\tau_*\lambda}$ is entire, the standard
pseudodifferential functional calculus applies, yielding that the principal symbol
of $e^{-\tau_* L}$ is obtained by functional composition with
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pr}}(L)$.
\end{proof}

Thus, statements such as ``Gaussian damping $e^{-\tau_* k^2}$'' should be read as
shorthand for Gaussian damping in the local UV covector variable $|\xi|_g^2$.
In local inertial or asymptotically flat limits, this reduces to the familiar
momentum-space expression.

%---------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Remarks on scope}

\begin{itemize}
\item The derivation above consumes the discrete schedule/support-gap axiom.
Current q79 geometry has not promoted that axiom to a selected physical law.
\item The Gaussian damping statement is local and microlocal; no claim of a global
Minkowski momentum variable is made on arbitrary curved backgrounds.
\item Extensions to specific sectors (e.g.\ TT gravitons) require only that the
corresponding kinetic operator be Laplace-type up to lower-order curvature terms,
as assumed elsewhere in the paper.
\end{itemize}

This completes the conditional factorization from the stated support-gap axiom.
