## Revision purpose

Version 1 preserved a useful methodological idea: test whether a candidate
supports a controlled physical description before assigning it a weight.
It then promoted that idea too far. It treated failure of one admissibility
description as nonexistence, treated exclusion as selection, inferred
physical inaccessibility from disconnected admissible regions, and
attributed landscape nonconvergence to those conclusions without a global
atlas, dynamics, measure, or observation map.

Version 2 separates five layers:

1. formal mathematical existence;
2. validity of a chart or approximation;
3. kinematic admissibility;
4. dynamical or statistical realization; and
5. empirical identification.

It is a standalone explanatory paper, not a correction list.

## Claim-by-claim audit

### Physics is undefined throughout non-admissible configuration space

Version 1 claim:
Failure of the selected stability or control conditions means that the
configuration is non-physical.

Decision:
Replace.

Reason:
A failed row can mean that one chart or approximation is uncertified.
Global exclusion requires a chart-independent obstruction or a
transition-compatible exhaustion theorem.

Version 2 resolution:
Separate formal existence, chart validity, and admissibility, and prove the
one-chart-failure no-go proposition.

### Hilbert spaces require spectral separation

Version 1 claim:
Hilbert-space structure itself presupposes a spectral gap.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Hilbert spaces and spectral gaps are different mathematical structures.
Particular perturbative reductions or spectral projectors may require a
gap, but Hilbert-space quantum theory does not do so universally.

Version 2 resolution:
Use model-specific operator-domain, positivity, stability, and
approximation rows.

### Selection is exclusion

Version 1 claim:
Once non-admissible candidates are excluded, admissibility itself performs
vacuum selection.

Decision:
Replace.

Reason:
If more than one inequivalent candidate survives, the predicate supplies
neither a unique outcome nor relative weights.

Version 2 resolution:
Prove the no-selection proposition and list the dynamics, measure,
variational, conditioning, or branch data needed in addition.

### Disconnected admissible regions are physically inaccessible

Version 1 claim:
Disconnectedness prevents physical transitions and defeats continuous
landscape searches by construction.

Decision:
Withdraw as a physical conclusion.

Reason:
Disconnectedness excludes only continuous paths that remain inside the
set. A selected process may jump, tunnel, leave the chart, or reset.

Version 2 resolution:
Prove the exact topological statement and identify transition dynamics as
separate data.

### Landscape nonconvergence is explained by inadmissibility

Version 1 claim:
The lack of a unique string vacuum is expected because landscape methods
assume physics is defined everywhere.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Landscape programs already use consistency constraints, ensembles,
transition dynamics, and conditioning in different combinations. No
evidence was supplied that one omitted admissibility boundary explains the
open problem.

Version 2 resolution:
Compare Bousso-Polchinski, Douglas, Denef-Douglas, Vafa, and Weinberg at
their actual logical tiers.

### Current MTT results select a physical vacuum

Version 1 implication:
Concrete MTT constructions show that an admissibility-only selection
framework is physically viable.

Decision:
Narrow.

Reason:
Current exact and certified results establish finite, arithmetic,
topological, projected, and rank-two analytic rows. They do not provide
global branch exhaustion, a complete physical visible-hidden endpoint, a
global dynamics or normalized measure, or observed four-dimensional
closure from vacuum selection.

Version 2 resolution:
Use those results as contextual examples and state all non-promotion
boundaries explicitly.
