\begin{abstract}
Finite functional-renormalization-group truncations can reveal fixed-point
structure, but a truncation fixed point is not automatically the shadow of an
exact ultraviolet completion. This paper gives a validation framework for
that inference. A contraction residual bounds the distance to an exact fixed
point when the exact map is already known to be contractive. A finite
truncation has its own fixed point only when an independent existence
condition, such as invariant contraction or a verified degree argument, is
supplied. If a lifted truncation fixed point has a certified defect relative
to the exact map, its distance from the exact fixed point is bounded by that
defect divided by the contraction margin. An additive-error Lipschitz
inequality alone proves none of these conclusions. Applied to Modal Triplet
Theory, the framework identifies the missing data: a selected map from
coherent variables to FRG couplings, a physical regulator and scheme, and
certified truncation defects. The paper therefore supplies a rigorous
truncation diagnostic, not a proof that current asymptotic-safety
truncations are MTT predictions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a separate truncation paper is needed}

The technical MTT--asymptotic-safety paper owns the conditional conjugacy
and quasi-compact unstable-subspace theorems. This companion paper addresses
a narrower question:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{When does a fixed point of a finite FRG truncation}\\
\text{approximate a fixed point of an exact map?}
\end{gathered}
\]

This question matters because practical asymptotic-safety calculations
replace an infinite-dimensional effective action by finitely many couplings.
Stability across larger truncations and regulator choices is valuable
evidence \cite{ReuterSaueressig}, but the logical implication to an exact
fixed functional requires error control.

\section{Exact map, truncation, and lift}

Let \(\X\) be a Banach space, \(\Kset\subset\X\) a nonempty closed set, and
\[
 \Tmap:\Kset\to\Kset
\]
the exact dimensionless RG step. For each \(N\), let \(X_N\) be a
finite-dimensional coupling space, with
\[
 \piN:\X\to X_N,\qquad
 \iotaN:X_N\to\X.
\]
The finite map
\[
 \TN:K_N\to K_N
\]
need not equal \(\piN\Tmap\iotaN\). Gauge fixing, regulator choice,
projection, closure assumptions, and numerical discretization can all
contribute to the defect.

\begin{definition}[Lifted defect]
For \(y\in K_N\), define
\[
 d_N(y)=\|\Tmap(\iotaN y)-\iotaN\TN(y)\|_{\X}.
\]
This is the error relevant to fixed-point transfer.
\end{definition}

Convergence of individual couplings or visual stability of a plot is not a
substitute for a bound on \(d_N\) in a declared norm.

\section{Residual validation}

\begin{theorem}[A posteriori fixed-point bound]
\label{thm:residual}
Suppose \(\Tmap:\Kset\to\Kset\) is a contraction with constant
\(0\leq q<1\), and let \(x_\ast\) be its unique fixed point. Then every
\(x\in\Kset\) satisfies
\[
 \|x-x_\ast\|_{\X}
 \leq
 \frac{\|\Tmap x-x\|_{\X}}{1-q}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Using \(\Tmap x_\ast=x_\ast\),
\[
 \|x-x_\ast\|
 \leq\|x-\Tmap x\|+\|\Tmap x-\Tmap x_\ast\|
 \leq\|\Tmap x-x\|+q\|x-x_\ast\|.
\]
Rearrange.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a validation result, not an existence result from a small
residual. Existence and uniqueness came from the exact contraction
hypothesis.

\begin{corollary}[Certified truncation shadow]
\label{cor:shadow}
Assume the hypotheses of \autoref{thm:residual}. If
\(\TN y_N=y_N\), \(\iotaN y_N\in\Kset\), and
\[
 d_N(y_N)\leq\varepsilon_N,
\]
then
\[
 \|\iotaN y_N-x_\ast\|_{\X}
 \leq\frac{\varepsilon_N}{1-q}.
\]
Consequently, \(\varepsilon_N\to0\) implies convergence of the lifted
truncation fixed points to \(x_\ast\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At a truncation fixed point,
\[
 \|\Tmap(\iotaN y_N)-\iotaN y_N\|
 =
 \|\Tmap(\iotaN y_N)-\iotaN\TN y_N\|
 =d_N(y_N).
\]
Apply \autoref{thm:residual}.
\end{proof}

\section{The finite fixed point needs its own theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-map existence by contraction]
\label{prop:finite-existence}
If \(K_N\) is complete and
\(\TN:K_N\to K_N\) is a contraction with constant \(q_N<1\), then
\(\TN\) has a unique fixed point \(y_N\).
\end{proposition}

This is one available route. In finite dimension, an invariant compact
convex set and continuity permit Brouwer existence, but not uniqueness or
the a posteriori error bound above. Interval Newton, Krawczyk, degree, or
Conley-index methods can provide stronger computer-assisted certificates.

\subsection{Why additive-error contraction is insufficient}

The inequality
\begin{equation}
 \|\TN x-\TN y\|
 \leq q\|x-y\|+\varepsilon,
 \qquad q<1,\quad\varepsilon>0,
 \label{eq:additive}
\end{equation}
does not make \(\TN\) a contraction.

\begin{example}[No existence from \eqref{eq:additive}]
Let \(K=\{0,1\}\) with its usual metric and let \(T\) exchange the two
points. Then \(K\) is complete, \(T:K\to K\) has no fixed point, and
\[
 |Tx-Ty|\leq0\,|x-y|+1
\]
for all \(x,y\in K\). Thus \eqref{eq:additive} can hold with \(q=0\)
without a fixed point.
\end{example}

This is the precise failure in the earlier version. An additive defect can
be useful after an exact or finite fixed point has been established, but it
cannot replace the existence theorem.

\section{Perturbing an exact contraction}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-point perturbation]
\label{thm:perturbation}
Let \(T,S:\Kset\to\Kset\) be contractions, with \(T\) having constant
\(q<1\), and fixed points \(x_T,x_S\). If
\[
 \sup_{x\in\Kset}\|Sx-Tx\|\leq\varepsilon,
\]
then
\[
 \|x_S-x_T\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1-q}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\[
 \|x_S-x_T\|
 \leq\|Sx_S-Tx_S\|+\|Tx_S-Tx_T\|
 \leq\varepsilon+q\|x_S-x_T\|.
\]
\end{proof}

The theorem requires \(S\) to possess a fixed point, here guaranteed by its
own contraction property. Closeness to \(T\) alone is not enough.

\section{Scheme and regulator dependence}

An FRG truncation is specified by more than a list of operators. It includes:
\begin{itemize}
\item field parametrization and background split;
\item gauge fixing and ghost sector;
\item regulator shape \(R_k\);
\item projection prescriptions for beta functions;
\item truncation basis and normalization conditions;
\item spacetime domain and boundary conditions.
\end{itemize}

Two calculations can be compared by treating one step map as a perturbation
of the other and applying \autoref{thm:perturbation} when a common invariant
domain and contraction estimate exist. Without those data, ``scheme
stability'' means empirical robustness across the tested schemes, not a
theorem of universal equality.

\section{Relevant directions and spectral error}

Let \(L=D\Tmap(x_\ast)\) and
\(L_N=D(\iotaN\TN\piN)\) at compatible points. Convergence of fixed points
does not alone imply convergence of critical exponents. One needs an
operator-norm or resolvent estimate and a spectral separation condition.
For an isolated spectral cluster enclosed by a contour \(\gamma\), the
relevant comparison is between Riesz projectors
\[
 P=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma(z-L)^{-1}\,dz,
 \qquad
 P_N=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma(z-L_N)^{-1}\,dz.
\]
If the resolvent perturbation is small uniformly on \(\gamma\), standard
spectral perturbation theory controls the cluster and its total algebraic
multiplicity \cite{Kato}. Counting positive critical exponents in a finite
matrix without such control does not prove the exact number of relevant
directions.

\section{Application contract for MTT}

For an MTT truncation to qualify under \autoref{cor:shadow}, the following
objects must be emitted from one selected branch:

\begin{enumerate}
\item an exact or controlled upper RG map \(\Tmap\);
\item a chart from coherent geometric variables to FRG actions and
couplings;
\item a compatible finite basis and lift \((\piN,\iotaN)\);
\item an invariant domain and a contraction or other existence certificate;
\item a computable lifted defect \(d_N\);
\item a regulator, gauge, and normalization convention;
\item a spectral error certificate for claimed relevant directions.
\end{enumerate}

The present MTT corpus does not yet provide this packet. It has exact finite
internal operators, conditional four-dimensional filters, and several
finite-domain constructive results. Those are ingredients, not the selected
FRG coupling map or truncation certificate.

\section{Discussion and conclusion}

The phrase ``truncation shadow'' is justified only when there is something
precise casting the shadow: an exact map, a lift, and a controlled defect.
Under those conditions, \autoref{cor:shadow} gives a clean quantitative
statement. Without them, a stable truncation fixed point remains meaningful
FRG evidence but not an MTT derivation.

This correction does not weaken the research program. It turns a broad
correspondence into a reproducible target. The next useful computation is
not another unconstrained search for fixed-point coordinates. It is a
same-source construction of the MTT-to-FRG chart and a certified residual
for one explicitly declared truncation.
