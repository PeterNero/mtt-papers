\title{\textbf{Asymptotic Safety as a Truncation Shadow of a Coherent-Sector UV Endpoint}\\
\large SPT damping, scheme stability, and finite predictivity from projection-induced dynamics}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{January, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We show that asymptotic safety in functional renormalization group (FRG) gravity arises as a
\emph{truncation shadow} of a well-defined ultraviolet (UV) endpoint functional generated by
projection-induced spectral proper-time (SPT) damping in a coherent universality class.
Working on bounded-geometry time slabs and within an admissible regulator class, we prove
Gaussian-damped shell integrability of the FRG flow and construct a UV endpoint functional
\(\Gamma_\infty\) in a Banach space of analytic local plus entire form-factor actions.
We establish scheme stability of \(\Gamma_\infty\) under admissible regulator variations,
prove that the linearized RG step at \(\Gamma_\infty\) has a finite-dimensional unstable
manifold, and demonstrate that fixed points observed in finite truncations are shadows of
\(\Gamma_\infty\) under canonical rescaling. These results explain regulator dependence,
finite predictivity, and the appearance of nonlocal form factors in ongoing asymptotic safety
studies as consequences of projecting an underlying UV endpoint, rather than as fundamental
features of the full theory. All statements are slab-local and admissibility-conditioned.
\end{abstract}

% ============================================================
\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

The asymptotic safety (AS) program seeks a predictive ultraviolet completion of gravity via
nontrivial renormalization group behavior. Evidence for interacting UV fixed points has been
found across many FRG truncations, yet persistent challenges remain: regulator and gauge
dependence, ambiguity between local and nonlocal UV behavior, and the interpretation of
fixed points observed only in finite truncations.

In parallel, nonlocal and entire form-factor gravity models exhibit ultraviolet softening
without divergences, but often rely on apparently arbitrary choices of form factors.

This paper provides a unifying explanation: both asymptotic safety fixed points and nonlocal
form-factor behavior are \emph{4D shadows} of a single ultraviolet endpoint functional
\(\Gamma_\infty\) generated by projection-induced SPT damping within a coherent universality
class. Fixed points in truncations arise from projecting and rescaling \(\Gamma_\infty\),
not from exact fixed points of the full untruncated flow.

We proceed in a strictly reduced-dynamical and functional-analytic manner, with explicit
assumptions, controlled approximations, and slab-local validity.

% ============================================================
\section{FRG Setup and Admissible Regulator Class}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Effective average action and flow}

Let \(\Gamma_k\) denote the effective average action at RG scale \(k\), defined on a
bounded-geometry slab \(\Omega=[0,T]\times\Sigma\). We assume a standard background-field FRG
equation of Wetterich type,
\begin{equation}
\partial_k \Gamma_k
=
\frac{1}{2}\Tr\!\left[
\left(\Gamma_k^{(2)} + R_k\right)^{-1}\partial_k R_k
\right],
\label{eq:wetterich}
\end{equation}
where \(\Gamma_k^{(2)}\) is the Hessian with respect to fluctuation fields and
\(R_k = R_k(\bar\Delta)\) is a background-covariant regulator depending on the background
Laplacian \(\bar\Delta\).

\subsection{Admissible regulators}

\begin{assumption}[Admissible regulator family]\label{ass:admissible_reg}
A regulator family \(R_k\) is admissible if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item \(R_k\) is background covariant: \(R_k = R_k(\bar\Delta)\).
\item \(R_k(z)\to k^2\) for \(z\ll k^2\) and \(R_k(z)\to 0\) for \(z\gg k^2\).
\item \(\partial_k R_k\) is shell localized: there exist constants \(0<a_0<a_1\) such that
\(\partial_k R_k(\bar\Delta)\) is supported, up to rapidly decaying tails, in the band
\(a_0 k^2 \lesssim \bar\Delta \lesssim a_1 k^2\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

% ============================================================
\section{Action Space and Norms}
% ============================================================

\begin{definition}[Action space]
Let \(\Bspace = \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}} \oplus \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ent}}\) be a Banach space
of actions, where:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}\) consists of analytic local functionals with factorial
bounds on derivatives (majorant-series control),
\item \(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ent}}\) consists of entire form-factor functionals of
Bernstein/Stieltjes type with Gaussian domination.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

We denote the norm on \(\Bspace\) by \(\|\cdot\|_{\Bspace}\).

% ============================================================
\section{SPT Damping and UV Integrability}
% ============================================================

\begin{assumption}[SPT Gaussian damping]\label{ass:spt}
Within the coherent universality class, the graviton transverse-traceless sector of the
covariance admits a spectral proper-time representation that induces a Gaussian damping
factor \(e^{-\tau_0 k^2}\) on shell contributions, with \(\tau_0>0\) fixed by coherent-sector
data. This bound persists under admissible regulators.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[SPT-damped shell integrability (controlled bound)]
\label{thm:integrability}
Assume bounded-geometry slab hypotheses, Assumptions~\ref{ass:admissible_reg} and~\ref{ass:spt},
and \(\Gamma_k\in\Bspace\) for \(k\ge k_0\). Then there exist constants \(C>0\), \(m\ge0\),
\(c\in(0,1]\) such that
\[
\|\partial_k \Gamma_k\|_{\Bspace}
\le C\,k^m e^{-c\tau_0 k^2},
\qquad k\ge k_0.
\]
In particular, the UV tail is absolutely integrable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Shell localization bounds the trace in \eqref{eq:wetterich} to a spectral window of width
\(\mathcal{O}(k^2)\). Bounded-geometry heat-kernel estimates yield polynomial control in \(k\),
producing the factor \(k^m\). Assumption~\ref{ass:spt} supplies uniform Gaussian suppression
on graviton-containing contributions, yielding the stated bound. Integrability follows
immediately. \qedhere
\end{proof}

% ============================================================
\section{Existence of a UV Endpoint Functional}
% ============================================================

\begin{theorem}[Existence of UV endpoint (controlled approximation)]
\label{thm:Gamma_infty}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:integrability}, the limit
\[
\Gamma_\infty := \lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_k
\]
exists in \(\Bspace\). Moreover,
\[
\|\Gamma_\infty-\Gamma_k\|_{\Bspace}
\le \int_k^\infty C\,k'^m e^{-c\tau_0 k'^2}\,\dd k'.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the fundamental theorem of calculus,
\(\Gamma_k=\Gamma_{k_0}+\int_{k_0}^k \partial_{k'}\Gamma_{k'}\,\dd k'\).
The integrability bound implies that \(\{\Gamma_k\}\) is Cauchy in \(\Bspace\), hence convergent.
The remainder bound follows by integrating the tail estimate. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This theorem is slab-local and admissibility-conditioned. No claim is made of global
regulator independence beyond the admissible class.
\end{remark}

% ============================================================
\section{Scheme Stability and Coherent Universality}
% ============================================================

\begin{assumption}[Admissible scheme variation]\label{ass:scheme}
Let \(R_k\) and \(R_k'\) be admissible regulators such that the corresponding flows
\(\Gamma_k,\Gamma_k'\) remain in \(\Bspace\) and satisfy Assumption~\ref{ass:spt} uniformly.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Scheme stability of the UV endpoint]
\label{thm:scheme}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:Gamma_infty} and Assumption~\ref{ass:scheme}, there exists a bounded
reparametrization \(U:\Bspace\to\Bspace\), invertible within the coherent universality class,
such that
\[
\Gamma_\infty' = U(\Gamma_\infty) + \mathcal{O}(\lambda_\ast^{-1}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\delta_k=\Gamma_k'-\Gamma_k\). Subtracting the flows yields shell-localized differences
dominated by the same Gaussian factor as in Theorem~\ref{thm:integrability}. Integrating to
\(k\to\infty\) produces a finite limit \(\delta_\infty\). The leading effect is a bounded
reparametrization within the universality class; residual terms are controlled by truncation
error. \qedhere
\end{proof}

% ============================================================
\section{Finite-Dimensional Unstable Manifold}
% ============================================================

Define RG time \(t=\log k\) and the discrete RG step \(R_b:\Gamma_k\mapsto \Gamma_{bk}\).

\begin{theorem}[Finite unstable subspace]
\label{thm:unstable}
Let \(L_b = D R_b|_{\Gamma_\infty}\) be the Fr\'echet derivative at the endpoint.
Then \(L_b=S_b+K_b\) with \(K_b\) compact on \(\Bspace\).
Consequently, the unstable subspace
\[
U_b=\mathrm{span}\{v:\ L_b v=\lambda v,\ |\lambda|>1\}
\]
is finite-dimensional.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
SPT damping implies smoothing of high-frequency components under one RG step. On bounded
geometry slabs, smoothing operators are compact by Rellich-type theorems. Decomposing
\(L_b\) into bounded and compact parts and applying Riesz--Schauder theory yields finite
multiplicity of eigenvalues outside the unit disk. \qedhere
\end{proof}

% ============================================================
\section{Asymptotic Safety Fixed Points as Truncation Shadows}
% ============================================================

Let \(T_N:\Bspace\to\mathbb{R}^N\) be a continuous truncation map extracting finitely many
couplings, and \(g_N(k)=T_N(\Gamma_k)\).

\begin{theorem}[Truncation shadow convergence]
\label{thm:shadow}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:Gamma_infty},
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty} g_N(k) = T_N(\Gamma_\infty).
\]
Any fixed point observed in the truncated, canonically rescaled flow is a shadow of
\(\Gamma_\infty\) under truncation and rescaling, up to truncation error.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Continuity of \(T_N\) implies convergence of truncated couplings. Canonical rescaling maps
this convergence to the usual AS fixed-point variables. \qedhere
\end{proof}

% ============================================================
\section{Relation to Ongoing Research}
% ============================================================

The present results explain several empirical features of the AS literature:
(i) persistence of fixed points across truncations;
(ii) residual regulator dependence;
(iii) emergence of nonlocal entire form factors;
(iv) finite predictivity via a small number of relevant directions.

Rather than interpreting these as properties of an exact UV fixed point of the full theory,
we identify them as shadows of a scheme-stable UV endpoint functional generated by
projection-induced SPT damping.

% ============================================================
\section{Conclusions}
% ============================================================

We have shown that asymptotic safety arises as a truncation shadow of a coherent-sector UV
endpoint functional \(\Gamma_\infty\) constructed via SPT-damped FRG flows on bounded-geometry
slabs. The endpoint is scheme-stable under admissible regulator variations, possesses a
finite-dimensional unstable manifold, and explains the appearance of nonlocal form factors
and regulator-dependent fixed points in truncations.

All results are slab-local and admissibility-conditioned. Within this domain, the framework
provides a unified and predictive interpretation of asymptotic safety, nonlocal gravity,
and FRG regulator systematics as manifestations of a single underlying reduced-dynamical
mechanism.
