# Introduction

The asymptotic safety (AS) program seeks a predictive ultraviolet completion of gravity via nontrivial renormalization group behavior. Evidence for interacting UV fixed points has been found across many FRG truncations, yet persistent challenges remain: regulator and gauge dependence, ambiguity between local and nonlocal UV behavior, and the interpretation of fixed points observed only in finite truncations.

In parallel, nonlocal and entire form-factor gravity models exhibit ultraviolet softening without divergences, but often rely on apparently arbitrary choices of form factors.

This paper provides a unifying explanation: both asymptotic safety fixed points and nonlocal form-factor behavior are *4D shadows* of a single ultraviolet endpoint functional $`\Gamma_\infty`$ generated by projection-induced SPT damping within a coherent universality class. Fixed points in truncations arise from projecting and rescaling $`\Gamma_\infty`$, not from exact fixed points of the full untruncated flow.

We proceed in a strictly reduced-dynamical and functional-analytic manner, with explicit assumptions, controlled approximations, and slab-local validity.

# FRG Setup and Admissible Regulator Class

## Effective average action and flow

Let $`\Gamma_k`$ denote the effective average action at RG scale $`k`$, defined on a bounded-geometry slab $`\Omega=[0,T]\times\Sigma`$. We assume a standard background-field FRG equation of Wetterich type,
``` math
\begin{equation}
\partial_k \Gamma_k
=
\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\!\left[
\left(\Gamma_k^{(2)} + R_k\right)^{-1}\partial_k R_k
\right],
\label{eq:wetterich}
\end{equation}
```
where $`\Gamma_k^{(2)}`$ is the Hessian with respect to fluctuation fields and $`R_k = R_k(\bar\Delta)`$ is a background-covariant regulator depending on the background Laplacian $`\bar\Delta`$.

## Admissible regulators

<div id="ass:admissible_reg" class="assumption">

**Assumption 1** (Admissible regulator family). A regulator family $`R_k`$ is admissible if:

1.  $`R_k`$ is background covariant: $`R_k = R_k(\bar\Delta)`$.

2.  $`R_k(z)\to k^2`$ for $`z\ll k^2`$ and $`R_k(z)\to 0`$ for $`z\gg k^2`$.

3.  $`\partial_k R_k`$ is shell localized: there exist constants $`0<a_0<a_1`$ such that $`\partial_k R_k(\bar\Delta)`$ is supported, up to rapidly decaying tails, in the band $`a_0 k^2 \lesssim \bar\Delta \lesssim a_1 k^2`$.

</div>

# Action Space and Norms

<div class="definition">

**Definition 2** (Action space). Let $`\mathcal{B}= \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}} \oplus \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ent}}`$ be a Banach space of actions, where:

- $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{loc}}`$ consists of analytic local functionals with factorial bounds on derivatives (majorant-series control),

- $`\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ent}}`$ consists of entire form-factor functionals of Bernstein/Stieltjes type with Gaussian domination.

</div>

We denote the norm on $`\mathcal{B}`$ by $`\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}}`$.

# SPT Damping and UV Integrability

<div id="ass:spt" class="assumption">

**Assumption 3** (SPT Gaussian damping). Within the coherent universality class, the graviton transverse-traceless sector of the covariance admits a spectral proper-time representation that induces a Gaussian damping factor $`e^{-\tau_0 k^2}`$ on shell contributions, with $`\tau_0>0`$ fixed by coherent-sector data. This bound persists under admissible regulators.

</div>

<div id="thm:integrability" class="theorem">

**Theorem 4** (SPT-damped shell integrability (controlled bound)). *Assume bounded-geometry slab hypotheses, Assumptions <a href="#ass:admissible_reg" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:admissible_reg">1</a> and <a href="#ass:spt" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:spt">3</a>, and $`\Gamma_k\in\mathcal{B}`$ for $`k\ge k_0`$. Then there exist constants $`C>0`$, $`m\ge0`$, $`c\in(0,1]`$ such that
``` math
\|\partial_k \Gamma_k\|_{\mathcal{B}}
\le C\,k^m e^{-c\tau_0 k^2},
\qquad k\ge k_0.
```
In particular, the UV tail is absolutely integrable.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Shell localization bounds the trace in <a href="#eq:wetterich" data-reference-type="eqref" data-reference="eq:wetterich">[eq:wetterich]</a> to a spectral window of width $`\mathcal{O}(k^2)`$. Bounded-geometry heat-kernel estimates yield polynomial control in $`k`$, producing the factor $`k^m`$. Assumption <a href="#ass:spt" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:spt">3</a> supplies uniform Gaussian suppression on graviton-containing contributions, yielding the stated bound. Integrability follows immediately. ◻

</div>

# Existence of a UV Endpoint Functional

<div id="thm:Gamma_infty" class="theorem">

**Theorem 5** (Existence of UV endpoint (controlled approximation)). *Under the hypotheses of Theorem <a href="#thm:integrability" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:integrability">4</a>, the limit
``` math
\Gamma_\infty := \lim_{k\to\infty}\Gamma_k
```
exists in $`\mathcal{B}`$. Moreover,
``` math
\|\Gamma_\infty-\Gamma_k\|_{\mathcal{B}}
\le \int_k^\infty C\,k'^m e^{-c\tau_0 k'^2}\,\mathrm{d}k'.
```*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* By the fundamental theorem of calculus, $`\Gamma_k=\Gamma_{k_0}+\int_{k_0}^k \partial_{k'}\Gamma_{k'}\,\mathrm{d}k'`$. The integrability bound implies that $`\{\Gamma_k\}`$ is Cauchy in $`\mathcal{B}`$, hence convergent. The remainder bound follows by integrating the tail estimate. ◻

</div>

<div class="remark">

*Remark 6*. This theorem is slab-local and admissibility-conditioned. No claim is made of global regulator independence beyond the admissible class.

</div>

# Scheme Stability and Coherent Universality

<div id="ass:scheme" class="assumption">

**Assumption 7** (Admissible scheme variation). Let $`R_k`$ and $`R_k'`$ be admissible regulators such that the corresponding flows $`\Gamma_k,\Gamma_k'`$ remain in $`\mathcal{B}`$ and satisfy Assumption <a href="#ass:spt" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:spt">3</a> uniformly.

</div>

<div id="thm:scheme" class="theorem">

**Theorem 8** (Scheme stability of the UV endpoint). *Under Theorem <a href="#thm:Gamma_infty" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:Gamma_infty">5</a> and Assumption <a href="#ass:scheme" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="ass:scheme">7</a>, there exists a bounded reparametrization $`U:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}`$, invertible within the coherent universality class, such that
``` math
\Gamma_\infty' = U(\Gamma_\infty) + \mathcal{O}(\lambda_\ast^{-1}).
```*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Let $`\delta_k=\Gamma_k'-\Gamma_k`$. Subtracting the flows yields shell-localized differences dominated by the same Gaussian factor as in Theorem <a href="#thm:integrability" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:integrability">4</a>. Integrating to $`k\to\infty`$ produces a finite limit $`\delta_\infty`$. The leading effect is a bounded reparametrization within the universality class; residual terms are controlled by truncation error. ◻

</div>

# Finite-Dimensional Unstable Manifold

Define RG time $`t=\log k`$ and the discrete RG step $`R_b:\Gamma_k\mapsto \Gamma_{bk}`$.

<div id="thm:unstable" class="theorem">

**Theorem 9** (Finite unstable subspace). *Let $`L_b = D R_b|_{\Gamma_\infty}`$ be the Fréchet derivative at the endpoint. Then $`L_b=S_b+K_b`$ with $`K_b`$ compact on $`\mathcal{B}`$. Consequently, the unstable subspace
``` math
U_b=\mathrm{span}\{v:\ L_b v=\lambda v,\ |\lambda|>1\}
```
is finite-dimensional.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* SPT damping implies smoothing of high-frequency components under one RG step. On bounded geometry slabs, smoothing operators are compact by Rellich-type theorems. Decomposing $`L_b`$ into bounded and compact parts and applying Riesz–Schauder theory yields finite multiplicity of eigenvalues outside the unit disk. ◻

</div>

# Asymptotic Safety Fixed Points as Truncation Shadows

Let $`T_N:\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{R}^N`$ be a continuous truncation map extracting finitely many couplings, and $`g_N(k)=T_N(\Gamma_k)`$.

<div id="thm:shadow" class="theorem">

**Theorem 10** (Truncation shadow convergence). *Under Theorem <a href="#thm:Gamma_infty" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:Gamma_infty">5</a>,
``` math
\lim_{k\to\infty} g_N(k) = T_N(\Gamma_\infty).
```
Any fixed point observed in the truncated, canonically rescaled flow is a shadow of $`\Gamma_\infty`$ under truncation and rescaling, up to truncation error.*

</div>

<div class="proof">

*Proof.* Continuity of $`T_N`$ implies convergence of truncated couplings. Canonical rescaling maps this convergence to the usual AS fixed-point variables. ◻

</div>

# Relation to Ongoing Research

The present results explain several empirical features of the AS literature: (i) persistence of fixed points across truncations; (ii) residual regulator dependence; (iii) emergence of nonlocal entire form factors; (iv) finite predictivity via a small number of relevant directions.

Rather than interpreting these as properties of an exact UV fixed point of the full theory, we identify them as shadows of a scheme-stable UV endpoint functional generated by projection-induced SPT damping.

# Conclusions

We have shown that asymptotic safety arises as a truncation shadow of a coherent-sector UV endpoint functional $`\Gamma_\infty`$ constructed via SPT-damped FRG flows on bounded-geometry slabs. The endpoint is scheme-stable under admissible regulator variations, possesses a finite-dimensional unstable manifold, and explains the appearance of nonlocal form factors and regulator-dependent fixed points in truncations.

All results are slab-local and admissibility-conditioned. Within this domain, the framework provides a unified and predictive interpretation of asymptotic safety, nonlocal gravity, and FRG regulator systematics as manifestations of a single underlying reduced-dynamical mechanism.
