\begin{abstract}
This paper asks when a Diosi-Penrose (DP) law can arise as an effective
shadow of Modal Triplet Theory (MTT). Completely positive reduction and
lifting channels give an exact Nakajima-Zwanzig memory identity; a
Davies-GKSL generator still requires an independently justified
weak-coupling, Markov, and secular limit. The central result is a
Penrose-Schoenberg completion theorem. Conditional on the selected
two-derivative Einstein/TEGR action, its static weak-field constraint, and a
declared external smearing map, the Newton self-energy of two branch mass
densities is one half of a squared Hilbert-space distance. Its pairwise
matrix is therefore conditionally negative definite, so the exponential
decay matrix defines a completely positive, trace-preserving dephasing
semigroup for every finite branch set. Its generator is exactly the
regulated DP double commutator, and it is the unique population-preserving
pure-dephasing semigroup with those real Penrose rates. This closes
positivity and Markovian completion; it does not make Penrose's provisional
lifetime estimate an MTT source theorem. MTT must still select the external
regularization, the exact Penrose rate law or an equivalent correlation
kernel, and, for objective single-outcome collapse, a stochastic instrument
and probability rule. Thus the DP law is a rigorous restricted completion,
not yet a derived fundamental law of MTT.
\end{abstract}

\section{Typed shadow dynamics}

\subsection{Reduction and lifting channels}

Let $\cH_U$ be an upstairs Hilbert space at a finite spectral cutoff and let
$\cH_S$ be the retained four-dimensional system space. Write
$\T(\cH)$ for trace-class operators on $\cH$. Assume completely positive,
trace-preserving maps
\[
 \R:\T(\cH_U)\to\T(\cH_S),\qquad
 \J:\T(\cH_S)\to\T(\cH_U),
 \qquad \R\J=I_{\T(\cH_S)}.
\]
Define superoperators on $\T(\cH_U)$ by
\[
 \cP=\J\R,\qquad \cQ=I-\cP.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Correct projection type]\label{thm:typed}
The map $\cP$ is a completely positive, trace-preserving idempotent:
$\cP^2=\cP$. Its range is $\J(\T(\cH_S))$. In particular, $\cP$ acts on
density operators; it is not a vector-space projector on $\cH_U$ inserted into a
Liouville-space formula.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Complete positivity and trace preservation are stable under composition. Moreover
\[
 \cP^2=\J\R\J\R=\J(\R\J)\R=\J\R=\cP.
\]
The range statement follows from $\cP X=\J(\R X)$ and $\cP\J Y=\J Y$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Normalized coherent filtering}

A physically interesting coherent filter may instead be a CP,
trace-nonincreasing map $\mathcal F$. It does not generally output a state.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional normalization]\label{prop:normalization}
For a density operator $\rho$ with
$p(\rho)=\Tr\mathcal F(\rho)>0$, the conditional retained state is
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal F}=\frac{\mathcal F(\rho)}{p(\rho)}.
\]
The map $\rho\mapsto\rho_{\mathcal F}$ is nonlinear unless $p(\rho)$ is constant
on the state space. Therefore an unnormalized filtered operator cannot be used as a
deterministic reduced density matrix, and postselection cannot silently be replaced
by linear open-system evolution.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The quotient has unit trace and is positive. If the normalized map were affine for
all mixtures, comparing a mixture of two states with different success probabilities
would contradict the state-dependent denominator. Hence affinity requires constant
success probability on the relevant convex set.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact memory before Markov approximation}

Let $\cL$ be a bounded generator on $\T(\cH_U)$; this is automatic at a finite
spectral cutoff. Let $X(t)=e^{t\cL}X_0$.

\begin{theorem}[Nakajima--Zwanzig identity]\label{thm:NZ}
The retained component obeys the exact equation
\begin{align}
\frac{d}{dt}\cP X(t)
={}&\cP\cL\cP X(t)
+\cP\cL\cQ e^{t\cQ\cL\cQ}\cQ X_0 \nonumber\\
&+\int_0^t
\cP\cL\cQ e^{(t-s)\cQ\cL\cQ}
\cQ\cL\cP X(s)\,ds .
\label{eq:NZ}
\end{align}
If $X_0=\J\rho_0$, the inhomogeneous second term vanishes. The final term remains
a memory kernel unless an additional limit controls it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply $\cP$ and $\cQ$ to $\dot X=\cL X$. Variation of constants gives
\[
\cQ X(t)=e^{t\cQ\cL\cQ}\cQ X_0+
\int_0^t e^{(t-s)\cQ\cL\cQ}\cQ\cL\cP X(s)\,ds.
\]
Substitution in the $\cP$ equation yields \eqref{eq:NZ}.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:NZ} is the correct exact output of projection
~\cite{Nakajima1958,Zwanzig1960}. It is generally
non-Markovian. Complete positivity of the exact reduced channel
$\R e^{t\cL}\J$ does not imply that its time-local generator exists or has GKSL
form at each time.

\section{When a GKSL generator is justified}

Consider a system--environment Hamiltonian at finite cutoff,
\[
 H_\lambda=H_S+H_E+\lambda\sum_\alpha A_\alpha\otimes B_\alpha,
\]
with a stationary environmental state $\rho_E$. Let
\[
 C_{\alpha\beta}(t)=
\Tr_E\!\left(\rho_E B_\alpha(t)B_\beta(0)\right).
\]

\begin{assumption}[Davies source conditions]\label{ass:Davies}
The initial state is factorized at the preparation time; the correlations are
stationary and integrable strongly enough for the weak-coupling limit; the rescaled
time is $t=\lambda^{-2}\tau$; and the secular average over distinct Bohr frequencies
exists. The Fourier matrices
\[
 \gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)=
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\omega t}C_{\alpha\beta}(t)\,dt
\]
are finite.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Davies--GKSL limit]\label{thm:Davies}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:Davies} and the standard finite-system weak-coupling
convergence hypotheses, the interaction-picture reduced dynamics converges on
bounded rescaled-time intervals to a completely positive semigroup. Its generator is
\[
 \cL_D(\rho)=-i[H_{\rm LS},\rho]
+\sum_{\omega,\alpha,\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}(\omega)
\left(
A_\beta(\omega)\rho A_\alpha(\omega)^\dagger
-\frac12\{A_\alpha(\omega)^\dagger A_\beta(\omega),\rho\}
\right).
\]
The matrices $\gamma(\omega)$ are positive semidefinite. Without this or another
explicit Markovian limit, Theorem~\ref{thm:NZ} does not imply a GKSL equation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the Davies weak-coupling theorem~\cite{Davies1974} applied to the declared source data.
Positivity of the correlation Fourier matrices is the positive-type/Bochner step
that produces complete positivity in the GKSL form
~\cite{GKSL1976,Lindblad1976}. The theorem is conditional here because MTT has
not yet emitted the required correlation functions.
\end{proof}

\section{The precise Di\'osi--Penrose limit}

\subsection{Newton self-energy from the gravitational constraint}

Let $G_{\rm eff}>0$ be the coefficient appearing in the selected four-dimensional
Einstein/TEGR law. Let $s_\ell$ be a normalized real spatial smearing function and
write $\mu_{a,\ell}=s_\ell*\mu_a$ for the regulated mass density of branch $a$.
Define
\[
 K_\ell(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)=
 G_{\rm eff}\int_{\mathbb R^3}\!\!\int_{\mathbb R^3}
 \frac{s_\ell(\mathbf x-\mathbf u)s_\ell(\mathbf y-\mathbf v)}
 {|\mathbf u-\mathbf v|}\,d^3u\,d^3v.
\]
Its Fourier multiplier is
$4\pi G_{\rm eff}|\widehat s_\ell(\mathbf k)|^2/|\mathbf k|^2\geq0$, so it is a positive
quadratic-form kernel on its regulated domain.

\begin{theorem}[Newton-constraint energy theorem]\label{thm:NewtonEnergy}
Assume the static weak-field limit of the selected MTT gravitational law obeys the
Poisson constraint
\[
 -\Delta u_a=4\pi G_{\rm eff}\mu_{a,\ell},
 \qquad u_a(\mathbf x)\longrightarrow0\quad(|\mathbf x|\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
For regulated densities for which the displayed integrals are finite, the positive
incompatibility energy of branches $a,b$ is
\begin{align}
 E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}
 &:=\frac{1}{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}
 \int_{\mathbb R^3}|\nabla(u_a-u_b)|^2\,d^3x \label{eq:field-energy}\\
 &=\frac12\int d^3x\,d^3y\,
 \delta\mu_{ab}(\mathbf x)K_\ell(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
 \delta\mu_{ab}(\mathbf y), \qquad
 \delta\mu_{ab}:=\mu_a-\mu_b. \label{eq:newton-energy}
\end{align}
In particular $E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}\geq0$, and it uses the same $G_{\rm eff}$ as the
gravitational action rather than a new collapse-sector coupling.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The decaying solution is
$u_a(\mathbf x)=G_{\rm eff}\int
\mu_{a,\ell}(\mathbf y)|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|^{-1}d^3y$.
Integration by parts and the Poisson equation give
\[
 \frac{1}{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}\int|\nabla\delta u|^2
 =\frac{1}{2}\int\delta u\,\delta\mu_\ell
 =\frac{G_{\rm eff}}{2}\int
 \frac{\delta\mu_\ell(\mathbf x)\delta\mu_\ell(\mathbf y)}
 {|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|}\,d^3x\,d^3y.
\]
Moving the two smearings from the densities to the Green kernel yields
\eqref{eq:newton-energy}. Positivity is immediate from \eqref{eq:field-energy}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is the controlled Newtonian content of the selected two-derivative
gravity law. It derives the spatial quadratic form once $G_{\rm eff}$, the boundary
condition and $s_\ell$ are supplied. It does not select the value of $G_{\rm eff}$ or
the external smearing map.

\subsection{Penrose energy is an admissible branch metric}

Let $\{|a\rangle\}_{a=1}^N$ be a finite set of approximate joint eigenstates of the
regulated mass-density operators. Define vectors in the real Hilbert space
$L^2(\mathbb R^3;\mathbb R^3)$ by
\[
 r_a=\frac{\nabla u_a}{\sqrt{4\pi G_{\rm eff}}}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:NewtonEnergy} says exactly that
\[
 E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}=\frac{1}{2}\|r_a-r_b\|^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Penrose--Schoenberg completely positive completion]
\label{thm:PenroseSchoenberg}
On the finite branch algebra define
\[
 \Gamma_{ab}:=\frac{E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}}{\hbar},\qquad
 M_{ab}(t):=e^{-t\Gamma_{ab}}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Gamma$ is conditionally negative definite;
\item $M(t)$ is positive semidefinite with $M_{aa}(t)=1$ for every $t\geq0$;
\item
\[
 \T_t(\rho):=M(t)\circ\rho
\]
is a completely positive, trace-preserving, population-preserving semigroup; and
\item its generator is the regulated DP generator
\begin{equation}
 \D_{\rm DP}(\rho)=
 -\frac1{2\hbar}\int d^3x\,d^3y\,
 K_\ell(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)
 [\widehat\mu(\mathbf x),[\widehat\mu(\mathbf y),\rho]].
 \label{eq:DP-generator}
\end{equation}
It is the unique strongly continuous, time-homogeneous Schur semigroup on this
branch algebra which preserves populations, has no additional diagonal Hamiltonian
phase, and has infinitesimal real decay rates $\Gamma_{ab}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For complex $c_a$ with $\sum_a c_a=0$, expansion of the squared distance gives
\[
 \sum_{a,b}\overline{c_a}c_b\Gamma_{ab}
 =-\frac{1}{\hbar}\left\|\sum_a c_a r_a\right\|^2\leq0.
\]
Thus $\Gamma$ is conditionally negative definite. Schoenberg's theorem
~\cite{Schoenberg1938} implies $M(t)\succeq0$ for all $t\geq0$.
Because $M_{aa}=1$, the Schur multiplier has a Gram/Stinespring representation and
is CPTP. Entrywise multiplication shows $M(t+s)=M(t)\circ M(s)$.

On a matrix unit $|a\rangle\langle b|$, the double commutator in
\eqref{eq:DP-generator} multiplies by
$\delta\mu_{ab}(\mathbf x)\delta\mu_{ab}(\mathbf y)$. Hence
\[
 \D_{\rm DP}(|a\rangle\langle b|)
 =-\frac{E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}}{\hbar}|a\rangle\langle b|
 =-\Gamma_{ab}|a\rangle\langle b|,
\]
which is the generator of $\T_t$. Finally, a strongly continuous scalar semigroup
on each matrix unit with derivative $-\Gamma_{ab}$ at zero is
$e^{-t\Gamma_{ab}}$; this proves uniqueness in the declared Schur class.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the missing specialization of the corpus's general Schoenberg
criterion: the physical branch separation is now the Newton field-energy distance,
not an arbitrary damping matrix. Penrose obtained this self-energy from the squared
difference of branch free-fall fields, but described its conversion into a lifetime
only as a provisional order estimate~\cite{Penrose1996}. The theorem proves the
mathematical completion of an exact rate rule; it does not promote that rule to a
selected MTT law.

\begin{corollary}[Davies realization of the same generator]\label{cor:DPDavies}
If the independently controlled Davies source in
Theorem~\ref{thm:Davies} couples through $\widehat\mu(\mathbf x)$ and its relevant
zero-frequency Kossakowski kernel is
$K_\ell(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)/\hbar$, then its dissipator is
\eqref{eq:DP-generator} and its branch rates are
$E_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}/\hbar$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Diagonalizing the positive kernel gives commuting Hermitian Lindblad operators.
Their GKSL sum is \eqref{eq:DP-generator}; evaluation on branch matrix units was
performed in Theorem~\ref{thm:PenroseSchoenberg}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gaussian realization is not an outcome theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Random-unitary realization and outcome obstruction]
\label{prop:unravelling}
Let $V=\operatorname{span}\{r_a\}$ and let $W_t$ be standard Brownian motion on
$V$. The diagonal random unitary
\[
 U_t(W)|a\rangle=
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{i}{\sqrt\hbar}\langle r_a,W_t\rangle\right]|a\rangle
\]
satisfies
\[
 \T_t(\rho)=\mathbb E\bigl[U_t(W)\rho U_t(W)^\dagger\bigr].
\]
Consequently the DP density-operator semigroup alone does not select objective
single-outcome collapse: it admits an unravelling in which every individual state
evolves unitarily with a random phase.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Gaussian characteristic function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left[-\frac{i}{\sqrt\hbar}
 \langle r_a-r_b,W_t\rangle\right]
 =\exp\!\left[-\frac{t}{2\hbar}\|r_a-r_b\|^2\right]
 =e^{-tE_{ab}^{(G,\ell)}/\hbar}.
\]
This is exactly the Schur multiplier of
Theorem~\ref{thm:PenroseSchoenberg}. Since this unravelling has no state-vector
reduction, the ensemble channel cannot by itself choose a collapse ontology,
instrument, outcome probabilities or realized branch. A nonlinear collapse
unravelling such as the Di\'osi construction~\cite{Diosi1989} is additional data.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What curvature dominance does not prove]
The statement that discarded modes are curvature dominated does not determine their
two-point correlation function and therefore does not select a Davies realization.
The selected Einstein/TEGR constraint fixes the Newton spatial form only after its
effective coupling, boundary condition and external smearing are declared. It still
does not prove the exact Penrose rate rule, Markov additivity, or an objective
single-outcome process.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{No smearing length from an internal gap alone}

\begin{proposition}[Scale-typing obstruction]\label{prop:scale}
Let $\lambda_{\rm int}$ be an eigenvalue of an internal operator on $X_6$. The datum
$\lambda_{\rm int}$ alone cannot canonically define an external spatial smearing
length on $Y_4$. Such a definition requires an explicit metric normalization and an
intertwiner, modulus, or response map that converts internal spectral units into
external length units.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Rescale the internal and external metrics independently. The internal eigenvalue
changes under the internal rescaling while an external length changes under the
external rescaling. No relation between them is invariant under these independent
changes unless extra cross-sector data constrain the rescalings. Therefore
$\ell_{\rm coh}=\lambda_{\rm int}^{-1/2}$ is at most an internal length before such
a map is supplied.
\end{proof}

The regularization is experimentally consequential, not a cosmetic cure of the
point-source divergence. A dedicated spontaneous-radiation search rules out the
natural parameter-free DP version and places a lower bound on the effective nuclear
mass-density radius~\cite{Donadi2021}. Any MTT-selected $s_\ell$ must therefore be
transported to the experiment's convention and tested rather than chosen only for
formal convenience.

\section{Thresholds, knees and protocols}

\begin{theorem}[Analytic finite-time response]\label{thm:analytic}
Let $s\mapsto\cL(s)$ be a real-analytic family of bounded GKSL generators on a
finite-dimensional state space. For fixed $t<\infty$, initial state $\rho_0(s)$ and
observable $A(s)$ analytic in $s$, the response
\[
 f_t(s)=\Tr\!\left(A(s)e^{t\cL(s)}\rho_0(s)\right)
\]
is analytic. Hence a literal finite-strength nonanalytic knee cannot be inferred
from such a family without a nonanalytic source, a stopping/conditioning operation,
or a singular long-time, zero-noise, thermodynamic, or continuum limit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exponential series for $e^{t\cL(s)}$ converges locally uniformly in operator
norm, and each term is analytic. Composition with analytic finite-dimensional data
and the trace preserves analyticity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-noise OU crossing is smooth]\label{prop:OU}
For the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process
\[
 dX_t=-\gamma(s)X_t\,dt+\sqrt{2D(s)}\,dW_t
\]
in a bounded interval with absorbing endpoints, if $\gamma$ and $D>0$ vary smoothly,
then the finite-domain mean first-passage solution varies smoothly through
$\gamma=0$. A logistic knee may approximate a rapid crossover but is not an exact
threshold theorem without a specified singular limit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean exit time solves a uniformly elliptic second-order boundary-value problem
with smooth coefficients. Standard parameter dependence for this finite-domain
problem is smooth while $D$ remains strictly positive. The sign change of the drift
does not destroy ellipticity.
\end{proof}

Repeated interventions can produce Zeno or anti-Zeno behavior, but the sign of the
crossover depends on the instrument, interval and spectral density. It is not forced
by noninvertible projection alone. A valid MTT prediction must therefore emit the
instrument and environment correlation data, rather than label protocol dependence
as universal.

\section{Conclusion}

The spatial part of the DP proposal is now substantially less mysterious. Once the
selected Einstein/TEGR law is placed in its static weak-field regime, branch
incompatibility is the positive Newton field energy and therefore a squared Hilbert
distance. The Penrose rate matrix consequently has a unique CPTP pure-dephasing
Markov completion, whose generator is exactly the DP double commutator. This is a
real theorem-level advance over merely assuming that a Kossakowski matrix is
positive.

The remaining gap is also exact. Penrose's argument motivates but does not derive
the equality between MTT's physical branch-decay rate and $E_G/\hbar$. MTT must select
that identity or an equivalent Davies correlation kernel, the external smearing and
effective Newton normalization, and controlled errors. Moreover, the ensemble DP
channel does not select a realized outcome; an objective-collapse claim additionally
needs a stochastic instrument and probability theorem. Version 3 therefore closes
the gravitational-metric and completely-positive-completion layers without claiming
that the source-selection or measurement problem is already solved.
