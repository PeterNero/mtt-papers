\begin{abstract}
This paper replaces a universal identification of measurement-induced
phase transitions, collapse thresholds, basin loss, and Zeno or
anti-Zeno behavior by a typed, model-dependent framework.  A hybrid
monitored circuit is specified by its Hilbert space, unitary layers,
measurement instrument, record law, conditioned trajectories, and
averaged channel.  The trajectory-averaged subsystem entropy is proved
to differ from the entropy of the averaged state by a nonnegative Holevo
quantity; consequently, the averaged channel does not retain the
trajectory data that define the entanglement transition.  Basin margins
are retained as an effective model class only after a metric, boundary,
generator, initial law, and stopping rule are fixed.  For a stable
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck coordinate with an absorbing boundary, we derive the
exact mean first-passage integral and a basin-local contraction bound.
The result is smooth in finite positive parameters and supplies no
universal logistic knee.  For a two-level system repeatedly projected at
intervals of length tau, we derive the exact survival probability and
its Zeno limit.  Anti-Zeno enhancement is not universal and requires a
specified reservoir or protocol.  Measurement remains an ordinary
physical interaction; outcome completion and the selection of one
record are separate from an ensemble entanglement transition.  The Modal
Triplet Theory interpretation is conditional on a same-source map that
emits the instrument, record weights, effective basin coordinate, and
comparison errors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Measurement-induced entanglement transitions occur in quantum dynamics
that combines entangling evolution with local monitoring.  In a
trajectory description, weak monitoring can permit volume-law
entanglement, while sufficiently strong monitoring can support an
area-law regime \cite{skinner2019,li2018}.  Related purification
transitions diagnose how rapidly a monitored system loses dependence on
an initially mixed state \cite{gullans2020}.  These are ensemble and
thermodynamic statements about conditioned quantum trajectories.

They are not automatically statements about a single run ``collapsing,''
about an averaged density operator, or about a classical particle
crossing a basin boundary.  The distinctions matter:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a quantum instrument gives record probabilities and conditional
post-measurement states;
\item averaging over records gives a completely positive
trace-preserving channel;
\item a trajectory entanglement statistic is nonlinear in the
conditional state; and
\item selecting or observing one record is a physical event whose
probability law is an input to, or a theorem about, the instrument.
\end{enumerate}

The earlier version of this paper compressed these layers into one
projection-induced basin transition.  That move obscured the actual
mathematics and produced claims that were stronger than their
assumptions.  The revised paper asks a more precise question: which
parts of basin language can be derived for a declared reduced model, and
what additional map would be required for that model to explain a
measurement-induced entanglement transition?

\section{Claim tier and ownership}

The exact results below are finite-dimensional or one-dimensional
stochastic statements.  We prove:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the instrument normalization and trajectory/ensemble distinction;
\item an exact entropy identity showing what is lost by record
averaging;
\item a basin-local contraction estimate and exact mean first-passage
formula for a declared Ornstein--Uhlenbeck model;
\item smoothness rather than a universal knee for that finite model; and
\item an exact repeated-projection survival law and Zeno limit.
\end{enumerate}

We do not prove the existence or universality class of a thermodynamic
MIPT for arbitrary circuits.  We do not derive an anti-Zeno regime, an
objective single-history law, or the Born rule for every apparatus
context.  The exact reduced-semigroup defect for evolve--coarse-grain
maps belongs to the companion ETH/MBL paper and is not duplicated here
\cite{nero_eth_mbl}.  Standard monitored-circuit and replica results are
imported from their primary sources
\cite{skinner2019,li2018,bao2020,gullans2020}.

\section{A typed monitored-circuit model}

\subsection{Circuit and instrument}

Let $\Hilb_L=(\bbC^q)^{\otimes L}$.  A depth-$T$ hybrid
circuit consists of brickwork unitary layers $U_t$ and local
measurements.  At each eligible site and layer, a Bernoulli choice with
parameter $p$ either applies no measurement or one of the projectors
$\{P_a\}_{a=1}^q$, where
\[
 P_aP_b=\delta_{ab}P_a,\qquad \sum_aP_a=\id .
\]
The complete record $r\in\Rec_{L,T}$ contains both the measurement
locations and their outcomes.  It determines a Kraus operator $K_r(p)$.
The Bernoulli weights can be absorbed into the Kraus operators so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:complete}
 \sum_{r\in\Rec_{L,T}}K_r(p)^\dagger K_r(p)=\id .
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Trajectory instrument]\label{def:instrument}
For an initial state $\rho_0$, the record probability and conditioned
state are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trajectory}
 \pi_r=\Tr[K_r\rho_0K_r^\dagger],\qquad
 \rho_r=\frac{K_r\rho_0K_r^\dagger}{\pi_r}
\end{equation}
whenever $\pi_r>0$.  The family of completely positive maps
$\Inst_r(\rho)=K_r\rho K_r^\dagger$ is the instrument.
\end{definition}

\Cref{eq:complete} implies $\sum_r\pi_r=1$.  Nothing in the
normalization makes measurement metaphysically special.  It is simply
the mathematical record of a physical coupling and readout protocol.

\begin{definition}[Record-averaged channel]\label{def:channel}
Discarding the record gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel}
 \Chan_{p,T}(\rho)=\sum_rK_r(p)\rho K_r(p)^\dagger
 =\sum_r\pi_r\rho_r .
\end{equation}
This map is completely positive and trace preserving.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The transition observable}

For a bipartition $A\cup\bar A$, define
\[
 \rho_{A,r}=\Tr_{\bar A}\rho_r,\qquad
 \bar\rho_A=\sum_r\pi_r\rho_{A,r}.
\]
The trajectory-averaged von Neumann entropy is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:traj-entropy}
 \overline S_A=\sum_r\pi_r S(\rho_{A,r}),
\end{equation}
while the averaged-state entropy is $S(\bar\rho_A)$.  A MIPT analysis
uses quantities such as \cref{eq:traj-entropy}, trajectory mutual
information, purification time, or replica moments.  It is not defined
by $S(\bar\rho_A)$ alone.

\begin{theorem}[Exact record-information gap]\label{thm:holevo}
For every finite instrument,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holevo}
 S(\bar\rho_A)-\overline S_A
 =
 \sum_r\pi_r
 D\!\left(\rho_{A,r}\middle\Vert\bar\rho_A\right)
 =:\chi(A{:}R)\geq0 ,
\end{equation}
where $D(\cdot\Vert\cdot)$ is quantum relative entropy.  Moreover,
\[
 0\leq\chi(A{:}R)\leq H(\{\pi_r\}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the relative entropies gives
\[
\sum_r\pi_r\Tr[\rho_{A,r}\log\rho_{A,r}]
-\Tr[\bar\rho_A\log\bar\rho_A],
\]
which is $S(\bar\rho_A)-\sum_r\pi_rS(\rho_{A,r})$.
Nonnegativity follows from positivity of relative entropy.  The upper
bound is the standard Holevo bound for the classical--quantum state
$\sum_r\pi_r|r\rangle\langle r|\otimes\rho_{A,r}$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:holevo} is the decisive type check.  Record averaging can
change an entanglement diagnostic by a positive information term.  Many
different ensembles can have the same average state, so the averaged
channel does not reconstruct the conditioned ensemble without the
instrument decomposition and record.

\begin{example}[Same state, different records]\label{ex:ensembles}
The maximally mixed qubit has both decompositions
\[
\frac{\id}{2}
=\frac12|0\rangle\langle0|+\frac12|1\rangle\langle1|
=\frac12|+\rangle\langle+|+\frac12|-\rangle\langle-|.
\]
The average state is identical, while the record-conditioned states and
their correlations with a chosen apparatus basis differ.
\end{example}

\section{Finite circuits and thermodynamic transitions}

For fixed $L$ and $T$, the record set is finite.  The unnormalized
weights $\Inst_r(\rho_0)$ are finite products of unitary matrices,
projectors, and Bernoulli factors.  Away from parameter values where a
record probability or a reduced-state eigenvalue vanishes, finite
trajectory observables are smooth functions of $p$.  Singular behavior
associated with a phase transition requires a declared limiting
procedure, commonly $L\to\infty$ followed by a long-time or steady-state
limit.

\begin{proposition}[Finite records do not imply a universal knee]
\label{prop:no-knee}
Let $F_{L,T}(p)$ be a finite-record statistic built from smooth Kraus
operators on an interval where all terms entering its logarithms have a
uniform positive lower bound.  Then $F_{L,T}$ is smooth on that interval.
No interior nonanalytic threshold follows from instrument normalization
or noninvertibility alone.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Finite sums, products, partial traces, and spectral functions restricted
away from zero eigenvalues preserve smooth parameter dependence.
\end{proof}

The finite-size transition question is therefore empirical and
model-specific.  A report should state the circuit ensemble, local
dimension, boundary conditions, initial state, record sampling,
entanglement estimator, disorder or circuit realizations, numerical
uncertainty, and order of limits.  One may test a scaling form such as
\[
 X_L(p)=F\!\left((p-p_c)L^{1/\nu}\right)
 +L^{-\omega}G\!\left((p-p_c)L^{1/\nu}\right),
\]
but the functions, exponents, corrections, fit window, and covariance
model are part of the hypothesis.  Large-$q$ mappings can select special
critical values in special circuit ensembles \cite{bao2020}; those
values are not universal consequences of projection.

\section{Basin margins as a declared model class}

\subsection{Complete stochastic record}

A basin model begins only after the following data are fixed:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basin-record}
 \mathfrak B=(X,\mathsf d,\mathcal L,\mu_0,B,\partial_{\rm abs}B,
 \partial_{\rm ref}B,\tau_B,\mathcal O,\mathcal E).
\end{equation}
Here $X$ is the reduced state space, $\mathsf d$ its metric,
$\mathcal L$ the generator, $\mu_0$ the initial law, $B$ the basin,
the two boundary parts carry absorbing and reflecting conditions,
$\tau_B$ is the stopping time, $\mathcal O$ the reported observables,
and $\mathcal E$ the approximation/error certificate.  A scalar
``margin'' without these rows is not a first-passage problem.

\subsection{An exact local model}

To exhibit what can actually be derived, consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ou}
 \dd X_t=-\kappa X_t\,\dd t+\sqrt{2D}\,\dd W_t,
 \qquad \kappa>0,\ D>0,
\end{equation}
on $(-\infty,b)$ with $b>0$, absorbing at $b$, and with the natural
boundary condition at $-\infty$.  The stable point $0$ lies inside the
basin, and
\[
 \tau_b=\inf\{t\geq0:X_t=b\}
\]
is the exit time.

\begin{proposition}[Basin-local synchronous contraction]
\label{prop:ou-contraction}
Let $X_t^x$ and $X_t^y$ solve \cref{eq:ou} with the same Brownian path
and initial values $x,y<b$.  Before either process is stopped,
\[
 |X_t^x-X_t^y|=e^{-\kappa t}|x-y|.
\]
Consequently, the unrestricted OU transition kernel contracts the
one-Wasserstein distance by at most $e^{-\kappa t}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The noise cancels in the difference, which solves
$\dd(X_t^x-X_t^y)=-\kappa(X_t^x-X_t^y)\dd t$.  The Wasserstein statement
follows by applying the synchronous coupling to an optimal initial
coupling.
\end{proof}

The statement is deliberately basin-local.  Stopping, conditioning on
survival, or resetting after exit changes the transition law and must be
specified separately.

\begin{theorem}[Exact OU mean first-passage time]\label{thm:mfpt}
For $x<b$, the mean exit time $T(x)=\E_x[\tau_b]$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mfpt}
 T(x)=\frac1D\int_x^b
 e^{\kappa y^2/(2D)}
 \left(\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{-\kappa z^2/(2D)}\,\dd z\right)\dd y .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The backward equation is
\[
 DT''(x)-\kappa xT'(x)=-1,\qquad T(b)=0,
\]
with the natural condition
$T'(x)e^{-\kappa x^2/(2D)}\to0$ as $x\to-\infty$.  Multiplying the
differential equation by $e^{-\kappa x^2/(2D)}$ and integrating from
$-\infty$ to $y$ gives
\[
T'(y)=-\frac1D e^{\kappa y^2/(2D)}
\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{-\kappa z^2/(2D)}\,\dd z .
\]
Integrating from $x$ to $b$ and using $T(b)=0$ yields
\cref{eq:mfpt}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No universal logistic law]\label{cor:smooth}
For $\kappa,D,b>0$ and $x<b$, $T(x)$ is smooth in the finite parameters
on compact subsets of that domain.  A logistic crossover is not implied
by the OU generator.
\end{corollary}

This model can be a useful local surrogate if a monitored-circuit
coordinate is independently shown to obey \cref{eq:ou} with a controlled
error.  It does not derive a MIPT, and its parameters cannot be read from
a generic capacity margin.

\section{Protocol-specific Zeno behavior}

Consider a two-level system with
\[
 H=\frac{\Omega}{2}\sigma_x,\qquad
 P_0=|0\rangle\langle0|,
\]
initially in $|0\rangle$.  Evolve for time $\tau$, measure
$\{P_0,\id-P_0\}$, and repeat.  Condition on obtaining the survival
record $0$ at every step.

\begin{theorem}[Exact repeated-projection survival law]\label{thm:zeno}
For $n=t/\tau\in\bbN$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeno}
 P_{\rm surv}(t;\tau)
 =
 \left[\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\Omega\tau}{2}\right)\right]^{t/\tau}.
\end{equation}
At fixed $t$,
\[
 \log P_{\rm surv}(t;\tau)
 =-\frac{\Omega^2t}{4}\tau+O(t\tau^3),
 \qquad
 \lim_{\tau\downarrow0}P_{\rm surv}(t;\tau)=1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One interval has amplitude
$\langle0|e^{-iH\tau}|0\rangle=\cos(\Omega\tau/2)$.
Conditional survival over independent repeated projections multiplies
the interval probabilities, giving \cref{eq:zeno}.  The expansion follows
from $\log\cos^2 u=-u^2+O(u^4)$.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine Zeno theorem because the Hamiltonian, projector,
timing, conditioning, and comparison quantity are explicit.  It is not a
theorem that every measurement protocol has an anti-Zeno regime.

\begin{proposition}[Anti-Zeno behavior is not unavoidable]
\label{prop:anti}
If a measured projector $P$ commutes with $H$ and the initial state lies
in $\operatorname{Ran}P$, then repeated measurements preserve the state
for every measurement interval.  There is no intermediate enhancement
of escape.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Commutation makes $\operatorname{Ran}P$ invariant under the unitary
evolution, so every survival probability equals one.
\end{proof}

Anti-Zeno enhancement can occur in models where measurement broadening
overlaps a reservoir spectrum, but its presence and crossover scale
depend on that spectral density and protocol \cite{kofman2000}.  It
cannot be inferred from noncommutativity or noninvertibility alone.

\section{Outcome completion is not the entanglement transition}

The instrument in \cref{def:instrument} supplies a normalized law for
records and a conditional state for each record.  The MIPT concerns the
large-system behavior of nonlinear statistics across that record
ensemble.  An actual detector run realizes one record as an ordinary
physical process.  These statements should not be collapsed:
\begin{enumerate}
\item decoherence or record averaging suppresses selected coherences;
\item conditioning updates the state associated with an observed record;
\item basin contraction can stabilize a record after it exists;
\item none of those facts alone derives the probability law from an
upper geometry; and
\item an ensemble phase transition does not select one ontic history.
\end{enumerate}

The companion MTT measurement paper gives the general completion-kernel
and record-stabilization framework and records the current limited
q79 one-anchor result \cite{nero_measurement}.  Its arbitrary-context
Born source and objective one-history obligations remain open.  This
paper neither re-proves nor enlarges those claims.

\section{Conditional interface with MTT}

MTT contains coherent spectral projectors and stability margins at its
current foundation tier \cite{nero_foundation}.  A coherent projector is
not automatically a measurement instrument.  To derive the monitored
model above from MTT, one same-source construction must emit:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the physical Hilbert space and circuit or continuous generator;
\item the outcome-indexed completely positive maps $\Inst_r$;
\item the normalization and record weights $\pi_r$;
\item the map from upper observables to trajectory entanglement
diagnostics;
\item any reduced basin coordinate, metric, generator, and absorbing
boundary; and
\item finite-volume and limiting comparison errors.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional instrument transport]\label{thm:transport}
Suppose an upper MTT instrument
$\{\widehat{\Inst}_r\}$ and a channel isometry $V$ satisfy, for every
allowed input state,
\[
 \left\|
 V\widehat{\Inst}_r(\widehat\rho)V^\dagger
 -\Inst_r(V\widehat\rho V^\dagger)
 \right\|_1\leq\epsilon_r
\]
and $\sum_r\epsilon_r\leq\epsilon$.  Then the total-variation distance
between the two record laws is at most $\epsilon/2$, and every bounded
record statistic $f$ satisfies
\[
 \left|\E_{\widehat\pi}f-\E_{\pi}f\right|
 \leq \epsilon\,\norm{f}_\infty .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Taking traces and using
$|\Tr A|\leq\norm{A}_1$ gives
$\sum_r|\widehat\pi_r-\pi_r|\leq\epsilon$.  The first statement is the
definition of total variation.  The second follows by summing
$f(r)(\widehat\pi_r-\pi_r)$ and applying the same bound.
\end{proof}

Transporting trajectory entropies additionally requires control of the
normalized conditional states when $\pi_r$ is small; a probability floor
or weighted continuity theorem must therefore be declared.  The theorem
shows exactly where an MTT source would enter, without pretending that
basin vocabulary already supplies the source.

\section{Validation and falsification protocol}

A serious numerical or experimental claim should publish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the complete instrument or enough circuit data to reconstruct its
Kraus maps;
\item record-resolved data or sufficient statistics, not only the
averaged density operator;
\item the distinction between $\overline S_A$ and $S(\bar\rho_A)$;
\item sizes, depths, samples, seeds, error bars, fit windows, and
correlated covariance;
\item at least one alternative crossover model and a held-out size or
observable;
\item for a basin claim, every row of \cref{eq:basin-record};
\item for a Zeno or anti-Zeno claim, the unmeasured comparison dynamics,
projector, timing convention, and reservoir spectrum if present; and
\item for an MTT claim, the source hashes and transport errors required
by \cref{thm:transport}.
\end{enumerate}

The framework is falsified as an explanation if its selected basin
coordinate does not predict record-resolved observables beyond the
standard instrument model, if the first-passage approximation fails its
error certificate, or if the proposed MTT source map is chosen only
after seeing the target critical data.

\section{Conclusion}

Measurement-induced entanglement transitions, averaged decoherence,
record completion, first passage, and Zeno physics can interact in one
experiment, but they are not one theorem.  The correct common language
is a typed physical instrument plus explicitly declared effective
models.  The trajectory/ensemble entropy identity proves why records
cannot be discarded when studying a MIPT.  The OU calculation shows what
a genuine basin theorem requires and why it does not produce a universal
knee.  The repeated-projection calculation shows how a Zeno limit is
derived from a protocol, while a commuting counterexample rules out
unavoidable anti-Zeno behavior.

MTT may ultimately explain why a particular instrument and effective
basin model are selected together.  That claim now has a precise exit:
construct the same-source instrument, record law, observable transport,
and error bounds.  Until then, basin margins are a useful model class,
not a universal identification of measurement phenomena.
