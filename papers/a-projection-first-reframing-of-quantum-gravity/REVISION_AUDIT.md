# Revision Audit

## Supersedes

`A Projection-First Reframing of Quantum Gravity`, version 1.

## Reason

The first edition treated gravity as categorically unquantizable, described
the metric as bookkeeping rather than a field with physical perturbative
degrees of freedom, characterized existing quantum-gravity programs as
repeated failures, and promoted horizons and singularities directly to
projection or admissibility breakdown. Those claims exceeded established
results and the current MTT construction.

## Resolution

The second edition:

- recognizes perturbative gravitons and General Relativity as a predictive
  low-energy quantum effective field theory;
- distinguishes a proposed deeper source ontology from effective metric
  degrees of freedom;
- presents "quantized source action stresses an effective geometric medium"
  as a two-stage construction target rather than an achieved derivation;
- treats horizons as global causal boundaries and observer-relative
  reconstruction limits, not automatic local failure;
- states singularities in terms of geodesic incompleteness and failed
  continuation estimates rather than universal curvature blow-up;
- compares the proposal fairly with EFT, semiclassical gravity, holography,
  string theory, and loop or spin-network approaches; and
- specifies eight constructive gates and explicit success and failure
  criteria for an MTT quantum-gravity sector.

## Retained Content

Noninvertible restriction maps provide a precise model of how globally
distinct states can become indistinguishable to a restricted observer without
fundamental information destruction. This keeps the projection-first
perspective useful as a possible organizing language for effective geometry,
horizons, and observer-dependent reconstruction.

## Remaining Boundary

Current MTT work does not derive a complete Lorentzian gravitational action,
positive physical graviton pole, universal conserved stress response,
Newton/Planck normalization, or ultraviolet completion from one selected
source. The exact internal helicity-two support result and the conditional
reduction theorem do not by themselves close those gates.
