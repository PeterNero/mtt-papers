\begin{abstract}
This paper formulates a projection-first dark-sector hypothesis within Modal
Triplet Theory (MTT) and identifies exactly what would be required to turn it
into a physical model.  The retained idea is that a reduced description may
have an availability profile in which statistical or relational encodings
remain usable while more local encodings do not.  We prove, however, that an
availability profile alone cannot determine a stress-energy tensor, a modified
field equation, an equation of state, or a perturbation spectrum.  In
particular, statistical encoding does not imply cold, pressureless,
collisionless matter, and relational encoding does not imply accelerated
expansion.

We therefore replace the former identification of dark matter with E9 and dark
energy with E8 by two explicit hypotheses.  Each requires a selected covariant
source map or a selected diffeomorphism-invariant effective action.  We derive
the associated conservation condition and state separate background,
linear-perturbation, lensing, cluster-collision, cosmic-microwave-background,
baryon-acoustic-oscillation, structure-growth, and equation-of-state gates.
The current MTT result is consequently an organized candidate taxonomy and a
completion contract, not a derivation of the observed dark sector.  This
revision records which earlier claims are retained, withdrawn, or left open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status, Purpose, and Logical Tier}

Observations constrain a successful cosmological model through several
independent channels.  The cosmic microwave background (CMB), baryon acoustic
oscillations (BAO), weak lensing, galaxy clustering, supernova distances, and
cluster collisions do not merely ask whether an additional gravitational
effect exists; together they constrain its background evolution, perturbations,
clustering, lensing response, and interactions
\cite{Planck2018CosmologicalParameters,Clowe2006BulletCluster,
DESY3Cosmology,DESIDR2Cosmology}.

The purpose of this paper is narrower.  It asks whether restrictions on the
availability of reduced encodings could organize candidate explanations of
dark-sector phenomenology.  The answer is:
\begin{quote}
\emph{possibly as a hypothesis, but not from encoding absence alone.}
\end{quote}

\begin{remark}[Current status]
The results proved here are classification and non-identifiability results.
No selected MTT action, covariant dark-sector stress tensor, cosmological
solution, likelihood, or held-out numerical prediction is supplied.  Thus this
paper is interpretive and diagnostic.  It is not a replacement for
$\Lambda$CDM, particle dark matter, or a specified modified-gravity theory.
\end{remark}

The central correction to version 1 is the distinction between three claims:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item an encoding is unavailable in a reduced description;
\item a covariant source or modified field equation is selected;
\item that dynamics reproduces the observed dark-sector data.
\end{enumerate}
Claim (i) does not imply (ii), and claim (ii) does not imply (iii).

\subsection{Position in the MTT program}

Program C distinguishes spacetime coordinates, internal bundles, connections,
operators, spectral projectors, and reduced descriptions as different typed
objects \cite{Nero2026ProgramC}.  This paper adopts that discipline.  An
``encoding'' is not a substance, a coordinate, a stress tensor, or an
interaction merely because the same verbal label occurs in several
descriptions.

The D-layer is therefore downstream of the following missing construction:
\[
\text{MTT source data}
\longrightarrow
\text{selected covariant dynamics}
\longrightarrow
\text{cosmological observables}.
\]
The first arrow is not supplied by naming an E8 or E9 regime.

\section{A Typed Encoding-Availability Hypothesis}

\subsection{Spacetime and availability data}

Let $(Y_4,g)$ be a time-oriented Lorentzian spacetime.  Let
\[
\mathcal E=\{E_1,\ldots,E_9\}
\]
denote a fixed catalogue of reduced encoding types.  Nothing in this notation
identifies an $E_i$ with a physical field or source.

\begin{definition}[Availability profile]
An \emph{availability profile} is a map
\[
\alpha:Y_4\longrightarrow \mathcal A,
\]
where $\mathcal A$ records which encoding contracts are satisfied in each
region.  In the simplest bookkeeping model,
$\mathcal A\subseteq\{0,1\}^9$; a continuous profile may instead take values
in $[0,1]^9$ once a quantitative availability functional has been defined.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Available encoding]
An encoding $E_i$ is available on an open set $U\subseteq Y_4$ if its declared
domain, codomain, overlap, locality, and stability conditions are all satisfied
on $U$.  It is unavailable if at least one declared condition fails.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Availability is relative to an explicit encoding contract.  It is not an
observer-independent physical scalar until a law defining $\alpha$ from
pre-projection data has been supplied.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The E8 and E9 labels}

For the present hypothesis, E8 and E9 have only the following meanings.

\begin{definition}[Relational profile, E8]
An E8 profile retains a declared set of relations or transition data while
some specified local descriptions are unavailable.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Statistical profile, E9]
An E9 profile retains a probability measure, state, or ensemble functional on
a declared measurable state space while some specified local descriptions are
unavailable.
\end{definition}

The measure or state in the E9 definition is mandatory.  Calling a description
``statistical'' without specifying its measurable space and probability law
does not define an encoding.

\begin{proposition}[Availability is not ontology]
Neither $\alpha_i=0$ nor $\alpha_i=1$ determines the existence or absence of a
particle, field, fluid, stress tensor, or metric degree of freedom.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The codomain of $\alpha$ records satisfaction of descriptive contracts.
Particle content, fields, fluids, and metric dynamics belong to different
typed spaces.  No map from $\mathcal A$ to any of those spaces has been given,
so no such conclusion follows.
\end{proof}

\section{The Availability-Only Non-Selection Theorem}

The main mathematical result of this revision is that the old physical
identifications were underdetermined.

\begin{theorem}[Availability-only non-selection]
\label{thm:nonselection}
Let $(Y_4,g)$ and an availability profile $\alpha$ be fixed.  If the premises
place no condition on an action, field equation, or source map, then they do
not select a unique gravitational source or spacetime dynamics.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Keep the same $(Y_4,\alpha)$.  One admissible completion may assign no
additional action,
\[
S_{\rm enc}^{(0)}[g,\alpha]=0,
\qquad
T_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}=0.
\]
A second completion may assign
\[
S_{\rm enc}^{(\Lambda)}[g,\alpha]
=-\int_{Y_4}\sqrt{-g}\,\Lambda_{\rm eff}\,d^4x,
\qquad
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\Lambda)}
=-\Lambda_{\rm eff}g_{\mu\nu},
\]
where
\[
T_{\mu\nu}
:=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta S_{\rm enc}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]
Both completions have the same availability profile, yet their sources and
metric equations differ whenever $\Lambda_{\rm eff}\ne0$.  Therefore
availability data alone do not select the source or dynamics.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No source from absence]
The unavailability of local encodings does not by itself source curvature.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[No dark-sector identification from labels]
An E9 label alone does not derive dark matter, and an E8 label alone does not
derive dark energy.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Why E9 does not imply cold dark matter}

A statistical state can describe many inequivalent effective media.  At the
level of a homogeneous stress tensor,
\[
T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_\mu u_\nu+p g_{\mu\nu}+\pi_{\mu\nu},
\]
the E9 label fixes none of $\rho$, $p$, $u^\mu$, or $\pi_{\mu\nu}$.

\begin{proposition}[Equation-of-state underdetermination]
The existence of an E9 statistical encoding does not imply
\[
p=0,\qquad c_s^2=0,\qquad \pi_{\mu\nu}=0,
\]
and hence does not imply cold, pressureless, collisionless behavior.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Probability measures exist for dust, radiation, warm fluids, interacting
fluids, and systems with anisotropic stress.  These descriptions share the
property ``statistical'' but have different equations of state, sound speeds,
collision terms, and perturbation evolution.  The label therefore cannot
select one of them.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why E8 does not imply acceleration}

For a spatially homogeneous and isotropic solution of Einstein gravity,
\[
\frac{\ddot a}{a}
=-\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho+3p)+\frac{\Lambda}{3}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Acceleration is dynamical]
An E8 relational encoding does not determine the sign of $\ddot a$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sign depends on the source combination $\rho+3p$, a cosmological term, or
the corresponding terms in a modified metric equation.  The definition of E8
specifies none of them.  The same E8 profile is therefore compatible with
accelerating, coasting, or decelerating solutions.
\end{proof}

\section{The Covariant Completion Contract}

A physical promotion requires one of two typed routes.

\subsection{Route A: an effective source action}

Specify fields or source variables $\chi$.  If the availability profile is
dynamical, include it among those variables and specify its equation of
motion.  A diffeomorphism-invariant functional then has the form
\[
S_{\rm enc}[g,\chi,\alpha].
\]
and equations obtained by varying all dynamical arguments.  Define
\[
T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm enc}
:=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}
\frac{\delta S_{\rm enc}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\]
The metric equation may then take the form
\[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=8\pi G\left(T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm visible}
+T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm enc}\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[On-shell covariant conservation]
\label{thm:conservation}
If $S_{\rm enc}$ is diffeomorphism invariant and the Euler--Lagrange equations
hold for every spacetime-dependent nonmetric argument, including $\alpha$ when
it is dynamical, then
\[
\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm enc}=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism generated by $\xi^\mu$, the metric
variation is
$\delta g^{\mu\nu}=-2\nabla^{(\mu}\xi^{\nu)}$.
The Euler--Lagrange terms of all nonmetric variables vanish on shell.
Diffeomorphism invariance and integration by parts then give
\[
0=\delta S_{\rm enc}
=-\int_{Y_4}\sqrt{-g}\,
(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm enc})\xi^\nu\,d^4x.
\]
Since $\xi^\nu$ is arbitrary, the result follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[External availability profiles]
If $\alpha$ is prescribed rather than varied, its Euler--Lagrange response
does not vanish in the Noether identity.  Separate conservation of
$T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm enc}$ then need not hold.  A completed model must either make
$\alpha$ dynamical, prove that its response term vanishes on the claimed
background, or state the resulting covariant exchange law.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Route B: a modified metric equation}

Alternatively, specify a covariant tensor
\[
\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}[g,\alpha,\chi]
=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm visible}.
\]
This route must state its initial-value problem, propagating degrees of freedom,
stability conditions, and divergence identity.  In particular, consistency
requires
\[
\nabla^\mu \mathcal E_{\mu\nu}=0
\]
on the declared solution space, or an explicitly balanced exchange equation.

\begin{remark}
Calling the proposal ``unchanged gravity'' commits it to Route A with a
nonzero effective source.  Calling it ``not a new source'' generally commits
it to Route B.  Version 1 implicitly used both descriptions.  A completed
model must choose and execute one.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Selection and parameter provenance}

Whichever route is used, an MTT derivation must provide:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item the domain and codomain of the source map;
\item the pre-projection data on which it depends;
\item the selector that fixes its functional form;
\item all dimensional scales and dimensionless parameters;
\item the conservation or exchange identity;
\item a well-posed and stable evolution problem;
\item an observable map and uncertainty model;
\item a separation of fitted, calibrated, and held-out quantities.
\end{enumerate}

A freely chosen action that reproduces cosmology would be a legitimate
effective model, but not yet an MTT selection theorem.

\section{Dark-Matter Promotion Gates}

The corrected hypothesis is:

\begin{conjecture}[E9 dark-matter hypothesis]
\label{conj:dm}
There exists a selected MTT source construction whose relevant reduced
description factors through an E9 statistical state and whose effective
dynamics pass the dark-matter gates below.
\end{conjecture}

This conjecture does not say that dark matter \emph{is} missing description.
It proposes a possible factorization of a future source law.

\subsection{Background and perturbation requirements}

At minimum, a candidate must derive a background density and pressure and the
linear perturbation variables
\[
\delta\rho,\quad \delta p,\quad \theta,\quad \pi_{\mu\nu}.
\]
To reproduce a cold-dark-matter limit over a stated range, it must show, with
error bounds,
\[
|w|=\left|\frac{p}{\rho}\right|\ll1,\qquad
c_s^2=\frac{\delta p}{\delta\rho}\ll1,
\]
and control anisotropic stress and any collision or drag term.  These
properties cannot be inserted by redefining ``statistical.''

\subsection{Independent observational gates}

The candidate must be executed against:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{DM\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{CMB:} acoustic-peak heights, lensing, and the transfer functions
through recombination;
\item \textbf{BAO and growth:} background distances and growth observables
such as $f\sigma_8$;
\item \textbf{weak and strong lensing:} the relation between dynamical and
lensing potentials;
\item \textbf{cluster collisions:} spatial offsets among lensing mass,
galaxies, and baryonic gas, including Bullet-Cluster-type systems
\cite{Clowe2006BulletCluster};
\item \textbf{nonlinear structure:} halo abundance, profiles, substructure,
and the matter power spectrum;
\item \textbf{local tests:} any transition scale at which the proposed
availability law changes behavior.
\end{enumerate}

Planck's CMB analysis constrains a successful baseline model far beyond a
generic statement of gravitational clustering
\cite{Planck2018CosmologicalParameters}.  DES weak-lensing and galaxy-clustering
analyses add independent structure-growth information
\cite{DESY3Cosmology}.  Passing only rotation-curve or direct-detection
considerations is therefore insufficient.

\section{Dark-Energy Promotion Gates}

The corrected second hypothesis is:

\begin{conjecture}[E8 dark-energy hypothesis]
\label{conj:de}
There exists a selected MTT source or metric construction whose relevant
reduced description factors through E8 relational data and whose effective
dynamics pass the dark-energy gates below.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Background requirement}

Under Route A in Einstein gravity, accelerated expansion requires an effective
source satisfying
\[
\rho_{\rm eff}+3p_{\rm eff}<0
\]
over the relevant epoch.  Under Route B, the modified metric terms must imply
the corresponding acceleration while preserving consistency and stability.
The function $H(z)$ or an equivalent background solution must be derived.

\subsection{Perturbations and stability}

The model must also specify dark-sector perturbations or prove their absence.
It must control:
\[
\delta\rho_{\rm eff},\quad
\delta p_{\rm eff},\quad
c_s^2,\quad
\pi_{\mu\nu},
\]
as applicable, and exclude ghost, gradient, and unacceptable strong-coupling
instabilities on its claimed domain.

\subsection{Independent observational gates}

A physical model must confront:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{DE\arabic*.}]
\item supernova luminosity distances;
\item BAO distance ratios and expansion history;
\item CMB distance, lensing, and early-time consistency;
\item structure growth and weak lensing;
\item the equation-of-state history $w(z)$ or the model's direct alternative;
\item correlations among all fitted probes and a held-out test.
\end{enumerate}

The observational target is not frozen to one verbal description.  Planck
found broad success for base $\Lambda$CDM in its final CMB analysis
\cite{Planck2018CosmologicalParameters}, while DESI DR2 reports increasingly
precise BAO constraints and examines departures from a cosmological constant
in combination with other probes \cite{DESIDR2Cosmology}.  A future MTT
completion must be tested against the data likelihoods, not declared
compatible from the E8 label.

\section{What the Hypothesis Does and Does Not Explain}

\subsection{Logical economy}

The availability viewpoint establishes a logical possibility: gravitationally
inferred phenomena need not, before a source theory is known, be identified
with one particular microscopic ontology.  This is a useful warning against
premature identification.  It does not show that no new particles, fields, or
modified dynamics are required by nature.

\begin{proposition}[Non-ontology is not an explanation]
Showing that a new microscopic object is not logically forced is weaker than
constructing a model that reproduces the observations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The former excludes a logical implication.  The latter requires a selected
dynamics and agreement with data.  Theorem~\ref{thm:nonselection} shows that
the availability premise leaves those dynamics undetermined.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Direct detection}

The absence of a confirmed non-gravitational dark-matter signal does not by
itself favor the E9 hypothesis.  Many particle models predict suppressed or
experiment-dependent signals, and the E9 proposal has no detection-rate
calculation.  Conversely, a robust non-gravitational signal would rule out
only those completed E9 models that explicitly predict its absence.

\subsection{Modified gravity}

Before Route A or Route B is specified, the proposal cannot be distinguished
from an effective source theory or a modified-gravity theory.  That distinction
is made by equations, not by interpretation.

\subsection{Homogeneity and clustering}

Relational data need not be homogeneous, and statistical data need not
cluster.  Homogeneity, isotropy, and clustering are properties of a state and
its dynamics.  They are not consequences of the words ``relational'' and
``statistical.''

\section{A Non-Circular Research Program}

The most efficient continuation is ordered by logical dependence.

\subsection{Step 1: select the source law}

Construct a pre-projection functional whose reduction produces either
$S_{\rm enc}$ or $\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}$.  Prove covariance, specify every
parameter, and show why the selected form is preferred over the
counterexamples in Theorem~\ref{thm:nonselection}.

\subsection{Step 2: derive conservation and well-posedness}

Use Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation} or the corresponding modified-gravity
identity.  Establish the initial-value problem and stability domain before
fitting cosmology.

\subsection{Step 3: solve the background}

Derive $H(z)$, the density evolution, and any transition law for availability.
Do not infer these functions from the data and then call the construction a
prediction.

\subsection{Step 4: execute perturbations}

Derive scalar, vector, and tensor perturbations as applicable.  Compute the
CMB, lensing, BAO, and growth observables using a reproducible solver.

\subsection{Step 5: execute nonlinear and held-out tests}

Confront cluster collisions and nonlinear structure, publish the likelihood
and covariance treatment, and reserve at least one informative data product
as a held-out test.

\section{Falsifiability}

The uncompleted availability hypothesis is not yet a quantitative physical
model and therefore has no unique observational prediction.  A completed
model becomes falsifiable through its selected equations.

\begin{definition}[Model-level falsifier]
A model-level falsifier is a predeclared observable or consistency condition
whose failure lies outside the model's propagated uncertainty and declared
validity domain.
\end{definition}

Examples include:
\begin{itemize}
\item failure of covariant conservation or the declared exchange law;
\item a ghost or gradient instability in the claimed cosmological epoch;
\item incompatible CMB peak or lensing spectra;
\item incorrect BAO or supernova distances;
\item incorrect growth or weak-lensing predictions;
\item failure in merging clusters or nonlinear structure;
\item a held-out likelihood decisively worse than the stated comparator.
\end{itemize}

The absence of a direct-detection signal is not, by itself, a positive
prediction of the present paper.

execution.

remain open.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The projection-first idea survives in a precise form.  MTT may classify
regimes in which different reduced encodings are available, and E8/E9 labels
may help organize future source constructions.  What does not survive is the
claim that missing encodings already explain the observed dark sector.

Availability does not determine stress-energy.  Statistical description does
not determine cold dark matter.  Relational description does not determine
accelerated expansion.  The next decisive object is a selected covariant
source action or modified field equation, followed by background,
perturbative, nonlinear, and held-out observational execution.  Until that
chain is supplied, Program D1 is a disciplined hypothesis and research
contract rather than a dark-sector theory.
