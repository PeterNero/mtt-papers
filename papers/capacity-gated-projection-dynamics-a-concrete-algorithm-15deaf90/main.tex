\title{\vspace{-1em}
Capacity-Gated Projection Dynamics:\\[0.25em]
\large A Typed Hybrid Stochastic Model and Executable Specification}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{Version 3, July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper turns the qualitative idea of capacity-gated projection into a
complete model specification.  The model contains finitely many particles,
declared relational summary fields, normalized admissibility rows, a
bottleneck reserve, within-mode drift and diffusion, flow and jump sets,
and a deterministic reset map or Markov reset kernel.  These data define a
hybrid stochastic particle system.  They are not consequences of a capacity
scalar alone.

The distinction repairs several ambiguities in the previous version.
The logarithmic expression used there is a barrier potential, and its
negative gradient is a genuine feedback force or penalty within the reduced
model.  A capacity-dependent speed cap and noise schedule are constitutive
choices.  Reaching a guard does not select a successor state until a reset
law is supplied.  Shared relational fields generally produce classical
collective dependence, not quantum entanglement.  The use of a Gaussian
kernel also creates all-to-all mean-field coupling rather than strictly
local interaction.

We give a typed model record, a smooth rowwise barrier, a conditional
well-posedness theorem up to guard accumulation, an explicit no-selection
result for an unspecified reset, a generator criterion for classical
nonfactorization, and an event-driven Euler--Maruyama specification that
does not silently step through the guard.  A two-particle circle model
shows every object in closed form, including a translation-covariant reset
kernel.  A parameter and provenance ledger separates quantities inherited
from an MTT admissibility problem from modeling choices and numerical
controls.  Boundary layers, hesitation, channels, sprouting, and persistent
basins are therefore simulation observables to test under stated
parameters, not inevitable consequences of projection.  The result is a
reproducible conditional model and a clear contract for promoting it to a
physical application.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, answer, and scope}
\label{sec:intro}

The question of this paper is deliberately concrete:

\begin{quote}
What must be specified before ``capacity-gated projection dynamics'' is an
actual dynamical system rather than a suggestive phrase?
\end{quote}

The answer is more structured than a single update equation.  A static
capacity record tells us where a chosen effective description is certified.
It does not say how a state moves, whether a boundary is invariant, what
happens at first exit, or how randomness enters.  The corrected MTT
capacity chain makes this separation explicit:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the MTT Foundation owns the admissibility ledger and the
      stop/continue/reset alternatives \cite{MTTFoundation2026};
\item the normalized-margin paper owns signed slacks, scales, reserve rows,
      bottleneck capacity, and metric clearance \cite{CapacityMargin2026};
\item the control-budget paper owns perturbation cost and cumulative
      consumption certificates \cite{CapacityBudget2026}; and
\item the transport paper explains which additional data promote a static
      reserve to a continuous-time model \cite{CapacityDynamics2026}.
\end{enumerate}

This paper supplies one such promotion for an interacting particle model.
It does not claim that the promotion is unique or selected by present MTT
geometry.  Its contribution is a typed construction whose assumptions can
be inspected, implemented, varied, and falsified.

The resulting mathematical neighbors are familiar: hybrid systems use
flow sets, jump sets, flow maps, and jump maps
\cite{GoebelSanfeliceTeel2009,GoebelSanfeliceTeel2012}; stochastic hybrid
systems add diffusion and reset kernels \cite{HuLygerosSastry2000}; viability
theory studies evolution constrained to a set \cite{Aubin2009}; barrier
functions enforce forward-invariance conditions \cite{AmesEtAl2017}; and
mean-field particle systems couple particles through aggregate empirical
fields \cite{ChaintronDiez2022}.  The MTT-specific feature is not a new
existence theory for these objects.  It is the provenance-aware use of a
multirow admissibility record as the gate and the refusal to confuse guard
hitting with a sourced physical outcome.

\section{Particle state, relational summaries, and reserve rows}
\label{sec:static}

\subsection{Microscopic state}

Let $\Omega$ be a smooth compact $d$-dimensional manifold.  A flat torus is
a convenient boundary-free simulation domain; a bounded Euclidean domain
may instead be used together with a declared reflecting, absorbing, or
periodic boundary rule.  For $N$ labeled particles, write
\[
 X=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\in\Omega^N,
 \qquad
 V=(v_1,\ldots,v_N)\in(T\Omega)^N.
\]
Let $q$ lie in a finite mode set $\cQ$.  The reduced state is
\[
 z=(X,V,q)\in\cE.
\]
Extra memory, records, or internal states must be appended to $\cE$ if an
application uses them.  They are not implicit.

\subsection{Relational field map}

Choose $K$ summary channels.  Channel $k$ has a kernel
$K_k:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb R$ and fixed weights $w_{kj}$.  Define
\[
 f_k(x;X)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{kj}K_k(x,x_j).
\]
The map
\[
 \cP:X\longmapsto(f_1(\,\cdot\,;X),\ldots,f_K(\,\cdot\,;X))
\]
is the relational summary map.  It is an explicit coarse observable, not a
new physical substance.

Compactly supported $K_k$ give finite-range coupling.  Gaussian kernels
are nonzero at every finite separation and therefore give all-to-all
coupling, even though their influence decays with distance.  Calling the
latter update ``strictly local'' would be incorrect.

\begin{proposition}[A sufficient source of noninjectivity]
\label{prop:noninjective}
Suppose particles $a\ne b$ have equal weights in every channel:
$w_{ka}=w_{kb}$ for all $k$.  If labels belong to the microscopic state,
then exchanging $x_a$ and $x_b$ leaves $\cP(X)$ unchanged.  Hence $\cP$ is
noninjective on labeled configurations whenever the exchange changes $X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every $f_k$ is a sum.  The two exchanged summands have equal coefficients,
so their sum and therefore every field $f_k$ is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proposition proves only a model-relative loss of label information.
If particles are physically indistinguishable and the microscopic state is
already quotiented by permutations, this argument does not prove further
noninjectivity.  Nor does noninjectivity alone imply irreversibility; a
section may exist on the selected range.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Signed normalized reserve rows}

For each particle $i$, mode $q$, and constraint row $a\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$,
let
\[
 \sigma_{ia}^{q}(X,V)
\]
be a signed slack and let $s_{ia}^{q}>0$ have the same units.  Define
\[
 r_{ia}^{q}(z)=\frac{\sigma_{ia}^{q}(X,V)}{s_{ia}^{q}},
 \qquad
 c_q(z)=\min_{i,a}r_{ia}^{q}(z).
\]
The vector $r^q=(r_{ia}^q)$ is primary.  The scalar $c_q$ is its
bottleneck compression.  No row is clipped at zero: negative values retain
which condition failed and by how much.

The exact admissible set in mode $q$ is
\[
 \cA_q=\{z:r_{ia}^{q}(z)>0\text{ for every }i,a\}.
\]
The active set is
\[
 I_q(z)=\{(i,a):r_{ia}^{q}(z)=c_q(z)\}.
\]
The minimum is generally nonsmooth when $I_q(z)$ contains more than one
row.  This is why the smooth barrier below is built row by row rather than
by differentiating the bottleneck without qualification.

\section{The typed hybrid record}
\label{sec:record}

\begin{definition}[Capacity-gated hybrid record]
\label{def:record}
A capacity-gated hybrid record is the tuple
\[
\mathfrak H=
(\cE,\cQ,\cP,r,\eta,\cC,\cD,b,\Sigma,U,\cR,\cB,\mathfrak s,\mathfrak p),
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $\cE$ is the state space and $\cQ$ the finite mode set;
\item $\cP$ is the relational summary map;
\item $r=(r_{ia}^{q})$ is one fixed normalized reserve record;
\item $\eta=(\eta_q)$ is a nonnegative numerical guard margin;
\item $\cC_q$ is the flow set and $\cD_q$ the jump or stop set;
\item $b_q$ and $\Sigma_q$ are within-mode drift and diffusion;
\item $U_q$ is a declared barrier or feedback potential;
\item $\cR_q(z,\dd z')$ is a deterministic or Markov reset law;
\item $\cB$ is the spatial boundary rule;
\item $\mathfrak s$ is the strong, weak, martingale, or numerical solution
      concept; and
\item $\mathfrak p$ records the source, units, version, and hash of every
      nontrivial ingredient.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The exact failure boundary is $c_q=0$.  Numerical simulation normally
jumps earlier.  For a chosen $\eta_q>0$, define
\[
 \cC_q=\{z:c_q(z)\ge\eta_q\},
 \qquad
 \cD_q=\{z:c_q(z)\le\eta_q\}.
\]
Their overlap at equality permits event handling.  One may instead use
disjoint conventions, hysteresis, or a set-valued rule, but the convention
must be stated.

\begin{remark}[Guard margin versus physics]
The value $\eta_q$ is a numerical or control margin unless a source theorem
assigns it physical meaning.  A result that changes when $\eta_q$ changes
is a property of the implemented guard, not of the exact boundary alone.
\end{remark}

\section{Within-mode dynamics and the barrier force}
\label{sec:flow}

\subsection{A smooth rowwise barrier}

Choose nonnegative barrier weights $\alpha_{ia}^{q}$.  On the exact
admissible set define
\[
 U_q(z)
 =
 -\sum_{i,a}\alpha_{ia}^{q}\log r_{ia}^{q}(z).
\]
On any closed numerical flow set $c_q\ge\eta_q>0$, this function is smooth
whenever the rows are smooth.  Its gradient is
\[
 \grad U_q(z)
 =
 -\sum_{i,a}
 \frac{\alpha_{ia}^{q}}{r_{ia}^{q}(z)}
 \grad r_{ia}^{q}(z).
\]
Unlike $-\log(\varepsilon+c_q)$, this expression does not hide active-row
ties inside an unjustified ordinary gradient.

\begin{proposition}[The barrier is dynamical feedback]
\label{prop:barrier-force}
If the reduced equation contains $-\grad U_q$, then $U_q$ is a potential
and $-\grad U_q$ is a force or feedback term in that reduced equation.
This remains true if $U_q$ was motivated by representational
admissibility rather than by microscopic energy.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A force term is identified by its role in the evolution equation, not by
its interpretation.  The displayed vector is the negative gradient of a
scalar potential and changes the acceleration or drift.  It is therefore
a gradient feedback force in the reduced model.  No claim that it equals a
fundamental physical force follows.
\end{proof}

The logarithmic barrier is familiar from constrained optimization and
control.  Control barrier functions can certify forward invariance when
their derivative inequalities are verified \cite{AmesEtAl2017}.  Merely
placing a logarithm in an update does not prove invariance.  That requires
a tangency, viability, or barrier inequality for the complete drift and
diffusion.

\subsection{Stochastic flow}

Within mode $q$, a transparent second-order model is
\begin{align}
 \dd X_t &= V_t\,\dd t, \label{eq:flow-x}\\
 \dd V_t
 &=
 \bigl[
 B_q(X_t,V_t)
 -\Gamma_q(X_t,V_t)V_t
 -G_q(X_t,V_t)\grad_X U_q(X_t,V_t)
 \bigr]\dd t
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad
 +\Sigma_q(X_t,V_t)\,\dd W_t,
 \label{eq:flow-v}
\end{align}
until the first hitting time of $\cD_q$.  Here $B_q$ is a declared base
drift, $\Gamma_q$ is damping, $G_q$ is a mobility or gain, and $W_t$ is a
Brownian driver of declared dimension and covariance convention.

Every term is optional but none is implicit.  Setting $\Sigma_q=0$ gives a
deterministic hybrid system.  A first-order model can omit $V$.  A
discrete-time model can be specified directly, but must still define what
happens when a step crosses the guard.

\subsection{Speed and noise gates}

A speed gate can be imposed through a projection
\[
 \Pi_{V_q(c)}(v)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 v,&\|v\|\le V_q(c),\\[0.3em]
 V_q(c)\,v/\|v\|,&\|v\|>V_q(c),
 \end{cases}
\]
or through a drift that enforces an invariant velocity set.  A diffusion
schedule may use $\Sigma_q(z)=\chi_q(c_q(z))\Sigma_q^0(z)$.

These choices can produce slow motion or reduced noise near a guard, but
that behavior has been inserted through $V_q$ or $\chi_q$.  It is not a
theorem about finite capacity.  Conversely, a model with constant
diffusion and no speed gate need not ``hesitate'' at all.

\section{Guards, resets, and continuation}
\label{sec:jump}

Let
\[
 \tau_1=\inf\{t>0:Z_t\in\cD_{q_0}\}
\]
be the first guard-hitting time.  At $\tau_1$ there are four distinct
contracts.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Stop.}  The effective trajectory terminates at $\tau_1$.
\item \textbf{Continue in another chart.}  A declared transition map
      changes coordinates while representing the same upper state.
\item \textbf{Deterministic reset.}  A measurable map
      $R_q:\cD_q\to\cE$ sets $Z_{\tau_1}=R_q(Z_{\tau_1^-})$.
\item \textbf{Stochastic reset.}  A Markov kernel
      $\cR_q(z,\dd z')$ samples the post-jump state.
\end{enumerate}

Boundary-hitting diffusions with deterministic resets and their
Fokker--Planck equations are established mathematical objects
\cite{BectBailiFleury2005}.  The reset is part of the model data, not a
consequence of the boundary.

\begin{definition}[Reset support contract]
\label{def:reset-support}
Choose a restart margin $\eta_q^{\rm reset}>\eta_q$.  A reset law is
admissibility restoring if
\[
 \cR_q\bigl(z,\{z':c_{q'}(z')\ge
 \eta_{q'}^{\rm reset}\}\bigr)=1
\]
for every guard state $z$ at which a reset is permitted, with $q'$ the
post-jump mode contained in $z'$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[A guard does not select an outcome]
\label{prop:no-selection}
Suppose a guard state $z$ admits two distinct admissibility-restoring
restart states.  Then the state space, flow law, reserve rows, and guard do
not determine a unique post-guard trajectory from $z$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $z_1\ne z_2$ be the two restart states.  The deterministic kernels
$\delta_{z_1}$ and $\delta_{z_2}$ have the same state space, flow law,
rows, and guard but produce different continuations.  A nontrivial convex
mixture of these kernels produces a third continuation law.  Therefore the
pre-guard data do not select one of them.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No automatic irreversibility]
\label{cor:no-irreversibility}
Guard hitting alone proves neither reversibility nor irreversibility.
Those properties depend on the continuation rule, retained records,
coarse-graining, and the state space on which reversal is tested.
\end{corollary}

A bijective chart transition may preserve information.  A many-to-one
reset may discard it.  A stochastic reset may introduce an outcome law.
Those are different mechanisms and should not be merged under the word
``selection.''

\section{Conditional well-posedness}
\label{sec:wellposed}

The next result states exactly what the model obtains from standard
stochastic and hybrid-system theory.

\begin{theorem}[Guarded hybrid existence and uniqueness]
\label{thm:wellposed}
Assume for every mode $q$ that:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $\Omega$ is compact, or the coefficients satisfy standard
      nonexplosion growth bounds;
\item every reserve row is $C^2$ on an open neighborhood of
      $\cC_q$ and $\eta_q>0$;
\item the drift and diffusion in \cref{eq:flow-x,eq:flow-v} are locally
      Lipschitz on that neighborhood;
\item the spatial boundary rule is well posed;
\item $\cR_q$ is a measurable probability kernel satisfying
      \cref{def:reset-support}; and
\item almost surely, jump times have no finite accumulation point.
\end{enumerate}
Then an initial state in a flow or restart set determines a unique strong
within-mode solution up to its next guard time.  Concatenating that solution
with the reset kernels defines a strong Markov hybrid process for all finite
times.  If the reset is deterministic, the same statement holds with a
deterministic jump map.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $c_q\ge\eta_q>0$ on the closed numerical flow set, every denominator
in $\grad U_q$ is bounded away from zero.  The assumed row regularity makes
the barrier gradient locally Lipschitz there.  Standard SDE existence and
pathwise uniqueness therefore apply until the first exit from the flow
domain.  At the exit, the measurable reset kernel supplies the conditional
law of the next initial state.  Iterating this construction gives the usual
piecing-out construction of a stochastic hybrid process
\cite{HuLygerosSastry2000}.  The no-accumulation hypothesis ensures that
only finitely many pieces occur on each finite time interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not prove]
The theorem does not prove that a guard is reached, that a reset state is
physically selected, that the process converges, that the safe set is
invariant, or that the model is a faithful description of a target system.
Those are separate reachability, source, stability, and validation
questions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Zeno behavior]
A restart margin alone does not always rule out infinitely many jumps in
finite time.  One may establish a positive dwell time from bounded drift
and a positive reserve gap, introduce hysteresis or a timer, or treat Zeno
completion explicitly.  The chosen route belongs in the model record.
\end{remark}

\section{Classical collective dependence}
\label{sec:coupling}

The shared fields make particle updates dependent.  This is important, but
it should be named correctly.

Let $\cL$ be the within-mode generator.  If particles were dynamically
independent, it would decompose as
\[
 \cL=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\cL_i,
\]
where $\cL_i$ acts only on the coordinates of particle $i$.  Shared-field
feedback generally violates this decomposition.

\begin{proposition}[Cross-dependence criterion]
\label{prop:cross-dependence}
Suppose the drift of particle $i$ contains
$\grad_{x_i}f_k(x_i;X)$ and for some $j\ne i$
\[
 w_{kj}\,\grad_{x_i}K_k(x_i,x_j)\ne0
\]
on an open set.  Then the drift of particle $i$ depends on $x_j$ on that
set.  The generator is not a sum of single-particle generators there, and
the finite-time transition law is not generically a product of independent
single-particle transition laws.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the field gives
\[
 \grad_{x_i}f_k(x_i;X)
 =
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{N}
 w_{k\ell}\grad_1K_k(x_i,x_\ell).
\]
The nonzero $j$ term changes when $x_j$ changes while $x_i$ is held fixed.
Thus the $i$ drift cannot be a function of the $i$ state alone.  The
generator consequently contains cross-coordinate dependence, which
generically prevents product transition kernels.
\end{proof}

This is classical collective correlation or mean-field dependence.  A
claim of quantum entanglement would additionally require a quantum state
space or observable algebra, a subsystem composition rule, a state, and a
separability or entanglement criterion.  Those data are absent here.
Quantum separability is a precise property of states on composite quantum
systems \cite{Werner1989,HorodeckiEtAl2009}; classical nonfactorization of a
particle transition law is not a substitute for it.

\section{An executable event-driven specification}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The continuous model can be simulated by an event-aware discretization.
The following specification is intentionally explicit about failure.

\begin{verbatim}
INPUT:
  model record H, final time T, initial state z0,
  step h, event tolerance tol, random seed

REQUIRE:
  z0 is in a flow or restart set
  all source hashes and units in the provenance ledger match

t <- 0
z <- z0
while t < T:
    q <- mode(z)
    compute every signed row r_ia^q(z)
    if min(r_ia^q(z)) <= eta_q:
        if policy is STOP:
            return trajectory with certified first-exit record
        z <- sample R_q(z, .) or evaluate deterministic reset R_q(z)
        verify reset support and record random draw
        continue

    compute relational fields and their derivatives
    compute rowwise barrier gradient
    propose one Euler--Maruyama step z_trial

    if z_trial stays in the flow set:
        accept z_trial
        t <- t + h
    else:
        bracket the first guard crossing on the same noise increment
        refine the crossing time to tolerance tol
        advance to the guard and apply the declared stop/reset policy

OUTPUT:
  path, mode history, row history, active rows,
  guard events, reset draws, seed, parameter ledger,
  source hashes, tolerances
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Why naive clipping is not enough}

Replacing every negative row by zero before event detection loses the
failed-row identity and violation size.  Accepting an Euler step that jumps
from $c_q>\eta_q$ to $c_q<0$ also invents dynamics outside the declared
flow domain.  The event-aware scheme brackets that crossing and invokes the
declared continuation rule.

\subsection{Numerical versus exact statements}

Euler--Maruyama is an approximation, not the hybrid process itself.
Convergence requires the usual coefficient and event-detection hypotheses;
hybrid simulators need additional care near jumps
\cite{SanfeliceTeel2010,Higham2001}.  A reproducibility packet should report
step refinement, event tolerance refinement, random seeds, and the fraction
of trajectories affected by guard localization.

Parameters may be fixed for a toy model without being fitted.  That makes
the example reproducible, not parameter free.  For a physical claim, the
ledger must also distinguish:

\begin{itemize}
\item values selected by an upstream theorem;
\item conventional choices that change coordinates but not predictions;
\item numerical controls removed by convergence;
\item genuine constitutive parameters;
\item quantities fitted to the same data later reported; and
\item held-out observables used only for validation.
\end{itemize}

\section{A closed-form two-particle circle model}
\label{sec:example}

This example is not a physical fit.  Its purpose is to show that every item
in the hybrid record can be filled without ambiguity.

Let $\Omega=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z$ and $N=2$.  Use the periodic kernel
\[
 K(y)=1+\cos\left(\frac{2\pi y}{L}\right).
\]
Define one relational field
\[
 f(x;X)=K(x-x_1)+K(x-x_2)
\]
and one reserve row per particle,
\[
 r_i(X)=4-f(x_i;X).
\]
At coincidence $x_1=x_2$, each row is zero because $f(x_i;X)=4$.  At
antipodal separation $x_2=x_1+L/2$, each row equals $2$ because the
self-term is $2$ and the cross-term is zero.

Choose a guard $\eta\in(0,2)$ and barrier
\[
 U(X)=-\alpha\bigl(\log r_1(X)+\log r_2(X)\bigr)
\]
on $r_1,r_2\ge\eta$.  Let the within-mode dynamics be
\[
 \dd x_i=v_i\,\dd t,
 \qquad
 \dd v_i=
 \bigl[-\gamma v_i-\partial_{x_i}U(X)\bigr]\dd t
 +\sigma\,\dd W_i.
\]
All four parameters $L,\alpha,\gamma,\sigma$ and the guard $\eta$ are
declared model inputs.

At the guard, choose a random global phase $\xi$ uniformly on the circle and
reset
\[
 (x_1,x_2,v_1,v_2)
 \longmapsto
 (\xi,\xi+L/2,0,0).
\]
This Markov reset is translation covariant and returns both rows to $2$.
It therefore satisfies the reset support contract for every
$\eta^{\rm reset}<2$.

The same flow and guard could instead stop, reverse the relative velocity,
or reset to another safe configuration.  \Cref{prop:no-selection} explains
why none of these outcomes follows from the guard itself.  The example also
shows classical dependence: the force on particle $1$ depends on
$x_2-x_1$.

Several simple countermodels show why the labels are not inevitable.

\section{Relation to established mathematical frameworks}
\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Hybrid systems}

The tuple of flow set, flow map, jump set, and jump map is standard in
hybrid dynamics \cite{GoebelSanfeliceTeel2009,GoebelSanfeliceTeel2012}.
The present model is not outside that class.  Its distinctive bookkeeping
is that the flow and jump sets are generated from a normalized
multi-condition capacity record.

\subsection{Viability and barrier methods}

Viability theory asks whether trajectories can remain in a constrained set
and studies viability kernels and exit tubes \cite{Aubin2009}.  Control
barrier functions provide derivative conditions for safety or forward
invariance \cite{AmesEtAl2017}.  A capacity row can participate in such a
certificate, but positivity at one instant does not establish invariance.
The derivative condition must be checked for the supplied dynamics.

\subsection{Mean-field and interacting particle systems}

Fields built by summing kernels over all particles are standard interacting
particle constructions.  Their large-$N$ limits can lead to
McKean--Vlasov equations and propagation-of-chaos questions
\cite{ChaintronDiez2022}.  The MTT vocabulary does not remove the need for
finite-$N$ estimates, mean-field scaling, or convergence proofs.

\subsection{What remains specifically useful here}

The construction still contributes a useful research discipline:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item begin with independently sourced, normalized admissibility rows;
\item preserve row identities rather than hiding them in one arbitrary
      scalar;
\item let the weakest row define a transparent guard;
\item declare the feedback and reset as separate model layers;
\item record whether the result stops, changes chart, or samples a new
      state; and
\item refuse downstream physical language until its required mathematical
      structure is present.
\end{enumerate}

That composition can be valuable even though its component mathematics has
close analogues.

\section{Claim status and theorem ownership}
\label{sec:status}

This paper owns the following local results and definitions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the typed capacity-gated hybrid record in \cref{def:record};
\item the rowwise barrier construction tied to the MTT reserve record;
\item the barrier-feedback classification in
      \cref{prop:barrier-force};
\item the reset support contract in \cref{def:reset-support};
\item the no-selection result in \cref{prop:no-selection};
\item the guarded hybrid well-posedness specialization in
      \cref{thm:wellposed};
\item the shared-field cross-dependence criterion in
      \cref{prop:cross-dependence}; and
\item the complete executable and provenance contracts.
\end{enumerate}

Standard SDE existence, strong Markov piecing-out, hybrid-system solution
theory, viability theory, barrier certificates, mean-field limits, and

The following claims are \emph{not} established:

\begin{itemize}
\item a universal MTT capacity evolution law;
\item a geometry-selected relational kernel or reset kernel;
\item inevitable guard hitting or inevitable basin formation;
\item quantum entanglement, Bell correlations, or collapse;
\item thermodynamic entropy production or an arrow of time;
\item gravity, horizons, biological decision making, or consciousness;
\item structural unpredictability beyond the chosen hybrid model; or
\item superiority to established hybrid, control, or particle methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Completion and falsifiability contract}
\label{sec:contract}

A proposed application is complete at the model level only if it supplies:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the exact upper state or action from which the rows are computed;
\item the fixed normalization, units, and hashes of all reserve rows;
\item the derivation or constitutive status of every kernel and coefficient;
\item the flow and jump sets, including tie and overlap conventions;
\item the reset map or kernel and its support proof;
\item a well-posedness or numerical-convergence certificate;
\item preregistered diagnostics and matched baselines;
\item uncertainty, finite-size, and discretization studies;
\item held-out validation data; and
\item a tier statement distinguishing toy model, reconstruction, fit,
      controlled numerical result, and selected prediction.
\end{enumerate}

The model is falsified as a claimed application if, for example, its rows
cannot be sourced, its reset leaves the declared admissible set, its
reported behavior disappears under event or step refinement, its claimed
locality uses a noncompact kernel without qualification, or its held-out
predictions fail outside the construction data.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Capacity-gated projection dynamics can be made precise, but precision
changes what may be claimed.  The concrete construction is a hybrid
stochastic particle model.  Its relational fields provide classical
coupling.  Its logarithmic barrier provides gradient feedback.  Its guard
marks the boundary of one declared flow regime.  Its reset law, not the
guard, determines continuation.

That correction does not empty the idea.  It turns it into a usable object.
The model can now be simulated without silently crossing its domain,
compared with established hybrid and mean-field methods, and promoted to a
physical application only when an upstream source supplies its coefficients
and reset.  The central methodological lesson is simple: capacity can gate
dynamics, but it does not write the dynamics for us.
