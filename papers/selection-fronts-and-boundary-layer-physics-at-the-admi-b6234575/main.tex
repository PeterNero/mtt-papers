\title{\vspace{-1em}
Selection Fronts at an Admissibility Boundary:\\[0.25em]
\large Local Exit Geometry, Conditional Boundary Layers, and Reset Semantics}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{Version 2, July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An admissibility boundary is the place where at least one declared control
condition loses positive reserve.  That static fact does not by itself
produce a boundary layer, metastability, a large-deviation law, a
measurement outcome, or a new physical phase.  Those conclusions require a
specified dynamical model.

This paper gives a precise conditional meaning to the term
\emph{selection front}.  The input is a finite vector of normalized signed
reserve rows, including the rows needed for spectral separation,
projector control, well-posedness, descent, coherent stability, truncation,
and numerical certification.  The exact boundary is distinguished from a
conservative numerical event guard.  At one regular active row the boundary
is a smooth facet; at transverse row ties it is locally an orthant corner.
A dynamical front additionally requires a drift, diffusion, initial law,
stopping rule, interior return set, observables, and a post-exit stop,
chart-transition, reset map, or reset kernel.

The central calculation is an exactly solvable normal diffusion on an
interval.  Its guard-hitting committor and mean exit time are derived in
closed form.  Drift away from the guard gives an exponential boundary
layer of thickness proportional to noise divided by drift; zero drift
gives a linear committor and inverse-noise exit time; drift toward the guard
gives neither behavior.  Thus no universal sigmoid, metastable law, or
first-passage scaling follows from admissibility alone.  Eyring--Kramers
and Freidlin--Wentzell asymptotics remain available when a small-noise
family, attracting basin, quasipotential barrier, and regularity hypotheses
are independently supplied.

The result is a reproducible local theory of first exit and a strict
application contract.  It does not derive vacuum selection, black-hole
dynamics, cosmological measures, quantum collapse, or limits on scalable
coherence.
\end{abstract}

scale, potential, observation protocol, or post-exit reset for all physical
applications.  The phrase ``selection front'' names a conditional
first-exit construction, not an additional fundamental interaction.

\section{The question in its corrected form}
\label{sec:intro}

The original paper asked whether the edge of an admissible region has
distinctive dynamics.  The corrected answer is:

\begin{quote}
It can, after both the admissible region and the dynamics approaching it
have been specified.  The static boundary determines where a selected
description first fails.  The generator determines how trajectories
approach that boundary.  A reset rule determines what, if anything, follows
the exit.
\end{quote}

These are three different mathematical objects.  Confusing them was the
source of most of the earlier overclaims.

The paper therefore separates:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the \emph{static ledger}: which inequalities define admissibility;
\item the \emph{local geometry}: whether their common boundary is a facet,
      a corner, or a singular set;
\item the \emph{within-chart process}: drift, diffusion, forcing, and
      observation horizon before first exit;
\item the \emph{exit statistics}: committors, stopping times, active rows,
      and exit locations;
\item the \emph{continuation semantics}: stop, change chart, reset
      deterministically, or sample a reset kernel; and
\item the \emph{physical interpretation}: a separate map from these
      mathematical objects to an experiment or fundamental theory.
\end{enumerate}

The static ledger and its normalized bottleneck are inherited from the
capacity papers
\cite{CapacityMargin2026,CapacityBudget2026,CapacityDynamics2026}.
The explicit stop/chart/reset alternatives are inherited from the
capacity-gated hybrid model \cite{CapacityGated2026}.  This paper owns the
local selection-front record, the transverse-corner description, the exact
normal-diffusion calculation, and the resulting diagnostic and application
contracts.

\subsection{What is not claimed}

No theorem below says that every admissibility boundary is physically
reachable, that every first exit is irreversible, or that an inadmissible
point is absent from reality.  It says only that a declared description no
longer has all of its certified positive reserves there.  Another chart,
theory, or scale may remain available.

Likewise, the word \emph{selection} does not mean that the boundary chooses
one successor.  It refers to the study of a stopping or transition layer.
Outcome selection begins only after a continuation law has been supplied.

\section{The complete static input}
\label{sec:static}

\subsection{A vector of signed normalized reserves}

Let \(U\) be a state-and-control region and let
\(\cI\) be a finite set of required rows.  Each row has:
\[
 s_i:\cD_i\longrightarrow\RR,
 \qquad
 \sigma_i>0,
 \qquad
 r_i=\frac{s_i}{\sigma_i},
 \qquad i\in\cI.
\]
Here \(s_i>0\) means that row \(i\) passes, \(\sigma_i\) is a declared
reference scale, and the common domain is
\(\cD=\bigcap_{i\in\cI}\cD_i\).  Every row also carries provenance:
its norm, tolerance, source theorem or assumption, uncertainty, and
evaluation method.

The primary object is the vector
\[
 r(x)=\bigl(r_i(x)\bigr)_{i\in\cI}.
\]
The signed bottleneck and admissible set are
\begin{equation}
 D(x)=\min_{i\in\cI}r_i(x),
 \qquad
 \cA=\{x\in\cD:D(x)>0\}.
\label{eq:bottleneck}
\end{equation}
The clipped visualization \(C=\min\{1,\max\{0,D\}\}\) may be useful, but it
must not replace the signed rows in an exit calculation.

For a physical application, a single spectral gap is not a complete
margin.  At minimum, every condition actually used by the reduced
description must appear as its own row.  Typical families are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.22\textwidth}Y Y@{}}
\toprule
Row family & Question answered & Example signed reserve \\
\midrule
Gap
& Is the selected spectral sector separated?
& \((\gamma-\gamma_{\min})/\sigma_{\rm gap}\).\\

Projector or resolvent
& Is the selected projection bounded and controlled?
& \((K_{\max}-K)/\sigma_{\rm proj}\).\\

Well-posedness
& Are the operator domain, evolution, or solution hypotheses valid?
& A normalized lower bound for the relevant estimate.\\

Descent or leakage
& Does the selected sector remain closed to the declared tolerance?
& \((\ell_{\max}-\ell)/\sigma_{\rm desc}\).\\

Coherent stability
& Is the complementary or transverse mode stable?
& \((\omega_Q-\omega_{\min})/\sigma_{\rm coh}\).\\

Truncation
& Is the omitted remainder below the accepted error?
& \((E_{\max}-E)/\sigma_{\rm tr}\).\\

Numerical certificate
& Does the interval, residual, or majorant test pass strictly?
& A normalized certified inclusion radius.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The table is a schema, not a claim that every problem uses exactly these
seven rows.  Omitting a row that the downstream argument needs makes the
front incomplete.

\subsection{Exact boundary, front layer, and active rows}

\begin{definition}[Static selection-front layer]
\label{def:frontlayer}
For a fixed normalized record and \(\delta>0\), define
\[
 \cF_\delta
 =
 \{x\in\cD:0<D(x)\le\delta\}.
\]
The exact admissibility boundary is
\[
 \partial_{\cD}\cA
 =
 \{x\in\cD:D(x)=0\}
\]
when no domain boundary intervenes.  The active-row set is
\[
 I_{\rm act}(x)
 =
 \{i\in\cI:r_i(x)=D(x)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

The layer thickness \(\delta\) is a declared diagnostic resolution in the
chosen normalized record.  It is not a universal length, energy, or time.
A controlled change of row normalization changes its numerical value.

\subsection{Exact and numerical guards are not the same}

Suppose a computation returns \(\widehat r_i(x)\) with certified errors
\[
 |r_i(x)-\widehat r_i(x)|\le e_i(x).
\]
Then
\[
 D^-(x)=\min_i\bigl(\widehat r_i(x)-e_i(x)\bigr)>0
\]
certifies exact admissibility.  A numerical event guard may be chosen as
\[
 \cG_{\rm num}
 =
 \{x:D^-(x)\le\delta_{\rm evt}\},
\]
where \(\delta_{\rm evt}\ge0\) includes event-location tolerance and any
declared safety reserve.

Reaching \(\cG_{\rm num}\) need not mean that \(D=0\).  It may be an early,
conservative stop.  Conversely, stepping across the exact boundary between
two discrete time samples is possible unless event detection is included.
Every numerical selection-front claim must record which guard was used.

\section{Local geometry: facets, corners, and singular points}
\label{sec:geometry}

The corrected capacity-dynamics paper proves that one uniquely active
\(C^1\) row with nonzero gradient defines a local codimension-one
hypersurface \cite{CapacityDynamics2026}.  The next question is what happens
when several rows become active together.

\begin{proposition}[Transverse active rows give an orthant corner]
\label{prop:corner}
Let \(U\subset\RR^n\) be open and let \(x_0\in U\).  Suppose a set
\(J\subset\cI\) of \(k\le n\) rows satisfies
\[
 r_j(x_0)=0\quad(j\in J),
 \qquad
 r_i(x_0)>0\quad(i\notin J),
\]
and the covectors
\(\{\dd r_j(x_0):j\in J\}\) are linearly independent.  Then there is a
local \(C^1\) coordinate chart
\[
 x\longmapsto
 \bigl((r_j(x))_{j\in J},y(x)\bigr)
 \in\RR^k\times\RR^{n-k}
\]
in which the admissible set is locally
\[
 (0,\infty)^k\times\RR^{n-k}.
\]
The common active stratum \(\{r_j=0:j\in J\}\) has codimension \(k\), and
the local boundary is the union of its coordinate faces.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map \(r_J=(r_j)_{j\in J}\) has rank \(k\) at \(x_0\).  The constant
rank theorem completes its components to local coordinates
\((r_J,y)\).  Continuity keeps every inactive row positive after the
neighborhood is reduced.  The sign conditions for membership in \(\cA\)
are therefore exactly \(r_j>0\) for \(j\in J\), giving the stated orthant.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
At a row tie, the scalar minimum \(D=\min_i r_i\) is generally
nondifferentiable even though every row is smooth.  A force, normal
coordinate, or boundary velocity should therefore be computed rowwise or
within a declared stratum.  Differentiating the bottleneck as though it
were globally smooth is not justified.
\end{remark}

If the active gradients lose rank, the boundary can have cusps,
self-contact, higher-order tangency, or an open zero set.  ``A front is a
smooth surface'' is consequently a local theorem under regularity and
transversality hypotheses, not part of the definition.

\section{The additional dynamical data}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{A typed front record}

\begin{definition}[Dynamical selection-front record]
\label{def:dynamicrecord}
A dynamical selection-front record consists of:
\[
 \cS_{\rm front}
 =
 \bigl(
 \cR_{\rm adm},
 \{\cL_\eta\}_{\eta\in E},
 \mu_0,
 \cG,
 \cB,
 \cK,
 \cO,
 T_{\rm obs}
 \bigr),
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \(\cR_{\rm adm}\) is the complete normalized row record;
\item \(\eta\in E\) is a declared model or noise parameter, and
      \(\cL_\eta\) is the within-chart generator;
\item \(\mu_0\) is the initial law;
\item \(\cG\) is the exact or numerical guard;
\item \(\cB\Subset\cA\) is an optional interior return or comparison set;
\item \(\cK\) is absent for stopping, a chart-transition map, a
      deterministic reset, or a Markov reset kernel;
\item \(\cO\) is the declared observable family; and
\item \(T_{\rm obs}\) is the observation horizon.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

For an Ito diffusion in local coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \dd X_t
 =
 b_\eta(X_t)\,\dd t
 +
 \sqrt{2\eta}\,\Sigma_\eta(X_t)\,\dd W_t,
\label{eq:diffusion}
\end{equation}
the generator is
\[
 \cL_\eta f
 =
 b_\eta\cdot\nabla f
 +
 \eta\,\operatorname{tr}
 \bigl(a_\eta\nabla^2f\bigr),
 \qquad
 a_\eta=\Sigma_\eta\Sigma_\eta^\top.
\]
Regularity, nonexplosion, degeneracy, and boundary accessibility
hypotheses must be stated for the chosen model.  A jump process, delay
equation, deterministic flow, or stochastic partial differential equation
requires its own solution concept rather than being hidden in
\(\cL_\eta\).

\subsection{Committors and stopping times}

Let
\[
 \tau_{\cG}=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\in\cG\},
 \qquad
 \tau_{\cB}=\inf\{t\ge0:X_t\in\cB\}.
\]
The guard committor is
\[
 q_\eta(x)
 =
 \PP_x(\tau_{\cG}<\tau_{\cB}).
\]
Under standard regularity assumptions it solves the boundary-value
problem
\[
 \cL_\eta q_\eta=0,
 \qquad
 q_\eta|_{\cG}=1,
 \qquad
 q_\eta|_{\cB}=0.
\]
This function is often a better operational definition of a dynamical
front than the raw strip \(\cF_\delta\): its level sets report the actual
competition between drift, diffusion, geometry, and the selected
comparison sets.

The equation is not determined by \(D\).  Two models with the same
admissible set and different generators can have entirely different
committors and exit-time laws.

\subsection{A diagnostic barrier becomes a force only when inserted}

On a positive-row domain one may define
\[
 U_{\rm bar}(x)
 =
 -\sum_{i\in\cI}\alpha_i\log r_i(x),
 \qquad
 \alpha_i>0.
\]
As a plotted scalar, \(U_{\rm bar}\) is only a diagnostic.  If the drift is
changed to
\[
 b_\eta=b_\eta^{(0)}-M\nabla U_{\rm bar},
\]
then \(-M\nabla U_{\rm bar}\) is a genuine feedback force or penalty in
that reduced model.  The coefficients \(\alpha_i\) and mobility \(M\) are
additional constitutive inputs.  The barrier does not become
non-dynamical merely because its intended interpretation is
``admissibility repair.''

This barrier should also not be confused with the potential in a
metastable Eyring--Kramers model.  They coincide only if that identification
is explicitly made and the resulting drift and basin structure are
verified.

\subsection{Exit is not continuation}

The stopped path and its exit location are determined by the within-chart
process.  A successor state is not.  At the guard the model must choose:
\[
 \text{stop},\qquad
 \text{change chart},\qquad
 \text{apply a reset map},\qquad
 \text{sample a reset kernel}.
\]
These choices produce different path laws.  In particular, an exit
distribution on \(\cG\) is not a distribution of post-exit outcomes unless
a map or kernel from guard states to restart states has been supplied.
Stochastic hybrid systems with boundary-hitting resets provide the
appropriate established framework
\cite{GoebelSanfeliceTeel2012,BectBailiFleury2005}.

\section{An exactly solvable normal front}
\label{sec:normal}

The simplest calculation already shows why no universal boundary-layer
law can follow from the static margin.

\subsection{Model}

Let \(R_t\in(0,L)\) be a signed inward normal coordinate, with \(R=0\) the
guard and \(R=L\) an interior comparison surface.  Consider
\begin{equation}
 \dd R_t=b\,\dd t+\sqrt{2\eta}\,\dd W_t,
 \qquad
 \eta>0,
\label{eq:normalSDE}
\end{equation}
stopped at
\[
 \tau=\tau_0\wedge\tau_L,
 \qquad
 \tau_a=\inf\{t\ge0:R_t=a\}.
\]
Positive \(b\) points away from the guard, negative \(b\) points toward it,
and \(b=0\) is unbiased diffusion.

\begin{theorem}[Exact committor and mean exit time]
\label{thm:normal}
For the stopped diffusion \eqref{eq:normalSDE}, define
\[
 p_\eta(x)=\PP_x(\tau_0<\tau_L),
 \qquad
 T_\eta(x)=\EE_x[\tau],
 \qquad 0<x<L.
\]
If \(b\ne0\), then
\begin{align}
 p_\eta(x)
 &=
 \frac{e^{-bx/\eta}-e^{-bL/\eta}}
      {1-e^{-bL/\eta}},
\label{eq:pnonzero}\\
 T_\eta(x)
 &=
 \frac{L}{b}
 \frac{1-e^{-bx/\eta}}{1-e^{-bL/\eta}}
 -
 \frac{x}{b}.
\label{eq:Tnonzero}
\end{align}
If \(b=0\), then
\begin{equation}
 p_\eta(x)=1-\frac{x}{L},
 \qquad
 T_\eta(x)=\frac{x(L-x)}{2\eta}.
\label{eq:zero}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The generator is
\[
 \cL_\eta=b\frac{\dd}{\dd x}
 +\eta\frac{\dd^2}{\dd x^2}.
\]
The committor solves
\[
 \cL_\eta p_\eta=0,
 \qquad p_\eta(0)=1,\quad p_\eta(L)=0.
\]
For \(b\ne0\), its general form is
\(A+Be^{-bx/\eta}\); imposing the boundary values gives
\eqref{eq:pnonzero}.  For \(b=0\), the solution is affine.

Dynkin's formula gives
\[
 \cL_\eta T_\eta=-1,
 \qquad T_\eta(0)=T_\eta(L)=0.
\]
For \(b\ne0\), integrating the ordinary differential equation twice gives
\[
 T_\eta(x)=-\frac{x}{b}+A+Be^{-bx/\eta}.
\]
The two boundary values yield \eqref{eq:Tnonzero}.  When \(b=0\), solving
\(\eta T_\eta''=-1\) gives \eqref{eq:zero}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A genuine boundary layer, under one sign of drift}

\begin{corollary}[Exact committor-layer thickness]
\label{cor:thickness}
Assume \(b>0\).  For any fixed \(q\in(0,1)\), the unique point
\(x_q\in(0,L)\) satisfying \(p_\eta(x_q)=q\) is
\[
 x_q
 =
 -\frac{\eta}{b}
 \log\!\left(q+(1-q)e^{-bL/\eta}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 x_q
 =
 \frac{\eta}{b}\log\frac1q
 +
 O\!\left(\eta e^{-bL/\eta}\right)
 \qquad(\eta\downarrow0).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Solving \eqref{eq:pnonzero} for \(x\) gives the exact expression.
For fixed \(q>0\), expand the logarithm around \(q\) as
\(e^{-bL/\eta}\to0\).
\end{proof}

Thus a thin exponential committor layer is real in this model, and its
scale is \(\eta/b\).  But it is not universal:
\begin{itemize}
\item If \(b>0\), \(p_\eta(x)\) is exponentially small away from the
      guard, while \(T_\eta(x)\to(L-x)/b\).
\item If \(b=0\), the committor is linear throughout the interval and
      \(T_\eta(x)\) scales as \(\eta^{-1}\).
\item If \(b<0\), the process follows the deterministic drift toward the
      guard and \(T_\eta(x)\to x/|b|\); on compact subsets away from \(L\),
      \(p_\eta(x)\to1\).
\end{itemize}

The same static interval therefore supports a thin exponential layer, a
domain-wide linear profile, or almost-certain guard hitting.  It supports
no universal sigmoid and no automatic exponentially long metastable time.

\subsection{What this model does and does not establish}

The normal model is a local reduced calculation.  It becomes an
approximation to a higher-dimensional front only after:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a regular active row provides a normal coordinate;
\item tangential motion and curvature terms are bounded or retained;
\item the projected drift and diffusion are derived with an error bound;
\item the approximation remains valid over the stopping-time scale of
      interest; and
\item the comparison surface \(R=L\) is chosen independently of the
      desired result.
\end{enumerate}

Without those items, fitting \(b\) and \(\eta\) to observed exit data is a
phenomenological model, not a derivation from MTT geometry.

\section{When large deviations and metastability are valid}
\label{sec:metastability}

\subsection{Large deviations require a family}

Large-deviation theory concerns a declared family such as
\[
 \dd X_t^\eta
 =
 f(X_t^\eta)\,\dd t
 +
 \sqrt{2\eta}\,\Sigma(X_t^\eta)\,\dd W_t,
 \qquad \eta\downarrow0.
\]
Under suitable regularity and nondegeneracy assumptions, paths have an
action functional.  With \(a=\Sigma\Sigma^\top\) invertible, one common
normalization is
\[
 I_{0T}(\phi)
 =
 \frac14\int_0^T
 \bigl(\dot\phi-f(\phi)\bigr)^\top
 a(\phi)^{-1}
 \bigl(\dot\phi-f(\phi)\bigr)\,\dd t.
\]
Path probabilities then have logarithmic asymptotics governed by
\(\inf I_{0T}/\eta\), subject to the standard open/closed set
qualifications
\cite{FreidlinWentzell2012,MatkowskySchuss1977}.

One simulation at one noise amplitude does not establish a
large-deviation principle.  Neither does a small reserve \(D\).  The
small-noise parameter, topology on path space, action, and limiting family
must all be supplied.

\subsection{Metastability requires a trap}

Metastability is stronger than proximity to a guard.  It requires a
long-lived region, a shorter local relaxation scale, and a much longer
escape scale.  For the standard reversible gradient diffusion
\begin{equation}
 \dd X_t
 =
 -\nabla V(X_t)\,\dd t
 +
 \sqrt{2\eta}\,\dd W_t,
\label{eq:kramers}
\end{equation}
suppose \(m\) is a nondegenerate local minimum and \(z\) is the relevant
nondegenerate index-one saddle.  In the standard ordered-well setting,
the Eyring--Kramers transition law has the form
\begin{equation}
 \EE_m\tau
 \sim
 \frac{2\pi}{|\lambda_-(z)|}
 \sqrt{
 \frac{|\det\nabla^2V(z)|}
      {\det\nabla^2V(m)}
 }
 \exp\!\left(\frac{V(z)-V(m)}{\eta}\right),
 \qquad \eta\downarrow0,
\label{eq:kramerslaw}
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_-(z)\) is the unique negative Hessian eigenvalue.  Rigorous
versions require precise basin, saddle, regularity, and ordering
hypotheses
\cite{BovierEtAl2004,Berglund2013}.

Equation \eqref{eq:kramerslaw} is powerful precisely because its
hypotheses are restrictive.  Degenerate saddles, non-gradient drift,
multiple equal escape routes, state-dependent or degenerate noise,
non-Markov memory, moving guards, and hybrid resets can alter the prefactor,
the exponent, or the whole asymptotic form.

\subsection{A strict promotion test}

A selection-front study may use the words \emph{metastable} or
\emph{Kramers} only after it records:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item the process family and small parameter;
\item an attracting set or local equilibrium;
\item a basin and exit set;
\item a quasipotential or actual potential barrier;
\item the relevant regularity and nondegeneracy conditions;
\item the initial or quasistationary law;
\item the asymptotic observable and error term; and
\item whether a reset changes the stopped process.
\end{enumerate}

The v1 paper supplied none of these as a common cross-domain record.
Its universal metastability claim is therefore withdrawn, not merely
renamed.

\section{Multiple rows and moving fronts}
\label{sec:multiple}

If one row \(r_j(t,x)\) is uniquely active and regular, its zero set has
normal velocity
\[
 V_n=-\frac{\partial_t r_j}{|\nabla r_j|}.
\]
If it obeys
\[
 \partial_t r_j+v\cdot\nabla r_j=s_j,
\]
then
\[
 V_n=v\cdot n-\frac{s_j}{|\nabla r_j|}.
\]
These are standard level-set identities, already derived in the corrected
capacity-dynamics paper and situated in the level-set literature
\cite{CapacityDynamics2026,OsherSethian1988}.  They track a front after its
row evolution has been supplied; they do not select that evolution.

At a transverse corner, several normal coordinates compete.  The
appropriate local object is the vector
\[
 R_J(X_t)=\bigl(r_j(X_t)\bigr)_{j\in J},
\]
not one differentiable scalar minimum.  Ito's formula gives each row its
own projected drift, quadratic variation, and cross-variation.  The
probability of first reaching a particular face then depends on the full
covariance matrix and tangential dynamics.  Even the identity of the
first failing row is generator-dependent.

If the rows themselves depend on time, the admissible set moves.  The
stopping problem must then be posed in space-time, and a row can reach zero
because the state moves, the tolerance changes, or both.  These mechanisms
should not be merged into a single claim that ``capacity was consumed.''

bounded, or measured.  Useful examples are:

\end{aligned}
\]

\section{Numerical execution and exactness}
\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Boundary events need special treatment}

A fixed-step Euler scheme can move from \(D>0\) to \(D<0\) without
recording the crossing.  For killed diffusions, boundary hitting can reduce
the weak convergence order of a naive discrete scheme
\cite{Gobet2000}.  A credible numerical front calculation should:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item retain every signed row rather than only the clipped bottleneck;
\item bracket the first row crossing within a step;
\item refine or interpolate the event using a stated method;
\item distinguish the exact guard from a numerical safety guard;
\item stop before applying a separately declared reset;
\item record random seeds and all constitutive parameters;
\item repeat across time-step and guard tolerances; and
\item report sampling error separately from discretization and model error.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What an exactness certificate would contain}

For a theorem-level computational claim, the archival packet should contain:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item source hashes for the row functions and generator;
\item interval or otherwise certified row-evaluation errors;
\item a verified positive reserve before each accepted step;
\item a certified event bracket or analytic exit solution;
\item convergence bounds for the chosen stopped-process observable;
\item the post-exit semantics and reset-support check, if any; and
\item a verifier independent enough to reject altered rows, parameters,
      or outputs.
\end{enumerate}

The present paper contains an analytic calculation, not a numerical
result packet.  No empirical or physical prediction is promoted.

\section{Completion and falsifiability contract}
\label{sec:completion}

A proposed physical selection front is complete only if another researcher
can answer all of the following from the archive:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item What is the state-and-control space?
\item What are all required signed rows, scales, norms, and provenances?
\item Which boundary point or stratum is under study?
\item Is the geometry regular, transverse, or singular?
\item What is the within-chart generator or deterministic evolution?
\item What parameter tends to zero, infinity, or a threshold?
\item What are the initial law, guard, comparison set, and observation
      horizon?
\item Which committor, exit time, exit law, or active-row statistic is
      claimed?
\item What are the analytic, numerical, and sampling errors?
\item Does the process stop, change chart, or reset after exit?
\item Which physical observable does the mathematical output represent?
\item Which held-out data could contradict the construction?
\end{enumerate}

The selection-front interpretation fails for a proposed application if:
\begin{itemize}
\item no complete common row record can be formed;
\item the claimed front disappears under allowed normalization or
      certification refinement;
\item the observed exit law disagrees with the supplied generator outside
      the declared error;
\item a purported universal scaling changes when an unrecorded drift,
      covariance, guard, or reset is exposed;
\item the physical observable map is nonunique and no source selects one;
      or
\item an overlapping effective description remains controlled across the
      alleged terminal boundary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An admissibility boundary is a precise and useful object: it is the first
place where one or more declared control rows lose positive reserve.  A
selection front becomes dynamical only after a process is supplied.

The exact normal calculation makes the central lesson unavoidable.  The
same static interval exhibits an exponential committor layer, a linear
profile, or almost-certain guard hitting depending only on the drift.
Metastability and Eyring--Kramers scaling are valid and important in their
own basin-and-barrier regime, but they are not consequences of a small
bottleneck reserve.

The corrected concept is therefore narrower than Version 1 and more useful.
It provides a disciplined bridge from MTT admissibility records to
first-exit analysis while keeping geometry, dynamics, numerics, reset, and
physical interpretation separate.  That separation is what makes future
applications testable.
