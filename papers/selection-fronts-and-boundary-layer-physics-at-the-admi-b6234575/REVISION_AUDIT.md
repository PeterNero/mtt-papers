## Revision purpose

Version 1 treated a single unspecified control scalar as a universal
distance to the boundary of physical description. It then claimed that
small positive values necessarily produce boundary layers, large
deviations, metastability, nonperturbative first-passage behavior, abrupt
effective-theory failure, measurement collapse, black-hole information
loss, cosmological measure failure, and universal limits on scalable
coherence.

Those conclusions do not follow from a static admissibility margin. They
require a complete set of signed normalized control rows, local boundary
regularity, a specified dynamical generator, an initial law, a stopping
set, an asymptotic family, and post-exit semantics.

Version 2 retains the useful term "selection front" but gives it a strict
conditional meaning: a local first-exit layer of a declared diffusion or
hybrid process relative to a complete MTT admissibility record.

The revision is a standalone explanatory paper, not a list of corrections.

## Claim-by-claim audit

### One scalar is a universal distance to failure

Version 1 claim:
One positive parameter can stand for a spectral gap, projector bound,
curvature remainder, or stability eigenvalue without further typing.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
The rows have different domains, units, norms, tolerances, and logical
roles. A raw minimum is meaningless without normalization, and a gap does
not imply projector control, descent, well-posedness, or truncation
control.

Version 2 resolution:
Use the full normalized row vector and retain every row's provenance.

### Every boundary is a smooth physical front

Version 1 claim:
The admissibility threshold is a universal boundary-layer surface.

Decision:
Replace by local geometry under hypotheses.

Reason:
A unique regular row gives a smooth facet. Transverse simultaneous rows
give a corner. Rank loss can produce singular or higher-order geometry.

Version 2 resolution:
Inherit the regular-facet theorem and prove the transverse orthant-corner
proposition.

### Small positive reserve implies a boundary layer

Version 1 claim:
The limit toward zero reserve is necessarily nonsmooth and boundary-layer
dominated.

Decision:
Withdraw as universal.

Reason:
A boundary layer belongs to a generator and asymptotic family, not a
static set.

Version 2 resolution:
Derive an exact committor layer only for outward drift with small noise.
The same interval has a linear profile at zero drift and nearly certain
guard hitting for inward drift.

### Metastability and Kramers scaling are universal

Version 1 claim:
Near the boundary, coherent states are metastable and escape times have
the Kramers exponential form.

Decision:
Restrict to a conditional imported theorem.

Reason:
Kramers laws require a small-noise family, an attracting basin,
quasipotential or potential barrier, regular minima and saddles, and
additional ordering and reversibility hypotheses for the sharp prefactor.

Version 2 resolution:
State the standard gradient-diffusion law with its hypotheses, cite the
rigorous literature, and publish a promotion checklist.

### Large deviations follow from rare events

Version 1 claim:
Rare behavior near a small reserve automatically invokes large-deviation
theory.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
A large-deviation principle is an asymptotic statement for a family of
probability laws in a specified topology with a rate function.

Version 2 resolution:
Declare the small-noise family, covariance, action normalization, and
limiting requirement.

### A diverging diagnostic is not a force

Version 1 implication:
The boundary behavior can be discussed independently of whether the
diagnostic enters the equations of motion.

Decision:
Clarify.

Reason:
A scalar plot is diagnostic. Once its gradient is inserted in the drift or
acceleration, it is a feedback force or penalty in the reduced model.

Version 2 resolution:
State both cases explicitly and keep the coefficients and mobility in the
constitutive ledger.

### Exit selects an outcome

Version 1 claim:
Crossing the front selects a new physical configuration.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
A stopping surface defines no post-exit state.

Version 2 resolution:
Separate stop, chart transition, deterministic reset, and Markov reset
kernel. The exit-location distribution is not a post-exit outcome law.

### Effective theory or RG fails abruptly and universally

Version 1 claim:
Approaching the front forces nonanalytic EFT or RG failure.

Decision:
Withdraw as general.

Reason:
One must identify a rigorous error or truncation row and compare to an
overlapping chart. Failure of one certificate need not terminate every
description.

Version 2 resolution:
Treat a front as the first failure of the declared record only.

### Cosmological measures become ill-defined

Version 1 claim:
Selection-front dynamics structurally explains global measure problems and
early-time probability failure.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
No cosmological state space, law, measure, constraint surface, or
observation map was supplied.

Version 2 resolution:
Publish those objects as an application contract rather than a result.

### Black-hole thresholds and information loss follow

Version 1 claim:
Horizon formation and loss of global unitary description are expected
front effects.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
No spacetime equations, semiclassical control rows, quantum state,
horizon observable, or continuation theorem was supplied.

Version 2 resolution:
State explicitly that no black-hole theorem is obtained.

### Quantum measurement collapse follows

Version 1 claim:
Collapse-like behavior emerges from crossing a coherence front.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
No system--apparatus algebra, interaction, instrument, outcome record, or
probability source was supplied.

Version 2 resolution:
List the missing quantum measurement contract and make no collapse or Born
claim.

### There is a universal limit on coherence or entanglement depth

Version 1 claim:
Selection fronts impose upper bounds on scalable coherence.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Such a statement requires an operational coherence measure, device and
noise model, control resources, geometry, and asymptotic family.

Version 2 resolution:
Treat this as an open application requiring its own source theorem.
