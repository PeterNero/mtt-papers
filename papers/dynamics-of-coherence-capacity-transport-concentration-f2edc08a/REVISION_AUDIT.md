## Revision purpose

Version 2 attempted to derive a divergence-free coherence-capacity
current, conservation, generic focusing, codimension-one exhaustion,
irreversibility, gravity, horizons, and thermalization from invertible
upper dynamics, noninjective projection, and a finite stability margin.
Those assumptions do not supply a current, an additive density, a
constitutive law, or an evolution equation for a velocity field.

Version 3 preserves the useful dynamical question but rebuilds the answer.
Transport is now a model-level promotion of the already corrected
normalized capacity record. The paper distinguishes a material scalar, an
additive density, and an advection-diffusion-reaction field; proves the
conditional balance and first-exit results appropriate to each; and
identifies the extra mechanisms needed for genuine exhaustion.

The revision is a standalone explanatory paper, not a correction list.

## Claim-by-claim audit

### A capacity current exists in every admissible basin

Version 2 claim:
Pushing forward admissibility structure through the projection produces a
local divergence-free current.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
A pushforward measure can define a density under absolute-continuity
hypotheses, but invertibility of upper evolution does not make the
pushforward density conserved. A current, reference measure, transport
velocity, and source law are additional data.

Version 3 resolution:
Define material scalar, additive density, and diffusive field models
separately. State each current or PDE as action-derived or constitutive
model input.

### Capacity is conserved within every admissible regime

Version 2 claim:
Capacity cannot be created inside an admissible basin and obeys a
divergence-free continuity equation.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Repair, feedback, reclassification, changing normalization, and changes in
the underlying operator can raise or lower a diagnostic reserve. The
bottleneck is not generally additive over regions.

Version 3 resolution:
An additive density obeys an integral balance only after a local balance
law is supplied. Total conservation further requires zero integrated
source and zero net boundary flux.

### Same-zero-set capacity representatives have the same dynamics

Version 2 claim:
An unspecified capacity scalar can be differentiated and transported
without fixing its normalization.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 3 resolution:
For `partial_t C + div(C v) = s`, the transformed field `f(C)` acquires
the explicit additional term
`[f(C) - C f'(C)] div(v)`. Only linear rescaling preserves a source-free
density law for arbitrary compressible velocities.

### Barriers create a distributional sink automatically

Version 2 claim:
Loss of regularity of the projection implies a distributional source
supported on the projected barrier.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
A distributional source must be computed from a weak balance law and its
trace or jump data. Failure of one description does not determine its sign,
magnitude, or even its existence.

Version 3 resolution:
Boundary loss, sink terms, moving admissible sets, singular flow, and chart
reclassification are listed as distinct mechanisms with distinct
mathematics.

### All irreversible changes are capacity flux through barriers

Version 2 claim:
Every effective irreversibility event is a capacity-flux event.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 3 resolution:
A first exit only ends one certificate. A post-exit rule must separately
choose stopping, chart continuation, deterministic reset, or stochastic
kernel. Irreversibility requires its own lower dynamics, coarse-graining,
record, or thermodynamic theorem.

### Downhill capacity transport is generic

Version 2 claim:
Capacity flows according to a universal downhill strain principle.

Decision:
Reclassify as a possible constitutive law.

Version 3 resolution:
Any mobility, gradient flow, force, or penalty must be supplied with its
metric, units, sign, source, and derivation.

### Projection implies Raychaudhuri-type focusing

Version 2 claim:
Persistent strain implies
`d theta/d tau <= -kappa theta^2`.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
The continuity equation does not determine the evolution of the velocity
gradient. Version 3 gives the exact counterexample
`v(x)=a x`, `rho(t)=rho_0 exp(-a t)`, with negative constant expansion
and zero expansion derivative.

Version 3 resolution:
A focusing equation requires independent dynamics for velocity,
acceleration, curvature, shear, and vorticity.

### Source-free focusing exhausts capacity

Version 2 claim:
Inward transport generically drives a positive capacity scalar to zero.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Passive source-free scalar transport is constant along characteristics.
A positive source-free continuity density remains positive under a regular
flow, and compression raises that density.

Version 3 resolution:
Exhaustion requires a sink, outward boundary loss, moving admissible set,
singular regime, chart change, or explicit constitutive link to another
field.

### Exhaustion is generically codimension one

Version 2 claim:
Capacity reaches zero on a surrounding codimension-one surface.

Decision:
Replace by a conditional regular-value theorem.

Version 3 resolution:
A codimension-one boundary follows locally only when one row is uniquely
active and its spatial gradient is nonzero. Ties and critical zeros can
produce singular or higher-codimension sets.

### Capacity bottlenecks are horizons

Version 2 claim:
A zero-capacity hypersurface is the structural origin of a horizon.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 3 resolution:
A certificate boundary is not a causal horizon. A horizon requires
Lorentzian geometry, field equations, and a trapped- or event-horizon
definition.

### Capacity transport derives gravity, entropy, and thermalization

Version 2 claim:
The transport framework supplies the dynamical substrate of gravity,
horizon formation, thermalization, and memory loss.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Version 3 resolution:
Gravity requires an action or field equation and normalization. Entropy
requires a state count, measure, density operator, or thermodynamic
potential. Thermalization requires specified many-body dynamics and an
equilibrium or statistical theorem.

### The illustrative drift-diffusion model generically forms a zero

Version 2 claim:
A sufficiently strong inward drift in the displayed source-free
drift-diffusion equation produces finite-time zero.

Decision:
Withdraw.

Reason:
Under regularity and positive data, standard transport and parabolic
maximum-principle behavior does not justify that claim without a sink,
absorbing boundary, or degeneracy.

Version 3 resolution:
Replace it with two explicit conditional models: advected reserve with a
declared sink, and compressive density transport with a declared decreasing
constitutive reserve.
