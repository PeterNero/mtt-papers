\title{\vspace{-1em}
Dynamics of Coherence Capacity:\\
\large Constitutive Transport, Balance Laws, and First Exit}
\author{Peter Nero}
\date{Version 3, July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Coherence capacity is a sourced diagnostic of the remaining validity of an
effective description.  A diagnostic does not move merely because it has
been named.  To discuss transport one must additionally choose a base
space, an ordering variable, a fixed normalized capacity representative,
a velocity or flux, source and repair terms, boundary conditions, and a
solution concept.  This paper supplies that missing model-level framework.

We distinguish three inequivalent equations: material transport of a
scalar reserve, a balance law for an additive density, and
advection--diffusion--reaction of a field.  We derive the corresponding
integral balance, show exactly how nonlinear reparameterization changes a
continuity equation, and extend the rowwise capacity budget to continuous
trajectories.  The bottleneck is the minimum of the reserve rows and is
generally nonsmooth when the active row changes.  Under a unique active row
and a regular-value hypothesis, its first-exit set is a moving
codimension-one hypersurface with an explicit normal-velocity formula.

Two no-go results correct the former transport picture.  Source-free
passive transport preserves a positive reserve along characteristics.
Source-free continuity transport preserves a positive density for every
finite interval on which the flow has finite integrated divergence.
Compression increases such a density; it does not by itself exhaust a
reserve.  A focusing inequality, finite-time zero, conservation law, or
capacity bottleneck therefore requires additional dynamics or a declared
constitutive relation.  We give one explicit conditional model in which a
compressing carrier lowers a reserve through a fixed decreasing
constitutive map.  No gravity, horizon, entropy, probability, collapse,
irreversibility, or arrow of time is derived from transport alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and roadmap}
\label{sec:intro}

The two preceding capacity papers answer static and operational questions.
The normalized-margin paper defines signed reserve rows, their scales, a
bottleneck scalar, and a metric clearance \cite{CapacityMargin2026}.  The
control-budget paper defines the least cost of an allowed perturbation that
leaves the admissible set \cite{CapacityBudget2026}.  Neither definition
contains a velocity field, a flux, a diffusion tensor, or a source.

This distinction matters.  A temperature field can be advected, diffuse,
or react, but those verbs come from a heat or transport model, not from the
definition of temperature.  A stability margin is similar.  It can change
along an evolving state, yet its change law must be calculated from that
state evolution or supplied as a reduced constitutive equation.

The paper proceeds in five stages:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Section~\ref{sec:promotion} lists the data needed to promote a static
      capacity record to a dynamical model.
\item Section~\ref{sec:pathwise} derives continuous-time rowwise budget and
      first-exit bounds without assuming a local conservation law.
\item Sections~\ref{sec:balance}--\ref{sec:pde} distinguish scalar
      transport, density balance, and advection--diffusion--reaction.
\item Sections~\ref{sec:nogo}--\ref{sec:boundary} prove the no-go and
      regular-boundary results that replace the former generic focusing
      argument.
\item Sections~\ref{sec:models}--\ref{sec:contract} give conditional
      models, locate theorem ownership, and state the completion contract
      for a physical application.
\end{enumerate}

\section{From a static record to a dynamical model}
\label{sec:promotion}

\subsection{The static input}

Let \(\cI=\{1,\ldots,m\}\) index a finite list of sourced admissibility
conditions.  Their normalized signed reserves are
\[
 r(t,x)=\bigl(r_i(t,x)\bigr)_{i\in\cI},
\]
and the signed bottleneck is
\[
 D(t,x)=\min_{i\in\cI}r_i(t,x).
\]
The state is admissible exactly when every declared row is positive.
The clipped display scalar
\[
 C(t,x)=\min\{1,\max\{0,D(t,x)\}\}
\]
is useful for plots but loses information outside the interval
\([0,1]\).  Dynamics should therefore be stated first for the full row
vector or for the signed bottleneck \(D\), not only for \(C\).

The symbol \(x\) can denote an effective spatial coordinate, a point in a
configuration manifold, or a control parameter.  Those interpretations
are not interchangeable.

\subsection{The dynamical promotion record}

\begin{definition}[Capacity-dynamics record]
\label{def:record}
A capacity-dynamics record is
\[
 \cD_{\rm cap}
 =
 \bigl(
 I,\cM,g,\mu,r,v,K,s,\cB,\cS,\pi
 \bigr),
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \(I=[0,T]\) is an ordering interval;
\item \((\cM,g,\mu)\) is the base manifold, metric, and reference measure;
\item \(r=(r_i)\) is one fixed normalized reserve record;
\item \(v\) is a supplied transport velocity when used;
\item \(K=(K_i)\) is a supplied diffusion or mobility record when used;
\item \(s=(s_i)\) contains consumption, repair, exchange, and
      reclassification terms;
\item \(\cB\) contains initial and boundary data;
\item \(\cS\) specifies regularity and the classical, weak, renormalized,
      or entropy solution concept; and
\item \(\pi\) records derivation, units, uncertainty, and provenance.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The record is deliberately larger than a single PDE.  Without its base
measure, a quantity cannot be classified as a density.  Without boundary
data, an integral conservation statement is incomplete.  Without a
solution concept, a discontinuous transport law can be ambiguous.

\subsection{Three meanings of transport}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.22\textwidth}Y Y@{}}
\toprule
Object & Representative equation & Meaning \\
\midrule
Material scalar
& \(\partial_t r+v\cdot\nabla r=s\)
& A label or reserve carried along trajectories.\\

Additive density
& \(\partial_t\rho+\Div(\rho v)=s\)
& An extensive amount per unit reference volume.\\

Diffusive/reactive field
& \(\partial_t r+v\cdot\nabla r
   =\Div(K\nabla r)+s\)
& A constitutive smoothing and source model.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Calling all three a ``capacity current'' would conceal their different
transformation laws.  In particular, the normalized bottleneck need not be
additive over disjoint regions, so it is not automatically a conserved
density.

\section{Pathwise reserve dynamics}
\label{sec:pathwise}

The weakest dynamical statement follows the actual state trajectory and
does not require a spacetime current.

\begin{assumption}[Continuous rowwise budget]
\label{ass:path}
Let \(x:[0,T]\to\cX\) be an absolutely continuous trajectory.  Suppose
each reserve
\[
 R_i(t)=r_i(t,x(t))
\]
is absolutely continuous and, for almost every \(t\),
\[
 \dot R_i(t)\ge-a_i(t),
 \qquad
 a_i(t)\ge0,
\]
with \(a_i\in L^1(0,T)\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous cumulative budget]
\label{thm:pathbudget}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:path},
\[
 R_i(t)
 \ge
 R_i(0)-\int_0^t a_i(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma
 \qquad(i\in\cI).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 D(t,x(t))
 \ge
 D(0,x(0))
 -
 \int_0^t
 \max_{i\in\cI}a_i(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma.
\]
The trajectory remains admissible whenever the right-hand side is
positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first inequality is the fundamental theorem of calculus for
absolutely continuous functions.  For every row,
\[
 R_i(t)
 \ge
 R_i(0)-\int_0^t\max_j a_j(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma
 \ge
 D(0,x(0))-\int_0^t\max_j a_j(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma.
\]
Taking the minimum over \(i\) proves the second inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Certified first-exit lower bound]
\label{cor:exit}
Define
\[
 \tau_{\rm exit}
 =
 \inf\{t\in[0,T]:D(t,x(t))\le0\}.
\]
If \(\max_i a_i(t)\le A\) almost everywhere for a constant \(A>0\), then
\[
 \tau_{\rm exit}\ge\frac{D(0,x(0))}{A}
\]
whenever the quotient lies in \([0,T]\).
\end{corollary}

The bound is one-sided.  It is not an exact lifetime unless a row is known
to consume its full allowance and actually reaches its boundary.

\subsection{Switching of the active row}

\begin{definition}[Active set]
At a point \((t,x)\), define
\[
 \cI_{\rm act}(t,x)
 =
 \{i:r_i(t,x)=D(t,x)\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[One-sided derivative of the bottleneck]
\label{prop:min}
Let the finite family \(R_i(t)\) be differentiable at \(t_0\).  Then the
right directional derivative of
\[
 D(t)=\min_iR_i(t)
\]
exists and satisfies
\[
 D'_+(t_0)
 =
 \min_{i\in\cI_{\rm act}(t_0)}\dot R_i(t_0).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Rows outside the active set have a strictly positive separation from the
minimum at \(t_0\) and cannot become minimizing for all sufficiently small
positive increments.  Expanding the finitely many active rows to first
order and taking their minimum gives the formula.
\end{proof}

Thus \(D\) can have a kink even when every row is smooth.  A scalar PDE
written directly for \(D\) must account for active-row switching; it is not
generally obtained by applying the same PDE to the minimum.

\section{Balance laws require additional structure}
\label{sec:balance}

\subsection{Local and integral balance}

Suppose a model promotes a quantity \(\rho\) to an additive density with
flux \(J\) and source \(s\):
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\Div J=s.
\]
For a material flux \(J=\rho v\), this is the continuity equation
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\Div(\rho v)=s.
\]
Balance laws of this form are standard in continuum physics
\cite{Dafermos2016}.  Their use here is conditional on the promotion of a
particular capacity-related object to a density.

\begin{theorem}[Integral balance on a fixed region]
\label{thm:integral}
Let \(\Omega\subset\cM\) be a compact region with smooth boundary and
outward normal \(n\).  For a classical solution of
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\Div J=s,
\]
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int_\Omega\rho\,\dd\mu
 =
 -\int_{\partial\Omega}J\cdot n\,\dd A
 +\int_\Omega s\,\dd\mu.
\]
Hence total \(\rho\) is conserved only when the net boundary flux and
integrated source vanish.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate under the integral, substitute the field equation, and use
the divergence theorem.
\end{proof}

This theorem does not derive the local balance law.  It states what follows
after that law and its regularity have been supplied.  On a moving region,
the Reynolds transport term must also be included.

\subsection{Normalization rigidity}

The capacity representative must be fixed before a density law is used.
An arbitrary same-zero-set reparameterization changes the law.

\begin{theorem}[Reparameterization identity]
\label{thm:reparam}
Let \(C>0\) satisfy
\[
 \partial_tC+\Div(Cv)=s
\]
classically, and let \(f\in C^1((0,\infty))\).  Then
\[
 \partial_t f(C)+\Div(f(C)v)
 =
 f'(C)s+
 \bigl[f(C)-Cf'(C)\bigr]\Div v.
\]
For arbitrary compressible velocities, a source-free density law is
preserved under \(C\mapsto f(C)\) only for
\[
 f(C)=aC
\]
with constant \(a\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The original equation gives
\[
 \partial_tC+v\cdot\nabla C=s-C\Div v.
\]
The chain and product rules yield
\[
 \partial_t f(C)+\Div(f(C)v)
 =
 f'(C)\bigl(\partial_tC+v\cdot\nabla C\bigr)+f(C)\Div v,
\]
which is the stated identity.  In the source-free case, invariance for
arbitrary \(\Div v\) requires \(f(C)-Cf'(C)=0\).  Solving this ordinary
differential equation gives \(f(C)=aC\).
\end{proof}

The functions \(C\), \(C^2\), and \(\sqrt C\) can have the same positive
region and zero set, yet they do not represent the same conserved density.
This is why the normalization and reference measure are part of
Definition~\ref{def:record}.

\section{Constitutive PDE classes}
\label{sec:pde}

\subsection{Passive scalar transport}

A reserve label carried by a supplied velocity satisfies
\[
 \partial_t r_i+v\cdot\nabla r_i=s_i.
\]
Along a characteristic \(X(t)\) solving
\[
 \dot X(t)=v(t,X(t)),
\]
the row obeys
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}r_i(t,X(t))
=s_i(t,X(t)).
\]
This form is appropriate when \(r_i\) is a local diagnostic rather than an
additive amount.

\subsection{Advection--diffusion--reaction}

A model can instead supply
\[
 \partial_t r_i+v\cdot\nabla r_i
=
\Div(K_i\nabla r_i)+s_i(t,x,r).
\]
The diffusion tensor \(K_i\), reaction law \(s_i\), and boundary conditions
are new physical or algorithmic inputs.  A positive-semidefinite \(K_i\)
can smooth spatial variation; it does not specify what is being conserved.

\subsection{Weak and rough transport}

Classical characteristic formulas require regular velocity fields.  For
Sobolev or bounded-variation velocities, existence, uniqueness, and
renormalization require the hypotheses of the appropriate transport
theory.  DiPerna--Lions theory treats Sobolev vector fields
\cite{DiPernaLions1989}, and Ambrosio extends the framework to bounded
variation under declared divergence control \cite{Ambrosio2004}.
Nonlinear scalar conservation laws require an entropy condition; the
Kruzhkov theory is a standard reference \cite{Kruzhkov1970}.  General
elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic solution concepts are reviewed in
\cite{Evans2010}.

These references matter because a formal current is not enough.  A paper
claiming transport must state whether it has a classical, weak,
renormalized, or entropy solution and which theorem supplies
well-posedness.

\section{What source-free transport cannot do}
\label{sec:nogo}

\subsection{Passive transport preserves positive reserve}

\begin{theorem}[No exhaustion from source-free passive transport]
\label{thm:passive}
Let \(r\) solve
\[
 \partial_t r+v\cdot\nabla r=0
\]
on a domain where the characteristic flow \(X(t;x_0)\) exists.  Then
\[
 r(t,X(t;x_0))=r(0,x_0).
\]
In particular, a strictly positive reserve cannot reach zero along a
characteristic by passive transport alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The chain rule gives
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}r(t,X(t;x_0))
=
\partial_t r+v\cdot\nabla r=0.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{A positive density stays positive under smooth continuity flow}

\begin{theorem}[No finite-time zero from regular source-free continuity]
\label{thm:densitypositive}
Let \(v\) generate a classical flow \(X(t;x_0)\), and let
\(\rho\) solve
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\Div(\rho v)=0.
\]
Then
\[
 \rho(t,X(t;x_0))
 =
 \rho_0(x_0)
 \exp\left(
 -\int_0^t\Div v(\sigma,X(\sigma;x_0))\,\dd\sigma
 \right).
\]
If \(\rho_0(x_0)>0\) and the divergence integral is finite, then
\(\rho(t,X(t;x_0))>0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along the flow,
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\rho(t,X(t))
=
-\rho(t,X(t))\Div v(t,X(t)).
\]
Solving this scalar linear equation gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compression raises a conserved density]
If \(\Div v<0\) along a trajectory, then a positive source-free density
increases along that trajectory.
\end{corollary}

This is the opposite of the former claim that generic focusing exhausts a
conserved capacity.  A reserve can still decrease under compression, but
only if the model supplies a separate relation between the carrier density
and the reserve.

\subsection{Continuity does not imply a focusing equation}

\begin{proposition}[No universal Raychaudhuri-type inequality]
\label{prop:nofocus}
The continuity equation alone does not imply
\[
 \dot\theta\le-\kappa\theta^2
\]
for \(\theta=\Div v\) and any fixed \(\kappa>0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On \(\mathbb R\), take
\[
 v(x)=ax,
 \qquad
 \rho(t,x)=\rho_0e^{-at},
\]
where \(\rho_0>0\) and \(a<0\).  Then
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\partial_x(\rho v)=0,
\qquad
 \theta=\partial_xv=a,
\qquad
 \dot\theta=0.
\]
The proposed inequality would require
\[
 0\le-\kappa a^2,
\]
which is false.
\end{proof}

A Raychaudhuri equation follows from geometric and dynamical equations for
a congruence, not from a scalar continuity law.  Capacity dynamics would
need an independently derived equation for \(v\), its acceleration,
curvature coupling, and any shear or vorticity terms.

\subsection{The actual routes to first exit}

A positive reserve can reach zero if at least one of the following is
present:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item a negative source or consumption term;
\item outward boundary loss;
\item a time-dependent admissible set, tolerance, or normalization;
\item a constitutive relation to another concentrating field;
\item singular or nonunique flow behavior outside the regular theorem;
\item a change of chart or model that reclassifies the active rows; or
\item initial data already touching the boundary.
\end{enumerate}
Each mechanism has different mathematics and must be recorded separately.

\section{First-exit geometry}
\label{sec:boundary}

\subsection{When is the boundary a hypersurface?}

\begin{theorem}[Regular active-row boundary]
\label{thm:regularboundary}
Fix a time \(t_0\).  Suppose \(r_j(t_0,\cdot)\) is \(C^1\) near \(x_0\),
\[
 r_j(t_0,x_0)=0,
\qquad
 \nabla r_j(t_0,x_0)\ne0,
\]
and \(j\) is the unique active row near \(x_0\).  Then the local
first-exit boundary
\[
 \{x:D(t_0,x)=0\}
\]
is a \(C^1\) codimension-one hypersurface near \(x_0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Uniqueness of the active row gives \(D=r_j\) locally.  The conclusion is
the regular-value theorem applied to \(r_j(t_0,\cdot)\).
\end{proof}

Without the nonzero-gradient condition, the zero set can be singular or
have codimension greater than one.  It can also contain an open region.
Without a unique active row, several hypersurfaces can meet and the
bottleneck can be nonsmooth.
Codimension one is therefore a theorem under hypotheses, not a generic
consequence of capacity language.

\subsection{Normal velocity of a regular boundary}

\begin{theorem}[Level-set motion law]
\label{thm:normalvelocity}
Let \(r_j(t,x)=0\) define a regular moving boundary and set
\[
 n=\frac{\nabla r_j}{|\nabla r_j|}.
\]
Its normal velocity is
\[
 V_n=-\frac{\partial_t r_j}{|\nabla r_j|}.
\]
If the row satisfies
\[
 \partial_t r_j+v\cdot\nabla r_j=s_j,
\]
then
\[
 V_n=v\cdot n-\frac{s_j}{|\nabla r_j|}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a boundary trajectory \(x_b(t)\),
\[
 0=\frac{\dd}{\dd t}r_j(t,x_b(t))
 =\partial_t r_j+\dot x_b\cdot\nabla r_j.
\]
Taking the normal component gives the first formula.  Substitution of the
transport equation gives the second.
\end{proof}

This is the standard level-set logic used for moving fronts
\cite{OsherSethian1988}.  It tracks a boundary after its PDE has been
supplied; it does not choose the PDE.

\subsection{Exit is not a reset}

At \(D=0\), one declared description has reached its boundary.  The model
must still choose among:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\text{stop},\qquad
 \text{continue in another chart},\\
 &\text{apply a deterministic reset},\qquad
 \text{apply a stochastic kernel}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Those choices define different hybrid systems.  A first-exit surface alone
does not define an outcome, a probability law, or an irreversible update.

\section{Concentration and reserve are different objects}
\label{sec:concentration}

Concentration describes how an additive measure or density occupies a
region.  Reserve describes distance from a declared failure condition.
They can be related, but the relation is constitutive.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.23\textwidth}Y Y@{}}
\toprule
Question & Density \(\rho\) & Reserve \(r\) \\
\midrule
Additive over regions?
& Yes, when promoted as a density
& Generally no\\

Compression under continuity?
& Raises \(\rho\)
& Undetermined without a relation to \(\rho\)\\

Zero means?
& Absence of represented amount
& Failure boundary of a declared condition\\

Conservation source?
& Continuity law or symmetry
& Not supplied by its definition\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional compression-to-depletion law]
\label{prop:constitutive}
Suppose a positive density obeys
\[
 \partial_t\rho+\Div(\rho v)=0
\]
and a reserve is defined by a fixed \(C^1\) constitutive map
\[
 r=h(\rho).
\]
Along characteristics,
\[
 \frac{\dd r}{\dd t}
=
-h'(\rho)\rho\,\Div v.
\]
If \(h'(\rho)<0\) and \(\Div v<0\), then the reserve decreases.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The continuity equation gives
\[
 \frac{\dd\rho}{\dd t}=-\rho\Div v.
\]
Apply the chain rule to \(r=h(\rho)\).
\end{proof}

This proposition is a valid route from concentration to reserve loss, but
the decreasing function \(h\) is part of the model.  It cannot be inferred
from projection alone.

\section{Two explicit conditional models}
\label{sec:models}

\subsection{Advected consumption on a circle}

Let \(x\in S^1\), let \(u\) be constant, and consider
\[
 \partial_t r+u\,\partial_xr=-\sigma(t,x),
\qquad
 \sigma\ge0.
\]
Along \(X(t)=x_0+ut\),
\[
 r(t,X(t))
=
r_0(x_0)-\int_0^t\sigma(\tau,X(\tau))\,\dd\tau.
\]
The first exit occurs when accumulated consumption reaches the initial
reserve.  If \(\sigma\le A\), Corollary~\ref{cor:exit} gives
\[
 \tau_{\rm exit}\ge\frac{\min_x r_0(x)}{A}.
\]

This example has a genuine transport velocity and a genuine sink.  The
sink is not produced by the word ``capacity''; it must be calculated from
the underlying operator or control model.

\subsection{Compression with a supplied constitutive reserve}

On \(\mathbb R\), choose
\[
 v(x)=-kx,
\qquad
 k>0,
\]
and a spatially uniform carrier density
\[
 \rho(t)=\rho_0e^{kt}.
\]
It solves the source-free continuity equation.  Now supply the decreasing
constitutive relation
\[
 r(t)=R-\alpha\rho(t),
\qquad
 R>0,\quad\alpha>0.
\]
If \(R>\alpha\rho_0\), the reserve is initially positive and reaches zero
at
\[
 t_*=\frac1k\log\left(\frac{R}{\alpha\rho_0}\right).
\]

The model cleanly separates the steps:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item continuity transport concentrates \(\rho\);
\item the chosen map \(r=R-\alpha\rho\) turns concentration into reserve
      consumption; and
\item the zero of \(r\) is a first exit.
\end{enumerate}
No gravity, entropy, or horizon follows from these equations.

\section{Role within Modal Triplet Theory}
\label{sec:mtt}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{0.28\textwidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
Source & Canonical responsibility \\
\midrule

\section{What is not derived}
\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{No automatic conservation}

Conservation requires a balance law plus boundary and source conditions.
In a fundamental field theory it can be derived from an action and
symmetry; in a reduced model it can be a constitutive postulate.  The
capacity definition supplies neither.

\subsection{No force or gravity}

A flux velocity is not a force law.  A gradient is not a force until an
action, Hamiltonian, or constitutive equation assigns it that role.
Einstein gravity requires Lorentzian geometry, a gravitational action or
field equations, and a sourced normalization.  None is contained in a
capacity transport template.

\subsection{No entropy or thermalization}

Mixing a scalar field does not by itself define entropy.  Thermalization
requires a state, dynamics, coarse-graining, equilibrium notion, and
thermodynamic limit or statistical theorem.

\subsection{No horizon or area law}

A regular zero-level set is only a failure boundary for the declared
certificate.  A horizon requires a causal metric and trapped or
event-horizon definition.  An area law requires state counting and a
coefficient.

\subsection{No probability, collapse, or arrow of time}

A deterministic PDE supplies no outcome probabilities unless a random
initial state, noise law, or stochastic kernel is added.  A first exit
does not choose a reset.  An arrow of time requires asymmetric dynamics,
boundary conditions, stable records, or thermodynamic structure.

\section{Conclusion}

Coherence capacity can have dynamics, but only after dynamics are supplied.
The mathematically clean starting point is the full normalized reserve
vector.  Along trajectories it supports cumulative consumption and
first-exit bounds.  On a base manifold it can be placed in a scalar
transport, density balance, or advection--diffusion--reaction model, each
with different meaning.

The main correction is constructive.  Source-free regular transport does
not generically exhaust positive capacity, and continuity does not produce
a focusing equation.  Genuine exhaustion needs a sink, boundary loss,
changing admissible set, singular regime, or explicit constitutive link to
another field.  Once one of those mechanisms is derived, the regular-value
and level-set results provide a precise way to analyze the resulting
first-exit boundary.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}

This paper reports structural definitions, identities, counterexamples,
and conditional PDE theorems.  It contains no fitted parameter or
numerical physical prediction.  The source TeX, bibliography, revision
audit, and generated PDF are versioned in the MTT papers repository.  A
future numerical dynamics result must additionally archive its capacity
record, PDE coefficients, initial and boundary data, solver, convergence
study, error bounds, code commit, and verification hashes.
