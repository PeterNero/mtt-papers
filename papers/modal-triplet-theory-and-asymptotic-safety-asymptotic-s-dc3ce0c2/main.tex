\begin{abstract}
This paper states the mathematically controlled relation currently available
between Modal Triplet Theory and functional-renormalization-group
asymptotic safety. An integrable bound on an effective-action flow gives a
Banach-space ultraviolet endpoint with an explicit tail estimate, but the
bound must be proved for the selected four-dimensional flow and is not
implied by an internal finite projector. Exact conjugacy between an MTT
coarse-graining map and an FRG step map transports fixed points; approximate
conjugacy transports only a residual unless a separate fixed-point theorem is
supplied. Scheme changes are controlled by an endpoint-difference estimate,
not by unrestricted scheme independence. Finally, a finite-dimensional
unstable subspace follows when the linearized RG step is quasi-compact with
essential spectral radius below one. These results define a rigorous
conditional bridge. The selected MTT-to-FRG chart, physical regulator,
four-dimensional damping estimate, and quasi-compactness certificate remain
open source obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and claim tier}

Asymptotic safety asks whether a renormalization-group flow for
dimensionless gravitational couplings approaches a non-Gaussian fixed point
with finitely many ultraviolet-relevant directions. Functional
renormalization group (FRG) calculations investigate this question through
the effective average action \(\Gamma_k\) and the Wetterich equation
\cite{Wetterich,Reuter,ReuterSaueressig}.

MTT uses a different starting language: coherent sectors, selected
projectors, fixed-point maps, and geometry-to-operator transfers. A relation
between the two frameworks therefore requires a map, not merely similar
words. The strongest claim defended here is:
\[
\text{selected MTT map}
\xleftrightarrow[\text{proved chart}]{\text{conjugacy}}
\text{FRG step map}.
\]
All fixed-point consequences are conditional on that chart and on the
analytic hypotheses below.

The paper does not claim that the present q79 internal finite Hessian is a
four-dimensional FRG regulator. The internal-filter UV no-go theorem in the
finite-filter paper shows why such an inference would fail without an
intertwiner.

\section{Typed spaces and maps}

Let \(\X\) be a Banach space of dimensionless effective actions. The exact
choice of norm is part of the model. A possible controlled setting is a
bounded-geometry slab with analytic local functionals plus entire form
factors, equipped with a majorant norm. The theorem statements below use
only completeness and differentiability where declared.

Let
\[
 \Rmap_b:\X\longrightarrow\X
\]
be the time-\(\log b\) step of an autonomous dimensionless FRG flow, for
\(b>1\). Let \(\U\) be an MTT upper-state space and
\[
 \Tmap_b:\U\longrightarrow\U
\]
its selected coarse-graining step. A chart
\(\Cmap:\U\to\X\) must specify how coherent variables become effective
couplings. This chart is not supplied by identifying a coherent projector
with an FRG cutoff.

\subsection{Dimensionful endpoints versus fixed points}

A limit of dimensionful actions \(\Gamma_k\) is not automatically an
asymptotic-safety fixed point. Canonical rescaling must first produce
dimensionless variables and an autonomous flow. This paper reserves
\(\Gamma_\ast\) for a fixed point of \(\Rmap_b\) and
\(\Gamma_\infty\) for a norm limit of a specified curve.

\section{What an integrable UV bound proves}

\begin{theorem}[Banach-space endpoint]
\label{thm:endpoint}
Let \(\Gamma:[k_0,\infty)\to\X\) be locally absolutely continuous. If
\[
 \|\partial_k\Gamma_k\|_{\X}\leq g(k)
 \quad\text{for almost every }k,
 \qquad
 g\in L^1([k_0,\infty)),
\]
then there is a unique \(\Gamma_\infty\in\X\) such that
\[
 \Gamma_k\longrightarrow\Gamma_\infty,
 \qquad
 \|\Gamma_\infty-\Gamma_k\|_{\X}
 \leq\int_k^\infty g(s)\,ds.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(K>k\), absolute continuity gives
\[
 \|\Gamma_K-\Gamma_k\|_{\X}
 \leq\int_k^K\|\partial_s\Gamma_s\|_{\X}\,ds
 \leq\int_k^\infty g(s)\,ds.
\]
The tail tends to zero, so \(\Gamma_k\) is Cauchy. Completeness gives the
limit, and \(K\to\infty\) gives the bound.
\end{proof}

The commonly used Gaussian estimate
\[
 g(k)=Ck^m e^{-c\tau_0k^2},
 \qquad C,c,\tau_0>0,
\]
satisfies the theorem. This proves an endpoint \emph{if the estimate is
available}. It does not prove the estimate from MTT geometry. In particular,
an internal q79 spectral gap does not supply the required suppression of
four-dimensional FRG shells.

\begin{proposition}[When an endpoint is a fixed point]
\label{prop:endpoint-fixed}
Let \(x(t)\) solve the autonomous Banach-space equation
\[
 \dot x=\beta(x)
\]
with locally Lipschitz \(\beta\). If \(x(t)\to x_\ast\) as
\(t\to\infty\), then \(\beta(x_\ast)=0\). Consequently the time-step map
fixes \(x_\ast\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(\beta(x_\ast)\neq0\), Hahn--Banach gives a bounded linear functional
\(\ell\) with \(\ell(\beta(x_\ast))>0\). By continuity,
\(\ell(\beta(x(t)))\) stays bounded below by a positive constant for all
sufficiently large \(t\). Then \(\ell(x(t))\) cannot converge, a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

The proposition explains why dimensionless autonomous variables are
essential. It cannot be applied to a dimensionful endpoint before the
rescaling and limiting flow are established.

\section{Exact and approximate conjugacy}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional MTT--FRG conjugacy]
\label{thm:conjugacy}
Suppose \(\Cmap:\U\to\X\) is injective and continuously differentiable near
\(u_\ast\), and
\begin{equation}
 \Rmap_b\circ\Cmap=\Cmap\circ\Tmap_b
 \label{eq:conjugacy}
\end{equation}
there. If \(\Tmap_bu_\ast=u_\ast\), then
\(\Gamma_\ast=\Cmap(u_\ast)\) is an FRG fixed point. If \(D\Cmap(u_\ast)\)
is a bounded linear isomorphism onto its image, then the restricted
linearizations are similar:
\[
 D\Rmap_b(\Gamma_\ast)|_{\Ran D\Cmap}
 =
 D\Cmap(u_\ast)\,D\Tmap_b(u_\ast)\,
 D\Cmap(u_\ast)^{-1}.
\]
Thus their spectra and algebraic multiplicities agree on that image.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:conjugacy} at \(u_\ast\), then differentiate it and use the
chain rule.
\end{proof}

This theorem is deliberately conditional. Conjugacy is the bridge; it is
not a consequence of having fixed points on both sides.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate conjugacy gives a residual]
\label{prop:approx-conjugacy}
Assume on a set \(V\subset\U\) that
\[
 \|\Rmap_b(\Cmap u)-\Cmap(\Tmap_bu)\|_{\X}\leq\varepsilon
\]
and that \(\Cmap\) is \(L\)-Lipschitz there. Then
\[
 \|\Rmap_b(\Cmap u)-\Cmap u\|_{\X}
 \leq \varepsilon+L\|\Tmap_bu-u\|_{\U}.
\]
In particular, an upper fixed point produces an FRG
\(\varepsilon\)-residual, not necessarily a nearby FRG fixed point.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert and subtract \(\Cmap(\Tmap_bu)\) and use the triangle inequality.
\end{proof}

A nearby fixed point follows only after a separate contraction, degree,
implicit-function, or validated-numerics theorem. This distinction is the
main correction to the earlier bridge formulation.

\section{Scheme variation without overclaiming}

Let \(\Gamma_k\) and \(\Gamma'_k\) be flows generated by two regulator
choices in a common normed action space.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint comparison]
\label{thm:scheme}
Suppose both curves satisfy the hypotheses of \autoref{thm:endpoint} and
\[
 h(k)=\|\partial_k\Gamma'_k-\partial_k\Gamma_k\|_{\X}
 \in L^1([k_0,\infty)).
\]
Then
\[
 \|\Gamma'_\infty-\Gamma_\infty\|_{\X}
 \leq
 \|\Gamma'_{k_0}-\Gamma_{k_0}\|_{\X}
 \int_{k_0}^\infty h(k)\,dk.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Integrate the difference of the two flows and take the norm and the limit.
\end{proof}

This is controlled regulator dependence. It is not scheme independence. A
stronger statement that the endpoints are related by a bounded
reparametrization requires that reparametrization to be constructed and its
remainder bounded. Physical universality additionally concerns observables,
not equality of off-shell effective actions.

\section{Finite relevant directions}

The previous version wrote a linearization as a bounded plus compact
operator and inferred finitely many unstable directions. That inference is
invalid unless the noncompact part has controlled essential spectrum.

\begin{theorem}[Quasi-compact unstable subspace]
\label{thm:quasicompact}
Let \(L:\X\to\X\) be bounded and suppose its essential spectral radius
satisfies
\[
 r_{\mathrm{ess}}(L)<1.
\]
Then the part of \(\spec L\) outside the closed unit disk consists of
finitely many isolated eigenvalues, each of finite algebraic multiplicity.
The associated Riesz projection
\[
 P_u=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma(z\Id-L)^{-1}\,dz
\]
has finite rank, where \(\gamma\) encloses precisely that spectrum.
Therefore the linear unstable subspace \(\Ran P_u\) is finite-dimensional.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the spectral decomposition theorem for quasi-compact operators:
outside every circle of radius strictly larger than
\(r_{\mathrm{ess}}(L)\), the spectrum is a finite set of poles of the
resolvent with finite-rank Riesz projections \cite{Kato}.
\end{proof}

If \(L=S+K\) with \(K\) compact, then
\[
 r_{\mathrm{ess}}(L)=r_{\mathrm{ess}}(S).
\]
Compactness of \(K\) therefore helps only after
\(r_{\mathrm{ess}}(S)<1\) is proved. A nonlinear local unstable manifold
also requires a sufficiently smooth RG map and hyperbolicity; it does not
follow from the linear statement alone.

\section{What SPT damping would have to establish}

The conditional SPT route can now be stated as a finite checklist:

\begin{enumerate}
\item select a four-dimensional, gauge-compatible TT or BV/BRST operator;
\item derive its filter from the same MTT geometry rather than insert it as
a regulator choice;
\item prove the FRG shell estimate in the declared action norm;
\item pass to autonomous dimensionless variables;
\item construct the chart \(\Cmap\) and verify exact conjugacy or a certified
defect;
\item bound the essential spectral radius of the linearized step;
\item compare regulator-independent observables, including errors.
\end{enumerate}

The current corpus supplies useful ingredients but not this complete chain.
The finite q79 Hessian is exact at its declared algebraic tier. The
Euclidean TT/SPT paper gives a conditional filtered model. The constructive
QG series provides finite-volume and finite-cutoff contracts. The
continuum physical q79 HYM operator, four-dimensional source map, and
nonperturbative QFT completion remain open.

\section{Discussion}

This corrected bridge is useful even before it closes. It tells a future
calculation exactly what would count as evidence. A numerically observed FRG
fixed point is evidence about a truncation. An MTT fixed point is evidence
about an upper map. They become the same mathematical result only after a
chart intertwines the maps with controlled domain and error.

The quasi-compactness criterion similarly sharpens ``finite
predictivity.'' Smoothing of one shell contribution does not settle the
essential spectrum of the full RG derivative. Proving
\(r_{\mathrm{ess}}<1\), followed by a Riesz-projection computation, would
give the desired finite-dimensional unstable sector in a form independent
of basis rhetoric.

\section{Conclusion}

MTT and asymptotic safety presently have a rigorous \emph{conditional}
relationship. Integrable FRG flow bounds, exact conjugacy, controlled scheme
variation, and quasi-compact linearization have precise consequences.
None of those hypotheses is supplied merely by the existence of an internal
coherent projector.

The next decisive result is a same-source construction of the
four-dimensional FRG variables and step map from the selected q79 geometry,
together with a verified shell bound and essential-spectrum estimate. Until
then, asymptotic safety is a promising diagnostic corner of MTT, not a
derived equivalence or completed UV theory.
