\author{Peter Nero}

\begin{abstract}
We formulate circle, lens, and nil (CLN) as three coarse, typed profiles of an
encoding atlas rather than as an exhaustive list of all possible descent
obstructions.  Circle records nontrivial return transport around a composable
loop; lens records nonfaithfulness or redundancy of a reduction; nil records
failure of a declared chart or transition to extend.  These predicates concern
different mathematical data, can occur independently or together, and are
invariant under suitably compatible re-encoding.  They do not by themselves
imply a literal circle, lens-space, or nilmanifold factor.  We distinguish
holonomy from curvature by exhibiting a flat connection on $S^1$ with
nontrivial holonomy.  A six-dimensional lower bound follows only in the
restricted realization class where three profiles are represented by
nonzero curvature two-forms on independent transverse coordinate factors:
each factor then has dimension at least two, giving $2+2+2=6$.  If a separate
four-dimensional physical base is supplied and dimensions add, the familiar
$4+6=10$ realization follows; neither the four-dimensional base nor
ten-dimensional necessity is selected by B0.  The shared circle is common
phase or holonomy data and is counted once.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Revision note for version 2}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Revision note for version 2}
\paragraph{Supersedes.}
Version~1 of Program B0.
\paragraph{Reason.}
The former paper claimed an exhaustive, mutually irreducible three-type
classification without fixing a category or higher descent problem.  It also
identified nontrivial holonomy with nonzero curvature and promoted a
four-dimensional input plus a conditional six-dimensional construction to a
forced ten-dimensional conclusion.
\paragraph{Resolution.}
Version~2 replaces the exclusivity theorem by a typed coarse taxonomy, proves
re-encoding invariance only under explicit compatibility, separates flat
holonomy from curvature, and confines the $2+2+2$ lower bound to independent
transverse nonzero-curvature realizations.  Dimension addition and the
four-dimensional base are stated as hypotheses.
\paragraph{Retained content.}
Return memory, descriptive redundancy, and chart termination remain useful
CLN diagnostics, and geometric circle-bundle models remain important examples.
\paragraph{Open boundary.}
No theorem here proves that CLN exhausts higher descent, that a lens space or
nilmanifold is physically selected, that the three roles are globally nested,
that physical time is a compact circle, or that spacetime must be
ten-dimensional.

\section{Scope and Imported Data}

Program A0 supplies typed reductions, representative sections, exact recovery,
autonomous descent, and admissibility domains~\cite{A0}.  Program A1 supplies
chart transitions and encoding trajectories, with physical causality entering
only after a Lorentzian and hyperbolic bridge~\cite{A1}.  Program A2 separates
chart partiality and branch underdetermination from algorithmic
undecidability~\cite{A2}.  B0 classifies three recurring \emph{diagnostics} of
these structures.

The word ``obstruction'' is used in a broad encoding sense.  It does not assert
a specific cohomology class unless a coefficient object, cover, cocycle degree,
and equivalence relation are declared.

\begin{definition}[Typed encoding atlas]\label{def:atlas}
A typed encoding atlas consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item upper domains $U_\alpha\subseteq X$;
\item effective images $Z_\alpha$ and reductions
$P_\alpha:U_\alpha\to Z_\alpha$;
\item declared overlap domains $D_{\beta\alpha}\subseteq Z_\alpha$;
\item partial transition maps or relations
$f_{\beta\alpha}:D_{\beta\alpha}\rightharpoonup Z_\beta$; and
\item equivalence relations $\simeq_\alpha$ specifying when two effective
representatives carry the same declared content.
\end{itemize}
All composites below are restricted to their actual domains.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
A representative section of a surjective reduction may exist even when the
reduction is noninjective.  Redundancy, exact upper recovery, and descent are
therefore separate questions, as proved in Program A0.
\end{remark}

\section{Three Coarse Obstruction Profiles}

\subsection{Circle: return memory}

\begin{definition}[Circle profile]\label{def:circle}
Let
\[
\gamma=(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n=\alpha_0)
\]
be a closed composable chain.  On the common domain of definition, let
\[
h_\gamma
:=
f_{\alpha_0\alpha_{n-1}}\circ\cdots\circ
f_{\alpha_2\alpha_1}\circ f_{\alpha_1\alpha_0}.
\]
The chain has a \emph{circle profile} if $h_\gamma$ is not equivalent to the
identity under the declared chart equivalence.
\end{definition}

Circle therefore means return memory, monodromy, or holonomy.  Whether it is an
inconsistency, a gauge transformation, or a lawful local-system action depends
on the target category.

\subsection{Lens: redundancy}

\begin{definition}[Lens profile]\label{def:lens}
A reduction $P_\alpha:U_\alpha\to Z_\alpha$ has a \emph{lens profile} on a
set $V\subseteq Z_\alpha$ if some $z\in V$ has two distinct upper
representatives,
\[
x\neq x',
\qquad
P_\alpha(x)=P_\alpha(x')=z,
\]
or, more generally, if the declared representation functor is not faithful on
the relevant objects or arrows.
\end{definition}

Lens therefore means quotient, projective, finite-sheet, or other redundancy.
It does not imply that a representative section is absent, that reduced
dynamics fails to descend, or that the global topology is a lens space.

\subsection{Nil: partiality or extension failure}

\begin{definition}[Nil profile]\label{def:nil}
A chart system has a \emph{nil profile} at a boundary datum $z$ if a declared
continuation or transition required by the encoding problem has no value at
$z$, and no extension in the declared atlas satisfies the stated compatibility
conditions.
\end{definition}

Nil therefore means termination or anchoring of the chosen encoding problem.
It does not imply that the upper object or upper evolution ceases to exist.
Nor does it imply a nilpotent Lie algebra without an additional triangular
realization.

\section{Independence, Coexistence, and Invariance}

\begin{proposition}[Logical independence]\label{prop:independence}
None of the predicates in Definitions~\ref{def:circle}--\ref{def:nil}
implies either of the other two without additional hypotheses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A flat complex line over $S^1$ with nontrivial monodromy has a circle profile
but can use injective local state descriptions and a globally defined
transport, so lens and nil need not occur.  The set map
$\{a,b\}\to\{\ast\}$ has a lens profile but no nontrivial loop or extension
boundary.  A partial identity map on $[0,1)$ that is not extended at $1$ has
a nil profile but is injective and has no loop.  These examples separate the
three predicates.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Coexistence]
The profiles are not mutually exclusive.  A partial many-to-one chart system
may also carry nontrivial return transport.  A primary CLN label can be
assigned by a diagnostic decision tree, but the ordering of that tree is a
convention, not a theorem of irreducibility.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[First-order diagnostic completeness]
\label{prop:firstorder}
Fix a finite chart query and test only:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item existence of every required transition;
\item uniqueness or faithfulness of the required representation; and
\item identity of every required closed-chain return.
\end{enumerate}
If a test fails, it is diagnosed respectively by nil, lens, or circle after
any earlier failures have been recorded.  If no test fails, this diagnostic
detects no CLN profile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the exhaustive decision tree for the three declared predicates.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No universal exhaustiveness]
Proposition~\ref{prop:firstorder} is not a classification of all mathematical
obstructions.  Higher Cech or gerbe descent, nonabelian coherence, analytic
blow-up, spectral-domain failure, non-Hausdorff quotients, and other
realization-specific phenomena require their own objects and equivalences.
An exhaustive theorem would have to specify a category or higher category of
encodings, its morphisms and refinements, and the full descent problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Compatible re-encoding]
Two typed atlases are compatibly re-encoded if there are chartwise
isomorphisms $g_\alpha:Z_\alpha\to Z'_\alpha$ and corresponding upper
isomorphisms that preserve reductions, equivalence relations, transition
domains, and transition maps:
\[
g_\beta\circ f_{\beta\alpha}
=
f'_{\beta\alpha}\circ g_\alpha
\]
where both sides are defined.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Profile invariance under compatible re-encoding]
\label{thm:invariance}
Under a compatible re-encoding:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a return map changes by conjugation,
$h'_\gamma=g_{\alpha_0}h_\gamma g_{\alpha_0}^{-1}$;
\item reduction-fiber multiplicity and faithfulness are preserved; and
\item the existence or nonexistence of a compatible transition extension is
preserved.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the three profile predicates are invariant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The transition intertwining identities telescope around a closed chain and
give the conjugation formula.  Bijective upper and lower re-encodings preserve
equality and inequality within reduction fibers.  The same commuting squares
transport an extension in either direction, so extension existence is
preserved.
\end{proof}

\section{Holonomy Is Not Curvature}

Let $\theta$ be the angular coordinate on $S^1=\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z$ and
consider the trivial complex line with connection
\[
\nabla=d+\mathrm i a\,d\theta,
\qquad a\in\mathbb R.
\]
Its curvature is
\[
F_\nabla=d(\mathrm i a\,d\theta)=0,
\]
but its holonomy around the positively oriented circle is
\[
\operatorname{Hol}_{S^1}(\nabla)
=
\exp(-2\pi\mathrm i a),
\]
which is nontrivial when $a\notin\mathbb Z$.

\begin{theorem}[Flat circle profile]\label{thm:flat}
A one-dimensional carrier can support nontrivial return holonomy with zero
curvature.  Therefore nontrivial circle-profile data does not imply a
nonzero curvature two-form or a two-dimensional carrier.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The connection above is flat and has the displayed nontrivial holonomy.
\end{proof}

More generally, flat bundles encode representations of the fundamental group,
while curvature controls infinitesimal holonomy~\cite{KobayashiNomizu}.  Global
monodromy can therefore survive when curvature vanishes identically.

\begin{corollary}[When two dimensions are required]
If a realization explicitly requires a nonzero differential two-form
$F\in\Omega^2(B)$, then $\dim B\geq2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The exterior square of a vector space of dimension zero or one is zero.
\end{proof}

This corollary applies to a \emph{nonzero-curvature realization class}; it does
not apply to the general circle profile of Definition~\ref{def:circle}.

\section{Lens and Nil as Geometric Examples, Not Consequences}

The names lens and nil have useful geometric models.

\subsection{Lens-space model}

The lens space $L(p,1)$ is the quotient $S^3/\mathbb Z_p$.  Up to orientation,
it is also the unit-circle bundle of a complex line bundle over
$S^2\cong\mathbb{CP}^1$ with first Chern number $p$~\cite{BoothbyWang}.
Its finite quotient and
projective structure make it a natural \emph{example} of lens-profile
bookkeeping.  A many-to-one reduction does not, however, select $p$, a Chern
class, or a lens-space total space.

\subsection{Nilmanifold model}

Let $H_3(\mathbb R)$ be the upper-unitriangular Heisenberg group,
\[
n(x,y,z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its multiplication contains the triangular cross-term
\[
n(x,y,z)n(x',y',z')
=
n(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy').
\]
Quotient by the integer lattice gives the compact Heisenberg nilmanifold
$\mathrm{Nil}_3$, a circle bundle over $T^2$~\cite{Thurston}.  This is a
natural model of
triangular transport or anchoring.  Chart termination alone does not select
this group, its lattice, or its metric.

\begin{remark}[Parallel circle bundles]
$L(p,1)$ and $\mathrm{Nil}_3$ are circle-bundle total spaces over different
bases and with different topology.  They are not literally nested.  A string
such as
\[
S^1\subset L(p,1)\subset\mathrm{Nil}_3
\]
is therefore not a general manifold construction.  The lawful recursive
object is a filtration of carrier or operator data, with any global
circle-bundle realizations supplied separately.
\end{remark}

\section{The Conditional 2+2+2 Curvature Realization}

\begin{definition}[Independent transverse curvature realization]
\label{def:curvatureclass}
An independent transverse three-profile curvature realization consists of a
smooth internal base $B$, subbundles
$H_C,H_L,H_N\subseteq TB$, three declared line or principal bundles with
connections $\nabla_C,\nabla_L,\nabla_N$, and their curvature two-forms
$F_C,F_L,F_N$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item $H_C\oplus H_L\oplus H_N$ injects as a direct subbundle of $TB$;
\item $F_i|_{\Lambda^2H_i}$ is nonzero somewhere for
$i\in\{C,L,N\}$; and
\item the three factors are independent coordinate channels rather than
three labels on one reused tangent plane.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Minimal nonzero-curvature realization]
\label{thm:six}
Every realization satisfying Definition~\ref{def:curvatureclass} obeys
\[
\dim B
\geq
\operatorname{rank}H_C+
\operatorname{rank}H_L+
\operatorname{rank}H_N
\geq 2+2+2=6.
\]
The lower bound is sharp within this realization class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A nonzero alternating two-form on $H_i$ requires
$\operatorname{rank}H_i\geq2$.  Direct-sum transversality adds the three
ranks, proving the lower bound.  For sharpness, take
$B=S^2_C\times S^2_L\times S^2_N$.  On each factor use the Hopf circle
bundle with its standard connection, and let $F_i$ be the pullback of its
nonzero curvature form to $B$.  Then $\dim B=6$ and all three conditions
hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not say]
The theorem does not show that every CLN profile needs curvature, that the
three channels must be transverse in nature, or that the realizing
six-manifold is unique.  Without (R1)--(R3), dimensions need not add.  Reused,
overlapping, quotient, or discrete data can occupy fewer independent
coordinate directions.
\end{remark}

\section{Dimension Bookkeeping and the 4+6 Branch}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional dimension additivity]\label{prop:additivity}
If a physical realization is supplied as a product
\[
M=Y\times X
\]
of smooth manifolds, or as a smooth fiber bundle with base $Y$ and fiber $X$,
then
\[
\dim M=\dim Y+\dim X.
\]
If $\dim Y=4$ and $\dim X=6$, then $\dim M=10$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a product, $T_{(y,x)}M\cong T_yY\oplus T_xX$.  For a smooth fiber bundle,
local trivializations give the same dimension sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The four-dimensional base is input]
B0 does not select $\dim Y=4$, Lorentzian signature, three spatial
directions, or a physical time variable.  These are supplied by a later
physical realization or selection theorem.  Consequently B0 proves no
unconditional ten-dimensional necessity.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The world-in-world component count}

Let $P$ and $I$ have rank-three tangent spaces and let
\[
Q_x\in\operatorname{Hom}(T_xP,T_xI)\cong\operatorname{Mat}(3,\mathbb R).
\]
The number $3\times3=9$ counts components of $Q_x$, not dimensions of a
manifold product.  With inner products and positive determinant, polar
decomposition gives locally
\[
\operatorname{Mat}(3,\mathbb R)
=
\mathfrak{so}(3)\oplus\operatorname{Sym}(3,\mathbb R),
\qquad
9=3+6.
\]
Further,
\[
\operatorname{Sym}(3,\mathbb R)
=
\mathbb RI_3\oplus\mathcal D_0\oplus\mathcal O,
\qquad
6=1+2+3.
\]
Thus the component census
\[
1+3\times3=(1+3)+(1+2+3)=4+6
\]
is exact as local linear algebra after the ordering component is supplied.
It is not, by itself, a globalization to $Y^4\times X^6$.

\subsection{The shared circle}

A common $U(1)$ phase or holonomy line may be pulled through all three carrier
lanes.  If it is the same line, it is counted once.  Treating that line as a
bundle fiber and then adding another Cartesian circle double-counts it.
Physical time is not identified with the compact phase circle without a
separate noncompact lift and dynamical bridge.

\section{Current MTT Interpretation}

The corrected structural reading is:
\begin{description}
\item[Circle.] Shared phase, return memory, or holonomy.
\item[Lens.] Finite, projective, signed-sheet, or quotient transport.
\item[Nil.] Triangular transport, anchoring, or termination in a selected
operator filtration.
\end{description}

This taxonomy can be represented by a rank pattern such as $1+2+3$ without
asserting three nested manifolds.  In the current q79 program, the trace line,
trace-zero plane, and reused rank-three carrier are a later selected carrier
construction.  Equality of ranks does not identify that carrier with the local
world-in-world strain space; a same-source connection and Hessian intertwiner
is still required.

The smooth model $L(3,1)\times\mathrm{Nil}_3$ remains a lawful auxiliary
six-manifold.  It is not thereby the selected q79 Fu--Yau compactification.
B0 supplies no theorem identifying those global spaces.

\section{Consequences for Downstream Papers}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A gravity paper may use circle-profile holonomy only after it supplies a
connection, metric, and field equation.  Holonomy alone is not Einstein
gravity.
\item A gauge paper may use lens-profile redundancy only after it supplies a
group action, principal or associated bundle, connection, and dynamics.
\item A quantization paper may use nil-profile termination only after it
supplies an algebra, representation, spectral or integral condition, and
physical observable map.
\item A proto-spinor paper may use CLN as typed carrier roles.  Spinorial,
particle, mass, and statistics claims need their separate structures.
\item A ten-dimensional action may use $Y^4\times X^6$ as a declared
realization.  B0 does not derive that action or its base dimension.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scoped B0 Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Circle--lens--nil taxonomy with conditional curvature bound]
\label{thm:scope}
For a typed encoding atlas:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Definitions~\ref{def:circle}--\ref{def:nil} give three distinct coarse
profiles: return memory, reduction redundancy, and extension failure;
\item the profiles are logically independent, may coexist, and are invariant
under compatible re-encoding;
\item the three-predicate decision tree is exhaustive only for its declared
first-order diagnostic, not for arbitrary higher descent;
\item nontrivial holonomy can be flat and one-dimensional;
\item if all three profiles are separately represented by nonzero curvature
two-forms on independent transverse factors, the internal carrier has
dimension at least six, and $2+2+2=6$ is sharp in that class; and
\item a ten-dimensional $4+6$ realization follows only after a separate
four-dimensional base and dimension-additive product or fiber-bundle structure
are supplied.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items 1--3 are Definitions~\ref{def:circle}--\ref{def:nil},
Propositions~\ref{prop:independence}--\ref{prop:firstorder}, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance}.  Item~4 is Theorem~\ref{thm:flat}.  Item~5 is
Theorem~\ref{thm:six}.  Item~6 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:additivity} and its stated hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\section{Version Delta and Research Frontier}

Relative to version~1, this revision:
\begin{itemize}
\item replaces ``exactly three and only three'' by a nonexhaustive typed
taxonomy plus an explicitly bounded first-order decision tree;
\item withdraws mutual irreducibility and tri-layer necessity as universal
theorems;
\item distinguishes flat monodromy from curvature and gives an explicit flat
$S^1$ example;
\item makes dimension additivity depend on independent transverse coordinate
factors;
\item retains $2+2+2=6$ only as the sharp minimum of the declared
nonzero-curvature realization class;
\item states that the four-dimensional base is imported, not selected;
\item removes unconditional ten-dimensional necessity; and
\item separates CLN carrier roles from literal lens-space, nilmanifold, product,
or nesting claims.
\end{itemize}

The next theorem target is not another dimension count.  It is a same-source
global intertwiner from the local $1+2+3$ strain decomposition to the selected
q79 carrier, preserving transitions, the shared line, connections, inner
products, Hessians, and the operators used downstream.

\section{Conclusion}

Circle, lens, and nil remain useful because they ask three different questions:
what returns after a loop, how many representatives survive a reduction, and
where a chosen encoding stops.  Their usefulness does not require an
unsupported exhaustiveness theorem.

The dimensional conclusion is likewise precise.  Three independent
nonzero-curvature channels require at least six transverse dimensions, and
this bound is attained.  Flat return memory can require less.  Ten dimensions
arise when a four-dimensional physical base is separately supplied and paired
with a six-dimensional internal realization.  This scoped result is strong
enough to organize the later MTT program without treating taxonomy as topology
or an input base as a derived theorem.
