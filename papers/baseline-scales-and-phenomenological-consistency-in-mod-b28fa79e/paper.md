# Role and claim status

This paper is a ledger, not a phenomenological fit. It introduces no new physical dynamics and assigns no numerical values. It distinguishes:

Structural condition.  
A mathematical gate such as a spectral or semigroup bound.

Physical bridge.  
A theorem connecting an abstract operator to a term in a selected physical action.

Calibration.  
A parameter value inferred from measured input.

Consistency witness.  
One common parameter point satisfying declared constraints.

Held-out prediction.  
An observable not used in construction, calibration, scale setting, or branch selection.

The absence of an identified contradiction is not evidence that a common parameter point exists. Conversely, a freely adjustable parameter choice is a consistency witness, not a prediction.

# Units and normalization

Every scale must be accompanied by units. In natural units, a Laplace-type operator has mass dimension two only after a physical length normalization has been selected. If an internal operator is initially dimensionless, write
``` math
A_{\rm int}^{\rm phys}=M_{\rm int}^2\widehat A_{\rm int}
```
with dimensionless $`\widehat A_{\rm int}`$ and a separately sourced conversion scale $`M_{\rm int}`$. Quoting an eigenvalue without this normalization does not define a particle mass or energy cutoff.

Rates generated by the stabilization parameter $`\tau`$ have units of $`\tau^{-1}`$. They are not physical decay widths or inverse seconds unless a bridge between $`\tau`$ and physical time $`t`$ is supplied.

# Structural scale registry

## Internal spectral gap

Let
``` math
A_{\rm int}\succeq\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}Q
```
on the noncoherent vertical sector. The quantity $`\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}`$ is an internal spectral separation. With a mass-squared normalization, $`m_\ast^{\rm int}=\sqrt{\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}}`$ is the corresponding internal mode scale.

The internal gap supports a vertical resolvent estimate and, under the generator bridge of the Foundation, complementary stabilization. It does not by itself:

- define a four-dimensional momentum cutoff;

- damp modes merely because their base energy is high;

- remove every light field from a physical spectrum;

- determine a coupling to visible matter; or

- fix a Planck, cosmological, or RG scale.

## Projector and mixing bounds

Record the Sobolev norms
``` math
C_P(s)=\|P\|_{H^s\to H^s}
```
and the typed mixing blocks $`L_{PQ},L_{QP}`$. Under the domain hypotheses of the Foundation Schur–Feshbach theorem,
``` math
\|L_{PQ}L_{QQ}^{-1}L_{QP}\|
 \le
 \|L_{PQ}L_{QQ}^{-1}\|\,\|L_{QP}\|.
```
Only after proving an inverse bound in terms of the internal gap or a stable semigroup may this be rewritten as an inverse-gap estimate. The norms, domains, and units of all three factors must be stated.

## Complementary stabilization rate

The noncoherent linearized generator is controlled by
``` math
\|e^{\tau L_{QQ}}\|\le M_Qe^{-\omega_Q\tau}.
```
Here $`M_Q`$ captures nonnormal transient amplification and $`\omega_Q`$ is a stabilization rate. In the special bounded-perturbation model
``` math
L_{QQ}=-\kappa A_{\rm int}+B_Q,
```
one may take $`\omega_Q=\kappa\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}-\|B_Q\|>0`$. This relation is conditional on the displayed generator and its invariant domain.

## Coherent contraction scale

For a time-step map on an invariant basin,
``` math
\|T_\tau u-T_\tau v\|\le q_{\rm coh}\|u-v\|,
 \qquad0\le q_{\rm coh}<1.
```
The dimensionless factor $`q_{\rm coh}`$, or a rate $`\mu_{\rm coh}=-\tau^{-1}\log q_{\rm coh}`$, controls basin-local convergence. It is independent of $`\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}`$ unless a realization proves a relation.

## Leakage and curved-cluster margins

Curvature or parameter variation introduces at least two different scales: the spectral perturbation norm $`\|\mathcal R\|`$ and the off-diagonal leakage $`\|Q\mathcal RP\|`$. FP IV controls cluster persistence and the old-projector leakage floor under explicit smallness and damping margins. These quantities are not automatically physical spacetime-curvature corrections to Einstein’s equations.

# Physical scales requiring a completion

## Four-dimensional EFT cutoff

Denote the cutoff of a selected four-dimensional effective action by $`\Lambda_{4D}`$. It is the scale at which that action loses controlled accuracy because of heavy thresholds, strong coupling, higher derivatives, nonlocality, unitarity limits, or omitted states. It is not definitionally equal to $`m_\ast^{\rm int}`$.

At most, a proved physical reduction may yield a relation such as
``` math
\Lambda_{4D}\le
 \min\{m_{\rm heavy},\Lambda_{\rm strong},
             \Lambda_{\rm derivative},\Lambda_{\rm nonlocal}\}.
```
Which entry is limiting is model dependent.

m_k^2=m_0^2+\lambda_k.

This familiar bridge is not supplied by abstract projection alone. Warping, mixing, boundary conditions, interactions, and normalization can change the mass matrix.

## External high-energy damping

Suppression of large four-dimensional momentum requires an external base operator, form factor, dissipative physical kernel, or EFT truncation theorem. A vertical gap distinguishes internal eigenmodes but acts on every base momentum carried by a fixed internal mode. Therefore the statement “high four-dimensional energy is exponentially damped by the internal gap” is not available without an external damping construction.

## Planck and curvature scales

The Planck scale $`M_{\rm Pl}`$ is the coefficient of the selected four-dimensional gravitational action. In a particular higher-dimensional Einstein–Hilbert compactification one may derive a volume relation such as
``` math
M_{\rm Pl}^2=M_{10}^{8}V_6
```
up to conventions, warping, and corrections. The abstract MTT internal gap does not imply this formula or fix any factor in it.

Higher-curvature scales $`\Lambda_{R^2},\Lambda_{R^3},\ldots`$ are coefficients in a physical derivative expansion. A schematic expression like $`\varepsilon_{\rm warp}/\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}`$ is dimensional analysis only until the physical action and matching calculation are supplied.

## Renormalization scale

The RG scale $`\mu_{\rm RG}`$ labels a family of renormalized descriptions. It is not stabilization time, the internal eigenvalue, or the EFT cutoff, though a matching calculation may compare them at a threshold.

# Observable-response requirements

An experimental constraint applies to an observable response, not directly to an abstract spectral gap. The following rows illustrate the missing bridges.

## New particles and fifth forces

A fifth-force test constrains both the physical mediator mass and its coupling to matter. A Yukawa correction has schematic form
``` math
V(r)=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r}
 \left[1+\alpha_5e^{-m_5r}\right].
```
Even if Theorem <a href="#thm:mass-bridge" data-reference-type="ref" data-reference="thm:mass-bridge">1</a> identifies $`m_5`$ with an internal eigenvalue, the strength $`\alpha_5`$ requires a coupling and matter-charge calculation. A large internal gap alone is not a fifth-force certificate.

## Lorentz and equivalence-principle tests

Lorentz violation is constrained through coefficients in the physical principal symbol and interactions. Equivalence-principle violation is constrained through species-dependent couplings and trajectories. Neither is bounded by $`\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}`$ without an explicit response map.

## Gravitational waves

Gravitational-wave observations constrain quantities such as propagation speed, damping, dispersion, polarization, and source generation in a selected gravitational model. These depend on the base principal symbol and physical action. The internal gap may enter after matching, but it is not itself a GW observable.

## General relativity and cosmology

Tests of GR constrain post-Newtonian parameters, lensing, binary dynamics, wave generation, and other sourced observables. Cosmology constrains a background solution and perturbations, including expansion, growth, and primordial data. Conditions such as $`\lambda_\ast^{\rm int}\gg H_0^2`$ are dimensionally meaningful only after normalization and are not sufficient to establish agreement with these data.

# Formal consistency set

Let $`\Theta`$ be a declared parameter and branch space, and let $`\Theta_{\rm struct}\subseteq\Theta`$ be the subset satisfying every structural gate. For each empirical row $`a`$, specify:

- a response map $`F_a:\Theta_a\to\mathcal O_a`$ derived from the same physical completion;

- a domain condition $`\Theta_a\subseteq\Theta`$;

- a theory-error set $`E_a(\theta)`$;

- an experimental acceptance set $`I_a`$; and

- provenance showing which data selected $`\theta`$, the branch, and the form of $`F_a`$.

Define
``` math
\mathcal C
 =\Theta_{\rm struct}
 \cap\bigcap_a
 \{\theta\in\Theta_a:
       F_a(\theta)+E_a(\theta)\text{ intersects }I_a\}.
```

<div id="prop:consistency" class="proposition">

**Proposition 2** (Consistency-witness criterion). *The declared model is jointly consistent with the listed rows exactly when $`\mathcal C`$ is nonempty. An exhibited $`\theta_\ast\in\mathcal C`$ is a consistency witness. It is a held-out prediction only for rows whose data did not enter the model, calibration, scale choice, branch choice, or uncertainty construction.*

</div>

This proposition is a bookkeeping identity, but it prevents a serious logical error: satisfying different bounds with different unlinked parameter choices does not exhibit one viable model.

# Fine tuning, parameter count, and falsifiability

The statement “no fine tuning is required” needs a declared measure, prior, sensitivity, or naturalness criterion on $`\Theta`$. Possible diagnostics include the local response Jacobian, condition numbers, or the fraction of a declared prior volume contained in $`\mathcal C`$. No such measure is selected here.

The ledger must classify every adjustable quantity as:

1.  structurally derived;

2.  independently measured input;

3.  calibrated parameter;

4.  nuisance/theory-error parameter; or

5.  discrete branch or model choice.

Parameter reduction can be claimed only when source theorems reduce this list before empirical replay.

The framework is falsified for a specified completion if $`\mathcal C=\varnothing`$, a required operator/domain gate fails, or a held-out observable lies outside its acceptance set after declared errors.

# Current status of the ledger

The corrected Foundation and Fixed Points papers provide several structural rows: internal gap, semigroup damping, contraction, leakage, truncation, and admissibility conditions. The signature paper provides the principal-symbol compatibility ledger. They do not provide one selected physical action and a common set of response maps covering particle, fifth-force, Lorentz, GR, GW, and cosmological observables.

Accordingly, this paper does not establish that current empirical bounds are simultaneously satisfied. It also identifies no contradiction. The phenomenological status is *not evaluated by this ledger* until the physical maps and a common consistency witness are supplied.

# Downstream citation rules

Downstream papers may cite this work for:

- the distinction among internal, control, EFT, curvature, Planck, and RG scales;

- the conditional internal-mass bridge;

- the consistency-set protocol; and

- the required parameter and provenance classifications.

They may not cite it as proof that MTT satisfies experimental constraints, has no fine tuning, predicts a scale, suppresses all UV physics, or reproduces GR or cosmology.

# Conclusion

MTT contains several useful but logically distinct scales. The internal gap can control vertical truncation and, in a specified product action, contribute to four-dimensional masses. It is not a universal cutoff or empirical shield. Phenomenological consistency begins only after one physical completion emits observable-response maps and one common parameter point survives all sourced constraints. This ledger now states that task without prejudging its outcome.
