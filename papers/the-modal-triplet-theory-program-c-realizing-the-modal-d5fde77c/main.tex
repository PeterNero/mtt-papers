The resulting conclusion is deliberately typed: realizations can establish

\section{How to Use Program C}

Program C is a translation manual.  It should be used whenever an abstract MTT
role is assigned a geometric or operator-theoretic realization.  The first
question is not whether the proposed object has the right informal shape, but
whether it has the right mathematical type and whether every required map is
defined.

\subsection{A concrete modal lane}

Take a compact internal space $X_6$, a Hermitian vector bundle
$\Ebundle\to X_6$, and a compatible connection $\nabla$.  The connection
Laplacian
\[
 A=\nabla^*\nabla
\]
is an operator on sections of $\Ebundle$, once its domain is declared.  If an
isolated spectral cluster lies in an interval $I$, functional calculus defines
the projector
\[
 P=\mathbf 1_I(A).
\]
These three objects play different roles:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Ebundle$ says what kind of internal field is carried;
\item $A$ tests or evolves sections according to geometric data; and
\item $P$ selects the modes satisfying the declared spectral criterion.
\end{itemize}
Calling all three ``the lens lane'' would hide the composition laws needed to
combine them.  The typed notation $(\Ebundle,A,P)$ keeps the carrier, test, and
selection operation visible.

\subsection{The realization pipeline}

A complete use of the dictionary proceeds in five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose the physical base and internal coordinate space.
\item Specify the bundles, transition functions, metrics, and connections.
\item Define closed operators on stated domains and prove the spectral
properties used by the projectors.
\item Construct the maps that compare local MTT decompositions with the
selected global carrier, preserving the structures actually used.
\item Add source selection, dynamics, states, observables, and empirical
conventions before making a physical prediction.
\end{enumerate}
The first three steps give a mathematical representation.  The fourth gives a
same-object or same-source bridge.  Only the fifth can turn that bridge into a
physical completion.

\section{Scope and Logical Status}

Program C begins after the typed structural and intersection results of
Programs A0--B5.  Its task is not to prove that a familiar physical theory is
inevitable.  Its task is to answer a narrower question:
\begin{quote}
What mathematical data must be supplied for an MTT constraint pattern to be
realized by a spacetime, an internal geometry, bundles, line bundles,
operators, and selected spectral sectors?
\end{quote}

A \emph{realization} in this paper means a typed map from declared abstract
data into a mathematical model that preserves the specified relations.
Calling two objects ``circle-like,'' ``lens-like,'' or ``nil-like'' is not
such a map.  A realization certificate must identify domains, codomains,
equivalences, connections, equations, boundary conditions, and any source
data used in its construction.

\subsection{Five statuses that must remain separate}

We use the following hierarchy.
\begin{description}
\item[Representation.]
Abstract data are mapped into mathematical objects with the required types.
\item[Conditional reconstruction.]
A known formalism is recovered after its additional hypotheses are supplied.
\item[Selected source realization.]
One candidate is selected by a declared source law independent of the target
observable.
\item[Physical completion.]
The selected realization has a state space, dynamics, observables,
probability rule where required, and a convention map.
\item[Empirical adequacy.]
Held-out observables agree within a declared uncertainty model.
\end{description}

No status follows merely from the one above it.  In particular, an elegant
representation is not yet a source theorem, and a source theorem is not yet a
quantum or phenomenological completion.

\[

If $X_6\simeq F_1\times F_2\times F_3$, then
\[
 \dim F_1+\dim F_2+\dim F_3=6.

If the factors have equal dimension, each has dimension two.

statement follows from $3\dim F_i=6$.

\begin{proposition}[Type noninterchangeability]\label{prop:types}
A coordinate factor, a vector bundle, a line bundle, an unbounded operator,
and a spectral projector cannot be identified solely because they are
assigned the same modal label.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A coordinate factor is an object in a manifold category.  A vector or line

Unqualified notation such as $B_i$ is deprecated when it could mean a base,

intertwiner; it is not evidence that transition functions, connections, or

\subsection{Joint spectral selection}

\begin{assumption}[Strong commutation]\label{ass:strong}
After transport to one Hilbert space, the spectral measures of
$A_1,A_2,A_3$ commute.  Equivalently, the operators admit a joint functional
calculus.  Their quadratic forms have a common dense domain.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Joint coherent projector]\label{thm:joint}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:strong},
\[
 \Pcoh=P_1P_2P_3
\]
is an orthogonal projector independent of the order of the factors, and
\[
 \operatorname{Ran}\Pcoh
 =
 \bigcap_{i=1}^3\operatorname{Ran}P_i.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Strong commutation makes the spectral projectors pairwise commuting.
The product of finitely many commuting orthogonal projectors is an orthogonal
projector.  Its range is their range intersection.
\end{proof}

The theorem says that three tests can be imposed simultaneously without an
order ambiguity.  It does not say that the three tests are independent or
that their common range is nonzero.  Both properties must be checked in the
selected realization.  This is why strong commutation is a compatibility
certificate rather than a physical selection rule.

\begin{corollary}[Single total operator]\label{cor:sum}
Suppose the nonnegative quadratic-form sum
$A_{\mathrm{int}}=A_1+A_2+A_3$ is closed.  Then
\[
 \ker A_{\mathrm{int}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^3\ker A_i.
\]
Thus the zero spectral projector of $A_{\mathrm{int}}$ may be used as
$\Pcoh$ without separately multiplying the $P_i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $u$ in the common form domain,
\[
 \langle u,A_{\mathrm{int}}u\rangle
 =
 \sum_i\langle u,A_i u\rangle.
\]
All summands are nonnegative, so the sum vanishes exactly when every summand
vanishes.
\end{proof}

\subsection{When discreteness is valid}

\begin{theorem}[Compact-resolvent discreteness]\label{thm:compact}
Let $A$ be self-adjoint, bounded below, and have compact resolvent on a
separable Hilbert space.  Then its spectrum consists of eigenvalues of finite
multiplicity with no finite accumulation point, apart from the standard
possibility of accumulation at infinity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $z$ in the resolvent set, $(A-z)^{-1}$ is compact and normal.  The spectral
theorem for compact normal operators gives a discrete nonzero spectrum with
finite-dimensional eigenspaces.  Applying
$\lambda\mapsto(\lambda-z)^{-1}$ transfers this statement to $A$.
\end{proof}

The compact-resolvent hypothesis is essential.  Multiplication by $x$ on
$L^2([0,1])$ is bounded and self-adjoint with spectrum $[0,1]$.  It is an
operator realization with continuous spectrum.  Therefore the version~1
claim that every admissible observable has purely discrete spectrum is
withdrawn.

\begin{proposition}[Typed connection comparison]

 U\circ\nabla=\nabla'\circ U

\section{Realization Contracts and Physical Selection}

\begin{definition}[Realization contract]\label{def:contract}
A realization contract is a tuple
\[
 \mathfrak R=
 \left(
 \mathcal S,\mathcal C,\sim,\Phi,
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{eq}},
 \mathcal B_{\partial},
 \mathcal D_{\mathrm{dyn}},
 \mathcal O_{\mathrm{obs}}
 \right),
\]
where $\mathcal S$ is the typed abstract source, $\mathcal C$ is the candidate
category, $\sim$ is the declared equivalence, $\Phi$ is the realization map,
$\mathcal E_{\mathrm{eq}}$ is the equation inventory,
$\mathcal B_{\partial}$ is the boundary/domain inventory,
$\mathcal D_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ is the dynamics, and
$\mathcal O_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the observable map.
\end{definition}

A purely mathematical realization may omit the final two rows, but then it
must not be called a physical completion.

\begin{theorem}[Nonuniqueness limits prediction]\label{thm:nonunique}
Suppose two inequivalent realizations $R_1,R_2\in\mathcal C/{\sim}$ satisfy
the same structural source data and all declared mathematical equations, but
an observable has different values,
\[
 \mathcal O(R_1)\ne\mathcal O(R_2).
\]
Then the structural source data do not predict $\mathcal O$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both values are compatible with all premises.  If the premises determined a
unique value, the two values would have to agree.  A selector, probability
measure on realizations, or stronger source law is therefore required.
\end{proof}

This theorem states the principal limitation of the C-layer.  Demonstrating
many compatible realizations is useful for consistency and model discovery,
but increases, rather than removes, underdetermination unless an independent
selection law is supplied.

\begin{enumerate}
\item source objects and source hashes;

\item base, fibers, bundles, ranks, and transition functions;
\item connections, domains, operator closures, and spectral assumptions;
\item equations, boundary conditions, and anomaly inventory;

\end{enumerate}
Missing rows define the theorem boundary; they are not filled by analogy.
