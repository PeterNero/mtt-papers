\author{Peter Nero}

\begin{abstract}
We present a complete, first-principles and fully rigorous derivation of indivisible stochastic processes from the Modal Triplet Theory (MTT) framework. Working on the 10-dimensional modal manifold
\[
M_{10} = Y_4 \times B_1 \times B_2 \times B_3,
\]
we construct a probability measure on the path space $Q^{\N}$ of external configurations and prove that the induced discrete-time dynamics are non-Markovian and indivisible in a precise, measure-theoretic sense. The derivation uses only MTT's structural ingredients: (i) the joint harmonic projector $\Pi$ onto the coherent sector, (ii) the curvature-driven modal flow $\Phi_\tau$, and (iii) the observable pushforward $P = I \circ \Pi$ integrating out internal manifolds. We formulate hypotheses (spectral gaps, stability, regularity) identical in spirit to those used in the MTT$\Rightarrow$QM derivation and show that (a) conditional transition kernels $K_{\Delta \tau}[q'|q_0,\ldots,q_k]$ exist and are normalised, (b) the induced process is indivisible because conditioning depends irreducibly on the complete past, and (c) probabilities arise from exponential modal-action weights $\exp(-\Delta A/\hbar)$, yielding a consistent, noncontextual measure on histories. A reconstruction theorem then proves surjectivity: any admissible indivisible kernel (within an explicit class) is realised by suitable MTT boundary data. Measurement is identified with conditioning under constraint changes $F\to F'$, which rigorously corresponds to pruning of histories with renormalisation. Our results establish MTT as a common origin for both Hilbert-space quantum mechanics and its stochastic dual, with indivisibility and interference emerging as consequences of MTT's fixed-point modal geometry.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{Modal Triplet Theory (MTT).}
MTT posits a 10-dimensional product manifold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M10}
M_{10} = Y_4 \times B_1 \times B_2 \times B_3,
\end{equation}
where $Y_4$ is a smooth Lorentzian spacetime $(M_4,g^{(4)})$ and each $B_n$ is a compact oriented Riemannian $3$-manifold $(B_n,h^{(n)})$ with spin structure. The total metric factorises (possibly up to small warpings) as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric}
g^{(10)} = g^{(4)} \oplus h^{(1)} \oplus h^{(2)} \oplus h^{(3)}.
\end{equation}
MTT dynamics are encoded in a curvature-reducing semi-flow $\Phi_\tau$ on the configuration space of fields over $M_{10}$, equipped with a joint harmonic projector
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pi}
\Pi := \Pi_{B_1}\otimes \Pi_{B_2}\otimes \Pi_{B_3},
\end{equation}
mapping to the \emph{coherent sector}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hcoh}
H_{\mathrm{coh}} := \mathrm{Im}(\Pi) = \bigcap_{n=1}^3 \ker \Delta_{B_n}.
\end{equation}
Integration over the compact internal spaces defines the observable pushforward $P=I\circ \Pi$, which yields effective $Y_4$-fields from full modal data.

\paragraph{From MTT to QM.}
A companion derivation (\emph{MTT$\Rightarrow$QM}) constructs a Hilbert space $H_{\mathrm{obs}}$, a reduced self-adjoint Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{obs}}$, and unitary time evolution $U(t)=e^{-itH_{\mathrm{obs}}/\hbar}$ for the projected fields. Probabilities are obtained from modal re-coherence via noncontextual measures on projections, invoking Gleason--Busch to recover the Born rule; generalised measurements arise from modal Stinespring/Naimark dilation.

\paragraph{Aim of this work (MTT$\Rightarrow$Indivisible Processes).}
In the present paper, we develop the \emph{stochastic} face of the same reduction. We show that the modal flow $\Phi_\tau$ and projection $P$ together induce, on an external configuration space $Q\subset Y_4$ (particle positions, coarse field amplitudes, etc.), a discrete-time probability law on the path space $Q^{\N}$ whose one-step \emph{conditional kernels}
\[
K_{\Delta \tau}\!\left[q_{k+1}\,\middle|\, q_0,\ldots,q_k\right]
\]
are \emph{history-dependent}. This renders the process \emph{non-Markovian}; we then give a rigorous criterion (in terms of conditional $\sigma$-algebras) under which no factorisation into Markovian semigroups exists on $(Q,\calB(Q))$, i.e.\ the process is \emph{indivisible}. We prove: (i) existence and normalisation of kernels under explicit spectral-gap and stability hypotheses; (ii) consistency (Kolmogorov extension) and uniqueness via a contraction argument on the space of conditional probability functionals; (iii) measurability and pathwise well-posedness; and (iv) a reconstruction theorem showing that any admissible indivisible kernel can be realised by suitable internal geometries and couplings in MTT.

\paragraph{Contributions and structure.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \S2 fixes the geometric/analytic hypotheses (spectral gaps, stability, regularity), identifies the configuration space $Q$, and defines the modal action increment $\Delta A$.
\item \S3 constructs the path-space measure and one-step kernels from the modal flow, proves normalisation, and establishes non-Markovianity and indivisibility under a precise conditional-independence witness.
\item \S4 gives a reconstruction theorem: for an explicit admissible class $\calK_{\mathrm{target}}$ of indivisible kernels (normalised, positive, Lipschitz in the complete-past metric, and generated by bounded modal actions), there exist MTT boundary data whose projection realises them exactly.
\item \S5 establishes probability weights $w=\exp(-\Delta A/\hbar)$ as a \emph{noncontextual} and \emph{consistent} measure on histories (cylinder $\sigma$-algebra), and proves equivalence with the Born probabilities upon Hilbert reconstruction.
\item \S6 formalises measurements as constraint changes $F\to F'$ in modal space and proves that the resulting update is Bayesian conditioning on a sub-$\sigma$-algebra of histories, i.e.\ pruning with renormalisation.
\item \S7 discusses physical implications (origin of nonlocal correlations, comparison with the Hilbert-space face).
\item \S8 concludes. Appendices supply technical proofs: boundedness of $P$, existence/uniqueness of the path measure (complete-connections contraction), indivisibility criteria, and worked examples.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mathematical stance.}
We work in discrete modal time $\tau\in \Delta\tau\,\N$, which suffices for rigour and aligns with the fixed-point iteration view. Continuous-time limits can be obtained under standard tightness and uniform integrability assumptions (not needed for the main claims). All probability statements are made with respect to the canonical product $\sigma$-algebra on $Q^{\N}$.
\section{Mathematical Setup and Hypotheses}

\subsection{Geometric background}

We work on the $10$-dimensional modal manifold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M10}
M_{10} = Y_4 \times B_1 \times B_2 \times B_3,
\end{equation}
with the following structures:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item $Y_4$ is a smooth Lorentzian manifold $(M_4,g^{(4)})$, representing emergent spacetime.
\item Each $B_n$ is a compact, oriented Riemannian $3$-manifold $(B_n,h^{(n)})$ with spin structure and volume form $d\mu_{B_n}$.
\item The total metric factorises as
\[
g^{(10)} = g^{(4)} \oplus h^{(1)} \oplus h^{(2)} \oplus h^{(3)},
\]
up to smooth warping terms in the internal sectors.
\end{itemize}

The field content consists of sections of bundles $E\to M_{10}$ with $L^2$ inner product induced by $g^{(10)}$. The Laplace--Beltrami operators $\Delta_{B_n}$ act fibrewise on $E|_{B_n}$.

\subsection{Harmonic projectors and coherent sector}

\begin{assumption}[Spectral gap]\label{H1}
For each $n$, the spectrum of $\Delta_{B_n}$ is discrete and admits a gap:
\[
\lambda_n := \inf\big(\mathrm{Spec}(\Delta_{B_n}) \setminus \{0\}\big) > 0.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Harmonic projection]\label{H2}
Let $\Pi_{B_n}:L^2(B_n)\to \ker\Delta_{B_n}$ be the orthogonal projector.
We require $\Pi_{B_n}$ to be bounded on $H^1(B_n)$ and to commute with external derivatives and chirality operators.
\end{assumption}

The joint harmonic projector is defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PiDef}
\Pi := \Pi_{B_1}\otimes \Pi_{B_2}\otimes \Pi_{B_3},
\end{equation}
which is self-adjoint, idempotent, and bounded. The coherent sector is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:HcohDef}
H_{\mathrm{coh}} := \mathrm{Im}(\Pi) = \bigcap_{n=1}^3 \ker \Delta_{B_n}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Observable pushforward}

Define the fibre integration
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pushforward}
I:L^2(M_{10})\to L^2(Y_4), \quad (If)(y) := \frac{1}{\prod_{n=1}^3 \mathrm{Vol}(B_n)} \int_{B_1\times B_2\times B_3} f(y,b)\, d\mu_{B_1} d\mu_{B_2} d\mu_{B_3}.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Boundedness of $P$]\label{H6}
The composition $P:=I\circ \Pi$ extends to a bounded operator on $L^2(M_{10})$, mapping modal fields to observable external fields on $Y_4$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Modal dynamics and fixed points}

Let $\Phi_\tau:H^1(E)\to H^1(E)$ denote the semi-flow generated by the MTT curvature-reducing equations. We posit:

\begin{assumption}[Fixed-point coherence]\label{H3}
There exists a globally attracting fixed point $\Psi^*\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}$ for the projected flow $\Pi\circ \Phi_\tau$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Stability under damping balance]\label{H4}
For disturbances with bundle-resolved growth rates $\delta_n$ and damping rates $\gamma_n$, the inequality $\gamma_n > \delta_n$ ensures that $H_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is dynamically invariant under $\Phi_\tau$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Finite-energy and regularity}

\begin{assumption}[Finite energy]\label{H5}
Initial data lie in the finite-energy domain of the 10D Hamiltonian $H_{10}$ induced by the quadratic MTT action, ensuring $\Psi(0)\in L^2(M_{10})$ with finite norm.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Regularity]\label{H7}
All background fields $(g^{(n)},A^{(n)},\ldots)$ are $C^\infty$, ensuring Sobolev embeddings and functional-analytic operations (spectral calculus, compactness) hold.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Configuration space and histories}

Let $Q\subset Y_4$ denote the external configuration space of observables (e.g.\ positions of $N$ particles, coarse lattice field values). We assume $Q$ is a Polish space with Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\calB(Q)$. 

\begin{definition}[History]
A (finite) history is a tuple $\mathcal{H}_k=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ with $q_i\in Q$ observed at discrete modal times $\tau_i=i\Delta\tau$. The path space of infinite histories is $Q^{\N}$ with product $\sigma$-algebra.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Modal action increment}

For $\Psi,\Psi'\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}$ related by a flow segment of $\Phi_\tau$, define the modal action increment $\Delta A(\Psi\to\Psi')\geq 0$ as the minimal deformation cost in modal configuration space. By stationary-phase arguments, $\Delta A$ determines stochastic weights. Precise definition is given in Appendix~\ref{app:Action}.

\subsection{Summary of hypotheses}

Under (H\ref{H1})--(H\ref{H7}) we have:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item A well-defined harmonic projector $\Pi$ with bounded range $H_{\mathrm{coh}}$;
\item A globally attracting, stable fixed point $\Psi^*\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}$;
\item A bounded observable pushforward $P:I\circ \Pi$ mapping modal fields to external fields;
\item A Polish configuration space $Q$ with history path space $Q^{\N}$;
\item A well-defined action increment $\Delta A$ generating weights.
\end{itemize}

These conditions suffice for rigorous construction of the induced stochastic process.
\section{Projection to Stochastic Dynamics}\label{sec:projection}

We now construct the stochastic dynamics on $Q$ induced by the MTT modal flow
$\Phi_\tau$ and projection $P=I\circ \Pi$. All results are proved under the
hypotheses (H\ref{H1})--(H\ref{H7}).

\subsection{One-step transition kernels}

Let $\Psi(\tau)\in H_{\mathrm{coh}}$ evolve under the projected flow
$\Pi\circ \Phi_\tau$ with initial data $\Psi(0)$. Define the external
configuration at time $\tau=k\Delta\tau$ as
\[
q_k := (P\Psi)(k\Delta\tau)\in Q.
\]

\begin{definition}[MTT one-step kernel]
For each history prefix $\mathcal{H}_k=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$, define the one-step
transition kernel $K_{\Delta\tau}[\,\cdot\,|\mathcal{H}_k]$ as the probability
measure on $(Q,\calB(Q))$ given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernelDef}
K_{\Delta\tau}(A \mid \mathcal{H}_k)
:= \bbP(q_{k+1}\in A \mid q_0,\ldots,q_k),
\qquad A\in \calB(Q),
\end{equation}
where $\bbP$ is the path-space measure constructed from modal weights
$w=\exp(-\Delta A/\hbar)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Normalisation]
For all histories $\mathcal{H}_k$,
\[
K_{\Delta\tau}(Q \mid \mathcal{H}_k) = 1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $\bbP(\cdot|\mathcal{H}_k)$ is a conditional probability
measure on $(Q,\calB(Q))$. Normalisation is automatic:
\[
K_{\Delta\tau}(Q\mid \mathcal{H}_k) =
\bbP(q_{k+1}\in Q \mid \mathcal{H}_k) = 1.
\]
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Complete connections / summable variations.}
Throughout we assume a complete‑connections regularity: there exists $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and
$L<\infty$ such that $h\mapsto k(\cdot|h)$ is $L^1(\nu)$–Lipschitz in the complete‑past metric
$d_\alpha$ (summable variations). This ensures measurability, Ionescu–Tulcea consistency, and
uniqueness/stability of the induced $g$‑measure on $Q^{\mathbb N}$. 

\subsection{Kolmogorov extension and path measure}

\begin{theorem}[Ionescu--Tulcea construction of the path measure]\label{thm:IT}
Let $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$ be a standard Borel space, fix an initial law $\lambda_0$ on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$, and for each finite history $H_k=(q_0,\dots,q_k)$ let $K_{\Delta\tau}(\,\cdot\,|H_k)$ be a probability kernel on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $H_k\mapsto K_{\Delta\tau}(A|H_k)$ is $\mathcal B(Q)^{\otimes(k+1)}$--measurable for all $A\in\mathcal B(Q)$;
\item $K_{\Delta\tau}(Q|H_k)=1$ for all $H_k$.
\end{enumerate}
Then there exists a unique probability measure $\mathbb P$ on $(Q^{\mathbb N},\mathcal B(Q)^{\otimes\mathbb N})$ such that for all cylinder sets
\[
\mathbb P(q_0\!\in\!A_0,\dots,q_k\!\in\!A_k) \;=\; \int_{A_0}\!\lambda_0(dq_0)\,\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} K_{\Delta\tau}(dq_{i+1}|q_0,\dots,q_i).
\]
\end{theorem}


\begin{proof}
Consistency requires that for $A_0,\ldots,A_{k-1}\in\calB(Q)$,
\[
\int_Q K_{\Delta\tau}(dq_k\mid q_0,\ldots,q_{k-1})
=1,
\]
and that the marginals of the joint distribution for $(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ reduce
to those for $(q_0,\ldots,q_{k-1})$. Both follow from the definition of
conditional kernels and normalisation. The Kolmogorov extension theorem
(standard reference: e.g.\ Billingsley, \emph{Probability and Measure}) then
yields existence and uniqueness of the path measure $\bbP$.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness and stability for complete connections]\label{thm:dobrushin}
Assume there exists $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and $L<\infty$ such that for histories $h,h'$ of the same length
\[
\int_Q \big|k(q'|h)-k(q'|h')\big|\,d\nu(q')\;\le\; L\, d_\alpha(h,h'),
\]
where $k(\cdot|h)$ is the density of $K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot|h)$ and $d_\alpha$ is the complete-past metric. If $\sum_{j\ge 0}\alpha^j<\infty$ and $L$ is sufficiently small (summable variations), then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the Ionescu--Tulcea measure $\mathbb P$ is unique among all consistent path measures with the same kernels;
\item the one-step transfer operator is a contraction in the $L^1$ Wasserstein metric induced by $d_\alpha$, hence the process is stable under small perturbations of the kernels.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Non-Markovianity}

\begin{definition}[Markov property]
A process $\{q_k\}$ is Markovian if
\[
K_{\Delta\tau}(A\mid q_0,\ldots,q_k) =
K_{\Delta\tau}(A\mid q_k),
\qquad \forall A\in \calB(Q).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Non-Markovianity of MTT kernels]\label{thm:nonMarkov}
Under hypotheses (H\ref{H1})--(H\ref{H7}), the induced process on $Q$ is
generically non-Markovian.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The kernel depends on the internal moduli path $m(\tau)$ generated by
$\Phi_\tau$, whose evolution obeys
\[
\dot m = -\nabla E(m; q_0,\ldots,q_k),
\]
where $E$ is a modal energy functional depending on the entire history through
spectral gaps $\lambda_n(m)$. Thus, given $(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$, the law of
$q_{k+1}$ depends on the whole prefix, not only $q_k$. Therefore the equality
above fails in general, so the process is non-Markovian.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Indivisibility}

\begin{definition}[Classical indivisibility]\label{def:indivisible}
A discrete-time process with family $\{K_{n\to n+1}(\cdot\mid H_n)\}$ on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$ is \emph{(classically) divisible} on an interval $\{0,\dots,t\}$ if there exist probability kernels $\{M_{s\to s+1}(\cdot\mid q_s)\}_{s=0}^{t-1}$, depending only on the \emph{current} state, such that for all $A\in\mathcal B(Q)$
\[
K_{0\to t}(A\mid q_0)\;=\;\int\cdots\int \mathbb 1_A(q_t)\,\prod_{s=0}^{t-1} M_{s\to s+1}(dq_{s+1}\mid q_s).
\]
Otherwise, the process is \emph{indivisible} on $\{0,\dots,t\}$. 
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Non-Markovianity $\Rightarrow$ indivisibility]\label{thm:nonmarkov-indiv}

If $K_{n\to n+1}(\cdot|H_n)$ depends on the complete past $H_n$, then there do not exist
one‑step probability kernels $\{M_{s\to s+1}(\cdot|q_s)\}_{s=0}^{t-1}$ on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$
such that, for all $A\in\mathcal B(Q)$,
\[
K_{0\to t}(A\,|\,q_0)=\int\!\cdots\!\int \mathbf 1_A(q_t)\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}M_{s\to s+1}(dq_{s+1}\,|\,q_s).
\]
Hence the process is indivisible on any nontrivial interval when the state space is fixed to $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$.

\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[On CP-divisibility]
Our notion is \emph{classical} divisibility (factorisation of classical kernels through single-time states). It should not be conflated with quantum CP-divisibility of dynamical maps. Upon Hilbert reconstruction, $\mathbb P$ reproduces Born probabilities, but CP-divisibility of the corresponding quantum channels is a separate property (typically violated for memoryful dynamics).
\end{remark}


\subsection{Summary}

From MTT’s modal dynamics and projection, we have constructed:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item A well-defined family of conditional kernels
$K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot\mid \mathcal{H}_k)$;
\item A unique path-space probability measure $\bbP$ via Kolmogorov extension;
\item Proofs that the induced process is non-Markovian and indivisible.
\end{itemize}

This establishes the stochastic face of MTT dynamics.
\section{Reconstruction Theorem: MTT Realises Indivisible Processes}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

This section establishes a rigorous reconstruction map from a broad class of
indivisible stochastic kernels on the configuration space $Q$ to suitable choices
of MTT boundary data. We first specify an admissible class of kernels with
precise regularity; we then show (i) every such kernel admits a Gibbs-type
representation by a nonnegative \emph{action density}, and (ii) this action can be
implemented as a \emph{modal action increment} in MTT by an explicit, finite-dimensional
moduli construction. A density result shows uniform approximation (in total
variation) of continuous kernels by exactly realisable ones.

\subsection{Complete-past metric and regularity class}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ and define the complete-past metric $d_\alpha$ on history
prefixes $h=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ and $\tilde h=(\tilde q_0,\ldots,\tilde q_k)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:past_metric}
d_\alpha(h,\tilde h):=
\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-j}\, d_Q(q_j,\tilde q_j),
\end{equation}
where $d_Q$ is a bounded metric that induces the topology of the Polish space $Q$.

\begin{definition}[Admissible kernel class $\calK_{\mathrm{adm}}$]
A family of conditional probability kernels
$K(\cdot \mid h)$ on $(Q,\calB(Q))$ indexed by finite histories
$h=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ is in $\calK_{\mathrm{adm}}$ if:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(K\arabic*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Strict positivity and normalisation:}
$K(\cdot\mid h)$ has a density $k(\,\cdot\,\mid h)$ w.r.t.\ a fixed reference
$\sigma$-finite measure $\nu$ on $(Q,\calB(Q))$ with
$0<k(q'\mid h)\in L^1(\nu)$ and $\int k(q'\mid h)\,d\nu(q')=1$ for all $h$.
\item \emph{Complete-past Lipschitz continuity:} There exists $L<\infty$
such that for all $h,\tilde h$ with the same length,
\[
\int \big|k(q'\mid h) - k(q'\mid \tilde h)\big|\, d\nu(q')
\;\le\; L\, d_\alpha(h,\tilde h).
\]
\item \emph{Indivisibility:} The process defined by $\{K(\cdot\mid h)\}$ is
non-Markovian in the sense of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonMarkov}, i.e.\ there exists
a set of positive $\bbP$-measure of pairs $(h,A)$ such that
$K(A\mid h)\neq K(A\mid q_k)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Assumption (K2) is a standard \emph{complete connections} (summable variations)
regularity, ensuring robustness of the path measure w.r.t.\ perturbations of the
complete past; it strengthens the measurability hypotheses used in \S\ref{sec:projection}.

\subsection{Gibbs representation by an action density}

\begin{lemma}[Gibbs representation with explicit normalizer]\label{lem:gibbs}
Let $K(\cdot|h)$ have strictly positive density $k(\cdot|h)$ w.r.t.\ a fixed $\sigma$-finite $\nu$.
Define
\[
A(q',h):=\hbar\big(-\log k(q'|h)\big)-C(h),\qquad
C(h):=\hbar\inf_{q''\in Q}\big(-\log k(q''|h)\big).
\]
Then $A(\cdot,h)\ge 0$ $\nu$-a.e. and
\[
k(q'|h)=\frac{e^{-A(q',h)/\hbar}}{\displaystyle\int_Q e^{-A(q'',h)/\hbar}\,d\nu(q'')}.
\]
Equivalently, with the unshifted action $A_0(q',h):=-\hbar\log k(q'|h)$ one has
$k=e^{-A_0/\hbar}$ and the normalizer is $\int k\,d\nu\equiv 1$. The shift by $C(h)$ enforces
$A\ge0$ without changing the normalized density. If $h\mapsto k(\cdot|h)$ is $L^1(\nu)$–Lipschitz
in $d_\alpha$, then $h\mapsto A(\cdot,h)$ and $h\mapsto \int e^{-A/\hbar}d\nu$ inherit this regularity.
\end{lemma}


\begin{proof}
Positivity of $k$ implies $-\log k(q'\mid h)$ is finite for $\nu$-a.e.\ $q'$.
Subtracting the $\inf$ (which is finite) produces $A\ge 0$ and does not affect
the normalised density because the shift cancels in \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}. Measurability
is inherited from $k$. For Lipschitz dependence, note
\[
\|e^{-A(\cdot,h)/\hbar}-e^{-A(\cdot,\tilde h)/\hbar}\|_{L^1(\nu)}
= \|k(\cdot\mid h)Z(h)-k(\cdot\mid \tilde h)Z(\tilde h)\|_{L^1(\nu)}
\]
and $Z(h)=1$ by construction. Hence the bound follows from (K2).
\end{proof}

Thus every admissible kernel admits a Gibbs form with a nonnegative
\emph{action density} $A(\cdot,h)$. The next step is to realise $A$ as a
\emph{modal action increment} in MTT.

\subsection{A representable quadratic-action subclass}

To obtain an exact, constructive realisation within MTT, we specify a rich
subclass of action densities.

\begin{definition}[Quadratic-action class $\calA_{J,G,\Phi}$]\label{def:quadratic_class}
Fix $J\in\N$, a positive-definite matrix $G\in\R^{J\times J}$, and a measurable
map $\Phi:Q\times \bigcup_{k\ge 0} Q^{k+1}\to \R^{J}$ assigning to $(q',h)$ a
feature vector $\Phi(q',h)$. Define the action density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic_action}
A(q',h) := \frac{1}{2}\,\big\|\Phi(q',h)-\Phi_\star(h)\big\|_{G}^2,
\qquad \|x\|_G^2 := x^{\mathsf T} G\, x,
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_\star(h)$ is a measurable anchor depending only on $h$.
Let $\calA_{J,G,\Phi}$ denote the set of all such $A$ with $\Phi,\Phi_\star$ that
are Lipschitz in $h$ under $d_\alpha$ and Borel in $q'$.
\end{definition}

The corresponding kernels have the Gibbs form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic_kernel}
k(q'\mid h) = \frac{\exp\!\big(-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\|\Phi(q',h)-\Phi_\star(h)\|_G^2\big)}
{\int \exp\!\big(-\frac{1}{2\hbar}\|\Phi(q'',h)-\Phi_\star(h)\|_G^2\big)\, d\nu(q'')}.
\end{equation}
These kernels are strictly positive, normalised, and Lipschitz in $h$ (by dominated
convergence and the Lipschitz hypotheses on $\Phi,\Phi_\star$), hence are in
$\calK_{\mathrm{adm}}$ whenever indivisibility (K3) holds.\footnote{Indivisibility
is generic unless $\Phi(q',h)$ collapses to dependence on $q_k$ only; see
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonMarkov}.}

\subsection{Exact realisation in MTT via finite-dimensional moduli}

We now show that for any $A\in \calA_{J,G,\Phi}$ there exist MTT boundary data whose
modal action increment equals $A$ and thereby produce exactly the kernel
\eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact realisation of the quadratic-action class]\label{thm:exact_realisation}
Fix $J\in\N$, $G>0$, and $\Phi,\Phi_\star$ as in
Definition~\ref{def:quadratic_class}. There exist choices of internal backgrounds
(gauge fields and metrics on $B_n$) and a $J$-dimensional smooth moduli submanifold
$\calM\subset \prod_n \mathrm{Met}(B_n)\times \mathrm{Conn}(B_n)$ with a Riemannian
metric $\mathbf{G}$ such that:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The moduli coordinate $m\in\calM$ couples to the external configuration through
boundary conditions so that the minimal modal action to move from the anchor
$m_\star(h)$ to the target $m(q',h)$ equals
\[
\Delta A(q',h)= \frac{1}{2}\,\big\| \Phi(q',h)-\Phi_\star(h)\big\|_G^2 = A(q',h).
\]
\item The one-step conditional kernel generated by weighting $e^{-\Delta A/\hbar}$
and pushing forward by $P$ coincides with \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel} $\nu$-a.e.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1 (Finite-dimensional moduli).} Consider a smooth $J$-dimensional
manifold $\calM\simeq \R^J/\Lambda$ (a flat torus for compactness) embedded as a
finite-dimensional submanifold in the space of smooth backgrounds on the $B_n$.
Choose coordinates $m\in\R^J/\Lambda$ and endow $\calM$ with the flat metric
$\mathbf{G}\equiv G$ (constant in these coordinates).

\emph{Step 2 (Anchor and target).} Define smooth maps
\[
m_\star:\bigcup_{k\ge 0} Q^{k+1}\to \calM,\qquad
m(\,\cdot\,,h): Q\to \calM,
\]
by $m_\star(h):= \Phi_\star(h) \bmod \Lambda$ and
$m(q',h):= \Phi(q',h)\bmod \Lambda$, using any fixed fundamental domain and the
canonical projection $\R^J\to \R^J/\Lambda$. Lipschitz dependence of $\Phi,\Phi_\star$
ensures measurability.

\emph{Step 3 (Action as geodesic energy).} On the Riemannian manifold
$(\calM,\mathbf{G})$, let $\gamma$ be the constant-speed geodesic from
$m_\star(h)$ to $m(q',h)$; since $\mathbf{G}$ is flat, $\gamma$ is the straight
segment in the universal cover modulo $\Lambda$. The (time-rescaled) geodesic
action with Lagrangian $L(\dot m)=\tfrac12 \dot m^{\mathsf T}G \dot m$ equals
$\tfrac12\|m(q',h)-m_\star(h)\|_G^2$ and is the minimal action among all smooth
paths with those endpoints. This matches $A(q',h)$ by construction.

\emph{Step 4 (Modal dynamics and weighting).} Implement the moduli dynamics in
MTT so that, conditional on the history $h$, the internal sector executes a
transition from $m_\star(h)$ to $m(q',h)$ with path-weight density proportional
to $\exp(-\mathrm{Action}/\hbar)$, which is standard in the (Euclideanised) modal
path-integral picture and can be made precise at the rigorous level by defining
the conditional kernel directly via $e^{-\Delta A/\hbar}$ as in \S\ref{sec:projection}.
Because $A\ge 0$ and $\calM$ is compact, the normaliser is finite. Pushing
forward by $P$ produces exactly the kernel in \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Compatibility with MTT hypotheses]
Compactness of $\calM$ (torus) is compatible with the compactness of $B_n$ and
ensures boundedness of energies; the flat metric ensures uniform spectral gaps
on the constructed submanifold, consistent with Assumptions (H\ref{H1})--(H\ref{H4}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Surjectivity onto the quadratic-action kernel class}

\begin{corollary}[Surjectivity]\label{cor:surjectivity}
The map
\[
\{\text{MTT boundary data}\}/\!\!\sim \;\longrightarrow\;
\left\{ K(\cdot\mid h)\text{ of the form \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}}\right\}
\]
is surjective, where $\sim$ denotes the natural gauge/diffeomorphism equivalence.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Given any $K$ of the form \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}, define $A$ by
\eqref{eq:quadratic_action}. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_realisation}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Density and approximation of continuous kernels}

We now show that the representable class is dense (in total variation) in a
natural space of continuous kernels.

Assume $Q$ compact metric and endow the set of fixed‑length histories with $d_\alpha$.
Let $\mathcal C(Q\times H_\alpha)$ be the continuous functions in the product topology.
By Stone–Weierstrass there exist finite feature maps $\{F_j\}_{j=1}^J\subset \mathcal C$ whose
algebra is dense in $\mathcal C(Q\times H_\alpha)$. Uniform approximation of the continuous
action $A(\cdot,h)$ by finite quadratic forms in $\{F_j\}$ implies, after normalisation,
uniform (in $h$) $L^1(\nu)$‑approximation of densities $k(\cdot|h)$, hence TV‑density of
the quadratic‑action class. 


\begin{theorem}[TV-density]\label{thm:TVdensity}
Let $Q$ be compact metric, and let $\nu$ be a Borel reference measure with full
support. Let $\calK_{\mathrm{cts}}$ denote the set of kernels with continuous
densities $k(\cdot\mid h)$ jointly continuous in $(q',h)$ (for the product of the
topology of $Q$ and $d_\alpha$ on histories). Then for every
$K\in \calK_{\mathrm{cts}}$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there exist $J,G,\Phi,\Phi_\star$
such that the kernel $\tilde K$ of the form \eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel} satisfies
\[
\sup_{h}\, \int_Q \big|k(q'\mid h)-\tilde k(q'\mid h)\big|\, d\nu(q')
\;<\; \varepsilon.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:Gibbs}, write $k\propto e^{-A/\hbar}$ with continuous $A\ge 0$
(after normalisation by subtracting a continuous $C(h)$). By Stone--Weierstrass,
there exist finite feature maps $F_j(q',h)$ spanning an algebra dense in
$C(Q\times \calH_\alpha)$, where $\calH_\alpha$ is the metric space of fixed-length
histories with metric $d_\alpha$. Approximate $A$ uniformly by a finite quadratic
form in features: $\sum_{j=1}^J \tfrac12 \theta_j (F_j-\mu_j(h))^2$. Absorb this
into the structure of \eqref{eq:quadratic_action} by setting
$\Phi(q',h)=(\sqrt{\theta_j}F_j(q',h))_{j=1}^J$ and
$\Phi_\star(h)=(\sqrt{\theta_j}\mu_j(h))_{j=1}^J$, with $G=\mathbf{1}$. The
uniform approximation of $A$ implies uniform (in $h$) approximation of the Gibbs
densities after normalisation; dominated convergence yields the claimed TV bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Approximate realisability of continuous kernels]\label{cor:approx}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:TVdensity}, for every continuous kernel
$K$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there exists MTT boundary data whose induced kernel
$\tilde K$ satisfies
$\sup_h \|K(\cdot\mid h)-\tilde K(\cdot\mid h)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:TVdensity} with Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_realisation}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Putting it together}

We summarise the reconstruction result.

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction, indivisible case]\label{thm:reconstruction_main}
Let $K\in \calK_{\mathrm{adm}}$ be indivisible. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item (\emph{Exact realisation}) If $K$ admits a quadratic-action representation
\eqref{eq:quadratic_kernel}, then there exist MTT boundary data realising $K$
exactly (Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_realisation}).
\item (\emph{Approximate realisation}) If $k(\cdot\mid h)$ is continuous, then
for any $\varepsilon>0$ there exist MTT boundary data yielding $\tilde K$ with
$\sup_h \|K(\cdot\mid h)-\tilde K(\cdot\mid h)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\varepsilon$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:approx}).
\end{enumerate}
In both cases the induced process is indivisible provided $K$ (or $\tilde K$)
fails the Markov property on $Q$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:indivisible}).
\end{theorem}
\section{Probabilities and Weights}
\label{sec:probabilities}

In the MTT$\Rightarrow$QM derivation, probabilities on projections were obtained
via noncontextual measures and Gleason--Busch. Here we work purely in the
stochastic/process picture. We (i) construct a path-space probability measure
from modal action increments, (ii) prove $\sigma$-additivity and consistency on
the cylinder $\sigma$-algebra, (iii) establish \emph{noncontextuality} of outcome
probabilities (independence from the measurement partition), and (iv) state
precise conditions under which these probabilities coincide with Born
probabilities upon Hilbert reconstruction.

\subsection{Stepwise action increments and $g$-functions}

\begin{definition}[Stepwise action increment and $g$-function]\label{def:gfunc}
For each finite history $H_k=(q_0,\dots,q_k)$ and candidate $q_{k+1}\in Q$, let $\Delta A(q_k\!\to q_{k+1}\mid H_k)\ge 0$ be the one-step modal action increment (Appendix~\ref{app:action}). Fix a $\sigma$-finite reference measure $\nu$ with full support on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$ and assume the uniform integrability
\begin{equation}\label{eq:UI}
0<\int_Q e^{-\Delta A(q_k\to q' \mid H_k)/\hbar}\,d\nu(q')\;\le M<\infty\qquad\text{for all }H_k.
\end{equation}
Define the $g$-function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gfunc}
g(q_{k+1}\mid H_k)\;:=\;\frac{\exp\!\big(-\Delta A(q_k\to q_{k+1}\mid H_k)/\hbar\big)}{\displaystyle\int_Q \exp\!\big(-\Delta A(q_k\to q'\mid H_k)/\hbar\big)\,d\nu(q')}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Kernel from $g$-function; measurability and normalisation]\label{lem:kernel-g}
For each $H_k$, the map $A\mapsto \int_A g(q_{k+1}\mid H_k)\,d\nu(q_{k+1})$ defines a probability kernel $K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot\mid H_k)$ on $(Q,\mathcal B(Q))$. Moreover, $H_k\mapsto K_{\Delta\tau}(A\mid H_k)$ is measurable for all $A$ and the family $\{K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot\mid H_k)\}$ satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:IT}.
\end{lemma}


\begin{lemma}[Kernel from $g$-function]\label{lem:kernel_from_g}
For each $h_k$, the map $A\mapsto \int_A g(q_{k+1}\mid h_k)\, d\nu(q_{k+1})$
defines a probability kernel $K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot\mid h_k)$ on $(Q,\calB(Q))$.
Moreover $K_{\Delta\tau}$ coincides a.s.\ with the kernel constructed in
\eqref{eq:kernelDef}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Normalisation follows from the denominator of \eqref{eq:gfunction}. Positivity is
clear. Measurability in $h_k$ holds because $h_k\mapsto \Delta A$ is Borel by
Appendix~\ref{app:Action}, and dominated convergence applies by
Assumption~\ref{A:UI}. Coincidence with \eqref{eq:kernelDef} follows from the
construction of $\bbP$ in Section~\ref{sec:projection}: $g$ is the Radon--Nikodym
density of the conditional law w.r.t.\ $\nu$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cylinder $\sigma$-algebra, consistency, and $\sigma$-additivity}

Let $\calC$ be the algebra of cylinder sets in $Q^{\N}$, i.e.\ sets of the form
\[
C = \{(q_i)_{i\ge 0} : q_0\in A_0,\ldots,q_k\in A_k\},
\qquad A_i\in \calB(Q).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Consistent pre-measure on $\calC$]\label{thm:premesure}
Define $\mu$ on $\calC$ by
\[
\mu(C) = \int_{A_0}\!\cdots\!\int_{A_k}
\prod_{i=0}^{k-1} K_{\Delta\tau}(dq_{i+1}\mid q_0,\ldots,q_i)\, \lambda_0(dq_0),
\]
where $\lambda_0$ is any fixed initial distribution on $(Q,\calB(Q))$.
Then $\mu$ is a consistent, finitely additive \emph{pre-measure} on $\calC$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Finite additivity is standard: if $C=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^n C_j$ is a disjoint union
of cylinders all of the same length $k$, then the integrals decompose into disjoint
unions of the sets $A_i$; the product structure preserves additivity. Consistency
(i.e.\ marginalisation) is immediate by integrating $A_k=Q$. The initial law
$\lambda_0$ is fixed, so overlapping prefixes are handled identically.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Carathéodory extension]\label{thm:caratheodory}
The pre-measure $\mu$ extends uniquely to a probability measure (also denoted
$\mu$) on the product $\sigma$-algebra $\calB(Q)^{\otimes \N}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\calC$ is a semi-ring generating the product $\sigma$-algebra. The family of
kernels is normalised (Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel_from_g}), so $\mu$ is tight on
compact $Q$; more generally, standard Kolmogorov consistency holds by
Section~\ref{sec:projection}. Carathéodory's extension theorem yields a unique
probability measure on $\sigma(\calC)$, which equals $\calB(Q)^{\otimes \N}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Noncontextual outcome probabilities}

We formalise measurement as a \emph{constraint change} $F\to F'$ at a fixed
stage $k^\star$ (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}). The apparatus induces a measurable
partition $\{Q_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in \mathsf{A}}$ of $Q$ at time $k^\star$.
Define the \emph{outcome event}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Ealpha}
E_\alpha := \{(q_i)_{i\ge 0}\in Q^\N: q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Partition‑invariance on the cylinder algebra]
A probability assignment $\alpha\mapsto \mu(E_\alpha)$ is partition‑invariant (``noncontextual''
on cylinder events) if it depends only on the measurable set $Q_\alpha$ at the measurement
stage, i.e. it is invariant under refinements/coarsenings that leave $Q_\alpha$ unchanged.

\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Noncontextuality of MTT outcome probabilities]\label{thm:noncontext}
Let $\{Q_\alpha\}$ and $\{Q'_\beta\}$ be two measurable partitions of $Q$ such
that for some fixed label $\alpha_0$ we have $Q_{\alpha_0}= \bigcup_{\beta \in B}
Q'_\beta$ for an index set $B$. Then
\[
\mu(E_{\alpha_0}) \;=\; \sum_{\beta\in B} \mu(E'_\beta),
\]
where $E'_\beta$ is defined as in \eqref{eq:Ealpha} with $Q'_\beta$ in place of
$Q_\alpha$. In particular, the probability of the outcome associated with
$Q_{\alpha_0}$ is independent of the measurement context.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $E_{\alpha_0}=\bigsqcup_{\beta\in B} E'_\beta$ as a disjoint union
of measurable events at the same time $k^\star$. By $\sigma$-additivity
(Theorem~\ref{thm:caratheodory}), $\mu(E_{\alpha_0})=\sum_{\beta\in B}\mu(E'_\beta)$.
Therefore the probability assigned to $Q_{\alpha_0}$ is invariant under refinement.
Coarsenings are handled by the same argument. Hence noncontextuality holds.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Weights for finite-outcome experiments}

For a finite partition $\{Q_\alpha\}_{\alpha=1}^m$ at stage $k^\star$, define the
\emph{branch weight}
\[
W_\alpha := \int_{Q_{\alpha}}\! g(q_{k^\star}\mid q_0,\ldots,q_{k^\star-1})
\, d\nu(q_{k^\star}),
\]
where the integral is taken w.r.t.\ the conditional law of $(q_0,\ldots,q_{k^\star-1})$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights_normalised}
\mu(E_\alpha)= \frac{W_\alpha}{\sum_{\gamma=1}^m W_\gamma}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, by \eqref{eq:gfunction} each $W_\alpha$ is an average of
$\exp(-\Delta A/\hbar)$ over the fibre $\{q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha\}$ and over
past histories; thus the \emph{exponential action weighting} appears explicitly.


\subsection{Relation to Born probabilities under Hilbert reconstruction}

The following places the stochastic probabilities in correspondence with Born
probabilities when a Hilbert-space realisation exists.

\begin{assumption}[Hilbert reconstruction]\label{A:Hilbert}
There exists a separable complex Hilbert space $\calH$ and a map assigning to
each finite measurable partition $\{Q_\alpha\}$ an orthogonal projection-valued
measure (PVM) $\{P_\alpha\}$ on $\calH$, together with a density operator $\rho$
on $\calH$, such that for every partition and corresponding event
$E_\alpha=\{q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha\}$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Born_match}
\mu(E_\alpha) = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho\, P_\alpha).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}
Assumption~\ref{A:Hilbert} is satisfied whenever one performs the (standard)
Hilbert-space reconstruction from the indivisible process (e.g.\ via the
stochastic--quantum correspondence), or, abstractly, by GNS construction from
the consistent family of cylinder events (Appendix~\ref{app:GNS}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Gleason--Busch consistency]\label{thm:gleason}
Assume $\dim \calH \ge 2$. If the assignment $P\mapsto \mu(P)$ on the lattice of
orthogonal projections (defined via \eqref{eq:Born_match} on all finite PVMs) is
noncontextual and $\sigma$-additive on orthogonal families, then there exists a
unique density operator $\rho$ such that $\mu(P)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P)$ for all
projections $P$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Idea]
For $\dim \calH\ge 3$ this is Gleason's theorem (extended to $\sigma$-additivity).
For $\dim \calH=2$ use Busch's generalisation to POVMs and the restriction to
PVMs. Noncontextuality and $\sigma$-additivity are guaranteed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:noncontext} and Carathéodory extension applied to partitions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Born rule equivalence]
Under Assumption~\ref{A:Hilbert}, the stochastic probabilities for all finite
partitions coincide with the Born probabilities for $(\calH,\rho)$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Summary}

From MTT's action geometry we obtained stepwise $g$-functions, kernels, and a
unique path-space measure. Measurement outcome probabilities are noncontextual
because they are measures of cylinder events independent of the partitioning
context. When a Hilbert reconstruction is performed, these probabilities agree
with Born probabilities, placing the stochastic and operator formulations in
one-to-one correspondence.
\section{Measurement and Conditioning}
\label{sec:measurement}

In the Hilbert-space derivation, measurement arose via Stinespring/Naimark
dilation and the Born rule. Here we formulate measurement \emph{entirely} within
the indivisible-process picture as conditioning on sub-$\sigma$-algebras of the
path space. No extra postulates are introduced: the update is the regular
conditional probability determined by the MTT-induced measure. We then show
how this coincides with POVMs and quantum instruments upon Hilbert
reconstruction.

\subsection{Constraint changes and measurable partitions}

Let $(\Omega,\calF,\mu)$ be the path-space probability space, where
$\Omega=Q^{\N}$, $\calF=\calB(Q)^{\otimes \N}$, and $\mu$ is the path measure
from Theorem~\ref{thm:caratheodory}. For $k\in\N$ let
$\pi_k:\Omega\to Q$ be the $k$-th coordinate map and denote by
$\calF_{\le k}:=\sigma(\pi_0,\ldots,\pi_k)$ the history $\sigma$-algebra up to
time $k$.

A measurement at step $k^\star$ is modelled by a \emph{constraint change}
$F\to F'$ in modal space that selects a measurable partition
$\{Q_\alpha\}_{\alpha\in\mathsf{A}}$ of $Q$ at time $k^\star$. The corresponding
\emph{outcome event} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outcome_event}
E_\alpha := \{\omega\in\Omega: \pi_{k^\star}(\omega)\in Q_\alpha\}
\in \sigma(\pi_{k^\star}) \subset \calF_{\le k^\star}.
\end{equation}
Since $Q$ is Polish and $Q_\alpha$ Borel, $\sigma(\pi_{k^\star})$ admits
regular conditional probabilities (RCPs).

\subsection{Regular conditional probabilities and Bayesian update}

\begin{theorem}[Regular conditional probabilities]\label{thm:RCP}
There exists a version of the conditional probability
$\mu(\,\cdot\,|\,\sigma(\pi_{k^\star})):\Omega\times \sigma(\pi_{k^\star})\to [0,1]$
such that for each $B\in\calF$ the map
$q\in Q\mapsto \mu\big(B\,\big|\,\pi_{k^\star}=q\big)$ is Borel and
\[
\mu(B\cap \{\pi_{k^\star}\in A\})
= \int_{A} \mu\big(B\,\big|\,\pi_{k^\star}=q\big)\,
\mu_{\pi_{k^\star}}(dq),
\quad \forall A\in\calB(Q),
\]
where $\mu_{\pi_{k^\star}}$ is the law of $\pi_{k^\star}$ under $\mu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $Q$ is Polish and $(\Omega,\calF)$ standard Borel, existence of an RCP
disintegrating $\mu$ along $\pi_{k^\star}$ is standard (see, e.g., Bogachev,
\emph{Measure Theory}, Thm.\ 10.4.6).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Posterior (collapse) measure]\label{def:posterior}
Given outcome $\alpha$ with $\mu(E_\alpha)>0$, define the posterior measure
$\mu^\alpha$ on $(\Omega,\calF)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior}
\mu^\alpha(B) :=
\mu\big(B\,\big|\, E_\alpha\big)
= \frac{\mu(B\cap E_\alpha)}{\mu(E_\alpha)}, \qquad B\in\calF.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse as pruning + renormalisation]\label{prop:prune}
Let $\Pi_\alpha$ denote the restriction map $\Omega\to E_\alpha$. Then
$\mu^\alpha$ is the unique probability measure supported on $E_\alpha$ such that
for all $B\in\calF$,
\[
\mu^\alpha(B) = \frac{\mu(B\cap E_\alpha)}{\mu(E_\alpha)}.
\]
In particular, $\mu^\alpha$ is obtained by \emph{pruning} to the measurable
subset $E_\alpha$ and \emph{renormalising}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Disintegration and regular conditionals]\label{rem:disintegration}
Since $Q$ is Polish and $(Q^{\mathbb N},\mathcal B(Q)^{\otimes\mathbb N})$ is standard Borel, the conditional measures $\{\mathbb P(\,\cdot\,|\pi_{k^\star}=q)\}_{q\in Q}$ exist and are unique up to $\mathbb P_{\pi_{k^\star}}$-null sets. Hence the pruning/renormalisation update $\mu\mapsto \mu_\alpha$ is the canonical regular conditional probability with respect to the sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\sigma(\pi_{k^\star})$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Definition~\ref{def:posterior} and properties of conditional
probabilities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Updated kernels and Doob--Dynkin measurability}

We now describe the post-measurement dynamics as new conditional kernels.

\begin{theorem}[Bayesian update of one-step kernels]\label{thm:kernel_update}
Let $k\ge k^\star$. Under $\mu^\alpha$, the one-step kernel from time $k$ to
$k+1$ given a history prefix $h_k=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ with $q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha$
equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:updated_kernel}
K_{\Delta\tau}^{(\alpha)}(A\mid h_k)
= \frac{\int_{A} g(q_{k+1}\mid h_k)\, d\nu(q_{k+1})}
{\int_{Q} g(q' \mid h_k)\, d\nu(q')}, \qquad A\in\calB(Q),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ it is the restriction of the pre-measurement kernel to the fibre
$\{h_k: q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha\}$, followed by normalisation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of $\mu^\alpha$ and the chain rule for conditional probabilities,
for cylinder sets $C$ depending only on $(\pi_0,\ldots,\pi_{k+1})$ we have
\[
\mu^\alpha(C) = \frac{\mu(C\cap E_\alpha)}{\mu(E_\alpha)}
=\frac{\int \mathbf{1}_C \prod_{i=0}^{k} K_{\Delta\tau}(dq_{i+1}\mid h_i)\,
\lambda_0(dq_0)}{\mu(E_\alpha)},
\]
where the integration domain is restricted to $q_{k^\star}\in Q_\alpha$.
Identifying the Radon--Nikodym density $g(\cdot\mid h_k)$ w.r.t.\ $\nu$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:kernel_from_g}) yields \eqref{eq:updated_kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Persistence of indivisibility]\label{cor:indiv_persists}
If the pre-measurement process is indivisible
(Theorem~\ref{thm:indivisible}), then the post-measurement process under
$\mu^\alpha$ is also indivisible unless the restriction to $E_\alpha$ removes all
history dependence (a $\mu$-null degeneracy).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $K_{\Delta\tau}^{(\alpha)}$ admitted a Markov factorisation, then, on
$E_\alpha$, the pre-measurement kernel would factorise as well, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:indivisible} except on a null set where the dependence on the
complete past collapses.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sequential measurements and instruments}

Consider two measurements at $k_1<k_2$ with partitions
$\{Q^{(1)}_{\alpha}\}$ and $\{Q^{(2)}_{\beta}\}$ and outcomes
$\alpha,\beta$. Define events $E^{(1)}_\alpha$, $E^{(2)}_\beta$ as in
\eqref{eq:outcome_event}. The \emph{instrument} associated with the first
measurement is the map
\[
\mathcal{I}_\alpha:\ \mathcal{P}(\Omega)\to \mathcal{P}(\Omega),
\qquad \mathcal{I}_\alpha[\mu] := \mu^\alpha.
\]
For the second measurement, the posterior is
$\mu^{\alpha\beta}:= \big(\mu^\alpha\big)^\beta$, i.e.\ successive pruning and
renormalisation. The joint probability for $(\alpha,\beta)$ is
\[
\mu(E^{(1)}_\alpha \cap E^{(2)}_\beta)
= \mu(E^{(1)}_\alpha)\, \mu^\alpha(E^{(2)}_\beta),
\]
the standard chain rule.

\begin{proposition}[Associativity of instruments]
For any finite sequence of measurements with outcomes
$\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$ at times $k_1<\cdots<k_m$,
\[
\mathcal{I}_{\gamma_m}\circ \cdots \circ \mathcal{I}_{\gamma_1}[\mu]
= \mu^{\gamma_1\cdots \gamma_m}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $m$, using Definition~\ref{def:posterior}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equivalence to POVMs and quantum instruments under Hilbert reconstruction}

Assume Hilbert reconstruction (Assumption~\ref{A:Hilbert}) holds for the process.
Then any measurement partition $\{Q_\alpha\}$ at time $k^\star$ corresponds to a
PVM $\{P_\alpha\}$ on $\calH$ with the same classical outcome probabilities:
$\mu(E_\alpha)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_\alpha)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:gleason}). For a
\emph{general} (possibly unsharp) measurement, the partition is replaced by a
family of \emph{effect functionals} $\{E_\alpha[\cdot]\}$ on cylinder events at
time $k^\star$ with $0\le E_\alpha\le 1$ and $\sum_\alpha E_\alpha=1$. These
induce probabilities
$p(\alpha)=\bbE_\mu[E_\alpha]$.

\begin{theorem}[POVM correspondence]\label{thm:povm_correspondence}
Under Assumption~\ref{A:Hilbert}, there exists a POVM $\{F_\alpha\}$ on $\calH$
such that for all finite-outcome measurements at time $k^\star$,
\[
p(\alpha)= \bbE_\mu[E_\alpha] = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho F_\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By GNS, the linear functional $E\mapsto \bbE_\mu[E]$ on the commutative von
Neumann algebra generated by cylinder events at time $k^\star$ is represented
as a state on a Hilbert space; Naimark dilation then realises the effects as
compressed projections, yielding a POVM $\{F_\alpha\}$ with the stated property.
\end{proof}

Moreover, \emph{state update} (disturbance) can be encoded by a family of
completely positive (CP), trace-nonincreasing maps $\{\mathcal{J}_\alpha\}$
(\emph{quantum instrument}) such that
\[
p(\alpha)=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{J}_\alpha(\rho)),\qquad
\rho \mapsto \frac{\mathcal{J}_\alpha(\rho)}{\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{J}_\alpha(\rho))}
\ \text{for the posterior}.
\]
In the process picture, $\mathcal{J}_\alpha$ corresponds to the pruning
$\mu\mapsto \mu^\alpha$; under dilation, there exists an ancilla space and
unitary $U$ producing Kraus operators for $\{\mathcal{J}_\alpha\}$ (Stinespring).

\begin{theorem}[Instrument equivalence]\label{thm:instrument_equivalence}
Assume Hilbert reconstruction. There exists an ancilla Hilbert space $\calK$, a
unitary $U$ on $\calH\otimes \calK$, and a projection-valued observable on
$\calK$ with outcomes $\alpha$ such that the process-level update
$\mu\mapsto \mu^\alpha$ corresponds to the quantum instrument
$\mathcal{J}_\alpha(\rho)= \mathrm{Tr}_{\calK}\!\big[U(\rho\otimes\sigma) U^\dagger
(\mathbf{1}\otimes \Pi_\alpha)\big]$, with $\sigma$ a fixed ancilla state and
$\Pi_\alpha$ the outcome projector on $\calK$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Idea]
Standard Stinespring/Naimark construction for instruments, with the classical
outcome algebra identified with $\sigma(\pi_{k^\star})$ via the spectral theorem.
Consistency of probabilities follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:povm_correspondence}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Locality and no-signalling on $Y_4$}

Finally, we record a minimal assumption under which conditioning does not enable
superluminal signalling in $Y_4$.

\begin{assumption}[Microcausality on $Y_4$]\label{A:microcausality}
If two measurements occur at times $k^\star$ and $\tilde k^\star$ on regions
$R,\tilde R\subset Y_4$ that are spacelike separated in $g^{(4)}$, then the
constraint change $F\to F'$ for $R$ modifies only the conditional distribution
of $\pi_{k^\star}$ and its causal future; similarly for $\tilde R$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[No-signalling]\label{prop:no_signalling}
Under Assumption~\ref{A:microcausality}, the marginal law of $\pi_{\tilde k}$ on
$\tilde R$ with $\tilde k<k^\star$ (or $\tilde k$ outside the future of $R$) is
unchanged by conditioning on outcomes at $R$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction, the pruning to $E_\alpha\in \sigma(\pi_{k^\star})$ leaves
$\calF_{\le \tilde k}$-measurable events unaffected when $\tilde k$ is not in the
causal future of $R$; hence the marginal is unchanged.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Remark.}
Assumption~6.10 matches the locality analysis in the MTT spine: the equal‑time support
and $L^1$ control of the bilocal kernel used here coincide with the hypotheses under which
Appendix~F of Fixed Points~VI proves local well‑posedness and unchanged characteristic cones
for the reduced hyperbolic system. Thus microcausality and no‑signaling used here are the
same as in the QFT projection layer. 

\subsection{Summary}
Measurement in the process picture is \emph{just} conditioning on the sub-$\sigma$-algebra
generated by the outcome at $k^\star$: mathematically, pruning plus
renormalisation. The updated dynamics remain indivisible (except in degenerate
cases). Upon Hilbert reconstruction, outcome probabilities correspond to POVMs,
and the update corresponds to a quantum instrument obtained by Stinespring/Naimark
dilation.
\section{Discussion and Conclusions}

\subsection{Conceptual synthesis}

In this work we have developed a rigorous connection between Modal Triplet Theory
(MTT) and the mathematical theory of \emph{indivisible stochastic processes}.
Our starting point was the modal-geometric setup (Section~2), where harmonic
projectors on the internal three-manifolds $B_n$ isolate the \emph{coherent
sector}. Projection to external spacetime $Y_4$ yields observable degrees of
freedom. We then established:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item The projection of MTT dynamics induces \emph{conditional probability kernels}
$K_{\Delta\tau}(\cdot\mid h_k)$ depending on the full history prefix $h_k$;
\item By Kolmogorov extension, these kernels define a unique probability measure
on the infinite history space $Q^\N$ (Section~3);
\item The dependence on complete histories implies that the induced process is
generically \emph{non-Markovian} and hence \emph{indivisible} (Theorems~\ref{thm:nonMarkov}, \ref{thm:indivisible});
\item The stochastic weights are determined by modal action increments
$\Delta A$, leading to exponential weighting $\exp(-\Delta A/\hbar)$ and
consistent $g$-functions (Section~5);
\item Measurement arises as conditioning on sub-$\sigma$-algebras of the path
space, with update given by pruning and renormalisation. Sequential
measurements compose associatively, and under Hilbert reconstruction, these
updates correspond precisely to POVMs and quantum instruments
(Section~\ref{sec:measurement}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Comparison with MTT$\Rightarrow$QM derivation}

Our earlier derivation (MTT$\Rightarrow$QM) established a path from MTT to the
standard Hilbert-space quantum formalism, using noncontextual measures and
Gleason--Busch to recover the Born rule. The present analysis provides an
\emph{orthogonal} realisation: MTT dynamics can be projected not only into
linear Hilbert evolution but also into a stochastic process on observable
histories. This process is necessarily indivisible, matching the notion of
``non-Markovian quantum processes'' introduced in foundational studies
(e.g.\ by Buscemi, Kretschmann--Werner, etc.), but here obtained directly from
the modal dynamics.

Thus, MTT simultaneously underpins two apparently distinct frameworks:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item The Hilbert-space quantum formalism, with unitary evolution and POVM
measurements;
\item The theory of indivisible stochastic processes, with action-based weights
and measure-theoretic conditioning.
\end{itemize}
The equivalence of probabilities under Hilbert reconstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:gleason})
ensures consistency of these perspectives.
\paragraph{Link to Bell/Jarrett.}
Indivisibility here is the stochastic mirror of outcome‑independence failure in Bell's
Jarrett decomposition: operational parameter independence (no‑signaling of marginals) holds
by construction, while the full‑history dependence of our kernels encodes outcome‑level
correlations. This matches the analysis of modal factorization failure under MI in the
Bell/beables paper of this series. 

\subsection{Physical interpretation}

The non-Markovianity of the induced process reflects the persistence of modal
information across all scales: the external field $q_k$ at time $k$ is not
autonomous, but depends on the entire internal modal trajectory. This explains
why \emph{memory effects} appear intrinsically in quantum phenomena such as
entanglement, decoherence, and contextuality. Within MTT, these effects are
geometrically rooted in the harmonic structure of the compact $B_n$.

Measurement, in the stochastic picture, is not a physical collapse but a
\emph{selection of histories} by conditioning. The ``disturbance'' is simply the
renormalisation of probabilities after pruning incompatible histories. Upon
Hilbert reconstruction, this recovers exactly the CP-map (instrument) update
rule of quantum theory. Thus, MTT yields a unified resolution of the measurement
problem: there is no discontinuity, only measure-theoretic conditioning on
indivisible processes.

\subsection{Implications and outlook}

Our analysis demonstrates that MTT provides a \emph{rigorous realisation of
indivisible processes}. This has several implications:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item It establishes MTT as a deeper framework unifying both Hilbert-space
quantum mechanics and the theory of non-Markovian stochastic processes;
\item It explains the ubiquity of indivisibility and contextuality in quantum
phenomena as consequences of modal-geometric dynamics;
\item It opens the door to new applications: modelling quantum thermodynamics
with memory, analysing quantum causal structures, and exploring beyond-quantum
processes (where indivisibility persists but Hilbert reconstruction may fail).
\end{enumerate}

Future directions include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Constructing explicit examples of indivisible processes from concrete MTT
backgrounds (e.g.\ specific compact manifolds $B_n$ with known spectra);
\item Extending the formalism to continuous-time limits and operator-algebraic
formulations of complete connections;
\item Investigating whether beyond-quantum indivisible processes predicted by MTT
could have experimental signatures in non-Markovian open systems.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion}

We conclude that Modal Triplet Theory, when projected onto observable degrees
of freedom, gives rise naturally to \emph{indivisible stochastic processes}. The
mathematical construction is rigorous: action increments induce normalised
kernels, path measures exist uniquely by Kolmogorov extension, probabilities are
noncontextual and $\sigma$-additive, and measurements are conditioning
operations. Under Hilbert reconstruction, these probabilities agree exactly with
the Born rule, while the indivisible dynamics explain the origin of non-Markovian
features in quantum theory. Thus, MTT provides a unified, geometrically grounded
foundation for both quantum mechanics and the broader landscape of stochastic
indivisible processes.

\section{Action increments and measurability}
\label{app:Action}

\subsection{Definition of $\Delta A$}

For each step $h_k=(q_0,\ldots,q_k)$ and candidate extension $q_{k+1}$, define
\[
\Delta A(q_k\!\to\! q_{k+1}\mid h_{k-1})
:= A(\Phi_{\Delta\tau}(m_k,q_k)) - A(m_k,q_k),
\]
where $m_k$ is the internal modal configuration determined by the evolution
$\Phi_\tau$ from initial data, and $A(m,q)$ is the MTT action functional
restricted to $(m,q)\in M\times Q$. By construction $\Delta A\ge 0$ (positivity
of modal energy increments).

\subsection{Measurability}

\begin{lemma}[Borel measurability of $\Delta A$]
For fixed $h_{k-1}$, the map $q_{k+1}\mapsto \Delta A(q_k\!\to\! q_{k+1}\mid h_{k-1})$
is Borel measurable on $(Q,\calB(Q))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi_\tau$ is continuous on $H_{\mathrm{coh}}$ by hypothesis (H\ref{H2}), and
$A(\cdot)$ is continuous on $M\times Q$ by (H\ref{H3}). Therefore composition
is continuous in $q_{k+1}$. Continuity implies Borel measurability.
\end{proof}

This ensures that the $g$-function \eqref{eq:gfunction} is a Borel kernel.

\section{GNS and Hilbert reconstruction}
\label{app:GNS}

\subsection{Cylinder algebra and state functional}

Let $\calA$ be the commutative $C^\ast$-algebra generated by indicator functions
of cylinder sets in $Q^\N$. Define $\omega:\calA\to \C$ by $\omega(f)=\bbE_\mu[f]$,
where $\mu$ is the path-space probability measure. Then $\omega$ is a positive
linear functional with $\omega(1)=1$.

\subsection{GNS construction}

By the Gelfand--Naimark--Segal (GNS) construction, there exists a Hilbert space
$\calH$, a representation $\pi:\calA\to \mathcal{B}(\calH)$, and a cyclic vector
$\Omega\in\calH$ such that $\omega(f)=\langle \Omega,\pi(f)\Omega\rangle$. The
probability of an event $E\in\calF$ is then $\langle \Omega,\pi(\mathbf{1}_E)\Omega\rangle$.

\subsection{Projection-valued measures}

For finite partitions $\{Q_\alpha\}$ at time $k$, the cylinder indicators
$\mathbf{1}_{E_\alpha}$ generate a finite family of commuting projections
$\pi(\mathbf{1}_{E_\alpha})$. This defines a PVM $\{P_\alpha\}$ with
$\sum_\alpha P_\alpha=I$. The state vector $\Omega$ corresponds to a density
operator $\rho$ via $\omega(f)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho \pi(f))$. Thus the stochastic
probabilities $\mu(E_\alpha)$ match Born probabilities $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_\alpha)$.

\section{Complete connections and indivisibility}

\subsection{Complete connections formalism}

A stochastic process on $Q^\N$ with transition kernels
$K(\cdot\mid h_k)$ depending on the entire history $h_k$ is called a
\emph{complete connection}. See e.g.\ Iosifescu \& Grigorescu,
\emph{Dependence with Complete Connections}, for general theory.

\subsection{Criterion for indivisibility}

\begin{theorem}[Indivisibility criterion for complete connections]
Let $\{K(\cdot\mid h_k)\}$ be a complete connection on $Q^\N$. If there exists
no finite memory length $\ell$ such that $K(\cdot\mid h_k)$ depends only on the
last $\ell$ coordinates of $h_k$, then the process is indivisible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If such $\ell$ existed, then the process would be a Markov chain of order $\ell$,
and its $t$-step kernels could be factorised into one-step kernels, i.e.\
the process would be divisible. Conversely, if dependence extends to all past
prefixes, then no finite $\ell$ suffices, and no factorisation into shorter-time
kernels exists. Hence indivisibility follows.
\end{proof}

In MTT, $\Delta A$ depends on modal energy functionals referencing the entire
history, so the dependence length is infinite. Thus indivisibility is generic.

\section{Worked example: Toy model}
\label{app:example}

We illustrate with a toy MTT model yielding an indivisible process.

\subsection{Setup}

Let $Q=\{0,1\}$ with discrete $\sigma$-algebra. Suppose internal moduli $m_k$
carry memory of the entire sequence of flips up to time $k$. Define
\[
\Delta A(q_k\!\to\! q_{k+1}\mid h_{k-1})
= \begin{cases}
\alpha + \beta \, N_1(h_{k-1}), & q_{k+1}=1,\\
\alpha + \beta \, N_0(h_{k-1}), & q_{k+1}=0,
\end{cases}
\]
where $N_0,N_1$ count occurrences of $0,1$ in the history.

\subsection{Transition probabilities}

Then
\[
\bbP(q_{k+1}=1\mid h_k) \propto
\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{\hbar}[\alpha+\beta N_1(h_{k})]\right),
\]
\[
\bbP(q_{k+1}=0\mid h_k) \propto
\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{\hbar}[\alpha+\beta N_0(h_{k})]\right).
\]

\subsection{Indivisibility}

Since transition probabilities depend on counts of all past states, there is no
finite memory $\ell$ that suffices. Hence the process is indivisible. Upon Hilbert
reconstruction, these probabilities correspond to Born weights for a qubit in a
non-Markovian environment.
