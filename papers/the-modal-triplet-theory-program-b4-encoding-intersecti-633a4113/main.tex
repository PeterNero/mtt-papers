\begin{abstract}
We give a typed formulation of encoding intersections and determine when such
an intersection is actually rigid.  Compatibility means that a declared set
of geometric, redundancy, quantum, anomaly, and overlap constraints has at
least one common realization.  Local rigidity means that a realization is
isolated after the declared equivalences have been quotiented.  Infinitesimal
rigidity, persistence, and global uniqueness are different properties.

On a finite-dimensional moduli chart, if the combined constraint map has
injective derivative at a solution, that solution is locally isolated.  For
clean transverse constraint submanifolds, the intersection dimension is the
ambient dimension minus the sum of codimensions.  These theorems make rigidity
conditional on the category, representation class, topology, overlap maps,
anomaly equations, and deformation notion.  Mere coexistence of three labels
does not imply rigidity: a compatible intersection can contain a continuum,
and several isolated realizations can all be locally rigid.

We distinguish classical bundle-cocycle consistency from quantum gauge
anomalies.  Anomaly cancellation is necessary for a declared chiral quantum
gauge realization but does not select a unique group or representation.
Within the fixed selected finite MTT carrier, later exact packets establish a
48-state family-diagonal chiral representation, the faithful global group
$(SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_6$, and a unique anomaly-free
hypercharge line for the completed finite algebra.  We reproduce the relevant
anomaly cancellations and state their exact scope.  They prove one selected
compatibility branch, not an exhaustive classification of alternative
representations, topologies, actions, or ultraviolet completions.  Program B4
therefore supplies a conditional rigidity theorem and a Standard Model
compatibility certificate, not a uniqueness theorem for the observed theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{How to Read Program B4}

This paper studies a question that is easy to phrase too strongly: if several
encodings agree on one realization, has that realization been selected
uniquely?  The answer depends on what kind of agreement has been proved.
Program B4 therefore uses four distinct levels of claim:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Compatibility:} at least one candidate satisfies all declared
constraints.
\item \textbf{Local rigidity:} a particular candidate has no nearby
inequivalent deformation that also satisfies them.
\item \textbf{Conditional uniqueness:} a datum such as the hypercharge line is
unique inside an already fixed carrier and representation class.
\item \textbf{Global selection:} every allowed candidate has been classified
and exactly one equivalence class survives.
\end{enumerate}
Most of the constructive Standard Model evidence used here reaches the first
level and selected instances of the third.  The Jacobian and transversality
results explain how the second can be certified.  The fourth remains a
classification problem and cannot be obtained merely by strengthening the
description of one successful branch.

\subsection{Object picture: filters on a candidate landscape}

Think of $\Mod$ as a landscape whose points encode complete candidate
realizations, not just numerical parameter values.  One point may include a
global gauge group, a bundle, matter representations, overlap maps, a quantum
algebra, and an action.  Each constraint locus removes the candidates that
fail one part of the contract.  Their intersection $\Sol$ is what remains
after all declared filters have been applied.

Three geometrically different outcomes are possible.  The filters may leave a
curve, so compatible theories still deform continuously.  They may leave
several isolated points, so every survivor is locally rigid but no survivor is
globally preferred.  Or they may leave one point in the declared candidate
class.  Only the last case proves uniqueness, and even then the declaration of
the candidate class is part of the theorem.

\subsection{Argument map}

Sections~3 and 4 define the candidate space, its quotient, and the local rank
tests.  Sections~5 and 6 separate classical gluing from quantum anomaly
cancellation.  Section~7 then inserts the selected A46--A50 finite-carrier
data and checks that they form one coherent Standard Model compatibility
packet.  Section~8 explains why this successful packet is not yet an
exhaustive selection theorem.  This order is deliberate: the local
mathematics first tells us exactly what the later computational packets do and
do not establish.

\section{Typed Encoding-Intersection Data}

\subsection{The category must be fixed}

\begin{definition}[Intersection datum]\label{def:intersection-datum}
An \emph{encoding-intersection datum} consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a category or structured class $\Enc$ of candidate realizations;
\item an equivalence relation or groupoid of admissible re-encodings;
\item a topology, smooth structure, or deformation functor when rigidity is
to be discussed;
\item constraint predicates or maps for geometric transport, redundancy,
quantum reconstruction, anomalies, and overlap compatibility; and
\item declared boundary conditions, regularity, and source data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Examples of data hidden by an untyped ``encoding space'' include spacetime
dimension and signature, spin or $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure, the global form
of a gauge group, principal-bundle topology, matter representations, the
observable algebra, and the class of allowed deformations.

\subsection{Constraint loci}

After quotienting or choosing a local slice for the declared equivalences, let
$\Mod$ denote a moduli object of candidate realizations.  Write
\[
 \Geo,\quad \Red,\quad \Qua,\quad \Ano,\quad \Ovl
 \subseteq \Mod
\]
for the loci satisfying the supplied geometric, redundancy, quantum, anomaly,
and overlap contracts.  The common solution locus is
\[
 \Sol
 =
 \Geo\cap\Red\cap\Qua\cap\Ano\cap\Ovl.
\]
The five labels are organizational.  They need not be independent, smooth, or
nonempty.

\begin{definition}[Compatibility and realization]
The datum is \emph{compatible} when $\Sol\ne\varnothing$.  A
\emph{realization} is an equivalence class $[e]\in\Sol$.
\end{definition}

Compatibility is an existence statement.  Exhibiting one realization proves
nonemptiness and nothing about the number of other realizations.

\subsection{Four different rigidity predicates}

\begin{definition}[Local rigidity]\label{def:local-rigidity}
A realization $[e]\in\Sol$ is \emph{locally rigid} if it is isolated in
$\Sol$ with the declared quotient topology.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Infinitesimal rigidity]\label{def:inf-rigidity}
On a smooth local moduli chart where the constraints are represented by a map
$F$, a solution $e$ is \emph{infinitesimally rigid} if
\[
 \ker dF_e=\{0\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Global uniqueness]
The datum is \emph{globally unique} if $\Sol$ contains exactly one equivalence
class.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Persistence]
A solution \emph{persists} under a declared parameter perturbation if a nearby
solution exists for every sufficiently small allowed parameter, with the
declared regularity.
\end{definition}

Local rigidity, infinitesimal rigidity, persistence, and global uniqueness do
not imply one another without additional hypotheses.  In particular, an
overdetermined isolated solution can disappear under a small perturbation.

\section{Conditional Rigidity Theorems}

\subsection{A full-rank criterion}

\begin{theorem}[Injective-Jacobian local rigidity]\label{thm:jacobian}
Let $\Mod$ be a smooth $n$-manifold, let
$F:\Mod\to\mathbb R^m$ be smooth with $m\ge n$, and let $e\in F^{-1}(0)$.
If
\[
 \rank dF_e=n,
\]
then $e$ is locally isolated in $F^{-1}(0)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $dF_e$ is injective, there is a linear projection
$L:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ such that
$d(L\circ F)_e$ is invertible.  By the inverse-function theorem,
$L\circ F$ is a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of $e$ onto a neighborhood
of zero.  In that neighborhood,
$F(x)=0$ implies $(L\circ F)(x)=0$, hence $x=e$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quotient directions]
If a group acts by equivalences, orbit directions normally lie in the kernel of
the unquotiented derivative.  Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian} must be applied on a
valid local slice or to the deformation complex modulo infinitesimal
automorphisms.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Transverse intersections}

\begin{theorem}[Dimension of a transverse compatibility locus]
\label{thm:transverse}
Let $\Mod$ be an $n$-manifold and let
$C_1,\ldots,C_k\subseteq\Mod$ be embedded submanifolds of codimensions
$c_1,\ldots,c_k$.  If they meet transversely at every point of their common
intersection, then that intersection is a submanifold of dimension
\[
 n-\sum_{j=1}^k c_j.
\]
In particular, dimension zero gives local discreteness; positive dimension
gives continuous compatible deformations~\cite{GuilleminPollack,Lee}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Locally represent each $C_j$ as the regular zero set of a submersion.
Transversality says that the combined derivative is surjective.  The
regular-value theorem applied to the combined map gives the stated dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What a rigidity certificate must show]
A finite-dimensional local rigidity claim is certified by an explicit
constraint map and a rank or equivalent deformation-complex calculation after
all equivalence directions are removed.  Merely listing several requirements
does not establish their independence.
\end{corollary}

This criterion also explains why adding more named principles need not make a
theory more rigid.  If two principles impose the same local equation, their
derivative rows are dependent and no new direction is removed.  What matters
is the rank of the combined constraint operator on the quotient tangent
space, not the number of labels attached to it.

\subsection{Three counterexamples}

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility need not be rigid]
\label{prop:continuum}
Three compatible constraint labels can have a continuous common solution set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\Mod=\mathbb R^2$ and let all three constraint loci be
\[
 C_1=C_2=C_3=\{(x,y):y=0\}.
\]
Their common intersection is a line.  Repeating one condition under three
names adds no codimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Local rigidity need not give uniqueness]
\label{prop:two-points}
Every point of a compatibility locus can be locally rigid while the datum has
more than one realization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $\Mod=\mathbb R$ and $F(x)=x^2-1$.  The solution locus is
$\{-1,+1\}$.  Both points are isolated and have nonzero derivative, but neither
is globally selected.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Full rank is sufficient, not necessary]
The equation $F(x)=x^2=0$ has an isolated solution at $0$, although
$dF_0=0$.  Singular methods may prove rigidity where
Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian} does not apply.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The word ``generic''}

A genericity theorem requires a topology or measure on the space of constraint
maps, a perturbation class, and a transversality or prevalence theorem.  Even
then, it answers a question inside that chosen ensemble.  Program B4 makes no
unqualified claim that geometric, gauge, and quantum compatibility is
generically empty, finite, or rigid.

\section{Representation, Overlap, and Quantum Contracts}

\subsection{Representation class}

A gauge-representation constraint is meaningful only after fixing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the local Lie algebra and global group;
\item the base dimension, signature, and spin structure;
\item the allowed principal bundles and topological sectors;
\item the matter and scalar representation category;
\item chirality, reality, and family assumptions;
\item boundary conditions and allowed large transformations; and
\item the equivalence relation on representations.
\end{enumerate}

A discrete list of irreducible representations can make every list entry
isolated in the discrete topology.  This does not make one entry globally
unique.  Conversely, continuous coupling or moduli directions can remain after
the representation labels are fixed.

\subsection{Overlap contract}

Let $P\to X$ be a principal $G$-bundle with local transition functions
$g_{\alpha\beta}$.  The classical bundle-gluing condition is
\[
 g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\beta\gamma}g_{\gamma\alpha}=e
\]
on triple overlaps.  A connection, associated matter bundle, and any survivor
or quantum source map must transform compatibly with this cocycle.

\begin{definition}[Typed overlap compatibility]
A candidate intersection is \emph{overlap compatible} when all declared
transition maps satisfy their cocycle laws and every structure map in the
intersection commutes with those transitions on its stated domain.
\end{definition}

Failure of this classical cocycle condition means that the proposed bundle or
descent datum was not constructed.  It is not, by itself, a quantum gauge
anomaly.

\subsection{Quantum contract}

By Program B3, a quantum layer requires at least a complex observable algebra,
a positive state, a representation, dynamics, and effects or instruments.
Gauge symmetry must act by the declared automorphisms and be compatible with
the physical state or constraint construction.  A finite representation table
and anomaly cancellation do not alone supply this quantum contract.

\section{Anomalies: Necessary Constraints, Not a Selection Theorem}

\subsection{Classical and quantum obstructions}

A local quantum gauge anomaly is an obstruction to preserving the gauge Ward
or BRST identities after quantization.  In perturbative chiral gauge theory it
is represented by local cohomological descent data and group-theoretic anomaly
coefficients.  A global anomaly is a possible nontrivial phase or determinant-
line obstruction under a large gauge transformation.  These depend on the
dimension, global group, representations, base topology, and spin data
~\cite{Bertlmann,Bilal,WittenSU2}.

\begin{proposition}[Anomaly cancellation is necessary but not sufficient]
\label{prop:anomaly-necessary}
For a declared chiral quantum gauge realization, cancellation of every anomaly
included in its consistency contract is necessary for gauge invariance.
Cancellation alone does not prove existence, locality, unitarity, ultraviolet
completion, or uniqueness of the theory.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A nonzero anomaly obstructs the declared gauge identity, so the candidate
fails that contract.  For the converse limitation, anomaly coefficients depend
only on part of the theory's data.  Distinct actions, couplings, global groups,
matter sectors, and vector-like extensions can have the same vanishing
coefficients.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anomaly equations do not fix abelian normalization}

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous hypercharge-scaling no-go]
\label{thm:scaling}
Fix the nonabelian representations of a four-dimensional chiral gauge theory.
Suppose a nonzero hypercharge vector $Y$ cancels the mixed
$G^2U(1)$, mixed gravitational-$U(1)$, and $U(1)^3$ anomalies.  Then
$cY$ also cancels them for every real $c$.  Thus anomaly cancellation alone
cannot isolate a nonzero abelian normalization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The mixed nonabelian and gravitational coefficients are homogeneous linear
functions of $Y$, while the cubic coefficient is homogeneous of degree three.
Their zero sets are invariant under $Y\mapsto cY$.
\end{proof}

A primitive charge lattice, global $U(1)$ group, coupling convention, or other
normalization condition must therefore be fixed.  A50 does precisely more than
solve homogeneous anomaly equations: it works inside a selected finite algebra,
fixes the phase coordinates, and selects the primitive null vector in that
declared representation.

\subsection{Anomaly-free extensions}

\begin{proposition}[Vector-like extension counterexample]
\label{prop:vectorlike}
Let a four-dimensional chiral gauge representation be perturbatively
anomaly-free.  Adding a left-handed representation $R_y$ together with its
conjugate $\overline R_{-y}$ leaves all local perturbative gauge and mixed
gravitational anomaly coefficients unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two members contribute opposite cubic nonabelian indices, opposite
$G^2U(1)$ and gravitational-$U(1)$ coefficients, and opposite $U(1)^3$
coefficients.  Their sum vanishes.
\end{proof}

Global anomalies and the rest of the physical contract must still be checked.
Nevertheless, Proposition~\ref{prop:vectorlike} gives infinitely many
representation categories in which anomaly cancellation cannot by itself
select the Standard Model.

\section{A Standard Model Compatibility Packet}

\subsection{Declared chiral carrier}

Use left-handed Weyl fields and one neutral singlet per family:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{field} & SU(3) & SU(2) & Y & \text{multiplicity}\\
\hline
Q     & \mathbf 3             & \mathbf 2 & 1/6  & 6\\
u^c   & \overline{\mathbf 3}  & \mathbf 1 & -2/3 & 3\\
d^c   & \overline{\mathbf 3}  & \mathbf 1 & 1/3  & 3\\
L     & \mathbf 1             & \mathbf 2 & -1/2 & 2\\
e^c   & \mathbf 1             & \mathbf 1 & 1    & 1\\
N^c   & \mathbf 1             & \mathbf 1 & 0    & 1
\end{array}
\]
This is the one-family $\Hilbert_{16}$ used by A46.  Three family copies give
the 48-state chiral carrier.

The table should be read as the concrete common object on which the following
certificates meet.  The anomaly sums test its local quantum consistency, the
center calculation tests which global group acts faithfully, and the A50
nullspace calculation tests which abelian phase direction survives inside the
completed finite algebra.  Agreement of all three calculations on these same

\subsection{Exact local anomaly sums}

Using the fundamental quadratic index $T(\mathbf 3)=T(\mathbf 2)=1/2$,
the one-family mixed anomalies are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{SU(3)^2U(1)}
 &=2\left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac16\right)
   +\left(\frac12\right)\left(-\frac23\right)
   +\left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac13\right)=0,\\
\mathcal A_{SU(2)^2U(1)}
 &=3\left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac16\right)
   +\left(\frac12\right)\left(-\frac12\right)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The abelian and mixed gravitational coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_{U(1)^3}
 &=6\left(\frac16\right)^3
  +3\left(-\frac23\right)^3
  +3\left(\frac13\right)^3
  +2\left(-\frac12\right)^3+1^3+0^3=0,\\
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{grav}^2U(1)}
 &=6\left(\frac16\right)
  +3\left(-\frac23\right)
  +3\left(\frac13\right)
  +2\left(-\frac12\right)+1+0=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the cubic $SU(3)$ anomaly, $Q$ contributes two fundamentals and
$u^c,d^c$ contribute two antifundamentals, so
\[
 \mathcal A_{SU(3)^3}=2-1-1=0.
\]
There is no perturbative cubic $SU(2)$ anomaly for these pseudoreal
representations.  The Witten global anomaly cancels because one family has
three color copies of the $Q$ doublet plus one $L$ doublet, hence four weak
doublets; three families have twelve~\cite{WittenSU2}.  These are exactly the
rows certified in A46.

\subsection{Faithful global group}

The direct product $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ does not act faithfully on
the displayed matter rows.  A47 enumerates the centers and obtains the
diagonal kernel $\mathbb Z_6$.  On that selected carrier the faithful global
group is
\[
 \GSM
 =
 \frac{SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)_Y}{\mathbb Z_6}.
\]
This is stronger than specifying the Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)$, because it records
the global action on the matter representation.

\subsection{The selected hypercharge line}

In A50 the abelian phase coordinates
$(\alpha_{\mathbb C},\mu_{M_3},\nu_{\mathbb C_N})$ satisfy two independent
linear anomaly equations,
\[
 \alpha_{\mathbb C}+3\mu_{M_3}=0,
 \qquad
 \alpha_{\mathbb C}-\nu_{\mathbb C_N}=0.
\]
Their nullspace is one-dimensional with primitive vector
\[
 (3,-1,3),
\]
which emits
\[
 6Y=(1,-4,2,-3,6,0)
\]
on $(Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c,N^c)$.  The cubic anomaly then vanishes.  The word
``unique'' here means one anomaly-free phase line inside the fixed completed
finite algebra and A46 edge representation.  It does not quantify over other
algebras, particle lists, global groups, or ultraviolet mechanisms.

\subsection{Geometric and quantum scope}

On a supplied four-dimensional Lorentzian spin background, these matter
representations can be coupled covariantly to the corresponding gauge bundle.
That establishes compatibility with background geometry; it does not derive
Einstein gravity or quantize gravity.  Likewise, a quantum Standard Model
requires a gauge-fixed or gauge-invariant quantum field construction, states,
renormalization, and observables.  The representation and anomaly packet is
necessary input to that construction, not its replacement.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped Standard Model compatibility]\label{thm:sm}
Fix:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a four-dimensional Lorentzian spin background and admissible gauge
bundles;
\item the A46 three-family chiral carrier;
\item the A47 faithful global group $\GSM$; and
\item the A50 selected hypercharge line.
\end{enumerate}
Then the displayed local gauge and mixed gravitational anomaly coefficients
vanish, the $SU(2)$ Witten parity obstruction vanishes, and the gauge action is
compatible with the declared family-diagonal representation.  This proves a
nonempty selected representation/anomaly intersection.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The local and global anomaly calculations are given above and are multiplied,
without changing zero or even parity, by the three family copies.  A47
identifies the faithful kernel of the action, and A50 identifies the primitive
abelian phase line on the same finite carrier.
\end{proof}

\section{Why This Does Not Select the Standard Model Globally}

\subsection{Existence is not exhaustiveness}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sm} exhibits one point in a declared compatibility locus.
It does not prove that:
\begin{itemize}
\item every other global gauge group or quotient has been classified;
\item every anomaly-free chiral or vector-like representation has been
excluded;
\item every bundle topology or spin background has been considered;
\item every Green--Schwarz, inflow, or ultraviolet cancellation mechanism has
been excluded;
\item the Higgs, Yukawa, flavor, neutrino, or symmetry-breaking sectors are
uniquely selected; or
\item the measured couplings and masses follow from the same source.
\end{itemize}

The neutral singlet $N^c$ itself illustrates the role of the representation
class: because it has $Y=0$ and is nonabelian-neutral, adding or removing it
does not change the elementary gauge anomaly sums above, although it changes
the finite geometry and neutrino sector.  A49-A50 select it through additional
finite-geometry axioms, not through the anomaly table alone.

\subsection{Local versus global uniqueness}

A46-A50 can establish uniqueness statements inside a fixed carrier:
the faithful kernel of one action, the minimal completion under declared
finite-geometry axioms, and the abelian null line in one phase space.  These
are valuable local or conditional selection results.  Proposition
~\ref{prop:two-points} shows why they cannot be promoted to global uniqueness
without a proof that the candidate class itself is exhaustive.

\subsection{Required exhaustive-selection certificate}

\begin{definition}[Exhaustive selection certificate]\label{def:selection}
An \emph{exhaustive selection certificate} for the observed Standard Model
must:
\begin{enumerate}
\item specify the candidate category and equivalence relation;
\item fix the dimension, signature, spin data, global gauge-group class, bundle
topologies, and representation bounds;
\item state all overlap, locality, anomaly, quantum, and action constraints;
\item prove that every candidate is represented in the classification;
\item decide every candidate against the same constraints;
\item prove that the surviving quotient has exactly one element, or report all
survivors; and
\item independently verify any computational enumeration and exactness bounds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Without items 1--4, failure to find an alternative is only a search result.
Without item~5, constraints may have been applied asymmetrically.  Without
item~6, local rigidity has been mistaken for uniqueness.

\section{Scoped B4 Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Typed intersections and conditional rigidity]
\label{thm:scoped}
For the declared data of this paper:
\begin{enumerate}
\item compatibility is nonemptiness of a common typed constraint locus;
\item one compatible realization does not imply local rigidity or uniqueness;
\item an injective combined Jacobian on a quotient moduli chart is sufficient
for local rigidity;
\item a clean transverse intersection has the dimension stated in
Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse};
\item anomaly cancellation is necessary for the corresponding chiral quantum
gauge contract but is not sufficient for a unique theory;
\item homogeneous anomaly equations do not fix nonzero abelian normalization;
\item vector-like pairs preserve local perturbative anomaly cancellation;
\item A46-A50 prove one exact selected finite representation/anomaly branch and
the conditional uniqueness statements internal to that branch; and
\item global Standard Model uniqueness would require the exhaustive certificate
of Definition~\ref{def:selection}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Items 1--2 follow from the definitions and
Propositions~\ref{prop:continuum}--\ref{prop:two-points}.  Item~3 is
Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian}; item~4 is
Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse}.  Item~5 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:anomaly-necessary}; item~6 is
Theorem~\ref{thm:scaling}; item~7 is
Proposition~\ref{prop:vectorlike}.  Item~8 is the scoped content of
Theorem~\ref{thm:sm} and the cited exact packets.  Item~9 is the distinction
between one selected branch and quantification over the candidate category.
\end{proof}

\section{Conclusion}

Intersections can be powerful.  Independent constraints can reduce a large
candidate space to isolated realizations, and the rank and transversality
theorems state exactly when that happens locally.  But the narrowing is a
property of specified equations in a specified category, not a consequence of
counting the words circle, lens, and nil.

The Standard Model provides a concrete compatibility success.  In the selected
finite MTT branch, the chiral carrier, anomaly table, faithful
$\mathbb Z_6$ quotient, and shared hypercharge line are exact and mutually
consistent.  This is stronger than an analogy.  Its honest meaning is also
narrower than inevitability: it establishes one selected branch and several
conditional uniqueness statements inside that branch.

Program B4 now provides the missing logical bridge.  Compatibility proves
existence, a Jacobian or deformation complex proves local rigidity, and an
exhaustive classification proves global uniqueness.  Keeping those three
certificates separate allows later MTT results to strengthen the Standard
Model case without repeatedly turning a successful realization into an
unsupported theorem that no alternative exists.
