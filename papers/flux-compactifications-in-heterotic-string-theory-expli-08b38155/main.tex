\begin{abstract}
We determine which parts of two proposed heterotic flux constructions survive
the full Hull--Strominger proof obligations.  The diagonal invariant Hermitian
structure on the Iwasawa manifold is balanced, and its torsion and
\(\dd H\) are computed exactly.  The proposed bundle, however, does not exist
as claimed.  One of its alleged first-Chern forms is not closed; the monad maps
were not constructed as global sections; the printed Dolbeault operator is not
integrable; and a connection on the stated trivial smooth rank-three carrier
cannot have third Chern number six.  Within the declared one-entry repair
ansatz, the Maurer--Cartan equation has a unique signed repair, but the repaired
family is a single complex-gauge orbit with a non-scalar holomorphic
commutant, so it cannot supply the claimed stable bundle.  The old anomaly,
three-generation, and normalized-Yukawa conclusions therefore do not follow.
The Lens--Nil model is a balanced real \(SU(3)\)-structure comparison model,
but its almost-complex structure is non-integrable and hence it is not a
Hull--Strominger compactification.  We finish by separating the exact
\(q=79\) finite and rank-two Hermitian--Yang--Mills evidence, and the
existential projective rank-nine hidden connection, from the still-open
common visible--hidden problem, and state a typed completion contract for a
future Fu--Yau endpoint.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Hull--Strominger system is attractive because geometry, gauge data,
torsion, and anomaly cancellation must all hold on the same compact complex
threefold.  That coupling also makes proposed solutions unusually easy to
overstate.  A balanced metric is not yet a heterotic vacuum; a formal matrix
of \((0,1)\)-forms is not yet a holomorphic bundle; a cohomological Chern-class
identity is not yet the differential Bianchi identity; and a topological
index is not yet a computed four-dimensional Yukawa coupling.

An earlier version of this paper presented explicit solutions on the Iwasawa
manifold and on a Lens--Nil geometry.  The present version re-executes those
claims against the mathematical type of every object.  The result is mixed but
useful:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item the local Iwasawa \(SU(3)\)-structure and torsion calculation survives;
\item the proposed Iwasawa rank-three bundle, anomaly match, generation count,
      and Yukawa normalization do not survive;
\item Lens--Nil survives as an auxiliary real \(SU(3)\)-structure model, but
      not as a Hull--Strominger background;
\item the selected \(q=79\) branch remains a plausible Fu--Yau-oriented
      research direction, with exact finite and separate rank-two HYM results,
      but its physical visible--hidden bundle endpoint is still open.
\end{enumerate}

This is not a no-go theorem for all heterotic solutions on the Iwasawa
manifold.  Invariant solutions on complex Lie groups and nilmanifolds are
known under carefully specified choices of tangent and gauge
connections~\cite{FernandezEtAl2009,FeiYau2015}.  The result here is narrower:
the particular bundle and coefficient chain printed in the earlier paper does
not establish such a solution.

\section{The proof obligations for a heterotic solution}
\label{sec:obligations}

Let \(X\) be a compact complex threefold with a nowhere-vanishing holomorphic
\((3,0)\)-form \(\Omega\), Hermitian form \(\omega\), dilaton \(\Phi\), gauge
bundle \(V\), and unitary connections \(A\) on \(V\) and \(\nabla\) on \(TX\).
In one standard first-order convention, the Hull--Strominger equations include
\begin{align}
\dd\!\left(\|\Omega\|_\omega\omega^2\right)&=0,
\label{eq:balanced}\\
F_A^{0,2}=0,\qquad F_A\wedge\omega^2&=0,
\label{eq:hym}\\
H=\ii(\bar\partial-\partial)\omega,\qquad
\dd H&=\frac{\alpha'}{4}
\left(\tr R_\nabla\wedge R_\nabla-\tr F_A\wedge F_A\right).
\label{eq:bianchi}
\end{align}
Trace normalization and the choice of \(\nabla\) must be fixed consistently.
If the supersymmetry equations are also required to imply the first-order
equations of motion, the tangent connection must obey the corresponding
instanton condition.  These choices cannot be changed midway through a
coefficient comparison~\cite{Strominger1986,Hull1986,DelaOssaSvanes2014}.

In the mathematical normalization one identifies
\(\|\Omega\|_\omega\) with the dilaton factor up to convention.  For constant
dilaton and constant \(\|\Omega\|_\omega\),
Equation~\eqref{eq:balanced} reduces to \(\dd(\omega^2)=0\).  This balanced
condition is one row of the system, not a replacement for the other rows.

\begin{definition}[Complete first-order certificate]
A complete certificate for a background in the convention above contains:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.1em]
\item an integrable complex threefold and global \(\Omega\);
\item a positive Hermitian form satisfying the conformally balanced equation;
\item actual holomorphic gauge bundles and HYM connections;
\item a specified tangent connection and its curvature;
\item equality of the differential four-forms in
      Equation~\eqref{eq:bianchi};
\item the required integral or differential-cohomological flux data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

Topological equality
\(c_2(TX)=c_2(V)\) can be necessary, but it does not determine the chosen
curvature representatives.  Likewise, the Li--Yau theorem supplies an HYM
connection after the relevant stability hypotheses are proved; it does not
turn a partial search for invariant sections into a stability
proof~\cite{LiYau1987}.  Established constructions make these dependencies
explicit, for example the Fu--Yau solutions and stable-bundle perturbative
constructions~\cite{FuYau2008,AndreasGarciaFernandez2012}.

\section{The surviving Iwasawa geometry}
\label{sec:iwasawa}

Let \(X=\Gamma\backslash H_3(\C)\) be an Iwasawa manifold with global
left-invariant \((1,0)\)-forms
\begin{equation}
\dd\omega^1=\dd\omega^2=0,\qquad
\dd\omega^3=\omega^1\wedge\omega^2.
\label{eq:iwasawa}
\end{equation}
For \(r_j>0\), set
\begin{equation}
\omega=\frac{\ii}{2}\sum_{j=1}^3 r_j^2
\omega^j\wedge\bar\omega^j,\qquad
\Omega=\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\wedge\omega^3.
\label{eq:su3}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact invariant geometry]
\label{prop:geometry}
The structure in Equation~\eqref{eq:su3} is complex, balanced, and
non-K\"ahler.  With \(H=\ii(\bar\partial-\partial)\omega\),
\begin{align}
H={}&\frac{r_3^2}{2}\left(
\omega^3\wedge\bar\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^2
+\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\wedge\bar\omega^3\right),
\label{eq:H}\\
\dd H={}&r_3^2\,
\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^1\wedge
\omega^2\wedge\bar\omega^2.
\label{eq:dH}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The structure equations have no \((0,2)\) component, so the declared
almost-complex structure is integrable.  Also \(\dd\Omega=0\), because the
only possible term contains
\(\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\wedge\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\).
The only nonclosed summand of \(\omega\) is the \(j=3\) summand.
When \(\dd\omega\) is wedged with either of the remaining diagonal summands,
a repeated \(\omega^1\) or \(\omega^2\) occurs.  Hence
\(\dd(\omega^2)=2\dd\omega\wedge\omega=0\).
Since \(\dd\omega\neq0\), the metric is not K\"ahler.  Splitting
Equation~\eqref{eq:iwasawa} into types gives
\[
\partial\omega=\frac{\ii r_3^2}{2}
\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\wedge\bar\omega^3,\qquad
\bar\partial\omega=\frac{\ii r_3^2}{2}
\omega^3\wedge\bar\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^2,
\]
which yields Equations~\eqref{eq:H} and~\eqref{eq:dH}.
\end{proof}

Define the invariant diagonal \((1,1)\)-forms
\begin{equation}
a=\frac{\ii}{2}\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^1,\qquad
b=\frac{\ii}{2}\omega^2\wedge\bar\omega^2,\qquad
c=\frac{\ii}{2}\omega^3\wedge\bar\omega^3.
\label{eq:abc}
\end{equation}
Then Equation~\eqref{eq:dH} is equivalently
\begin{equation}
\dd H=-4r_3^2\,a\wedge b.
\label{eq:dHab}
\end{equation}
The earlier construction treated all three forms in
Equation~\eqref{eq:abc} as first-Chern representatives.  That is the first
decisive type error.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form obstruction]
\label{prop:closed}
Within the span of \(a,b,c\), a form \(x a+y b+z c\) is closed if and only if
\(z=0\).  In particular, \(c\) cannot represent the first Chern class of a
line bundle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first two forms are closed, while
\begin{equation}
\dd c=\frac{\ii}{2}\left(
\omega^1\wedge\omega^2\wedge\bar\omega^3
-\omega^3\wedge\bar\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^2\right)\neq0.
\label{eq:dc}
\end{equation}
The \((2,1)\) and \((1,2)\) summands in Equation~\eqref{eq:dc} are linearly
independent, so \(z\,\dd c=0\) exactly when \(z=0\).
A Chern--Weil representative of \(c_1\) is closed; therefore \(c\) is
ineligible.  Closedness is only the first gate: integrality must still be
checked by periods on the chosen lattice.
\end{proof}

\section{Why the proposed rank-three bundle fails}
\label{sec:bundle}

\subsection{The monad data were not global bundle data}

For a genuine monad
\[
0\longrightarrow K_1\stackrel{f}{\longrightarrow}
\bigoplus_i L_i\stackrel{g}{\longrightarrow}K_2
\longrightarrow0,
\]
the \(L_i,K_j\) must first exist as line bundles.  Every entry of \(f\) and
\(g\) must then be a global holomorphic section of the appropriate Hom bundle,
for example
\[
g_i\in H^0\!\left(X,\Hom(L_i,K_2)\right)
=H^0\!\left(X,K_2\otimes L_i^{-1}\right).
\]
A ``constant matrix in a left-invariant frame'' is not automatically such a
section when its source and target line bundles are nonisomorphic.

The former monad used combinations containing \(c\) as first-Chern data.
Proposition~\ref{prop:closed} prevents those line bundles from being defined
with the advertised curvature.  No global Hom sections, exactness loci, or
locally free cohomology sheaf were supplied independently.  Consequently the
formal Chern-character arithmetic did not compute the Chern classes of an
actual rank-three bundle.

The old stability argument had a second logical gap.  The statement
\(H^0(X,E)=0\), even if proved, excludes a map \(\cO_X\to E\); it does not
exclude every line bundle \(L\) of nonnegative slope from mapping into \(E\).
Nor does checking invariant candidates exhaust all coherent subsheaves.
Slope stability requires every proper torsion-free subsheaf to have smaller
slope.  The Li--Yau theorem becomes applicable only after that global
condition is established.

\subsection{The printed Dolbeault operator is not integrable}

Write a left-invariant candidate as
\begin{equation}
\bar\partial_E=\bar\partial+A,\qquad
A=B_1\bar\omega^1+B_2\bar\omega^2+B_3\bar\omega^3.
\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
Because
\(\bar\partial\bar\omega^3=\bar\omega^1\wedge\bar\omega^2\),
the Maurer--Cartan equation is
\begin{equation}
\bar\partial_E^2=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_3+[B_1,B_2]=0.
\label{eq:MC}
\end{equation}

The earlier paper used
\begin{equation}
B_1=\sqrt{\mu}\,E_{13},\qquad
B_2=-\sqrt{\mu}\,E_{31},\qquad
B_3=\mu E_{12},\qquad \mu>0.
\label{eq:oldB}
\end{equation}
Substitution gives
\[
B_3+[B_1,B_2]
=\mu(E_{12}-E_{11}+E_{33})\neq0.
\]
Thus the printed operator does not define a holomorphic structure.

\begin{theorem}[Minimal signed repair and its obstruction]
\label{thm:repair}
Within the declared one-entry signed repair in which the \(B_2\) arrow is
moved to the composable Heisenberg arrow, Equation~\eqref{eq:MC} forces
\begin{equation}
B_1=\sqrt{\mu}\,E_{13},\qquad
B_2=-\sqrt{\mu}\,E_{32},\qquad
B_3=\mu E_{12}.
\label{eq:repair}
\end{equation}
This family is one \(SL(3,\C)\) complex-gauge orbit.  Its holomorphic
commutant has complex dimension two, so it is not simple and cannot be the
claimed stable rank-three bundle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(E_{13}E_{32}=E_{12}\) and \(E_{32}E_{13}=0\),
\([B_1,B_2]=-\mu E_{12}\), proving integrability.
For
\[
G_\mu=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{\mu},\mu^{-1/2},1)
\in SL(3,\C),
\]
one has \(B_j(\mu)=G_\mu B_j(1)G_\mu^{-1}\) for all three matrices.  Hence
\(\mu\) labels complex gauge, not distinct holomorphic data.
The matrices commuting with \(E_{13}\), \(E_{32}\), and \(E_{12}\) are exactly
\[
\{\,\lambda I+\nu E_{12}:\lambda,\nu\in\C\,\}.
\]
The non-scalar element \(E_{12}\) is therefore a holomorphic endomorphism.
A slope-stable holomorphic vector bundle is simple, so the repaired object
cannot satisfy the claimed stability conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Topological obstruction on the stated carrier]
\label{cor:trivial}
If Equation~\eqref{eq:A} is a connection on the stated smooth carrier
\(E_{\mathrm{sm}}=X\times\C^3\), then
\[
c_1(E_{\mathrm{sm}})=c_2(E_{\mathrm{sm}})
=c_3(E_{\mathrm{sm}})=0.
\]
In particular, it cannot have \(\int_Xc_3=6\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The total Chern class of a trivial smooth bundle is one.  Changing a connection
changes a Chern--Weil representative by an exact transgression form, not the
underlying characteristic class.  A nonzero local six-form expression cannot
override this topological fact.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences for anomaly cancellation and Yukawa claims}
\label{sec:downstream}

The preceding obstruction propagates to every downstream claim that used the
old bundle.

\subsection{The Bianchi identity}

Complex parallelizability makes the holomorphic tangent bundle of the Iwasawa
manifold trivial, so \(c_2(TX)=0\).  This gives a cohomological simplification,
not the differential identity in Equation~\eqref{eq:bianchi}.  That identity
still requires specified connections and their actual curvature four-forms.

The old hidden abelian flux also does not pass the stated HYM check.  Its
nonzero component was proportional to \(a+2b\).  For the positive diagonal
metric,
\[
\Lambda_\omega(a+2b)>0,
\]
so it is not primitive.  Placing the negative of the same form along an
independent Cartan generator does not cancel a Lie-algebra-valued primitivity
condition; each independent component must vanish.  In addition, integer
coefficients in an invariant coframe do not prove flux quantization until the
period lattice is computed.

Because the visible connection is invalid, the hidden flux is not HYM as
written, and the tangent-curvature convention was not independently certified,
the former coefficient equation for \(r_3\) is withdrawn.  A rebuilt solution
must recompute all three curvature terms from one declared set of connections.

\subsection{Generation number and Yukawa coupling}

For a genuine special-unitary bundle on a compact complex threefold, an index
formula can relate net chirality to \(\frac12\int_Xc_3(V)\) under the standard
hypotheses.  Corollary~\ref{cor:trivial} shows that the old carrier does not
provide the advertised value.  The three-generation conclusion therefore
does not follow from this Iwasawa construction.

A Yukawa coupling requires considerably more data:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item a valid holomorphic bundle and its relevant cohomology groups;
\item normalized harmonic representatives for the matter modes;
\item the bundle contraction or invariant tensor;
\item evaluation of the corresponding overlap integral.
\end{enumerate}
Orthonormality of three representatives does not imply that their wedge
product equals \(\bar\Omega\), even up to phase.  The old assertion
\(\lambda_{123}=1\) and its rank-one mass interpretation are therefore
withdrawn.  This does not conflict with separate MTT finite/profile Yukawa
results; it says only that this compactification did not derive them.

\section{Why Lens--Nil is not a Hull--Strominger solution}
\label{sec:lensnil}

A real six-manifold can carry an \(SU(3)\) structure \((\omega,\Omega)\) even
when its induced almost-complex structure is not integrable.  Conditions such
as \(\dd(\omega^2)=0\) still make sense in that setting.  The
Hull--Strominger system, however, uses a complex threefold, holomorphic
bundles, and the Dolbeault operators \(\partial,\bar\partial\) with
\(\bar\partial^2=0\).

The former Lens--Nil section explicitly found \(\dd\Omega\neq0\) and described
the structure as non-integrable.  It then used
\(H=\ii(\bar\partial-\partial)\omega\), holomorphic bundle language, and
Hull--Strominger anomaly equations as if integrability had been established.
Those steps are incompatible.

\begin{proposition}[Correct status of the Lens--Nil construction]
The balanced Lens--Nil ansatz from the earlier paper is an auxiliary real
\(SU(3)\)-structure model.  It is not a solution of the Hull--Strominger
system in the complex-geometric sense used in Section~\ref{sec:obligations}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrability is a prerequisite for the Dolbeault complex and for holomorphic
gauge bundles.  The ansatz fails that prerequisite by its own
\(\dd\Omega\neq0\) calculation.  Balancedness alone cannot restore it.
\end{proof}

This conclusion does not say that Lens or Nil geometry is useless in MTT.
It can remain an auxiliary spectral, transport, or rank-filter model.  What
must be retired is the inference from that auxiliary role to a literal
physical Hull--Strominger compactification.  The literal
\(L(3,1)\times\mathrm{Nil}_3\) model is also not the selected \(q=79\)
Fu--Yau candidate; distinct topology cannot be erased by assigning the same
interpretive labels.

the earlier rank-two numerical witness and it should not be relabelled

three factors stable and the rank-nine orbit polystable; twisted
Kobayashi--Hitchin then supplies a qutrit-equivariant projective HYM
connection. The calculation has not emitted a numerical $M$, a selected
$t$, or executable connection coefficients. Those are reproduction and
source-selection tasks, not reasons to reopen the existence conclusion.

\subsection{September update: local observability and the global obstruction}

The baseline above is not an exhaustive inventory of the later q79 research.
There is now a full selected $164\times164$ local Gauss--Manin coefficient

Separately, the completed same-source affine Deligne test proves
$\beta_C(B89)\neq0$. Thus B89 is rejected from the zero locus; neither the
small local interval nor that rejection determines the global answer for
every other cover. These results narrow an actual construction problem
without changing the ranks or carriers of the baseline HYM theorems.

One correction is particularly important for subsequent searches.

\section{A typed completion theorem}
\label{sec:contract}

The useful way forward is to make completion compositional without allowing
objects from different carriers or ranks to be silently merged.

\begin{theorem}[Hull--Strominger assembly contract]
\label{thm:contract}
Fix one compact complex threefold \(X\), one Hermitian metric \(\omega\), one
holomorphic volume form \(\Omega\), one dilaton \(\Phi\), holomorphic bundles
\(V_{\mathrm{vis}},V_{\mathrm{hid}}\), unitary connections
\(A_{\mathrm{vis}},A_{\mathrm{hid}}\), and a specified unitary tangent
connection \(\nabla\).  Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.1em]
\item \(\Omega\) is nowhere vanishing and
      \(\dd(\|\Omega\|_\omega\omega^2)=0\);
\item both gauge curvatures have type \((1,1)\) and are primitive;
\item \(R_\nabla\) obeys the tangent-instanton condition required by the chosen
      first-order convention;
\item with \(H=\ii(\bar\partial-\partial)\omega\), the differential identity
\[
\dd H=\frac{\alpha'}4\left(
\tr R_\nabla^2-\tr F_{\mathrm{vis}}^2-\tr F_{\mathrm{hid}}^2\right)
\]
holds with one fixed trace normalization;
\item the resulting \(H\) and Chern--Simons data define the required global
      differential-cohomological flux class.
\end{enumerate}
Then these data form a first-order heterotic Hull--Strominger background in
the declared convention.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each hypothesis is exactly one typed equation or global patching requirement
from Section~\ref{sec:obligations}.  Because every term is defined on the same
carrier with fixed connections and traces, the equations can be assembled
without changing representatives or importing a result from a different
rank.  The conclusion is therefore the conjunction of the verified rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not add]
Theorem~\ref{thm:contract} is an assembly theorem, not an existence theorem.
It prevents a common logical error: satisfying each row somewhere is not the
same as satisfying all rows on one selected object.  The open MTT task is to
construct the tuple satisfying its hypotheses on \(X_{79}\).
\end{remark}

Three-family physics requires an additional layer.  One must prove the relevant
index on \(V_{\mathrm{vis}}\), identify the massless cohomology groups, choose
the physical embedding and normalization, and compute the overlap map.  None
of those conclusions is supplied merely by Theorem~\ref{thm:contract}.

\section{Interpretation inside MTT}
\label{sec:mtt}

The MTT language of selection and admissibility remains useful if it is kept
typed.  It can organize a search over candidate complex manifolds, bundles,
connections, and finite operators.  It cannot convert a failed
Maurer--Cartan equation into a holomorphic bundle or replace an analytic
existence theorem.

The corrected relation among the recurring geometric labels is:
\[
\text{local Circle--Lens--Nil filtration}
\;\not\equiv\;
L(3,1)\times\mathrm{Nil}_3
\;\not\equiv\;
X_{79}.
\]
Circle, lens, and nil may label phase/holonomy, finite transport, and
anchoring or shear sectors after projection.  The shared circle is common
line-bundle phase data counted once; it is not automatically a compact time
coordinate.  A connection-preserving intertwiner is required before the local
filtration can be identified with vertical operators on the global \(q=79\)
geometry.

This distinction explains why the rank-two HYM theorem is valuable but not
decisive for the physical compactification.  It demonstrates that the
selected analytic machinery can close a nontrivial HYM problem.  It does not
change the bundle rank, Chern class, or carrier on which that theorem was
proved.

\section{Discussion}

The main positive result is methodological as well as algebraic.  The
Iwasawa calculation supplies a clean example of how far local geometry can
progress before the global bundle layer becomes decisive.  The exact repair
in Theorem~\ref{thm:repair} is also informative: integrability can be restored
without producing the desired physics.  Gauge redundancy and simplicity are
independent gates.

The negative conclusions are deliberately local to the constructions audited
here:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item they do not rule out other invariant Iwasawa solutions with properly
      chosen instantons;
\item they do not rule out generalized or nonsupersymmetric uses of a
      non-integrable Lens--Nil \(SU(3)\) structure;
\item they do not invalidate the exact \(q=79\) finite theorem, the topological
      rank-three candidate, or the separate rank-two HYM theorem;
\item they do prevent those separate results from being advertised as one
      completed physical compactification.
\end{itemize}

The most direct research target is now the physical visible bundle gate V1.
A successful construction should begin with an actual holomorphic
rank-three bundle on the selected Fu--Yau carrier, prove its stability in the
same balanced chamber used by the geometry, and only then solve the hidden and
Bianchi rows.  Reusing the rank-two Wiener-contraction architecture is
reasonable; reusing its conclusion without a rank-three operator is not.
